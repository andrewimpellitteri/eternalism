## Verdict

**Partly occupied.** The general growth-to-completed-path representation is
already explicit in classical sequential growth and path-space formulations.
Within Brightwell et al.'s generic Sec. 2 CSG family under discrete general covariance, the relevant measurable
covariant questions are already characterized through relabeling-invariant
events, with stem observables generating them up to null sets. If records are
defined as measurable functions of that same covariant history, equal
distributions follow conditionally from the shared measure; a result claiming
equivalence for that class and observable algebra would risk being true by
definition or an immediate restatement of the existing construction. The
reviewed causal-set comparison makes this scope explicit. It does not model a
physical observer's access to such records.

The remaining bounded question is whether a specified, physically implemented
observer-record process with an explicit access algebra/bound can distinguish
growth-order information from the completed unlabeled history when the records
are not stipulated to be measurable functions of a shared covariant history,
or in a complex-growth model whose measure extends.
This search found records formalisms with explicit local/redundant access and
quantum causal-set growth with a covariant-measurability boundary, but no
predecessor that combines them into that comparison. This is only a scope-limited
search observation, not a novelty or priority claim. Even equality of all
specified record distributions would establish operational equivalence only
for those access assumptions; it would not establish ontological equivalence.

## Six-item comparison: classical CSG and its completed-history representation

1. **Ontology, passage, future:** CSG gives a stochastic law; the completed
   history and growth descriptions are mathematical presentations of that law.
   Passage is an interpretive claim, not a consequence of the measure; stochasticity
   does not imply that the future is ontologically absent.
2. **State, dynamics, causal structure, clock:** finite causal sets and natural
   growth paths with Markov transition probabilities; causal order is the poset
   relation. Element count is a stage index, not by itself an operational clock.
3. **Symmetry and access:** labels are auxiliary/gauge structure in the CSG model,
   not a preferred physical foliation. Brightwell et al.'s theorem concerns the
   generic Sec. 2 family and its covariant sigma-algebra; it supplies no bounded
   embedded-observer access model.
4. **Predictions and empirical status:** probabilities for covariant measurable
   events agree under the growth/path-measure presentation by construction. This
   is model-internal; no empirical discriminator or validated cosmological
   prediction is supplied by these anchors.
5. **Bridge premises:** interpreting growth as objective passage or treating
   observational equivalence as ontological equivalence needs additional
   philosophical premises. Records-as-stems is a modeling choice; records require
   a specified measurable access map.
6. **Known equivalences and limits:** Brightwell et al. characterize covariant
   events within the generic CSG family described in Sec. 2 (exceptional cases
   excluded; at least one coupling with index k ≥ 2 positive). Surya–Zalel Theorem 2.1 is a
   complex-valued extension theorem on the full cylinder-generated sigma-algebra,
   not a general theorem for arbitrary Hilbert-valued quantum measures. Neither
   result proves ontology equivalence or arbitrary observer-record equivalence.

## Distinctions and limits

- CSG growth is stochastic; a completed-history measure does not imply a fixed
  future or determinism.
- Causal order is the partial order on causet elements. It is not clock time,
  objective passage, or the thermodynamic arrow.
- Covariant measurability is a mathematical condition on events; it is not the
  same as empirical accessibility by an embedded observer.
- The source authors' passage/gauge descriptions and the project's proposed
  bridge from records to ontology must remain labeled as interpretations.
- No novelty conclusion follows from unreturned search hits. Versions and
  citation paths beyond those listed were not systematically searched.
