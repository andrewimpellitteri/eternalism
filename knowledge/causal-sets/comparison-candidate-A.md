# Comparison for NOVELTY.md candidate A: growth model versus completed order

Unit of comparison: Rideout-Sorkin classical sequential growth (CSG), arXiv gr-qc/9904062v3,
with Wuthrich-Callender (arXiv:1502.00018v1) as interpretive analysis. Not a novelty claim.
Per knowledge/README.md contract, items 1-6.

1. What exists / passage / future. CSG: a probability measure on (labelled, future-infinite)
   causets; interpretations differ. Authors (Rideout-Sorkin, Sorkin quoted by W-C): growth is
   passage ("constituting time"). W-C: the same mathematics is equally a tenseless space of
   histories with a measure; a 'cardinality-only' becoming is possible at the price of
   ontological indeterminacy. The law can be stochastic; this does not settle whether a
   realized history already exists. Determinism and completed-history ontology are distinct.
2. State space / dynamics / causal structure / clock. State space: finite causets modulo
   isomorphism (poset P) with labelled paths; dynamics: Markov transition probabilities
   alpha = lambda(w,m)/lambda(n,0) with parameters t_n. Causal order is the poset order;
   element count n is a stage index, not an operational clock (label time is fictitious).
   Proper-time estimators require further correspondence assumptions, such as Madsen F3.
3. Symmetry / preferred structure / observer / boundary. Labelling is auxiliary gauge
   structure, not a preferred physical foliation. The completed-history measure uses
   Omega(infinity). No explicit measurable observer-record map is supplied by these anchors.
   A past sub-causet or partial stem could encode a record only after an extra modelling choice.
4. Predictions. Label-independent (covariant) events such as "a partial stem isomorphic to P
   occurs" have probabilities fixed by the same measure whether one narrates it as growth or as
   a completed ensemble; the measure on unlabelled causets is the restriction/pushforward of
   the labelled one (coordinator note; Brightwell et al., unread here). Stage-relative
   statements ("at 8 elements it was a chain") are not covariant; the authors call them
   meaningless. Hence, within covariant classical CSG, a growth reading and a completed-order
   reading give identical statistics for records defined as measurable functions of that
   same covariant history. This conditional statement is our inference, not a theorem about
   arbitrary physical observers. Coordinator finite check
   (n <= 5, exact rationals; knowledge/defeat-search/csg_ancillarity_check.py): birth-order data
   are ancillary to the t_n given the unlabelled causet. Empirical status: none; CSG is not a
   tested theory and is not known to recover general relativity (authors).
5. Bridge premises. (a) label = gauge is imposed, not derived; (b) records = sub-causets is ours;
   (c) any passage reading needs a premise beyond the measure (W-C); (d) Markov form is claimed
   forced by covariance (footnote 5), not independently checked.
6. Equivalences / counterexamples / domain limits. The equivalence is a representation
   equivalence within classical, covariant, generic (no vanishing alpha), labelled-vs-unlabelled
   CSG; it does not establish ontological equivalence or gauge symmetry of ontologies.
   Changing the law or access model requires a fresh comparison; a classical non-Markov
   process can also admit a path-space measure. Quantum complex-amplitude growth may face
   different measure-extension obstructions (Surya-Zalel
   per coordinator note: measure may fail to extend to the covariant algebra; unread here).
   Madsen's deterministic embedding theorem concerns well-conditioned completed causets;
   its probabilistic application concerns Poisson sprinklings.

Reading for candidate A (our inference, flagged as such): in the covariant classical sector the
answer to "can records distinguish growth from a completed order?" appears to be no, and that
looks true by construction of the covariant sigma-algebra, which is exactly candidate A's
rejection gate ("true by our definitions"). A nontrivial version would need a model where some
access assumption (finite-stage observer, stage-dependent record, non-covariant or
non-Markov law) makes stage-relative events physically meaningful.
Nearest predecessors: Rideout-Sorkin 2000; Brightwell et al. 2003; Wuthrich-Callender 2015 (the
tenseless-measure reading and gauge-invariant cardinality growth); Huggett 2014, Earman 2008,
Arageorgis 2012, Pooley 2013 (cited by W-C, unread).
What would defeat any suggested gap: a published statement that stage-relative or record
statistics are fully determined by the unlabelled measure for the relevant model class; or a
proof that any proposed non-covariant observable is a measurable function of covariant ones.
Distinctions kept apart: temporal ontology (eternalism/presentism/growing block: not decided
here); objective passage (claimed by authors, not derived); determinism (CSG is stochastic,
eternalist readings can be stochastic); causal order (poset order); clock time (absent);
thermodynamic arrow (not addressed by any of the three papers).

## Wuthrich-Callender v1: six-item comparison

1. Ontology/passage/future: competing readings, including indeterminate cardinality growth;
   no established passage or determinism conclusion.
2. Mathematics: causets and CSG; no operational clock.
3. Structure/access: relabelling and event identity matter; no record instrument.
4. Predictions: interpretive analysis, no new tested discriminator.
5. Bridges: vague existence and identity assumptions sustain the exotic reading.
6. Limits: the four-element example defeats a proposed causal-set analogue; the prose Stein
   reconstruction is not a primary theorem audit. Locators: Secs. 3-4.

## Madsen v1: six-item comparison

1. Ontology/passage/future: completed finite orders; passage and determinism unaddressed.
2. Mathematics: well-conditioned embeddings, F1-F3; chain/proper-time correspondence.
3. Structure/access: global hyperbolicity, auxiliary foliations, interior restrictions;
   no observer model.
4. Predictions: claimed geometric reconstruction and Poisson bounds, not an experiment.
5. Bridges: spacetime reconstruction alone selects no temporal ontology.
6. Limits: deterministic theorem needs F1-F3; sprinkling application is probabilistic.
   Locators: Def. 2.6, Remark 2.4, Thm. 4.18, Thm. 5.1, Cor. 5.6; proofs unaudited.
