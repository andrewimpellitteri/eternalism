## Verbatim findings (details and full quotations in extraction.md)

**1. Sector-wise maps.**
- HSL Eq. (38): `R_PW^sigma(tau) := <tau,sigma| ⊗ I_S`, with factor 1/sqrt2 (Eq. 39) and
  Corollary 2 / Eq. (47): `<psi|phi>_phys = (1/2) sum_sigma <psi_S^sigma(tau)|phi_S^sigma(tau)>`.
- HL Sec. II.2: "the reduction maps `R_C(t)` must be defined sector-wise [32]. In that case these
  maps are partial isometries".
- HL Appendix A: `R_sigma(y)` is an isometry "if and only if" Eq. (42) holds.

**2. State-independent normalisation failure.** HL Sec. III.1: the naive map "coherently combines
the different superselection sectors [...] Crucially, `R~(x0)` is not invertible, and the
relational clock states [...] cannot be normalised in a state-independent manner". HL works with
position conditioning on a non-degenerate clock, where the sigma label is the particle's momentum
sign. HSL footnote 16 makes the related claim for a coherent clock state `sum_sigma c_sigma |tau,sigma>`:
no resolution of the identity, and "a reduction map `<tau| ⊗ I_S`, which only conditions on the
clock time, would not be invertible".

**3. Inaccessibility.** HSL Sec. V.1.1 after Eq. (33): "such coherence is not physically accessible
because it does not affect the expectation value of any relational Dirac observable on account of
the decomposition in Eq. (33). In other words, superpositions and classical mixtures across the
`sigma`-frequency sectors are indistinguishable."

**4. External or internal reference.** The HSL [117] paragraph is complete, not cut off. It runs
"can be lifted [117]. This interpretation seems unsuitable here. Firstly, lifting the superselection
rule would entail undoing the group averaging, in violation of gauge invariance. Secondly, [...] The
above superselection rule can thus not be associated with the lack of a shared physical reference
frame."

**5. BRS.** BRS treat internal, gauge-invariant (G-invariant) quantised reference frames that lift
superselection rules. Sec. IV.2, Eq. (100) gives the $-map, and Eq. (106) shows it reproduces the
statistics. This is done only for superselection rules generated by twirling over a compact or
finite group; non-compact groups are left open in the Outlook. No source read treats an internal
reference system carrying the sigma label in a Hamiltonian-constraint setting.

**6. Time-dependent constraint.** HSL footnote 14 is only "E.g. when `C_H = p_t^2 - H^2(q_i,p_i,t)`
and `H^2>0 ∀ t∈R`." The clause "no superselection rule across the sigma-sectors would arise" belongs
to the main text. No normalised conditioning rule for this case was found.

**7. Sector-coherent normalised rule.** None was found in the sources read. The rules actually
stated are sector-mixed: HSL Eq. (62), HL Eq. (28), and the 2607.24976 Example 30 frame `M_t`,
which keeps the sectors in orthogonal clock states.

## Disagreements with the coordinator lead and the earlier defeat notes

1. **Footnote 14 attribution.** The "no superselection rule" clause is main text; the footnote only
   gives the example constraint.
2. **"Sentence cut off".** The earlier defeat search was wrong here; the paragraph is complete.
3. **Narrowest open case.** "Non-twirled conditioning with an explicit external phase reference" is
   partly occupied at the level of interpretation: HSL argue against the reference-frame reading,
   though they construct nothing.
4. **Proposed narrower open case** (my conjecture, unverified). It needs no external reference.
   G-twirled, `C_H`-commuting effects that are off-diagonal in sigma already exist; one example is
   the clock reflection `P_C ⊗ I_S`. A two-outcome refinement `|tau,b> = (|tau,+> + b|tau,->)/sqrt2`
   of the HSL POVM formally gives a normalised, tau-independent-denominator, sector-coherent rule
   whose b-marginal is HSL Eq. (62). HSL's "relational Dirac observable" algebra excludes these
   effects. Whether they are physical is the open bridge question, and Marolf gr-qc/9508015 is the
   likeliest defeater.
5. **2409.06479 is more than "direct sum only".** Example 14 counts all of `L(H_phys)` (sector
   mixing included) as Dirac observables. HL Sec. V cites its Example 9 for non-interference
   persisting. This tension is unresolved; the two papers may concern different observable algebras.
