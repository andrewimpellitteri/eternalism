## Items that bear on open repo claims

### 1. Chataignier & Smith, "Interacting quantum reference frames", arXiv:2610.28732v1 (2026-10-20)

- **Read depth:** partial full-text, from alphaXiv extracted text. Read: abstract; §I
  introduction; the statements of Theorems 1 and 2 (§II); §IV.A Eqs. (39)–(50); §V
  conclusion. Not read: the proofs, Theorems 3 and 4 in full, Appendix B, §IV.B.
- **The authors claim:** physical states are built by group averaging over a unimodular gauge
  group G. Conditioning on a coherent-state frame gauge-fixes the physical inner product
  through a Faddeev–Popov factor Δ_R(r). This gives a generalised Page–Wootters reduction map
  R(r). Theorem 2 says the change-of-frame map U(r̃,r) = R̃(r̃)∘R⁻¹(r) is unitary, and that this
  does not need G to act as a tensor product across H_R ⊗ H_S. So it covers clock–system
  interactions. For temporal frames, Eqs. (44)–(46) give a unitary Schrödinger equation with
  a generator H_{S|C}(t) (Eq. 45) that "is t-dependent and gauge-nonlocal". The Smith–Ahmadi
  time-nonlocal equation is recovered when Δ is t-independent. The authors attribute the
  non-unitarity reported earlier for interacting clocks to normalising with the naive inner
  product. The result is conditional on Δ_R(r) existing, which needs G[E_R(r) ⊗ I_S] to be
  invertible. They flag a Gribov-like obstruction as open (§V).
- **Bearing on the repo:** this is a direct hit on the
  [theorem-matrix candidate question](theorem-matrix.md#candidate-question-unverified-do-not-call-an-open-problem).
  Contract items (ii) and (iii) asked for injective reduction maps, a physical inner product
  and a proof of isometry. If the paper is right, it supplies these under its invertibility
  hypothesis. The remaining question narrows to two points. First, when is H_{S|C}(t)
  t-independent? Second, when does Δ_R(r) fail to exist? The paper itself names the second
  as open. The candidate's "isometry versus memory kernel" contrast is, on the authors'
  claim, partly an artefact of the choice of inner product. Treat the candidate as heavily
  narrowed. Do not implement it before a full-text audit of this paper and of item 2.

### 3. Horvat, "Locality, Probability and the Relevance of History", arXiv:2610.41944v1 (2026-10-30)

- **Read depth:** partial full-text. Read: abstract; Definitions 1–3; Principle 1; the
  statements of Propositions 1–3; the sections on irrelevance and relevance of history; the
  time-symmetry passage; footnotes 17–18. Not read: the proofs, or the probabilistic
  formalism in detail.
- **The author claims:** a "world-theory" is a triple (M, E, W) of worlds W : M → E. Locality
  means that any two worlds agreeing on J⁻(y) ∩ Σ, for past surfaces Σ arbitrarily close to y,
  agree at y. Locality implies determinism, but not conversely (Proposition 1). Probabilistic
  Locality says that the probability at y supervenes on the probabilities in its immediate
  causal past. It and Bell's Local Causality are logically independent (Proposition 2).
  Probabilistic Locality plus "Irrelevance of History" implies Local Causality
  (Proposition 3). So Bell violations show that history is relevant, not that locality fails.
  In time-symmetric theories, relevance of history implies relevance of the future.
- **Bearing on the repo:** Horvat's deterministic Locality has the same cross-world
  supervenience form as Part II's "x is fixed from b under 𝓛" (two solutions agreeing on
  I⁻(b) agree at x; see `research/what-the-past-can-fix/parts/fixing_guaranteed.tex`).
  Horvat's version is localised at J⁻(y) ∩ Σ and has a probabilistic extension. Part II is
  deterministic. The finite-prefix and whole-central-history results ask how much of the
  history fixes what, which is close to his "relevance of history". The paper postdates our
  results, so it does not threaten priority. A Part II reader would still expect a comparison
  with it. The Earman-antecedent paragraph in `fixing_models.tex` is the natural place for one.

### 4. Builes & Impagnatiello, "An Empirical Argument for Presentism", *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 14 (2025), pp. 63–88, doi:10.1093/9780198952763.003.0003

- **This is an older gap, not new work.** The repo has zero mentions of this paper or of its replies.
- **Read depth:** abstract and introduction, from the publisher's excerpt via search.
  Builes's site lists it as winner of the Sanders Prize in Metaphysics.
- **The authors claim:** the fundamental laws are plausibly Markovian: the present screens off
  the past, and they give a relativity-friendly version too. Eternalism gives no reason to
  expect this. Non-Humean, causal-powers versions of presentism entail it. So physics
  supports presentism abductively.
- **Replies located:**
  - Lettie, "Explaining Markovian time", *Analysis* (online 2024-12-04),
    doi:10.1093/analys/anae070. PhilArchive preprint `LETEMT`, §§1–3 read in excerpt. It
    gives a non-ontological explanation by treating Markovianism as one member of a class of
    "Markovian-like" properties.
  - Ryoo, "Temporal Ontology and Non-Markovian Quantum Dynamics", *Philosophies* 11(2):45
    (2026). Abstract only. It argues the converse: non-Markovianity would pressure presentism.
  - Howarth, "Temporal Membranes", *Philosophies* 11(6):201 (2026-10-30). Read in partial
    full-text through §5.2. It distinguishes reduced-state Markovianity, Markovian embedding,
    fundamental-law Markovianity and metaphysical screening-off. It names the needed bridge
    "BP1/TDEB" and argues that Markovianity underdetermines temporal ontology.
- **Bearing on the repo:** this is the most important gap found. Parts II–III and the
  [causal-production reconstruction](../../research/ontology-reconstruction/coordinator/causal-production/RESULT.md)
  ask what the past fixes beyond what the present or the causal past fixes. That is the
  relativistic form of B&I's screening-off premise. Two repo results look directly relevant
  to their premise P3 (that eternalism cannot explain Markovianity). One is the
  [finite-prefix corollary](../../research/lawful-completions/coordinator/prefix-mass-audit/RESULT.md).
  The other is the law-variation test separating persistence within a history from
  invariance across law changes. Whether either helps or hurts B&I is unexamined. This needs
  a full-text read before any claim is made.
