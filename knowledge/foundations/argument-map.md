# Foundations argument map (first pass)

This is a bounded map, not a verdict about the correct ontology. “Present” may
mean an observer's local here-now, a frame-relative simultaneity surface, a
privileged global now, or the edge of an accumulating history. Those notions
must not be swapped silently. Physical theory constrains causal and metric
structure; conclusions about what exists or whether passage is objective need
additional bridge premises.

## Anchor 1 — Rietdijk–Putnam inference to a block ontology

Physical premises: (P1) Special relativity has inertial frames with differing
simultaneity hyperplanes for spacelike-separated events. (P2) Each observer's
“now” includes spacelike-separated events relative to that frame. The geometry
supports these frame-relative relations.

Bridge premises: (B1) If all and only things standing in a relation R to me-now
are real, and you-now are also real, then the things R-related to either are
equally real (Putnam's explicitly stated principle III). (B2) Reality/status
inferences can be propagated across overlapping observer-now relations. These
are metaphysical principles, not Lorentz transformation laws.

Conclusion: Putnam argues that no sharp ontological difference between past,
present and future follows consistently with these commitments, motivating a
block view. This is not a derivation of physical determinism: a fixed
spacetime representation does not itself entail deterministic laws, and
relativity of simultaneity is not the same as causal order.

Objections/alternatives: Stein says the argument misapplies relative
simultaneity by treating frame-relative simultaneity as a global spatial
present and rejects the transitivity step. A local/event-relative becoming can
retain a past/future distinction by causal cones without a distant global now.
An ontology of “equally real” events remains a bridge choice.

Locators/read depth: Putnam, *Time and Physical Geometry*, pp.240-242, scanned
primary text (partial full-text read; principle III is visible at p.241); Stein,
“On Relativity Theory and Openness of the Future,” pp.148-152, especially the
criticism at pp.151-152. The remaining pages of Putnam were not audited in this
pass.

## Anchor 2 — Stein's relativistic/local becoming

Physical premises: (P1) Minkowski spacetime distinguishes timelike/null causal
relations from spacelike separation and carries a time orientation. (P2) There
is no frame-independent distant simultaneity surface selected by SR. Local
relativistic laws use local/infinite­simal structure, not a universal
instantaneous configuration.

Bridge premises: (B1) “Has become” may be tied to an event's causal past rather
than a universe-wide present slice. (B2) A coherent notion of becoming need not
assign a unique global present to every event. These are proposed
interpretive conditions, not consequences forced by the metric alone.

Conclusion: Stein argues SR permits a reasonable event-relative becoming/open
future notion, so absence of a global present does not by itself imply
determinism or eternalism. He notes that general relativity lacks a good
analogue to Einstein distant relative simultaneity (p.159), making global
present claims more, not less, model-dependent.

Objections/alternatives: Maxwell's response disputes Stein's compatibility
claim on the basis of probabilism and SR; whether a probabilistic account
requires present alternative actualities is contested. A light-cone relation
alone establishes causal accessibility/order, not objective passage.

Locators/read depth: Stein 1991, pp.147-149 for setup and time orientation,
pp.151-152 for the transitivity/global-present criticism, pp.158-159 for local
relativistic laws and the GR qualification; full text read. Maxwell 1993,
section III, p.343 is a directly relevant reply; full text read.

## Anchor 3 — Malament's causal definability uniqueness result

Physical/mathematical premises: (P1) Minkowski causal connectibility is
available as a relation. (P2) An inertial observer worldline is supplied as
additional structure. (M1) Candidate simultaneity is required to be definable
from those structures, an equivalence relation, non-universal, and relates
some point on the observer's worldline to a point off it.

Conclusion: Under those restrictions, standard Einstein synchrony is the
unique nontrivial equivalence relation meeting the conditions. This is a
conditional mathematical uniqueness result about definability, not a theorem
that metaphysical presentness is unique or that eternalism is true.

Objections/alternatives: Enlarging available observer structure or changing
definability assumptions changes the question. Besnard's later analysis
explicitly explores the gap between Malament's uniqueness setup and broader
coordinate freedom; exact theorem hypotheses remain to be extracted from the
primary paper before formalization.

Locators/read depth: Malament 1977, pp.293-300 (primary source abstract and
bibliographic record checked; primary proof not obtained). SEP archived entry,
“Malament's Theorem,” gives the conditions summarized above (full secondary
text read). Besnard 2011, arXiv:1104.1263 abstract only, is a follow-up lead.

## Anchor 4 — Presentism as an empirical hypothesis (a counter to blanket
empirical-equivalence claims)

Physical premise: A theory can add physically specified structure or dynamics
that yields observables in principle distinguishable from standard
Lorentz-invariant models. The empirical consequences depend on the proposed
model, not on the word “presentism.”

Bridge premise: To call a privileged present physically real requires an
interpretive identification between the extra structure and presentness; a
measurement of preferred structure alone does not logically settle that
metaphysical interpretation.

Conclusion: Brading's proposal is an existence claim about a testable
presentist variant, and therefore blocks the inference that every presentism
is empirically idle. It does not establish empirical success or make all
presentist theories testable.

Objections/alternatives: The proposal's specific physical implementation,
constraints, and empirical status have not been read past the abstract in this
lane. Lorentz-violation bounds apply only to specified observable coefficients;
an empirically hidden preferred structure remains underdetermined.

Locators/read depth: Brading, *Philosophy of Science* 80(5) (2013), DOI
10.1086/673897, author abstract at the Duke record; abstract only. This anchor
is deliberately provisional pending full-text extraction.

## Assumptions with computational-analysis potential

1. Represent Minkowski events, causal connectibility, inertial worldlines, and
   definable candidate simultaneity relations. Vary the observer structure and
   equivalence/nontriviality requirements to find finite or symbolic models
   illustrating which premises do work. A finite search would illustrate only
   its bounded domain; Malament's general result still needs its proof.
2. Formalize Putnam's principle III separately from Lorentz geometry, then
   ablate bridge premises (especially transitivity of “equally real”) and test
   whether local causal-past becoming remains consistent. Do not encode the
   desired ontology in the definition of reality.
3. Compare global foliation, event-relative causal past, and causal-set growth
   as structures over the same event/record observables. State exactly which
   observables and histories are preserved; representation equivalence alone
   does not establish ontological equivalence.
4. For empirical presentism, specify a concrete preferred-structure model,
   observable coefficients and existing bounds before asking whether any
   ontological distinction is testable.
