# Source-to-model sanity cases

These ten constructed checks test whether an extraction or formalization agent
keeps essential assumptions and distinctions. They are known mathematics, not
new physics or a novelty claim. They were motivated by the coordinator review
and are separate from the GLM methods worker's independent proposal.

Convention: the Minkowski event space is nonempty, with at least one spatial
dimension; `R(a,b)` means that event b lies in the specified past of event a.

`correspondence-cases.jsonl` gives five tempting false claims, the expected assessment,
an explicit witness, and the permitted conclusion. Evaluate the claim together
with its stated assumptions. An agent must explain the witness, not merely
guess a reject label. `positive-controls.jsonl` contains five paired true
statements, so blanket rejection cannot succeed. Hide the answers from the
evaluated agent; assess its reasoning as well as its classification.

The two relation cases are tests of isolated statements, not allegations about
an original printed theorem. The clock case concerns exact finite canonical
operators, not all finite clocks. The conditioning case tests one instrument,
not a characterization of every affine conditional map. The divisibility case
keeps one-time map properties separate from full multi-time process memory.

These are provisional, hand-constructed regression cases. They have not been
used to measure any model, and they do not validate a general synthesis system.
Before scoring model outputs, independently review the cases and add
source-backed examples with held-out answers.

Review record: a separate GPT-6 Luna agent at high effort checked all ten
statements and witnesses on 2026-10-19 and found no substantive mathematical
defect. The coordinator checked the conditioning fractions and dephasing
arithmetic. These are explanatory checks, not proof-assistant certificates or
validation of any source-paper transcription.
