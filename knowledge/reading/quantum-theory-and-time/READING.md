# Quantum theory and temporal ontology — reading note

11 November 2026. GLM reading lane. Depths follow sources.jsonl; "full-text" there means
partial full-text via open web extraction (its `limits` fields say which sections). Entries
marked [S] are secondary (SEP entries, reviews). Paraphrase throughout; only short phrases
are quoted, all verified against the extracted text. No gated source is treated as read.

## 1. The main positions

**Nonlocality without action at a distance.** Myrvold, *Lessons of Bell's Theorem*
(PhilSci-Archive 12382; full-text partial): any theory reproducing even approximately the
quantum probabilities is nonlocal in Bell's factorizability sense, but a stochastic theory can
violate factorization through irreducible correlations without parameter independence, and
"need not invoke a distinguished relation of distant simultaneity"; a deterministic theory can
look relativistic only "by introducing a preferred foliation". [S] SEP "Bell's Theorem"
(Myrvold, Genovese, Shimony; rev. 25 Jan 2024), §8.2, adds that trajectory theories cannot keep
Born-rule distributions on arbitrary hypersurfaces (Berndl et al. 1996; Dickson–Clifton 1998),
and that Bell himself moved from incompatibility only for deterministic hidden-variable theories
(1964) to an "apparently essential conflict" of any sharp formulation with relativity (1984
remarks).

**Collapse needs a hidden frame; worth paying for.** Maudlin, *Quantum Non-Locality and
Relativity* (3rd ed. 2011) — **discovery depth only**; the book was not accessible. Reconstructed
from SEP §8.2 and the NDPR review of Craig & Smith (eds.), *Einstein, Relativity and Absolute
Simultaneity* (2008) [S]: quantum phenomena force either superluminal causal connections or a
hidden preferred frame; Lorentz invariance is phenomenal; the foliation is a metaphysical price,
friendly to absolute simultaneity. Treat this paragraph as a placeholder until the book is read.

**Relativistic collapse, flash ontology.** Tumulka (arXiv:quant-ph/0406094v2; J. Stat. Phys. 125
(2006) 821–840; full-text partial): carrying out Bell's 1987 program, stochastic collapse whose
local beables are the flash events at collapse centres — matter is, in Bell's phrase quoted
there, "a galaxy of such events". The theory is "nonlocal and violates Bell's inequality though
it does not make use of a preferred slicing", works on Minkowski and curved backgrounds, and
**time-reversal invariance is broken** while rotation, space and time translation, parity and
gauge invariance hold.

**Relativistic collapse, matter-density ontology.** Bedingham, Dürr, Ghirardi, Goldstein,
Tumulka, Zanghì (arXiv:1111.1425v4; J. Stat. Phys. 154 (2014) 623–631; full-text partial): even
with a relativistic collapse law, different Lorentz frames may yield conflicting matter densities
at a point, because the wavefunction is hypersurface-dependent (the Aharonov–Albert objection to
Hellwig–Kraus). Their fix defines matter density at x from the wavefunction on x's past light
cone.

**Everett: branching as objective uncertainty.** Saunders & Wallace, "Branching and Uncertainty"
(BJPS 59 (2008) 429–450 metadata; author PDF read, full-text partial): no collapse; under a
natural semantics (tokens of "I" refer to the speaker's continuant; Lewisian tensed identity),
pre-branching speakers "cannot say in advance which branch or world is theirs" and are genuinely
uncertain — solving the incoherence problem. Wilson, *The Nature of Contingency* (OUP 2020;
abstract): quantum chance is branch-inclusion structure in a modal realism; actuality is
indexical. [S] Barrett's SEP "Everettian Quantum Mechanics" (rev. 20 Jun 2023) for the
pure-wave-mechanics framing.

**Retrocausal / time-symmetric.** Price (arXiv:1002.0906v3; SHPMP 43 (2012); full-text partial):
under realist ontologies with outcome discreteness, time-symmetry *without* retrocausality is
unavailable — Everettian and Bohmian ontologies escape by providing the continuity elsewhere.
Wharton & Argaman (arXiv:1906.04313v3; Rev. Mod. Phys. 92, 021002 (2020); full-text partial):
"locally mediated" reformulations with future-input dependence; correlations are introduced only
via spacetime mediators ("Continuous Action"), with no signalling into the past, and histories
solved "all-at-once" rather than unfolding from the past.

**Against presentism (and its quantum rescue).** Saunders ("How Relativity Contradicts
Presentism", author PDF; published R. Inst. Philos. Suppl. 50 (2002) 277–292; full-text
partial): presentism needs an absolute-simultaneity partition, and "it is of the essence of
special relativity that absolute simultaneity as such does not exist" — the presentist "will
literally need a river for there to be time". [S] Wüthrich ("The fate of presentism in modern
physics", arXiv:1207.1490v1; in Ciuni, Miller, Torrengo eds. 2013, 91–131; full-text partial)
taxonomizes presentist responses in a 2×2 matrix and evaluates the collapse route — a
"worldwide tide of actualization" constituting a preferred foliation — concluding that what
survives is "metaphysically repugnant". The pro-presentism quantum route is the collapse-foliation
literature itself (Maudlin's line; rGRWm/CSL).

## 2. Canonical arguments

**A1. Bell's theorem.** Primary: Bell 1964 (discovery depth, accessed through [S] SEP §1–3,
which quotes its close) and Myrvold 2016 (full-text). Premises: (i) factorizability — given the
complete state of the past, outcome probabilities are independent of distant settings and
outcomes; (ii) settings independence; (iii) quantum predictions violate the resulting
inequalities, confirmed loophole-free in 2015. Conclusion: one of (i)–(ii) fails. Standard
objections: deny settings independence (superdeterminism, or retrocausality as law rather than
conspiracy — Price); deny that violating factorization is causal action (Myrvold: non-causal
irreducible correlations); and no-signalling is insufficient, since de Broglie–Bohm is
signalling-free yet needs a foliation (SEP §8.2).

**A2. Relativity contradicts presentism (Saunders 2002).** Premises: (i) presentism is a
tenseless thesis that all and only present events exist; (ii) the present is a universal
simultaneity partition; (iii) Minkowski spacetime supplies no intrinsic such partition.
Conclusion: presentism makes SR radically deficient. Standard objections: Stein's point-like
becoming (the repo's Part I formalism); and the compatibilist escape of adding structure —
a collapse-foliation or cosmological function — which Wüthrich's taxonomy prices.

**A3. Everett uncertainty is legitimate (Saunders–Wallace 2008).** Premises: (i) post-branching
persons are continuants overlapping pre-branching (tensed identity); (ii) utterances at t are
attributed to the continuant speaking, so a pre-branching token of "I" refers to each future
continuant; (iii) each is uncertain which it will be. Conclusion: ordinary uncertainty talk is
true; the incoherence problem dissolves. Standard objections: the "in-between" worry — nothing
settles which successor one should expect to be, so the uncertainty is not about the future's
contents; and the charge of an ad hoc semantics.

**A4. Lorentz-invariant collapse exists (Tumulka 2006).** Premises: (i) Bell's observation that
GRW's multi-time translation invariance is a nonrelativistic surrogate of Lorentz invariance;
(ii) flashes as the primitive ontology; (iii) a Lorentz-invariant stochastic rate for flashes
defined from the initial multi-time wavefunction. Conclusion: a collapse theory with no preferred
foliation exists, refuting the "collapse needs a preferred frame" intuition. Standard objections:
non-interacting particles only (multi-time consistency forbids potentials); the positive-energy
restriction; and the theory's broken time-reversal symmetry is, to a time-symmetric critic, a
posit.

**A5. Time-symmetric conditional probabilities (ABL 1964; TSVF).** Primary: Aharonov, Bergmann,
Lebowitz, "Time Symmetry in the Quantum Process of Measurement", Phys. Rev. **134**, B1410 (1964)
(abstract read at APS; body not accessed). Premises: (i) a system is both pre- and post-selected;
(ii) the ABL rule computes outcome probabilities from both boundary conditions. Conclusion:
quantum probabilities can be formulated without privileging the earlier end, grounding
retrocausal and two-state readings (Aharonov–Vaidman 1991, per Wharton–Argaman). Notably, ABL's
own abstract attributes the measurement asymmetry to how *ensembles are constructed*, and
recovers ordinary prediction only by adding a postulate that preselection-only ensembles are
legitimate — the direction enters as a posit, not as part of the rule. Standard objections:
Kastner 1999 (abstract) — counterfactual uses (the three-box) are illicit; Barandes 2026
(full-text partial) — the rule is symmetric under reverse *orderings*, not time symmetry, and
rests on a category error between single-system and ensemble observables; post-selection
manufactures artifacts. ABL's abstract already concedes the postulate carries the asymmetry,
which supports Barandes's direction if not his category-error diagnosis.

## 3. Live open problems

1. **Interacting relativistic collapse.** rGRWf is non-interacting; matter-density and
   Bedingham's CSL cover more at other costs. Progress: a Lorentz-invariant interacting collapse
   theory with a settled ontology, or a no-go.
2. **Empirical discrimination.** Collapse models are rival theories with in-principle testable
   deviations [S: SEP qm-collapse, rev. 30 Sep 2025]; parameter bounds are shrinking. Progress:
   an exclusion zone forcing the rate/width constants, or a confirmed deviation from standard QM.
3. **Foliation semantics.** Is a collapse-foliation ontologically committing? Progress: an
   argument from foliation to actuality that survives the defined/primitive distinction (the
   repo's own Part IV diagnostic).
4. **Everett probability and tense.** Whether the first-person future-branch asymmetry can be
   grounded without thermodynamic input (Wallace's decision theory: unread). Progress: a
   derivation from the formalism alone.
5. **Retrocausal generality.** Wharton–Argaman call a Lorentz-covariant, locally mediated account
   of all quantum phenomena a "grand challenge". Progress: one interacting entangled system so
   treated, without exponential parameter growth.
6. **The special question — do the laws alone single out a present or direction?** Answer so far:
   **direction, yes; present, no.** GRW-family laws break time-reversal invariance as a matter of
   law (Tumulka's own list of rGRWf's symmetries), and causal-set sequential growth
   (Rideout–Sorkin, arXiv:gr-qc/9904062; PRD 61, 024002; abstract) is lawfully stochastic growth
   with a built-in direction — neither needs a boundary condition. But no interpretation read here
   derives a *present* from its laws: rGRWf has no foliation at all; matter-density/CSL collapse
   adds a foliation as extra structure; and the step from a distinguished foliation to "what
   exists" is a philosophical bridge premise, not physics. Ismael ("The Open Universe",
   Australasian Philosophical Review, 2022; abstract) locates the residual openness in
   self-reference against a thermodynamic gradient — in the embedded agent, not the laws.

## 4. What in this repository depends on this area

- **main.tex Part I, premises (LC)/(T)/(CB), Remark rem:lc, sec:becoming-rival.** Not
  contradicted — but the conceded rival horn ("a rival with a preferred clock changes the
  category"; "in effect Monton's move") is occupied by named, live theories: rGRWf sits on the
  non-foliation horn (its becoming relation would be nonlocal and state-dependent, failing (LC)
  while remaining Lorentz-invariant), and matter-density/CSL collapse occupies exactly the
  clock-equipped category GH_T the paper builds. The paper cites no collapse or Bell literature
  (its only quantum anchors are the AQFT category, Bigaj 2008, Monton 2006, Savitt 2009), so a
  reader from this field would take the horn to be vacant. Cite the occupants.
- **main.tex sec:direction: "We know of three candidates, and the paper is neutral between
  them."** Incomplete by this literature's standards. A fourth candidate exists: lawful stochastic
  collapse dynamics, time-reversal non-invariant with no boundary condition (Tumulka; Myrvold:
  "Things are otherwise with dynamical collapse theories"). The paper's adjacent open question —
  whether a non-reversal-invariant law can create one-way fixing — has its natural test case
  here, but F_L (agreement of solutions) is defined only for deterministic classical classes and
  cannot currently say what a stochastic law fixes. Extending F to process laws, with rGRWf as
  the first case, would vindicate or bound the paper's moral that laws give exact fixing no
  arrow of their own.
- **research/time-direction/fixing-exchange/RESULT.md, Proposition 4.** True as stated, but the
  conclusion drawn — that a stationary-chance view "gets a more settled past only from a
  non-stationary posit such as a Past Hypothesis" — omits the collapse case, which is lawful
  non-stationarity without a boundary condition. The dichotomy "boundary condition vs. non-nomic
  grounding" is not exhaustive; add the nomic-asymmetry alternative.
- **research/horizons/cone-algebras/RESULT.md.** Its closing caveat is already right by this
  literature's standards. One supplement: the algebra/state story is interpretation-dependent —
  a collapse theorist's local beables (past-light-cone matter density; Myrvold §7) change what
  "the observer's past" contains, and "independence without ignorance" holds only on
  collapse-free readings.
- **research/horizons/horizons-and-news/RESULT.md §4.** The quantum-mirror quotations are fine as
  AQFT; the note's own limit (fixed-background quantum fields) covers the main risk. Minor:
  collapse theories add state variables, so the "no knowledge of the global state" point would
  need restatement there.
- **research/stein-gr/local-clock/RESULT.md.** Unaffected. Note only that Theorem 4's clock
  criterion is geometric while GRW supplies a direction with no local clock at all — a nonlocal
  horn the note's taxonomy, like the paper's, does not enumerate.
- **main.tex sec:truth (policies L and S).** Both policies are defined over classical
  realizations; Everett branching supplies a third semantic environment (branch-relative truth).
  "The physics fixes the comparison and not the policy" stands, but the policy space is larger
  than the two stated.

Nothing checked in these notes is *wrong* by this literature's standards; the gaps are
omissions. The one place that is naive is sec:direction's three-candidate list, as above.

## 5. Ten primary sources to read next, ranked

1. **Bell, "Are there quantum jumps?" (1987)** (venue/pages to fix when read; cited by Myrvold
   and Tumulka). Settles the flash ontology's origin and exactly what Bell's Lorentz-invariance
   remark claimed.
2. **GRW 1986, "Unified dynamics for microscopic and macroscopic systems"** (Physical Review D;
   volume/pages to fix when read). Settles the exact stochastic law and its two constants.
3. **Maudlin, *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity*, 3rd ed. (2011).** Settles the
   anti-compatibilist premises and whether no-signalling suffices; replaces the discovery-depth
   reconstruction above.
4. **Wallace, *The Emergent Multiverse* (2012),** decoherence/branching and probability chapters.
   Settles whether the open-future claim is argued there, and how branching grounds tense.
5. **Wilson, *The Nature of Contingency* (2020).** Settles quantum chance as branch-inclusion and
   its consequences for truth policies and the growing block.
6. **Bell 1964, 1971, 1976, 1990** (the four formulations, years per SEP). Settles the locality
   conditions at source; Myrvold's reading of "La nouvelle cuisine" needs primary checking.
7. **Aharonov–Bergmann–Lebowitz 1964 (Phys. Rev. 134, B1410; abstract read)** and
   **Aharonov–Vaidman 1991 (TSVF)**. Settles what the rule actually claims and which postulate
   carries the direction, against Barandes's 2026 critique.
8. **Price, *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point* (1996)** — already on the parity map's gated
   list. Settles the perspectival candidate sec:direction quotes and the retrocausal/perspectival
   combination.
9. **Bedingham 2011 (relativistic CSL).** Settles whether foliation-free *interacting* collapse
   exists — Bell's "second-class difficulties" verdict, per Myrvold §5.
10. **Rideout–Sorkin 2000, "Classical sequential growth dynamics for causal sets."** Settles the
    formal structure of lawfully stochastic growth with a built-in direction — the closest formal
    cousin of the repo's causal-set and growth lanes.
