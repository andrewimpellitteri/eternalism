## 1. Main positions

- **Temporal neutrality is rationally required** (Sullivan 2018; Greene and Sullivan 2015, full-text).
  Prudential rationality forbids caring about *when* an experience occurs relative to the present,
  whether the bias is toward the near or toward the future. Future bias is not a safe exception.
  Dougherty 2011 (full-text) leans the same way, with an admitted lack of an error theory.
- **Near bias is irrational, future bias permissible** (Parfit 1984, ch. 8, as reported by
  Dougherty, Brueckner-Fischer and Hare; Heathwood as quoted by Greene and Sullivan). Greene and
  Sullivan say almost no one objects to future bias; Parfit's own use of it, as reported by the
  Stanford entry, is that it helps explain why death is worse than late birth.
- **Tensed metaphysics rationalises future bias** (Prior 1959, full-text, but see below on what Prior
  actually wrote; Zimmerman 2007 as quoted by Suhler and Callender; Craig 1999 and Pearson 2018 as
  cited by Greene et al. 2022). If pastness is a real feature, "over and done with" is a reason.
  Hare 2007 (full-text, first half) states what its defenders can honestly claim: a monadic tenser
  can *stipulate* that future pain is worse than past pain and so keep time-biased preferences
  consistent over time; this does not *ground* the stipulation, except perhaps on a shrinking-block
  picture. A four-dimensionalist cannot do even that.
- **Tenseless replies to the tensed-attitude argument** (Mellor, as reported by Garrett 1988 and
  Hoerl, full-text partial): first, that "Thank goodness" expresses a feeling of relief with no
  intended object; later, after MacBeath, that the cause of gladness is the *belief* that the pain
  is past, and the belief's truth needs only a tenseless fact. Garrett 1988 (full-text) adds a third:
  tensed *facts* are compatible with the tenseless view, only tensed *properties* are not.
  Suhler and Callender 2012 (full-text) add indexical modes of presentation (after Perry).
- **The asymmetry is explained by causal asymmetry and evolution, not by metaphysics** (Hume,
  Horwich 1987 and Parfit as cited; Suhler and Callender 2012; Maclaurin and Dyke 2002 as cited).
  We can affect the future and not the past; caring about the past would not have been selected.
- **Birth and death** (as reported by SEP "Death" 2026, secondary; Brueckner and Fischer 1986,
  full-text excerpt). Lucretius: the time before birth mirrors the time after death, so we should not
  fear death. Nagel's reply (as reported): one could not have been born much earlier though one could
  have lived longer. Brueckner and Fischer: death deprives us of future goods we care about, prenatal
  nonexistence deprives us of past goods we do not care about. Feldman 2011 (abstract) says this does
  not solve the problem on any reading of their principle.
- **Empirical work** (Suhler and Callender report Caruso, Gilbert and Wilson 2008; Latham et al.
  2020 to 2023, abstracts). The temporal value asymmetry is real but possibly less robust than
  assumed; future bias persists when the past can be affected, so practical irrelevance is only
  partial; belief in robust passage did not predict future bias.

## 2. Canonical arguments

**(A) Prior's "Thank goodness that's over" (Prior 1959, p. 17, read; Mellor's restatement as quoted
by Garrett 1988).** (1) One says "Thank goodness that's over!" and is understood without a date.
(2) It does not mean "thank goodness the date of the conclusion is 15 June 1954". (3) Nor does it
mean "thank goodness the conclusion is contemporaneous with this utterance"; why would anyone thank
goodness for that. (4) So a tensed copula is not analysable as a tenseless copula plus a date.
Mellor's restatement removes the synonymy dependence: what is one relieved *about*? Apparently that
the headache is now past; and that is a tensed fact. *Objections:* the context is non-extensional so
the fact need not be the tenseless one (Garrett; Suhler and Callender); indexical modes of
presentation exist for persons and places without extra ontology (Perry, as cited by Suhler and
Callender); tu quoque, since presentism stipulates the appropriateness (Mellor, as cited);
Hoerl holds the existing replies fail to account for relief.

**Important qualification, from reading the paper.** Prior's passage (p. 17) is a four-sentence aside
at the end of a reply to N. L. Wilson's tenseless substance-language. It attacks the date-analysis
of tensed sentences. It does not mention value, the A-theory by name, or presentism, and it does not
say the attitude is *rational*. The metaphysical argument is the later readers' construction, which
Hoerl calls a transcendental argument from psychology to the nature of time.

**(B) The regret argument against future bias (Greene and Sullivan 2015, conclusion read).**
(1) It is permissible to avoid certain regret. (2) A future-biased agent who avoids certain regret
acts in ways that produce scheduling and meager-returns problems. (3) Such acts are irrational.
(4) So future bias is irrational. *Objections* (as anticipated by the authors): deny (1), or restrict
it ad hoc; the full objection literature was not read. Sullivan's book argues from Success and
Non-Arbitrariness (draft introduction; NDPR review); both principles are contestable.

**(C) The pain pump (Dougherty 2011).** A risk-averse, future-biased agent facing a sequence of
pill offers takes both and ends with strictly more pain. (1) Risk aversion is permitted. (2) Time
bias is permitted. (3) Being pumped is forbidden. These are jointly inconsistent. *Objection:* Greene
and Sullivan say the pump needs increasing marginal disutility of pain; Hare's PEA Soup commentary
accepts the case but notes it arises only when the agent cannot self-bind.

**(D) Lucretian symmetry and the future-bias reply (Brueckner and Fischer 1986).** (1) Death is bad as
deprivation of later goods. (2) Late birth deprives of earlier goods equally. (3) So by symmetry we
should regret late birth as we regret early death. Their reply: asymmetric attitudes to experienced
goods break the symmetry. *Objection:* Feldman 2011; and the reply uses the rationality of future bias
as an input rather than a result. See also the Stanford entry's remark that forward-looking projects
extend only forward (secondary).

**(E) Explanation without tense (Suhler and Callender 2012).** (1) We act on the future and not on the
past; (2) selection favours affect toward what we can affect; (3) affect mediates valuation (Caruso et
al.'s mediation analysis, as reported); so (4) the asymmetry has a detensed causal explanation; (5) so
Prior's explanandum does not need tensed ontology. *Objections:* the authors concede the fork: either
the causal and epistemic asymmetries can be stated detensed, or the argument moves to whether they
need tense. Latham et al. find residual future bias where the past can be affected; Caruso, Latham
and Miller 2023 (abstract) find the value asymmetry less robust than assumed and doubt future bias
is a manifestation of it. Explanation also need not vindicate.

## 3. Open problems and what would count as progress

1. **Is future bias rationally permissible?** Progress: a normative argument whose premises do not
   assume a temporal ontology, or an error theory for the intuition (Dougherty says he lacks one).
2. **Does tensed metaphysics supply a reason or only a stipulation?** Progress: an account on which
   pastness *reduces* value, not merely coincides with caring less; Hare and Garrett both say this is
   missing. Presentism does not distinguish past from future, since both are unreal (Greene and
   Sullivan).
3. **Do the causal and knowledge asymmetries themselves presuppose tense?** Progress: a detensed
   statement of Horwich's argument, or a proof it cannot be given.
4. **Are the temporal value asymmetry and future bias one phenomenon?** Progress: replicated studies
   (Caruso et al. 2023 raise doubts).
5. **What is temporal relief, and does it need tense?** Progress: Hoerl's planning account tested
   against the tenseless replies (his later sections not read).
6. **Prenatal and posthumous nonexistence.** Progress: a defence of the Nagel-style or
   Brueckner-Fischer reply that survives Feldman's objection.

**Answer to the special question.** In the sources read, no argument supports a tensed or dynamic
ontology on its own. (i) The Prior argument is about the semantics of tensed speech; the tenseless side
has three published replies and none is decisive. (ii) The step to rationality is separate and, per
Garrett, equally unexplained by tense. (iii) Presentism does not favour the future over the past, so the
argument does not even point to presentism. (iv) Belief in robust passage did not predict future bias
in the experiments (abstracts only). (v) Causal-evolutionary explanation accounts for much but not all
(Latham et al.). So the attitudes are *not* better explained by tense; they are also not fully
explained without it, and it is not shown that the explanation is tense-free. Our own inference, not
found in any source read: a view on which value tracks reality, applied to a growing block, would
favour the *past*, not the future.

## 5. Ten primary sources to read next, ranked

1. **Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984), ch. 8.** Every source here depends on it;
   settles what exactly he claims and what he says about the shrinking of past value.
2. **Mellor, *Real Time* (1981) and *Real Time II* (1998), on tense and relief.**
   Settles whether Garrett and Hoerl report him fairly, and the status of his tu quoque.
3. **MacBeath, "Mellor's Emeritus Headache", *Ratio* 25 (1983).** The belief reply in the
   original.
4. **Sullivan, *Time Biases* (OUP 2018), chs. 2, 3, 6.** Settles the premises of Success and
   Non-Arbitrariness and the answer to Dougherty.
5. **Hoerl, "Tense and the Psychology of Relief", section 4 onward.** Settles the planning account.
6. **Caruso, Gilbert and Wilson 2008, *Psychological Science* 19, 796-801.** The founding experiment
   for the value asymmetry; settles effect sizes.
7. **Latham et al. 2020 and 2021/22 at full text.** Settles how much of future bias is accounted for.
8. **Nagel, "Death" (1970; venue not checked).** Settles the original reply and the origin-essentialism
   premise.
9. **Maclaurin and Dyke 2002, the evolutionary reply to Prior (as cited by Suhler and Callender; venue not checked).** The
   original evolutionary reply that Suhler and Callender build on.
10. **Zimmerman 2007 (as quoted by Suhler and Callender; venue not checked), with Craig 1999.** The tensed
    side's strongest statement; settles what they claim the attitude data establish.
