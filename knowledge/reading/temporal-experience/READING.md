## 1. The main positions

**Retentional model.** An episode of experiencing is (nearly) momentary; its content represents a
stretch of the immediate past, so change is had within one act, not across acts. Defenders: C. D.
Broad (two versions: *Scientific Thought* 1923, refined in *An Examination of McTaggart's
Philosophy* 1933; read only via Rashbrook 2012 and Prosser's quotation); Geoffrey Lee's
"extended atomism" variant, on which the experience itself may take time but the sequence A-B-C is
one experience (as classified in Prosser 2017). Classical ancestor: Husserl's
impression–retention–protention structure (book not accessed; described in the repo only at
from-memory depth, and via Dainton's SEP entry §4.2).

**Extensional (overlap) model.** Episodes of experiencing are themselves temporally extended, and
inherit the temporal structure of their contents; successions of overlapping episodes make the
stream. Defender: Barry Dainton (*Stream of Consciousness* 2000; earlier Foster; book not accessed;
position reconstructed from Dainton's own SEP entry "Temporal Consciousness", rev. 2023-03-17, and
Prosser 2017's taxonomy, which lists Foster, Dainton, Hoerl 2009/2013, Phillips 2010/2011/2014,
Rashbrook 2013 as extensionalists).

**The naïve view.** "When all goes well, your stream of consciousness inherits the temporal
structure of the events that are its contents" (Phillips 2014, ch. 7, read). Phillips rejects the
principle of simultaneous awareness (PSA: "If one is aware of a succession or duration, one is
necessarily aware of it at some one moment", quoted from Miller 1984: 109) rather than accepting
either retentional or extensional apparatus.

**Cinematic model.** Momentary static-snapshot experiences; change known only by combining a
snapshot with very recent memory. Named defender: Chuard 2011 (per Prosser 2017). A scientific
version is committed to by Tononi and Koch's IIT ("each experience encompassing a hundred
milliseconds or so"; discrete snapshots; quoted-in-source, SEP entry, at Tononi & Koch 2015: 6, 16).

**Dynamic snapshot theory.** Prosser's own replacement for the specious present: experiences have
instantaneous content, but content can include instantaneous parts of essentially extended
processes (with finite perceptual resolution); continuous change is perceived this way, and
discontinuous change needs a second, separate account (Prosser 2017, read).

**Perceptualism.** Perceptual experience represents objective presentness and strong passage, and
this is evidence that they are real (Lee 2017's label for the metaphysics-literature target).
Defenders named in Paul 2010 (read): Craig 2000, Schlesinger 1991 (presentism); Broad 1938,
Quentin Smith 1993 (moving spotlight); Skow 2009 ("The motivation that I like best appeals to the
nature of our conscious experience", quoted-in-Paul); Hare ("realism about tense is uniquely
capable of making sense of the phenomenology of temporal experience", quoted-in-Paul); Maudlin 2007:
135, 142 for the direction of time.

**Anti-Perceptualism (deflationary).** (a) Prosser 2013 (read): there can be no mental
representation of objective tensed features, whether or not such features exist; perceptual
experience cannot be evidence for passage. (b) Lee 2017 (read) accepts the conclusion — no
experiential justification for the A-theory — but argues Prosser's "perceptual sensitivity"
mechanism over-generalizes (it would also rule out knowing through experience that we live in a
spatial world) and that a real argument needs conditions on the theory/observation distinction
Prosser does not give. (c) Callender 2017 (not accessed; via Gołosz 2022 §1, read): science shows
there is no objective present or flow, and physical asymmetries (records, efficacy, openness)
explain our experience of time; manifest time is observer-relative. (d) Hoerl & McCormack 2018
(read, abstracts and framing): a dual-systems account — a phylogenetically primitive "temporal
updating system" plus a later "temporal reasoning system" — explains why adult humans tend to think
about time in ways that "appear ultimately self-contradictory", without any ontological posit.
(e) Deng 2013 (abstract): B-theory can accommodate our experience of passage.

**Transcendental phenomenological anti-eternalism.** Bernáth & Inan 2023 (read, introduction): the
"primal fact" that one experiences a succession of experiences is incompatible with standard
perdurantist eternalism, on either its tenseless or tensed version. They classify the older
phenomenological arguments into a rational-attitudes route (Craig 1999, Prior 1959, Kiernan-Lewis
1991, Pearson 2018) and a phenomenal-content route (Craig 2000: 138–165, Geach 1972: 304–311,
Schuster 1986, Schlesinger 1991).

**Tensed-attitude datum.** Prior 1959 (read): tensed attitudes and their expression are a datum any
theory owes an account of. Relief, time-bias and their metaphysical import are now a separate live
literature (Bacharach 2022, abstract).

## 2. Canonical arguments, premise by premise

**(A) The phenomenological argument from experience to nowness and passage** — reconstructed
verbatim in Paul 2010: 337–338 (the paper's own numbering):
(1) We have experiences as of the nowness of events. (2) We have experiences as of passage (and as
of change). (3) The thesis that there are temporal properties of nowness and passage provides the
only reasonable explanation of why we have these experiences. (4) That thesis therefore provides the
best explanation. (5) Hence there are temporal properties of nowness and passage (inference to the
best explanation). *Standard objections:* Paul herself attacks (3): a reductionist explanation from
the brain's processing of static inputs (apparent motion and related effects) is available, which
shifts the burden. Prosser attacks (1)–(2) upstream: there is no such content to be explained.
Lee 2017 concedes no evidence follows but disputes Prosser's mechanism (objection above).

**(B) Prior's "Thank goodness that's over"** (Prior 1959, Philosophy 34: 12–17, read; the passage
is at the end of the paper): (1) One says "Thank goodness that's over!" — clear without any date
appended. (2) It says something "which it is impossible that any use of a tenseless copula with a
date should convey" — not equivalent to thanking goodness that the event's conclusion is dated
June 15, 1954. (3) Nor does it mean "Thank goodness the conclusion of that thing is contemporaneous
with this utterance": "Why should anyone thank goodness for that?" (C) So the tensed copula is not
analysable into a tenseless copula plus a date. *Standard objection:* the contemporary reply is
Cohen's in the same volume (Philosophy 34: 360–362, with Prior 362–363 and Cohen 363–364;
bibliographically verified, content not read), and the standing family of replies is that the datum
shows at most that tensed attitudes are not reducible to dated propositions — an indexical or
self-locating content suffices, and no tensed fact is established. Bernáth & Inan file the argument
under the rational-attitudes route.

**(C) Broad's argument from perceived motion to the specious present** (Broad 1923: 351, quoted in
Prosser 2017; Broad read only at quoted-excerpt depth): (1) We do not merely notice that something
has moved; we "see something moving", e.g. a watch's second-hand, with a phenomenology "quite unlike
anything that can be encountered in the perception of a static scene", within a single sensible
field. (2) An experience whose content is a single instant can contain only what could occur in an
instant. (3) Motion essentially takes time. (C) So content must be extended — a specious present.
*Standard objections:* Prosser 2017: premise (2) is false; instantaneous parts of essentially
extended processes can figure in instantaneous contents, and the surrounding temporal context is
detected by the perceptual system (dynamic snapshot theory). Phillips 2014: PSA fails, so the
dilemma (memory theories vs specious present theories) never arises. Rashbrook 2012 (read):
Broad's genuine problem comes from two structural commitments, "Thin-PSA" and the "Overlap" claim,
not from sense-data theory; the same defect reaches Tye's intentionalist "One Experience" view.

**(D) The transcendental phenomenological argument against eternalism** (Bernáth & Inan 2023: 260,
read): (1) It is a primal fact that one experiences a succession of experiences. (2) Perdurantist
eternalism (tenseless or tensed) must either deny this fact or embrace "ad hoc and metaphysically
implausible assumptions about the nature of the mind" (experiences become experiences of distinct
successive subjects). (C) Eternalism is false. *Standard objection (our reconstruction — the
paper's later sections were not read):* experiential-memory relations between successive
stage-experiences deliver experienced succession without any single continuing subject, so (2)
needs a premise that experienced succession requires co-subjecthood, which the eternalist denies;
the argument risks begging the question against perdurance. An ancestry is claimed in Kant
1781/1998: 422–425.

**(E) The rate-of-passage objection** (read at introductory depth only, Skow 2011): (1) If time
really passes, it should have a rate. (2) "One second per second" seems trivial or incoherent, so
passage talk is obscure. *Reply:* Skow distinguishes several precisifications; on the most
straightforward moving-spotlight formulation time passes at one second per supersecond, and he
argues that primitive tense operators using "superseconds" threaten the theory's intelligibility,
exploring an alternative formulation. This argument targets the coherence of passage, not the
experience datum itself.

## 3. Live open problems, and what would count as progress

1. **Is there any phenomenal character as of robust passage, over and above succession?** Paul's
antireductionist vs Prosser's no-such-content vs the newest twist, Miller 2023 "Against Passage
Illusionism" (abstract), which attacks the non-dynamist claim that we merely seem to experience
robust passage. Progress: an agreed protocol for deciding whether experience has
passage-content distinct from succession-content; Lee's demand for conditions on the
theory/observation distinction is the sharpest statement of what is missing.
2. **Which structural model of temporal consciousness is right?** Retentional, extensional,
cinematic and dynamic-snapshot accounts are all live; the methodological split (Lee's bottom-up
neuroscience vs Dainton's phenomenology, with Kon & Miller 2015's proposed integration — all
via the SEP entry) is itself unresolved. Progress: neuroscientific theories that address
experienced succession beyond the ~300 ms window (Kent & Wittmann 2021, as discussed in the SEP
entry; locator unverified), and a resolution of the postdiction-phenomena objection to the naïve
view (Phillips 2014).
3. **Is perception discrete (the perceptual-moment question)?** Oscillatory-phase effects predict
detection (Busch, Dubois & VanRullen 2009, abstract; beta cycles in somatosensation, Baumgarten,
Schnitzler & Lange 2015, abstract, which states that empirical evidence for discreteness "remains
scarce"). Progress: causal intervention on oscillatory phase; and a philosophical settlement
mapping discrete sampling onto the content-instant vs resolution distinction Prosser draws.
4. **The special question: does any argument from experience require a primitive index or present
that physics does not supply?** On what is now in print, no: the IBE (A) is blocked if a
reductionist explanation of (1)–(2) works, which Paul argues it does; the transcendental argument
(D) is question-begging against perdurance on the standard reply; the relief argument (B) yields at
most tensed attitudes; and the deflationary program (Prosser, Callender, Hoerl–McCormack) explains
the appearances using exactly the local structure physics supplies — cones, records, an updating
system. What would reverse this: a formulation of Perceptualism that survives Prosser's sensitivity
argument as repaired by Lee's constraints, or empirical confirmation that experience carries
A-theoretic content.
5. **Relativity and temporal consciousness.** The SEP entry has a section "8.2 The Argument from
Experience" (title verified; content not read) on whether temporal consciousness favours a temporal
ontology — a lead, not a result.

## 4. What in this repository depends on this area

`research/what-the-past-can-fix/main.tex` (read in full):

- **§`sec:direction` ("The direction of time").** The paper offers three candidates for the
  source of the past-base asymmetry — thermodynamic, perspectival (Price), causal (Tooley). The
  experience literature supplies a fourth family the paper does not list: cognitive-functional
  accounts (Callender 2017; Hoerl & McCormack 2018), which explain records, efficacy and openness
  asymmetries without inventory distinctions and without a primitive arrow. The paper's own bullet
  in §`sec:revise` ("the asymmetry of records has a candidate explanation that uses no inventory
  distinction") is exactly this family; it should be named and cited. The characterisation of
  Price ("He presupposes that the present and flow are perspectival") is consistent with how the
  wider literature reads Price, but this task did not read Price; the repo's credit rests on the
  fixing-exchange note's three passages and is not independently confirmed here.
- **§`sec:bounds`, "Stated candidates" table and the diagnostic.** The table contains no
  experience-based candidate. Paul's argument (A) and Bernáth–Inan's argument (D) are support "of
  a different kind" — abductive and transcendental respectively — and fall in the same
  out-of-sorting class as Tooley's and the no-fact principle, a class the paper acknowledges
  ("Three candidates need support of a different kind") without listing these. Adding rows would
  strengthen, not weaken, the defined/primitive diagnostic.
- **§`sec:defeat` ("an independently defended tensed premise yielding the upper bound").** Such
  premises exist in print — Craig 2000: 138–165 and Schlesinger 1991 (locators via Bernáth &
  Inan), Skow 2009's experience-based motivation, Hare — but every one carries named published
  objections (Prosser 2013; Lee 2017; Callender per Gołosz; Cohen 1959). The conditional is
  well-calibrated; a reader could nonetheless take the paper to imply the shelf is empty, which
  it is not.
- **Bibliography.** Verified by grep: the paper cites Broad 1923 (for the growing block, not
  experience) and Callender 2000 only. There is no Dainton, Prosser, Paul, Prior, Husserl, James,
  Phillips or Lee. Part IV's hunt for upper-bound candidates therefore proceeds without the
  literature containing the most-discussed tensed premises. Nothing checked there is wrong by the
  standards of this literature, because nothing there engages it; the gap is incompleteness, not
  error.
- **`research/ontology-reconstruction/coordinator/ROLE_OF_EXISTENCE.md` §8 and §10** name the
  experience objection as the hardest burden ("why does experience have this present, renewed
  character?"). This area prices that burden: every available answer — retentional, extensional,
  functional, illusion-theoretic — is contested, so the burden is real but no unresolved datum
  currently forces a primitive.
- **`research/stein-gr/local-clock/RESULT.md`**: "A present can be thick in time and stay local.
  It cannot be selective in space." Correct as physics, but naive if quoted against the experience
  literature: experiential thickness in that literature is thickness of *content* (retentions,
  specious presents, resolution windows), not a co-presence relation over events, and experience
  never presents a spacelike extended present as such. The note should not be used as a reply to
  experience-based arguments without this distinction.
- **`research/time-direction/fixing-exchange/RESULT.md`**: its Price credit is at three-passage
  depth (its own label). The note's Proposition 3 entropy measure of openness-asymmetry is a
  measure of exactly what Callender's programme explains functionally; the two are complementary,
  not competing, and a future synthesis should connect them.
- **`research/horizons/horizons-and-news/RESULT.md`** and **`research/horizons/cone-algebras/RESULT.md`**:
  nothing in them depends on temporal experience. The cone-algebras note's caution that "a
  state-independent algebraic relation is not a present" is supported by this literature's
  deflationary lesson; the "news" concept is the epistemic asymmetry that Callender and
  Hoerl–McCormack explain, and could cite them if it ever draws an experiential moral.

## 5. Ten primary sources to read next, ranked

1. **Dainton, *Stream of Consciousness* (Routledge 2000)** — the full extensionalist case with
   overlap; would settle whether extensionalism needs a determinate specious-present duration, and
   whether Dainton's passage is experiential-only (as the repo's redteam assumes) or ontological.
   Already a gated-source request in PARITY_MAP.md.
2. **Prosser, *Experiencing Time* (OUP 2016)** — the complete perceptual-sensitivity argument and
   the semantics of tense; would settle the strongest anti-evidence case and whether Lee's
   objection lands. Partially covered by Prosser 2013 (read) and Lee 2017 (read).
3. **Broad, *An Examination of McTaggart's Philosophy*, vol. II (1933)** — the mature many-level
   retentional account; would settle Broad's own position against Rashbrook's reconstruction, and
   the exact status of Broad 1923: 351 in context. Scans exist on archive.org (the 1923 book is
   at `scientificthough00broauoft`; the 1933 volumes should be checked there too).
4. **Husserl, *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time (1893–1917)*, Brough
   trans. (Kluwer)** — the primary retentional ancestor; would settle what Husserl actually claims
   (the repo currently has only from-memory descriptions). Note a version discrepancy to resolve:
   Paul 2010 fn. 1 cites Kluwer 1990; the repo's phenomenology note says 1991.
5. **Callender, *What Makes Time Special?* (OUP 2017)** — the full functional account of manifest
   time; would settle whether it is a fourth candidate for §`sec:direction` and how it differs
   from Price's perspectivalism.
6. **Skow, *Objective Becoming* (OUP 2015)** — the deflation of passage and the moving spotlight;
   would settle what "passage" claims amount to for the paper's mirror/reciprocity discussion.
7. **Miller, "Against Passage Illusionism", *Ergo* (2023)** — full text is open; would settle the
   taxonomy of phenomenal-passage positions and the newest live dispute (open access,
   doi:10.3998/ergo.2914).
8. **Deng, "Our Experience of Passage on the B-Theory", *Erkenntnis* (2013)** — full text open;
   would settle the B-theoretic explanation the repo's §`sec:revise` bullet presupposes
   (doi:10.1007/s10670-013-9489-5).
9. **Baumgarten, Schnitzler & Lange, "Beta oscillations define discrete perceptual cycles...",
   *PNAS* (2015)** — full text open; would settle the current strength of the discrete-perception
   data (doi:10.1073/pnas.1501438112).
10. **Busch, Dubois & VanRullen, "The Phase of Ongoing EEG Oscillations Predicts Visual
    Perception", *J. Neurosci.* (2009)** — full text open; the canonical phase-of-perception
    result behind the perceptual-cycle programme (doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0113-09.2009).

Also queued, unverified locators: Kent & Wittmann (2021) on theories of consciousness and
