## 1. Main positions (stated as defenders would put them)

- **Endurantism.** An ordinary object persists by being wholly present at each time at which it
  exists; it has no temporal parts, and it has different spatial parts at different times. Defenders:
  van Inwagen, Lowe, Thomson, Haslanger (named in SEP temporal-parts and IEP lists; Haslanger 2003 read
  at abstract level only). Its own difficulty, which Sider presses, is saying what "wholly present"
  means without mereological constancy (Sider 1997, §2, full-text).
- **Perdurantism (worm theory).** An object persists by having a temporal part at each time (or
  subregion of its career); the object is the four-dimensional sum. Defenders: Lewis 1986 (book, not
  accessed; his terms "endurance/perdurance" and the problem of temporary intrinsics are cited at pp.
  202-205 by SEP identity-time), Sider 1997, Heller. Perdurance is not "no persistence"; the worm exists at many times.
- **Stage theory (exdurantism).** Ordinary objects are momentary stages; they persist by having
  temporal counterparts, and tensed talk about "Socrates" is made true by counterparts. Defenders:
  Sider (1996, 2001) and Hawley (2001), both books/papers not accessed; the characterization is from
  SEP temporal-parts, IEP and Haslanger's comments on Sider (all secondary or excerpt depth). Stage
  theorists need not deny the worms exist; the dispute with worm theory is semantic and about what the
  ordinary name refers to.
- **Spacetime reformulation by location.** Replace "wholly present" with exact location in regions.
  Endurance: persists and is exactly located at (many) achronal slices of its path. Perdurance: exactly
  located only at its whole path. Exdurance: located at exactly one achronal region. Balashov's chapter
  (D11-D13) and Gilmore 2006 (worm vs "saint") give these, both read in full-text excerpts.
- **Mixed and anti-temporal-parts views.** Some persistents have temporal parts, others not; four-dimensional
  extension without parts (Parsons, Miller). Reported only via SEP temporal-parts.
- **Reductionism about persons (Parfit).** Personal identity over time consists in psychological
  connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause (Relation R), non-branching; identity is not
  what matters, R is. Parfit 1984 ch. 10 excerpts only. Opponents: animalism (van Inwagen, Olson) and
  those who think identity matters (Sosa, Merricks), reported via SEP personal identity §1 (discovery).
- **The persistence-time link.** Three positions, all reported secondarily. (a) Presentism needs enduring
  objects, or at least an account of past-tensed truths (SEP temporal-parts §6; Merricks 1995/1999,
  not accessed). (b) Persistence and time are independent (Sider 1996b, cited in Sider 1997 n. 9, full-text).
  (c) Relativity removes the question's foundation: Gibson and Pooley take relativity to rule out
  non-eternalism and tensed theories, and say that "there is no time" in Minkowski spacetime, so every
  persistence doctrine stated in terms of times must be restated with regions.

## 2. Canonical arguments

**A. Temporary intrinsics (Lewis 1986, 202-205, via SEP identity-time §2.3; Haslanger 1989, 2003).**
(1) Things change their intrinsic properties (a plate is round then square). (2) Nothing is both round
and square. (3) So the change must be compatible with those properties being had at different "times"
without relativity of intrinsics to times. (4) Relating the plate to times makes shape extrinsic;
treating it as a property of temporal parts does not. (5) So persistents have temporal parts.
*Standard objection:* the endurantist relativizes instantiation or the copula, uses adverbial modification,
or (presentist) takes only present tensed facts as basic (SEP identity-time §2.4 via a summarising fetch;
Johnston, Haslanger 1989, Lowe, Lewis 2002 replying). Haslanger also notes the stage view struggles with
history-dependent intrinsics (comments on Sider, excerpts).

**B. Vagueness (Sider 1997, §3, read).** (P1) If not every assignment of parts to times has a minimal
diachronic fusion, some continuous series of cases has a cutoff. (P2) No continuous series has an abrupt
cutoff in whether such a fusion exists. (P3) Existence of such fusions is never vague. So (U) every
assignment has one; with a temporal-locality principle this entails four-dimensionalism. Sider assumes
that vagueness never comes from logic. *Objection:* mereological essentialism gives a non-vague
restriction (Sider rejects it because nothing then survives losing a part); Koslicki and Haslanger doubt
that the premises are dialectically neutral (Haslanger, comments on Sider: it assumes things the endurantist
is not committed to). Sider's later "Existence" paper defends non-vague existence (abstract-level only).

**C. Balashov's coexistence argument (Balashov 2000/2005; reconstructed from Gibson-Pooley §3, full-text).**
(1) In relativistic eternalism non-trivial coexistence is grounded in spacelike separation. (2) The
endurantist, not the perdurantist, must read coexistence as temporally loaded. (3) A temporally loaded,
spacelike-based coexistence leads to absurdity. So endurance fails in Minkowski spacetime.
*Standard objection:* Gilmore 2002 (abstract only) rejects (1) in favor of frame-relativity and (2)'s
asymmetry; Gibson and Pooley reply that Balashov relies on a privileged chain of coexistence sequences
and that chronologically coherent sequences exist.

**D. Hales-Johnson (2003, 535, quoted in full by Gibson-Pooley).** (1) Endurance means every proper part
is present at every time of the object's existence. (2) In a frame moving relative to the object, the object
has parts at different times. (3-6) Relativity holds, so every object has proper parts at different times. (7)
So endurantism is false. *Objection (Gibson-Pooley, §4):* the argument needs frame-relative "times" and
mereological constancy; premise 6 is what an eternalist endurantist says anyway, so 7 does not follow.

**E. Fission and the teletransporter (Parfit 1984, ch. 10, excerpts).** (1) In the Branch-Line case the
replica and I are two, and I will die. (2) Replica and I are exactly alike psychologically. (3) Identity
requires one-to-one relations; R does not. (4) So identity cannot be what matters; R does. *Objection:* animalism
and non-reductionist views deny that psychological continuity constitutes persistence; Sosa and Merricks
defend the importance of identity (SEP personal identity §1, discovery).

## 3. Live open problems and what would count as progress

1. **Stating the endurance-perdurance dispute at all.** Wholly present, temporal part, exact location. Progress:
   a definition acceptable to both sides and invariant under the choice of spacetime formalism (Sider; Gilmore;
   Donnelly cited in SEP).
2. **Where an enduring object is located in a relativistic spacetime.** Gilmore's Location Question: Rest-Frame,
   Top-Down, Bottom-Up or Every-Slice principles. Gibson and Pooley defend Every Slice, restricted to maximal
   achronal slices. Progress: a principle that works in curved, non-globally-hyperbolic spacetimes.
3. **Does persistence theory constrain the inventory of times?** Merricks versus Sider. Progress: an explicit
   argument from a stated persistence thesis plus a truthmaker or tensed-truth premise to eternalism, or a
   proof of independence.
4. **Whether the dispute is substantive** (SEP temporal-parts §2: McCall and Lowe, Hirsch). Progress: a
   metaontological criterion applied to the specific disagreement.
5. **Reductionism and what matters** (Parfit against Sosa and Merricks), and whether it needs a view of
   time. Not read beyond excerpts.

## 4. What in this repository depends on this area

Line numbers refer to `research/what-the-past-can-fix/main.tex` as read on 12 November.

- **Vocabulary collisions, which need fixing now.** The paper uses "persistence" for three different things.
  (i) Cumulativity of existence realms, "b <= c implies E_b contained in E_c" (§Realm structure, Proposition sp,
  l. 451, 463-465). (ii) Persistence of truths under entailment (l. 634-640). (iii) The abstract's "bridges about constitution and persistence" (l. 50), which reads
  as the cumulativity of (i) but invites the object sense. In this literature persistence
  means continuing to exist through time. The statement "A presentist denies persistence" (l. 465, and the
  table row at l. 767 "Denies persistence") is **wrong in that sense**: presentist endurantists exist
  (Zimmerman 1996 and Merricks 1999 are on persistence and presentism; Sider thinks the two issues are
  independent). The paper means *permanence* (Loss's word, used in Proposition tetrad, l. 645-650); rename it.
  Likewise "stage" in §sec:maximal and l. 677 means a stage of a growing block, not a stage-theory stage;
  and "constitution" (§sec:process, Brading's activity constitution) is not the clay and statue constitution
  of the persistence literature. A reader from the literature will misread all three.
- **Is the sorting recognisable and fair?** Partly. The paper's central observation (§sec:process, l. 723-730;
  §bounds, l. 591) is that constitution premises give only a lower bound R inside E_b, not an upper bound. In
  this literature the nearest relative is the independence of persistence theory from the theory of time
  (Sider) against the inference from perdurance to eternalism (Merricks). The structure is similar: a worm's
  parts need to exist if the worm does, but a worm's existence does not follow from what is in E_b. That makes the
  logic fair to perdurantists and to event realists. It is **not yet fair to endurantists or stage theorists**: the
  paper treats "constitution" as one lower-bound premise, whereas the three theories give three different R for
  the same activity (worm: its whole path; endurance: its achronal slices; stage theory: only counterparts), and
  those differences matter exactly where the paper compares pointy and bow-tie realms. This is our conjecture,
  untested.
- **Missing sources.** main.tex cites no work from the persistence literature (no Lewis, Sider, Haslanger,
  Hawley, Merricks, Balashov, Gilmore, Gibson-Pooley). Its bibliography does include Saunders 2002 and Savitt 2006.
  "No priority audit has been run" (l. 630) applies here too; Merricks 1999 and Zimmerman 1996 must be read before
  the lower-bound claim is asserted as new or not new.
- **Realms and slices.** The pointy and bow-tie choice (Prop. sp, Bigaj 2008) is a choice about which regions a
  standpoint includes; Gilmore's Every-Slice question is the same sort of choice for an object. Whether they
  coincide for a given law is not addressed in the paper, and no persistence source says.
- **The listed notes.** research/time-direction/fixing-exchange/RESULT.md, research/stein-gr/local-clock/RESULT.md
  and the horizons notes contain no persistence or constitution claims (a text search found only mathematical
  identities). Nothing in them need be revised for this lane. PARITY_MAP's "Parfit absent" stands: nothing in the repository
  depends on personal identity, and Parfit's argument (ch. 10 excerpts) is stated in terms of relations among events,
  with no claim about which events exist; any link to time ontology is a bridge premise nobody has argued here.

## 5. Ten primary sources to read next

1. Lewis, *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986), pp. 202-205 (plus Lewis 2002 reply to relativizers): settles what the premises of argument A are.
2. Merricks, "Persistence, Parts and Presentism" (Nous 1999), and 1995: whether perdurance needs eternalism.
3. Zimmerman, "Persistence and Presentism" (1996): the presentist endurantist's own statement.
4. Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001), the presentism and tense parts and ch. 4 §9: independence versus entailment.
5. Gilmore, "Where in the Relativistic World Are We?" §§4.2-5, and Gilmore 2008 (Compass): whether any location principle suits curved spacetime.
6. Gibson and Pooley (2006), §§2.2, 3, 7: complete the read; their preferred relativistic endurantism.
7. Balashov, *Persistence and Spacetime* (2010), chs. 5-8, and 2005 reply: the strongest relativistic case for the worm.
8. Hawley, *How Things Persist* (2001): the stage-theory statement, and its treatment of presentism.
9. Haslanger, "Persistence through Time" (2003): complete the read; relativizing instantiation.
10. Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Part III in full: whether the reductionist account presupposes a time ontology.
