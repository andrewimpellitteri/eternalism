## 1. Main positions

Each ends with what "production" (making the effect come about) amounts to on that view.

- **Reichenbach, statistical direction** (Reichenbach 1956, via SEP Hitchcock-Redei). Causal direction
  is the temporal direction in which conjunctive forks are open: correlated events have a prior
  screening-off common cause, rarely a later one. He treated it as the macrostatistical sibling of the
  second law. *Production:* none; causation is probabilistic dependence plus screening-off.
- **Lewis, contingent counterfactual direction** (Lewis 1979, via SEP Menzies-Beebee and Price-Weslake).
  Causal direction is the direction of counterfactual dependence. It is not built in, since Lewis
  allows backward causation; it holds because causes are massively overdetermined by their traces and
  effects are not. *Production:* none; dependence among events across worlds.
- **Salmon and Dowe, process and conserved-quantity theories** (Dowe 2000 abstract; Salmon via SEP).
  A causal process is a world line of an object that possesses (Dowe) or transmits (Salmon) a conserved
  quantity; an interaction is an intersection of world lines exchanging one. *Production:* persistence
  and exchange along world lines. Direction is added from outside: both appeal to a fork asymmetry, and
  Dowe fixes direction by the majority of open forks, so backward causation stays possible
  (SEP Frisch-Blanchard, SEP Dowe 2016 excerpt; discovery depth).
- **Woodward and Pearl, interventionism** (Woodward 2003 abstract; SEP Woodward intro). X causes Y if a
  possible intervention on X changes Y. Non-reductive; intervention is itself causal. The arrows of a
  causal graph encode direction, not temporal priority, and models can lack time order (Woodward's
  Spohn note, excerpts). *Production:* none beyond invariance. Pearl was not read at all.
- **Price and Menzies, agency and perspective** (Menzies-Price 1993 via SEP; Price-Weslake 2008, full
  text). There is no intrinsic causal asymmetry; the cause-effect distinction and its temporal
  orientation come from the standpoint of a deliberating agent, characterised epistemically. Price and
  Weslake assert this is now a "considerable consensus", which is their claim. *Production:* "bringing
  about" is ostensively given by agency, not a worldly relation.
- **Albert, Kutach and Loewer, the Past Hypothesis (PH) route** (via Price-Weslake sec. 3, SEP). PH plus
  the statistical postulate make small local changes ("causal handles") ramify into the future but not
  the macroscopic past: a branching tree toward the future. That grounds counterfactual and causal
  asymmetry. Frisch objects that the structure is a web or an upside-down tree. *Production:* none.
- **Tooley, causation as primitive and prior to time** (Tooley, online ch. 14, read directly in
  14.1 and 14.4.1). The causal relation is asymmetric and basic; earlier-than is defined by a finite
  chain of causal and spatial links with at least one causal link, given principle R (a cause is earlier
  than its effect). Direction of time is then pervasive and independent of boundary conditions.
  *Production:* the primitive relation, plus causal laws and logical probability in the argument the
  project note reconstructs. Price and Weslake call this "hyperrealism". His 2024 abstract says he now
  treats causation as a theoretical term, not analytically basic.
- **Deflationary.** Russell, and Hawking in Price-Weslake's quotation: with deterministic reversible
  laws, "A causes B" and "B causes A" are interchangeable, so causation fixes no direction. Opposed by
  Frisch (causal structure is part of physics, SEP) and Maudlin (direction in the laws, via SEP only).

**Does any account support a time-asymmetric inventory?** Only Tooley's, and only through a further
argument, not through production as such. Salmon-Dowe production is world-line based and symmetric. The
Lewis, Reichenbach, PH and interventionist views give an asymmetry of dependence, probability or
agency, not of what exists. Price-Weslake object that a primitive arrow leaves the same physics
compatible with a reversed arrow, making it epistemically idle (their sec. 1.3, a premise-level
objection I could not check against a Tooley reply).

## 2. Canonical arguments

**A. Lewis's asymmetry of overdetermination, and Elga's objection** (Elga 2001, full text; Lewis 1979 via
secondary). (1) Laws deterministic; (2) the nearest antecedent-world matches the past and diverges by a
small miracle, or matches the future and converges by a small miracle; (3) convergence needs a large
miracle because traces of the antecedent must be faked; so (4) the past is held fixed. Elga: (3) fails,
because in thermodynamically irreversible processes a small change to the end state, run backwards by the
laws, yields a past where the event never happened and all "traces" are misleading. Without a
Past-Hypothesis-type restriction there is no asymmetry of miracles. Reply: add PH (argument C).

**B. Reichenbach's fork asymmetry** (via SEP). (1) Correlated, non-causally related events have a screening
common cause; (2) in our world forks are open to the future, not the past; so (3) the open side is the
direction of causation. Objections: Horwich (the inference from correlation to past interaction does not
run through entropy); Arntzenius (reversible deterministic dynamics gives every fork a closed
counterpart); and causal modelling infers direction from colliders, not forks.

**C. The causal-handle argument** (Albert, Kutach, Loewer; via Price-Weslake sec. 3). (1) Dynamics reversal
invariant; (2) PH and the statistical postulate hold; (3) so small local changes spread to a
macroscopic range of futures and not of pasts; (4) causes are what handles affect. Objections: Frisch's
two-chamber gas, where the past also diverges; Price-Weslake's Future-Hypothesis symmetry (a future
boundary condition would not remove the sense of acting on the future, so PH cannot explain why we do not
act on the past); Loew 2018 (abstract only) that it misconstrues the asymmetry.

**D. Price and Weslake on deliberation** (full text). (1) An account of causal asymmetry must explain
why we deliberate for later ends, not earlier (the Practical Relevance Constraint); (2) Hume's convention
and hyperrealism and the third-arrow route fail it; (3) deliberation has a temporal asymmetry
characterisable without causal notions; so (4) start from deliberation. Objection, which the authors
record: it makes causation less objective, and the genealogy of causal concepts is not completed.

**E. Tooley's causal theory of time** (online ch. 14, sec. 14.4.1 direct). (1) Causal and temporal
priority share formal structure; (2) reductions of time's direction to entropy or expansion give wrong
answers in simple or inverted worlds; (3) R holds because backward causation is impossible (his Ch. 4,
not read); so (4) define "earlier" by causal-plus-spatial chains. Objections: Smart-type circularity
(he replies in 14.5, via summary); Lewis, Dowe and Price take backward causation as possible, which
removes premise (3); the account assumes absolute simultaneity.

## 3. Live open problems

1. **Does a past boundary condition ground causal asymmetry?** Progress: a stated criterion for a
   "handle" and "macroscopic branching" applied to Frisch's two-chamber case, with the coarse-graining
   named; or Loew-style objection answered in print.
2. **Is there an intrinsic causal direction?** Progress: a case where primitive direction and the
   statistical direction come apart and something differs (Dowe appeals to quantum backward causation,
   by his chapter summary), or an argument that the epistemic-access objection fails.
3. **Can backward causation be excluded without assuming time order?** The growing-block route to an
   upper bound needs it. Progress: an argument for or against Tooley's Ch. 4 premise that does not use
   an earlier/later ordering.
4. **Direction without time.** Colliders and graph arrows need no time order (Woodward), yet applied
   physics supplies initial-randomness assumptions. Progress: which assumption is prior (Loewer and
   Hausman-Woodward disagree, per SEP Frisch-Blanchard).
5. **Can production carry direction?** Progress: a fork-free directional criterion for conserved-quantity
   processes (Earman's well-posed initial-value suggestion, via SEP, is the one lead).
