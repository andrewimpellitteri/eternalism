## Primary-text checks

The main audit correctly distinguishes the reflexive convention from the more common strict chronological relation: Clifton and Hogarth explicitly take `I−`, `I+`, `J−`, and `J+` as reflexive (p. 360). Their definability condition is invariance under time-orientation-preserving metric automorphisms, with implicit definability allowed (pp. 361–362). The time orientation is part of the structure.

The lemma (p. 364) derives `I− ⊆ B` from worldline becoming, cross-worldline transitivity, all timelike worldlines, and meshing. It does not derive `J− ⊆ B`; the past-null boundary remains an additional possible premise. The proof’s geometric construction and its use of meshing are represented accurately in the main audit.

Theorem 1 (pp. 365–367) assumes an absolute relation, implicit definability from the time-oriented metric, transitivity, and chronological inclusion. Its alternatives are exactly reflexive `I−`, `J−`, and universal `U`. It does not assume non-universality, and it does not establish a unique relation under chronological inclusion. With the stronger causal inclusion and non-universality, the listed alternatives leave only `J−`; that is a consequence of the classification, not the theorem’s stated premise set.

Theorem 2 (pp. 370–375) quantifies over every inextendible timelike line and requires each line-relative relation to be definable from the time-oriented metric plus that line. It also requires worldline becoming and transitivity across different lines. The alternatives are uniform across the family. Meshing connects the family to an absolute relation, and the proof then invokes Theorem 1 after its invariance argument. The audit appropriately marks the group-generation step as read but not independently reconstructed. Corollary 2 and the examples on pp. 376–380 preserve the limit: adding distinguished lines or initial surfaces changes the structure and falls outside the theorem’s all-lines premise set.
