## Definitions and conventions needed to state the results correctly

The model is 3+1-dimensional Minkowski spacetime `(R^4, η)` with an added choice `T` of time orientation (pp. 359–361). Events are points. `I−`, `I+`, `J−`, and `J+` denote past/future chronological and causal connectibility. The authors explicitly make all four relations reflexive “for convenience,” including `I−` (p. 360). This is not the common convention where chronological connectibility is irreflexive. Thus their `I−` candidate is the reflexive closure of strict chronological past; their `J−` already includes its standard reflexive boundary. In this paper a becoming relation includes its diagonal by the chronological-becoming premise or worldline-becoming premise, rather than by a separate general reflexivity axiom.

For an absolute relation `B`, “definable” is taken in the weak sense of implicit definability, with invariance under every time-orientation-preserving metric automorphism (pp. 361–362). Equivalently in their formulation, the induced set map commutes with all such automorphisms. Time orientation is an objective primitive of the structure, not derived in the argument. Transitivity is an additional requirement.

For the worldline case, `B_λ` is a relation defined only for a second event on the worldline `λ`. The family ranges over **all** inextendible timelike worldlines in Minkowski spacetime, not merely the actual observers or a selected set. Three conditions must not be collapsed:

* **Worldline becoming:** if `b ∈ λ`, then every event in the reflexive chronological past of `b` that also lies on `λ` belongs to `B_λ(b)` (pp. 362–363). In particular `b ∈ B_λ(b)`.
* **Worldline transitivity:** for arbitrary worldlines `λ, μ`, if `a ∈ B_λ(b)` and `b ∈ B_μ(c)`, then `a ∈ B_μ(c)` (p. 363). It includes transitivity within one relation but is stronger: it quantifies across different worldlines. Taking `b=c`, together with worldline becoming, makes observers whose lines intersect agree at that event.
* **Meshing:** an absolute `B` is defined by agreement of all worldline relations at an event: `a B b` iff `a B_λ b` for every `λ` through `b` (pp. 362, 372). The paper treats this as definitional for connecting absolute and worldline-dependent becoming, though it acknowledges readers may instead regard it as an extra assumption.

In Theorem 2, each `B_λ` must be implicitly definable from the time-oriented metric **and its corresponding λ**: it is invariant under each time-orientation-preserving metric automorphism that preserves that same λ (pp. 370–371). This does not allow a distinguished collection of other lines, a matter distribution, or an initial global surface as extra structure.

## Result extraction and proof scope

**Lemma (p. 364).** For a family over all worldlines, worldline becoming plus worldline transitivity entails chronological becoming, `I− ⊆ B`. The proof chooses, for any `a` in the chronological past of `b`, another timelike line through `a` that intersects the first line at a suitable `c` in the chronological future of `a`; line-local becoming supplies the two links and cross-line transitivity composes them. Meshing then yields the absolute inclusion. This proves only chronological inclusion, not null-cone inclusion or causal-past inclusion. It does not require the line-definability premise later used in Theorem 2.

**Theorem 1 (pp. 365–367).** For an absolute, worldline-independent relation in time-oriented Minkowski spacetime: if `B` is implicitly definable from time-oriented metric relations, transitive, and satisfies chronological becoming (`I− ⊆ B`), then `B` is exactly one of `I−`, `J−`, or the universal relation `U`. No separate non-universality axiom is stated; consequently `U` is explicitly an allowed result. No stronger causal-inclusion premise is assumed. The proof starts with a pair in `B` outside `I−`; an extra spacelike or future pair, using invariance under rotations/boosts/translations and transitivity, forces `U`, while an added past-null pair expands `B` to `J−` unless the relation has already become universal. Otherwise it remains `I−`. Corollary 1(A) (p. 367) says no non-universal objective becoming can be defined from unoriented Minkowski metric alone. Corollary 1(B) says metric definability from the time-oriented metric is equivalent, for these candidates, to causal definability.

This is a weakened version of Stein's causal-becoming setup: Clifton–Hogarth explicitly question the additional assumption `J− ⊆ B`, motivate only `I− ⊆ B`, and show that the weaker premise admits chronological past as an additional candidate. Their section-2 discussion explains why the status of past-null events is contested; it does not present `J− ⊆ B` as derived from `I− ⊆ B`.

**Theorem 2 (pp. 371–375).** For the family over all timelike worldlines, suppose (i) worldline becoming, (ii) cross-worldline transitivity, and (iii) for every λ, implicit definability from the time-oriented metric plus that corresponding λ. Then uniformly across the whole family, every `B_λ` is the restriction of `I−`, or every one is the restriction of `J−`, or every one is the restriction of `U`. It is not a menu permitting a different choice for each line.

Proof architecture: by worldline becoming and worldline transitivity, observers through `b` agree on `B_λ(b)`; meshing identifies it with `B(b)`. The lemma gives `I− ⊆ B`; cross-line transitivity gives absolute transitivity. The technical step upgrades line-preserving invariance to invariance under the full group of time-orientation-preserving metric automorphisms: translations are generated from line-preserving timelike translations, spatial rotations/reflections are obtained using translations and line stabilizers, and boosts from compositions of rotations about different timelike lines (pp. 373–375). Theorem 1 then applies. I read the presented proof but did not independently reconstruct or verify each group-generation calculation.

**Corollary 2 and scope limits (pp. 376–380).** For a reflexive family of worldline-dependent relations satisfying worldline transitivity, each relation is definable from metric, orientation and its line iff the induced absolute relation is definable from metric and orientation alone. This is the paper's route to a Malament-related application: a family of line-relative equivalence relations cannot evade the corresponding absolute definability obstruction while preserving the stipulated transitivity. This corollary is not an unconditional theorem about every simultaneity relation; it depends on the stated family and transitivity structure.

Theorem 2 expressly does **not** cover enriched structures `(R^4, η, T, {λ_i})` with privileged lines or initial surfaces. Clifton–Hogarth explain that Peacock's and Muller's proposals can work only by introducing such extra structure (or otherwise failing their all-worldlines assumptions). In particular, their argument against standard-simultaneity worldline becoming is not that a line-relative simultaneity relation is undefinable from its own line; it is that the family does not meet cross-worldline transitivity without a distinguished line/family. For Muller, the initial now surface is an additional input unless a special common initial event is used; even then a selected finite particle/worldline family is extra structure relative to all possible events/worldlines.

Section 4 (pp. 381–387) does **not** infer that objective becoming is impossible. It records objections to `I−`/`J−` as lacking a spatially extended now and to `U` as erasing a fixed past/open future, then argues that rejecting extended nows is not automatically fatal and that `I−`/`J−` have a determinism/domain-of-dependence feature. The discussion of GR and relativistic quantum theory is expressly prospective and tentative, not a classification result for those theories. The authors do not advocate objective becoming and do not establish that Minkowski spacetime selects an ontology.

* **Savitt (2011 draft; secondary for Clifton–Hogarth).** His main paraphrase of Lemma, Theorems 1 and 2 and the three alternatives is substantially faithful. His note that the authors make `I−` and `J−` reflexive “to smooth the proofs” matches the primary convention, though the primary itself says it adopts reflexivity “for convenience.” Savitt explicitly omitted the proofs, so his report cannot substitute for this primary read. His own warning that the 2020 published version was not read remains applicable to the Savitt record, not to the primary article.
* **Saunders (2000 preprint, secondary).** The lane records his p. 3 statement that Clifton–Hogarth showed “no transitive worldline-dependent relation” can be defined and therefore no equivalence relation/present follows. As an unqualified paraphrase of Theorem 2 this is too broad: Theorem 2 permits `I−`, `J−` and `U` restrictions, all transitive under the paper's convention. The narrower presentist point can be made if Saunders' target is an equivalence relation for extended simultaneity/present: neither causal nor chronological past is symmetric, while `U` is trivial/universal, so none supplies a nontrivial equivalence-class present. That narrower reading is consistent with the paper's theorem; attribute the stronger wording to Saunders and do not quote it as the theorem statement.
* **Thyssen (2023 review draft/preprint, secondary).** His p. 39 Stein-condition summary is not a restatement of Clifton–Hogarth Theorem 1: it attributes reflexivity, transitivity, non-universality, causal-past inclusion and definability to “Stein's Theorem 1,” without the paper's chronological-only weakening or its three alternatives. His footnote 51, as recorded in the lane, says adding chronological inclusion collapses a condition to `I−`; that must not be confused with a reading of the primary theorem: Theorem 1 says chronological inclusion plus definability and transitivity permits `I−`, `J−`, **or** `U`. Thyssen's discussion is a secondary map of several arguments, not proof evidence for this theorem.
* **Callender / Saunders “no-go” lineage.** The lane correctly marks Callender's *Shedding Light on Time* as discovery-depth and reports divergent secondary condition lists from Savitt and Thyssen. This audit does not resolve Callender's own theorem because the primary was not read. Theorem 1's U alternative is not itself a non-universality result; one cannot silently splice Callender's extra condition(s) into Clifton–Hogarth or vice versa.
