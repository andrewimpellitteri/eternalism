# Literature defeater check for F1–F4 (Z_3 clock + qubit model)

Bounded check, 2026-10-21. Primary arXiv texts only, fetched as arXiv HTML with
curl and searched as plain text. Quotes are verbatim from that HTML. Inline math
appears as the HTML `alttext` LaTeX. Equation and section numbers are the
paper's own. "Reviewer inference" marks my reasoning, not anything the authors
say. Read depth is given per source at the end. No novelty claim is made or
implied. A "not found" verdict covers only the sources listed here.

Model under test (from `SPEC.md`, `model_output.txt`): clock ℂ³, `H_C = diag(0,1,2)`,
qubit `H_S = σ_z/2`, `H_int = g K⊗σ_x` with `[K,H_C] ≠ 0`. Constraint surfaces are
eigenspaces `V_λ` of the full H. Reduction is `R(k) = √3(⟨τ_k|⊗1)`, and the twirl is
`G[X] = Σ_λ P_λ X P_λ`.

## F1: Dirac (twirled) and Page–Wootters (PW) agree on physical states for any interaction; disagreement only through normalization

**Verdict: immediately implied.** The identity is the standard property that a
twirl is self-adjoint and fixes invariant states. HSC use it in exactly this
role: to rewrite an interacting-constraint PW probability as a ratio of twirled
observables. The literature does **not** state it as a general "trinity with
interactions" result. The trinity paper names that question as open.

Decisive quotes:

- Hausmann, Schmidhuber, Castro-Ruiz, arXiv:2308.10967v4, App. F, around Eq. (112).
  The PM probability comes from an *interacting* constraint (Eq. 109).
  > "Then, using the cyclicity of the trace and the fact that $\outerproduct{\eta}{\eta}_{CSA}$ is left unchanged under incoherent G-twirling $\mathcal{T}$ (with respect to the TO time translation), we can rewrite eq. 108 as [Eq. 112] … where we used that $\mathcal{T}$ is self-adjoint"

  > "The operators in the numerator and denominator of eq. 112 are explicitly invariant under the action generated by the TO constraint."

  Caveat: HSC twirl over the *non-interacting* TO group. In F1 the twirl is over
  the full interacting H. The algebra is the same; the group is not.
- HSC, Sec. 4.2.4 (last paragraphs). This is the normalization point:
  > "Moreover, the denominator of the probability rule, eq. 14 becomes non-trivial, which is a departure from the Born rule of ordinary quantum mechanics."

  HSC Sec. 4.2 (opening):
  > "The probability rule remains formally the same as eq. 14 (changing $\ket{t}_{C}\rightarrow\ket{\phi_{t}}_{C}$ ), with the important difference that the denominator is now not always equal to 1."
- Chataignier, Höhn, Lock, Mele, arXiv:2409.06479v3, Theorem 3 and Corollary 3
  (Sec. VI.2.3). Expectation values agree between Dirac and PW, and the PW reduction
  "preserves the physical inner product". This is proved for **non-interacting**
  constraints only. Sec. II.2 says:
  > "we shall henceforth assume that no interactions between $C$ and $S$ are present."
- Höhn, Smith, Lock, arXiv:1912.00033v3, Sec. IX (Conclusion):
  > "the most pressing generalization of our work is to explore the validity of the trinity in the context of interactions between the chosen clock and the evolving system."
- Ludescher, Mekonnen, Galley, Müller, arXiv:2607.24976v1, App. B.1. With an
  interaction, the PW reduction is no longer the perspective-neutral frame reduction:
  > "The physical Hilbert space is now determined by the full interacting constraint, while the clock coherent state system is still generated only by the clock Hamiltonian. These constraints now generate different structures in general, which is neither the case (for the version of the reduction maps) in [34], nor in our generalized framework."

**What would still be needed.** (i) Say exactly which "Dirac observable" is meant.
The literature's relational observable is the *clock-cycle (partial) twirl* of
`|τ⟩⟨τ|⊗f_S` (Chataignier Eq. 66). F1 uses the full pinching
`Σ_λ P_λ(E_k⊗O)P_λ`, and these are different objects once `H_int ≠ 0`. F1 as
computed is the trivial pinching identity. (ii) Show whether a *τ-parametrized
family* of relational Dirac observables (Heisenberg picture) still exists and
matches PW under interaction. Nothing read establishes this.

## F2: `[H_int,H_C] ≠ 0` makes p(k) non-uniform and R(k) non-isometric; a commuting interaction keeps p(k) = 1/N and R(k) isometric

**Verdict: partly covered. The unqualified wording is defeated.** That
interactions can break unitarity and make the conditional norm time-dependent is
well established, for continuous ideal or bandlimited clocks. The commuting case
being norm-preserving is stated for "gravitational-like" (`H_C⊗X`) couplings in
finite dimensions. Primary texts, however, contain **finite-dimensional examples
where the interaction does not commute with the clock Hamiltonian and yet
evolution is unitary or isometric.** So `[H_int,H_C] ≠ 0` is not sufficient for F2.

Covering quotes:

- Smith & Ahmadi, arXiv:1712.00081v3, Sec. 3.1, the unnumbered display after
  Eq. (15). That display (paraphrased) shows
  `⟨⟨Ψ|[H_int, |t⟩⟨t|⊗I_S]|Ψ⟩⟩ = −i d/dt ⟨⟨Ψ|(|t⟩⟨t|⊗I_S)|Ψ⟩⟩`: the commutator of
  `H_int` with the clock projector is the clock-time derivative of the conditional
  norm. The text continues:
  > "which vanishes if the physical inner product is independent of $t$ . Therefore, the condition given in Eq. (15) is equivalent to demanding that the physical inner product is independent of $t$ , as demanded by Eq. (12)."

  Here a t-independent norm is *imposed* (Eq. 12), not derived.
- HSC Sec. 4.2.2, after Eq. (17):
  > "In general, the evolution corresponding to eq. 17 is non-unitary."

  This is for a bandlimited clock `L²[−E,E]` (Sec. 2, footnote 2) with a
  time-dependent measurement coupling `N_C∫dt|φ_t⟩⟨φ_t|⊗K_SA(t)` (Eq. 15). It is not
  a finite periodic clock.
- Cafasso et al., arXiv:2407.00161v2, Sec. VI (Discussion):
  > "Furthermore, this coefficient can only be constant in $t$ if the constraint is of the type discussed in Sec. IV. A non-constant coefficient and the corresponding contribution to the dynamical laws are expected if a different constraint is assumed"

  App. B:
  > "The same is true when $\ket{\Psi}$ is an eigenstate of the non-interacting Hamiltonian (13) or in the presence of a gravitational-like interaction (37). Indeed, it is easy to see that the non-normalized state $\bra{t}_{C}\ket{\Psi}$ evolves unitarily in $t$ , and thus $a(t)$ must be a constant."

  Sec. IV.1:
  > "when the interaction Hamiltonian $\mathcal{\hat{H}}_{int}$ commutes with the local Hamiltonians $\mathcal{\hat{H}}_{J}$ of the adopted clock, we can still use the machinery of the previous sections"

  This is the closest prior statement of F2. Note that `a²(t)` ∝ p(t) (App. B, Eq. 95).
- Rijavec, arXiv:2504.01579v4, abstract and Secs. III–VI. Necessary and sufficient
  conditions for unitarity, up to "constraint equivalence", via the rate operator
  `α = i[H_U, T_C]` (Eqs. 7–9). The result is for **ideal clocks only** (Sec. II):
  > "it would be useful to consider how these results extend to non-ideal clocks, which are often treated using POVMs rather than projective measurements … I leave these investigations for future work." (Sec. VI)
- Baumann & Lock, arXiv:2603.08845v1, Sec. VI.1:
  > "In general such interactions may result in non-unitary evolution of the system with respect to the clock [41, 42, 25, 47]."

Defeaters of the unqualified "`[H_int,H_C] ≠ 0` ⇒ non-uniform p, non-isometric R":

- Smith & Ahmadi, Sec. 4.3, Eqs. (24)–(26). Qubit clock `H_C = Ωσ_z`,
  `H_int = a(σ_x⊗σ_x − σ_y⊗σ_y) + b(σ_z⊗σ_z + I⊗I)`:
  > "We conclude that the coupling between the clock and and system described by the interaction Hamiltonian in Eq. (24) has no effect on the relational dynamics"

  > "the fact that $\ket{\psi_{S}(t_{0})}$ can be an arbitrary state in $\mathcal{H}_{S}$ follows from the fact that the dimension of the null space of the total Hamiltonian … is equal to the dimension of the system Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{S}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2}$ , $\dim\left[{\rm Null}\left(H\right)\right]=\dim\left[\mathcal{H}_{S}\right]=2$ ."

  Reviewer inference, analytic and not run: `[σ_x⊗σ_x − σ_y⊗σ_y, σ_z⊗I] = −2i(σ_y⊗σ_x + σ_x⊗σ_y) ≠ 0`, so this interaction does **not** commute with `H_C`. It annihilates the constraint surface `span{|10⟩,|01⟩}`, which is why nothing changes.
- Boette & Rossignoli, arXiv:1806.00956v2, Sec. II, Eqs. (1)–(5). Finite cyclic
  N-dimensional clock with constraint `𝒰|Ψ⟩=|Ψ⟩`, `𝒰 = Σ_t U_{t,t−1}⊗|t⟩⟨t−1|`:
  > "Note, however, that ${\cal J}$ will contain $S-T$ interaction terms in the general case where $U_{t,t-1}$ depends on $t$ ."

  > "Each eigenvalue has degeneracy equal to the dimension $d_{S}={\rm dim}{\cal H}_{S}$ of the system space"

  > "The states $|S_{t}\rangle$ will then have a unit norm if $|\Psi\rangle$ is normalized."

  Reviewer inference: `𝒰` commutes with `1⊗(clock shift)` iff `U_{t,t−1}` is
  t-independent. Hence `[𝒥, 1⊗P_T] ≠ 0` exactly in the interacting case. This is a
  finite periodic clock with a non-commuting interaction, full-dimension surfaces,
  uniform p and isometric reduction.
- Ludescher et al., arXiv:2607.24976v1, Example 32 (App. B.2). On `ℤ_n`,
  `H = P_1+P_2+K(X_1−X_2)²` with particle 1 as the local clock. The authors note
  that `(X_1−X_2)²` commutes with `P_1+P_2`, and give `H_phys = span{|d;Kd²⟩}`
  (dimension n). Their local-seed perspectival state is
  `|ψ(g)⟩^{(1)} = … Σ_d c_d e^{−i2πgKd²/n}|g,d+g⟩`. Reviewer inference: its norm is
  g-independent, so p(g) is uniform, even though `K(X_1−X_2)²` does not commute
  with the local clock generator `P_1`. The authors' own point is that the local
  clock gives a g-dependent (non-autonomous) generator:
  > "The perspectival system state does evolve, but not under a time-independent Schrödinger generator."

**What would still be needed.** Restate F2 with a sufficient condition that is
actually used. For example: "for the specific `K` tested, and for generic `g`".
Or give a structural criterion: the interaction must act non-trivially on the
surface, and/or the constraint spectrum must not be commensurate with the clock
period. Reviewer caution on the commuting half: if `[H_int,H_C] = 0` and a surface
`V_λ` mixes two `H_C` sectors n≠m (an accidental cross-sector degeneracy), then
`p(k)` contains cross terms `⟨φ_m|φ_n⟩`. These need not vanish, because `φ_n`, `φ_m`
are eigenvectors of different blocks `H_S + h_n`, `H_S + h_m`. "Commuting ⇒ p(k)=1/N"
has been shown here only on surfaces inside a single `H_C` sector. This is the
generic case for the `|0⟩⟨0|` control, but it is not a theorem. No source read
gives a finite-dimensional, non-ideal-clock analogue of Rijavec's iff condition.

## F3: generic interactions split every degenerate surface into 1-D surfaces, so no family of initial states and no classifiable dynamical map; is Smith–Ahmadi footnote 1 the same observation?

**Verdict: partly covered.** The *non-interacting* version is stated explicitly:
incommensurate spectra give a ≤1-D physical Hilbert space and a trivial relational
theory. The link "family of arbitrary initial states ⇔ surface dimension =
dim H_S" is stated in one finite example. No source read states the
*interacting, generic-splitting* version, or the consequence for memory
classification. **Smith–Ahmadi footnote 1 is related but not the same
observation** (see below).

Covering quotes:

- Chataignier et al., arXiv:2409.06479v3, Sec. IV.1, text after Eq. (61):
  > "Noting that the left-hand side is an integer, we see that if there do not exist two eigenvalues of $\hat{H}_{S}$ which differ by an integer multiple of the clock frequency $\omega_{t}$ , then $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ can be at most one-dimensional (i.e. $\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys}\simeq\mathbb{C}$ ), and the relational theory is thus trivial."

  Example 12 (Sec. V.2):
  > "It is therefore clear that in this model there do not exist any quantum relational Dirac observables which describe how some system degrees of freedom evolve relative to the clock."

  Sec. II.2, the interaction remark:
  > "Furthermore, interactions, unless fine-tuned, would typically ruin the periodicity of the dynamics."

  This covers commensurability as the criterion for surface dimension, which is
  task item 4. It is proved only for `H = H_C + H_S`.
- Smith & Ahmadi, Sec. 4.3 (quoted under F2). An arbitrary initial system state
  is available *because* `dim Null(H) = dim H_S`.
- Rijavec, arXiv:2204.11740v3, Sec. VI.2 (qubit clock, non-interacting):
  > "In this case, to avoid a trivial evolution, one must consider only non-degenerate Hamiltonians of clock and system such that their sum has a doubly degenerate zero eigenvalue"

  His interacting analysis (Sec. V.1, Eqs. 47–52) restricts to `V = X⊗h` with
  `[V, h] = 0`. That is the commuting class, which does not split this way.
- Smith & Ahmadi, footnote 1, attached to the inner-product choice in Eq. (11),
  Sec. 3.1:
  > "We should emphasize this is a choice of inner product and normalization of the physical states, which may severely reduce the size of the physical Hilbert space."

  **Is this the same as F3? Not as written.** The footnote says that *imposing* a
  t-independent conditional norm (Eqs. 11–12) restricts which constraint solutions
  are admitted. F3 says that the *spectrum* of the interacting H already leaves only
  1-D eigenspaces, before any inner-product choice. Reviewer inference: the two
  meet only on degenerate surfaces. There, demanding constant `p(k)` would cut the
  surface to a subspace. On 1-D surfaces the footnote's restriction becomes a
  pass/fail on a single state. The footnote does not compute either effect.

Formulation dependence (bears directly on F3):

- HSC Sec. 5 and Boette–Rossignoli Sec. II pose the constraint as invariance under
  a clock-step unitary, not as an eigenvalue equation for a generic Hermitian H.
  Boette–Rossignoli then get degeneracy `d_S` for every eigenvalue *by
  construction*, interactions included. Ludescher Example 32 likewise keeps an
  n-dimensional `H_phys` with an interaction, because the constraint generates a
  `ℤ_n` action. F3's "generic splitting" therefore depends on choosing an
  eigenvalue constraint for an `H` whose spectrum is not commensurate with the
  clock period.

**What would still be needed.** (i) State F3 as conditional on the eigenvalue
formulation and on non-commensurate spectrum. Lifting of degeneracy by a generic
perturbation is textbook, so the PW-specific content is only the consequence.
(ii) Show that no alternative definition rescues a family of initial states: for
example a group-averaged (`ℤ_N`-twirl) physical space, Rijavec-style "constraint
equivalence", or Ludescher's non-local seeds. (iii) The "no classifiable memory"
consequence was not found in any source read.

## F4: on an accidental 2-D interacting surface, reduced maps are non-isometric and, the clock being periodic, trace distance must revive (backflow)

**Verdict: not found** for PW constraint models with finite periodic clocks.
Revival given periodicity is **immediately implied** (reviewer inference): a
non-constant, period-N sequence of distances must rise after it falls. The
interpretive step "revival = information backflow / non-Markovianity" is **not
supported**, and nearby sources point the other way.

Nearest sources:

- Nandi & Petruccione, arXiv:2606.08595v1 (main text). Clock-conditioned reduced
  dynamics is "generically non-Markovian". The setting differs: Schwinger–Tomonaga
  evolution in an external foliation, a bath B, a finite-resolution clock
  *instrument*, no Wheeler–DeWitt constraint, no periodic finite clock. It also
  flags the normalization nonlinearity (after Eq. 7):
  > "Complete positivity therefore applies at the level of the conditioned map and subnormalized state, while the normalized conditioned state evolves nonlinearly due to relational conditioning."
- Rijavec, arXiv:2504.01579v4, Sec. II:
  > "Incidentally, this shows that non-unitarity can also appear at the level of the Universe, where no external entangled system has been traced out. Therefore, this effect is different from the non-unitary dynamics of open quantum systems"
- Mendes, Brito, Soares-Pinto, arXiv:2107.11452v1, Sec. V. A finite quasi-ideal
  clock with gravitational coupling gives non-unitary, non-linear terms:
  > "the non-unitary and non-linear effects arise due to the non-ideal nature of the chosen clock model, and the “idealness” can be related to its dimension"

  It contains no divisibility or backflow analysis.
- HSC Sec. 4.2.2, after Eq. (19). Non-unitarity is transient:
  > "The evolution … is only unitary for $F(t-\tau)=0,1$ , which is the case for $t\gg\tau$ or $t\ll\tau$ ."

  The periodic-clock solution (App. E.3, Eqs. 97–107) gives no trace-distance
  analysis.
- Chataignier et al., abstract item (3). For non-interacting periodic clocks the
  dynamics in all three pictures is "necessarily periodic". This is where the
  revival half comes from.

**What would still be needed.** The A_k are non-isometric linear maps. After
normalization they are nonlinear and need not be CPTP on any fixed state family.
So the BLP backflow criterion, CP-divisibility, and the process tensor do not
apply as stated. Calling the revival "memory" needs either (i) a linear CPTP family
defined on the surface, which a 2-D surface of a qubit supports only once a
normalization convention is fixed, or (ii) the explicit statement that this is
revival of a nonlinear, normalized map, which is not open-system backflow. The
web searches found no paper computing divisibility, backflow, or a process tensor
for PW clock-conditioned dynamics with a constraint.

## Answer to task item 1 (HSC Sec. 5)

- **Clock.** Not finite and not periodic. The clock is the bandlimited `L²[−E,E]`
  clock (Sec. 2, footnote 2) restricted to an orthonormal sampling basis
  `{|t_k⟩ | t_k = kπ/E, k ∈ ℤ}` (Eq. 115). Quote:
  > "Note that making time discrete is crucial here, as then such an orthonormal basis exits for clocks with bounded energy spectrum."
- **Interaction.** It is not a Hamiltonian term. The constraint is invariance under
  one clock step, `Û|Ψ⟩=|Ψ⟩` with `Û = Σ_k |t_{k+1}⟩⟨t_k|⊗Û^{(k)}_{SA}` (Eqs. 24–25,
  116–119). The step unitaries are reading-controlled and k-dependent. HSC say:
  > "Note, however, that this model is not obtained by directly discretising the continuous-time model of section section 4.2."
- **What is proved** (App. G, Eqs. 120–122). Solutions are
  `Σ_k|t_k⟩⊗|ψ(t_k)⟩` with `|ψ(t_{k+1})⟩ = Π_{j=0}^{k} Û^{(j)}|ψ(t_0)⟩`:
  > "We then see that, in this model, the evolution of the system relative to the clock is unitary as long as we consider time steps of size $\frac{\pi}{E}$ ."

  The conclusion is hedged:
  > "our analysis provides evidence that the underlying time … has to be discrete. Note, however, that this model does not exclude the possibility that discrete time evolution could be non-unitary."
- **Does Sec. 5 cover our case?** No. It covers neither a finite periodic clock nor
  an eigenvalue constraint `(H_C+H_S+H_int)|Ψ⟩ = λ|Ψ⟩` with `[H_int,H_C] ≠ 0`. The
  finite cyclic analogue of HSC Sec. 5 is Boette–Rossignoli Sec. II, which predates
  it. HSC cite Mondragon–Perez–Rovelli [19] as "A similar model"; that paper was
  not read. HSC treat periodic clocks only in the continuous PM setting (App. E.3),
  and there only a consistency condition (Eq. 107) is derived.
