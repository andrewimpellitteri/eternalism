# Individual cylinder magnitudes determine total variation

## Claim

Let X be the space of infinite growth histories with its cylinder sigma
algebra, and let G be the algebra generated by finite-history cylinders. Let
nu_1 and nu_2 be finite complex measures on sigma(G). Suppose

    |nu_1(C)| = |nu_2(C)|

for every individual finite cylinder C (including X). Then their positive
total-variation measures agree on the whole sigma algebra:

    |nu_1| = |nu_2| on sigma(G).

This uses labelwise equality for every cylinder, finite total variation, and
the fact that the cylinder algebra generates the measurable space. Matching
only levelwise multisets or selected cylinder magnitudes would not suffice.

## Refined-partition proof

Write C_n for the finite partition into all depth-n cylinders. For a cylinder
C of depth k and m >= k, let C_m(C) be the depth-m cylinders contained in C,
and set

    V_m^nu(C) = sum_(D in C_m(C)) |nu(D)|.

Refinement and the triangle inequality make V_m^nu(C) nondecreasing in m.
Every C_m(C) is a measurable partition of C, so

    V_m^nu(C) <= |nu|(C).

In fact,

    |nu|(C) = sup_(m >= k) V_m^nu(C).                 (1)

To prove the reverse inequality, take any finite measurable partition
E_1,...,E_r of C. The finite positive measure lambda=|nu| allows each E_i to
be approximated in lambda symmetric-difference measure by a set B_i in G.
This approximation property follows because G generates sigma(G): the class
of sets approximable in lambda measure contains G and is closed under
complements and countable unions (truncate a countable union using finiteness
of lambda, then approximate the finite union).

Intersect the B_i with C, disjointize them in order, and use the remainder in
C as the final part. This gives an algebra partition D_1,...,D_r of C with
sum_i lambda(E_i symmetric-difference D_i) arbitrarily small. Each D_i is a
finite union of cylinders and, after refining to one common depth m, the
partition C_m(C) refines every D_i. The triangle inequality yields

    sum_i |nu(D_i)| <= V_m^nu(C).

Also |nu(E_i)-nu(D_i)| <= lambda(E_i symmetric-difference D_i). Taking
arbitrarily accurate approximations shows

    sum_i |nu(E_i)| <= sup_(m >= k) V_m^nu(C).

Taking the supremum over all finite measurable partitions of C proves (1),
by the partition definition of total variation.

Now assume the cylinder magnitudes of nu_1 and nu_2 match. For every cylinder
C and every m >= depth(C), the finite sums V_m^nu1(C) and V_m^nu2(C) match
term by term. Equation (1) gives |nu_1|(C)=|nu_2|(C). Cylinders, together
with the empty set, form a pi-system generating sigma(G); the two finite
positive measures therefore agree on sigma(G) by uniqueness of measures.

## Consequence for variation type

The complete positive variation measure, not just its total mass, is fixed by
the individual cylinder-magnitude data. Thus all properties determined by
|nu| are shared: its point masses, its measure-theoretic atoms, whether it is
purely atomic, and whether it is atomless. In particular, changing only
cylinder phases or choosing a different extension cannot turn the old
matched-cylinder data into non-atomic variation.

The matched-norms construction in `../bc-matched-norms/RESULT.md` has finite
atomic extensions supported on a countable set of eventually-timid histories.
Its variation measure is consequently purely atomic. Any finite complex
measure with exactly those individual cylinder magnitudes has that same
variation measure and is also supported, in variation, on the same countable
atomic set. A non-atomic continuation therefore needs new individual-cylinder
magnitude data; a new phase assignment or completion of the old data cannot
achieve it.

Terminology qualification: “no point atoms” means |nu|({x})=0 for each
individual history x. “Atomless” means there is no measurable A of positive
variation such that every measurable subset of A has either zero variation
or all of |nu|(A). On a standard Borel path space, every atom of a finite
positive measure contains a point atom, so these two conditions agree. The
infinite-history space here is a closed subset of a countable product of
finite discrete birth-coordinate spaces, hence standard Borel with its
cylinder sigma algebra. On a completely abstract measurable space, absence
of point atoms alone need not imply atomlessness; the equality theorem still
transfers the full measure-theoretic atom structure.

This result is a data-boundary statement only. It does not construct a new
non-atomic BC/MSR model, establish physical meaning, or imply any claim about
instruments or temporal ontology.

## Relation to the diffuse continuation

The completed construction in `RESULT.md` specifies a common later radius
R_n=1000^n, hence |q(C)|=1000^(-n) for parents of size n>=3. This differs
from the recursively chosen R_n in the matched-norms construction. In
particular, the all-gregarious antichain cylinder includes the product of
these q magnitudes and has a different magnitude. The construction therefore
uses new individual-cylinder magnitude data, as required to avoid the
impossibility of obtaining non-atomic variation by reusing the previous
data. The completed proof establishes an L1(P0) extension with atomless
variation; this paragraph records the data-boundary relation, not a separate
construction argument.
