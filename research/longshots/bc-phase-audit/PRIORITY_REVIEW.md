# Priority and defeat review: scalar norm-GC phase model

Checked 2026-10-22. This is a bounded priority audit, not a systematic review. Primary-source versions and precise locators are in `prior-sources.jsonl`.

## Verdict

**Classification: known in substance / immediate adaptation, within the stated restrictions.** The gregarious factorization, MSR reconstruction, and product over intrinsic element-pasts mirror the Rideout–Sorkin (RS) derivation. Their classical Lemma 2 obtains a cardinality-only gregarious transition from general covariance and Bell causality; their Section 4.4 proves path independence by factoring the product into stage factors and past-poset factors. Replacing equality of complex amplitudes by equality of their moduli makes the same architecture work. The phase-valued proof is not literally in RS: its assumptions and conclusion differ, and the scalar norm-GC hypothesis must be stated with care. But the max-element deletion argument in the sidecar is a short modulus adaptation of RS’s replacement argument, not a compelling independent classification theorem.

**Explicit model: potentially a narrow worked example, not a new general completion theorem.** The nearest contrary evidence is unusually direct: Srivastava–Surya (SS), Sec. 3.1 discussion and Sec. 4, explicitly proposes imposing GC only on the norm and inserting path-dependent phases, including phase-modified QBC, then says the resulting dynamics becomes complex and leaves it for future exploration. The sidecar supplies a tractable scalar realization of that option under a sharply limited ratio-BC rule. Surya–Zalel (SZ) already proves the relevant bounded-variation criterion and constructs non-real extendible complex sequential-growth families. Thus the sidecar’s use of summable variation and an L1 limit is an application of known machinery. A narrowly defensible delta could be the explicit shape-dependent, nonzero scalar seed plus its percolation-reference tail completion, if it is not already present in the cited papers or their cited primary sources. This audit does not establish global priority.

**No physical or ontological conclusion.** Equality of cylinder norms is weaker than equality of complex amplitudes, the decoherence functional, or quantum measures on all events. It does not establish a gauge symmetry or operational equivalence. A scalar complex measure, its rank-one decoherence functional, its diagonal quantum measure, and laboratory probabilities are distinct objects. No instrument is specified, so no detector probability or measurement claim follows.

## Strongest defeat case

The proposed “classification” may be a repackaging of the known classical one-parameter-per-stage argument in scalar notation. Under nonzero, locally isomorphism-invariant scalar transitions, Bell pairing with the gregarious transition determines each transition from a precursor weight; MSR determines the remaining timid transition. The all-stage norm condition then constrains only the modulus of the gregarious factor. The sidecar’s maximal-element replacement changes the classical equalities to equalities of absolute values but does not introduce a new mechanism. Do not market it as a classification of general QSG, operator QBC, zero-transition models, or label-dependent rules.

The novelty threat to the *example* is SS’s explicit discussion of exactly the norm-phase freedom and SZ’s established phase-bearing extendible families. Their statements do not prove the exact proposed example was already published, but they leave a narrow gap between a named future-work option and known extension techniques. Searching a few targeted queries cannot prove absence; no absence claim is made here.

## Scope and assumptions that must stay visible

- The theorem is restricted to nonzero scalar transitions, local isomorphism invariance of parent-plus-distinguished-precursor data, MSR, scalar ratio BC, and norm-only cylinder covariance. It does not cover zero amplitudes, label-dependent rules, phase-modified BC in general, or operator-valued QBC.
- “Norm covariance” must say whether it compares path amplitudes for natural labelings of the same finite causet at a common stage, and must state the nonzero common-prefix premise used in the necessity argument. It is not full vector GC.
- The late-tail construction’s use of a real-percolation reference and L1 convergence can establish a finite countably additive complex measure if its displayed estimates and identification of cylinders are correct. That extension does not upgrade norm covariance to full GC or make the diagonal quantum measure countably additive.
- The proposed completed event E = “exactly two minimal elements” is intrinsic to the unlabelled completed event. The coordinator owns its finite enumeration and tail bound, which this review does not reproduce. Comparing u=(3+4i)/5 with u=1 changes q for the two-element antichain and therefore changes later finite magnitudes too; it is not the same cylinder data under a pure gauge transformation. A difference in scalar measure or rank-one quantum measure is not an instrument-level probability without a specified measurement model.
- Keep mathematical assumptions, measure-theoretic conclusion, physical interpretation, and philosophical bridge premises separate. Nothing here bears on temporal ontology, objective passage, determinism, or a physical arrow of time.
