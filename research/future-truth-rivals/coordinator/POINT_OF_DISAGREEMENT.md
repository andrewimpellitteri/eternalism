# The strongest rival permits future-directed facts now

27 October 2026. Preliminary philosophical assessment after reading the
accessible author reply. Not an adjudication of the whole debate.

## What the new source check changes

The challenge that future truth either lacks current grounds or smuggles
the future into present reality is already developed by Dorato and Hoefer
in *Nothing to Come in a Relativistic Setting* (2021 issue,
DOI10.2478/disp-2021-0025), author manuscript pp.2–4. Our generic dilemma
is not new. Their critique must be read together with its reply.

Correia and Rosenkranz's *Replies to Critics* (2021 issue, released 2023),
DOI10.2478/disp-2021-0026, §8 pp.482–484, denies that later truth-grounding
is self-grounding. They distinguish a present fact that a future claim is
true from a future fact/event explaining it, and allow derivative present
facts to depend on what will exist. The future event need not be present
merely because the current truth/fact is. Those are the authors' replies,
not conclusions we have independently established.

## Our counterexample tests a narrower base

Two solutions agreeing on an entire classical causal past do not thereby
agree on every present fact admitted by this rival. If one future outcome
occurs and the other does not, the rival can say that the corresponding
future-directed truth facts already differ, although their later
explanatory grounds are absent from the present event domain.

That is why our sufficiency test remains valid but does less than a
refutation. It shows that intrinsic field data in the past do not fix the
future outcome. The rival need not claim that they do. Counting the
future-directed truth itself as an additional present fact passes an
extensional discrimination test trivially; the remaining question is
whether it gives an independently motivated account of explanation and
existence.

Nor does factivity alone settle the issue. Grounding may be factive where
the grounding operator is evaluated later. To infer a current concrete
future event, one would additionally need a principle making the later
explanans currently exist. That is what the temporally distributed
proposal resists. Whether grounding should allow this is a substantive
metaphysical disagreement, not an error of elementary logic.

## The exact premise now at issue

Consider the principle:

> All truths from a standpoint supervene on the intrinsic constitution
> of its existing concrete event realm, together with fixed laws.

This principle is stronger than merely denying current existence to
future concrete events. It also excludes additional future-directed
facts not fixed by that constitution. A theory can deny the principle
while retaining a past-and-present concrete event ontology. Our field
models put pressure on this principle; they do not make it mandatory.

The next argument must defend the proposed principle, or find a
different consequence of rejecting it. Simply repeating that future
events do not exist would not engage the rival.

There is a further bridge even in testing that principle: identical
modeled metric and field data must exhaust the intrinsic constitution
of the proposed existing event realm. The scalar models alone do not
establish this exhaustiveness. Nor does variation within a mathematical
solution class by itself establish metaphysical possibility under every
candidate ontology. Without both bridges, the unconditional conclusion
remains only that the stipulated field-past map fails to fix the outcome.

## What a fair comparison must require

**From the bivalent later-grounded view:** specify which present facts
exist, whether and how they differ from concrete events, which grounds
are later, and why later grounding is explanatory rather than a verbal
restatement of eventual truth. If facts have future events as literal
constituents, state whether constituent existence is required now; do
not leave that bridge implicit.

**From the gappy view:** explain why current truth should coincide with
settlement, how successful predictions are evaluated retrospectively,
and how an appropriate grounding base differs from the mere definition
of truth as invariance over chosen completions.

**From an eternalist:** do not infer that the rival already accepts
future events merely because it admits truths about them. Explain why
an ontology with later grounds is inferior to one with tenselessly
existing grounds, beyond the demand for present grounds that the rival
has explicitly rejected.

Both sides owe explanations. Mathematical models help isolate their
commitments but do not adjudicate this dispute without a defended bridge.
