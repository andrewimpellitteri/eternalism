# Two interpretations tested against the same future field claim

27 October 2026. Candidate contracts for comparison, not new temporal
ontologies and not reconstructions of Correia–Rosenkranz's full theory.
The source gate remains open. All conclusions below are conditional on
the explicitly stated semantics and physical model.

## Hold the physical comparison fixed

Use the reviewed fixed closed-dust spacetime M, all smooth real solutions
Ω of the same linear massive scalar equation, and a standpoint b. Fix a
target x in the timelike future of b and a rational threshold q. The
proposition under comparison is

    Q: the field value at x exceeds q.

The spacetime point x is a common mathematical label across solutions.
That is not an existential quantifier over currently real concrete
events. A complete philosophical theory still owes the reference clause
that legitimizes this use; the two constructions below share that debt.

For s∈Ω define C_b(s) as all solutions agreeing with s on I^−(b). Fix an
actual realization s* for comparing outcomes and records. Calling this
complete mathematical realization “actual” in the metamodel is not a
postulate that every event it represents currently exists. Nor is its
designation derived from the restriction to b's past.

Both contracts provisionally interpret concrete event existence from b
as E_b=J^−(b). This is an explicit shared ontological proposal. Neither the
PDE nor the fixed-M metamodel establishes it. The comparison therefore
asks whether two truth policies can coexist with that proposal, not
whether the proposal is correct.

## Contract L: bivalent truth with possibly later grounds

Q is true from b iff φ_s*(x)>q, and false otherwise. An opposite-outcome
solution sharing the same current past need not have the same current
truth value. Therefore the truth assignment need not factor through the
currently existing classical past alone.

Instead of demanding a current truthmaker, allow a grounding witness at
a standpoint c at or after x, when x belongs to the proposed E_c. For this
restricted one-point language the candidate witness is the field-value
fact at x; a separate bridge connects that fact to the truth of the
earlier claim about it. The temporal qualification is important: it is
not a claim that the candidate witness exists from b already.

This proposes two extra principles, which must be defended:

1. Bivalent outcome-matching truth can hold at b without complete grounds
   existing from b.
2. A fact existing later can ground the truth of an earlier future-tense
   claim. Truth conditions alone do not establish this grounding relation.

This is a logical rival to our earlier *current-ground completeness*
premise. The visible Correia–Rosenkranz chapter7 abstract and note3 offer
a related temporalized demand, but these simplified clauses are ours.
They do not reproduce the book's quantified tense logic or its full
account of future contingency and ground.

The later *Replies to Critics*, §8 (printed pp.482–484), has now been read
in an accessible copy of the authors' text. It makes a further distinction:
they can allow a present derivative fact that Q is true while denying
that the future explanans-event currently exists. Our current-base test
must distinguish complete reality, current fundamental grounds and
intrinsic classical past data. Equal field restrictions do not prove all
such facts equal. See POINT_OF_DISAGREEMENT.md and the source register.

E_b above concerns concrete EVENTS. It is not a completed unrestricted
quantifier domain for all objects, propositions and facts. Allowing
present future-directed facts may require an additional sort and
existence clauses. Whether facts require their putative future
constituents to exist is a further premise, not one assumed here.

Nothing here proves a physical selection mechanism. Nor is one assumed
necessary merely to designate the actual realization in a semantics.
Whether all truth must supervene on what exists from b is the disputed
principle, not a rule we may impose without argument.

## Contract S: current truth identified with settlement

Q is true from b iff every s∈C_b(s*) satisfies φ_s(x)>q. It is false iff
every such solution satisfies φ_s(x)≤q, and otherwise lacks a current
truth value. The class is nonempty because it contains s*.

The proposed current base is the existing past data plus the stipulated
laws and global admissibility conditions. This base extensionally
determines the displayed three-way verdict. Turning that sufficiency
into a grounding explanation requires a separate account of laws and
modal admissibility, just as in the previous round.

On this policy an utterance about the future need not be true now just
because its content will be borne out. Logical connectives must be
evaluated in each solution before supervaluation: excluded middle is
true even when neither Q nor its negation has a settled value.

An actual realization s* can still represent the outcome of the process.
It need not be treated as an outcome already selected by current grounds.
The representation alone supplies no fundamental passage or branch
creation; those require interpretation beyond these clauses.

## The same mathematical example gives different present-truth verdicts

Before maximum expansion, at every target outside J^−(b), the reviewed
positive-mass result plus linear scaling gives the full real line of
attainable field values in C_b(s*). Hence our threshold Q is unsettled.

| Test | Contract L | Contract S |
|---|---|---|
| Actual value at x exceeds q | Q already true from b | Q presently gappy while its compatibility class straddles q |
| Actual value at x is at most q | Q already false from b | Same gap while the class straddles q |
| At/after τ_b=π, where the entire solution is fixed by the past | Bivalent actual value | Same bivalent value, because C_b(s*) is a singleton |
| Candidate x outside proposed E_b | Allowed as an abstractly referenced future event | Also allowed; a settled future truth need not have an existing future event as current witness |

The table compares truth policies, not empirical predictions. Give the
same realized solution, detector models and access assumptions to both,
and the modeled field records are the same. If probability or assertion
norms are used to separate them, those must be independently specified;
none follows merely from having the word “true” in a different clause.

The disagreement is substantive at the semantic level: for the same
content Q and the same current compatibility class, one policy assigns a
Boolean value and the other does not. It is not resolved by a translation
that silently replaces truth with settled truth. Conversely a semantic
disagreement does not alone yield a new experimental discrimination.

## Three tests that prevent easy victories

### Do not smuggle complete grounds into “same past”

The travelling-wave example in the previous round has identical entire
local pasts and different later outcomes under deterministic Cauchy
evolution. Its complete Cauchy data differ. Any account using complete
initial data, lawmakers or extra tensed facts must say whether those
facts exist from b and whether the compared models agree on them.

L can reject current-ground completeness rather than add a hidden seed.
S can identify present truth with settlement rather than claim that the
equations are globally indeterministic. Both responses remain live.

### A representative is not the actual continuation

A mathematical procedure may choose one element of C_b without choosing
the actual s*. If it gives the same selected representative for two
models with the same past but opposite Q outcomes, it cannot match both
outcomes. Covariance or a preferred simplicity convention does not fix
this mismatch. A selector's existence and a true actual-future claim are
different questions. The disjoint selection lane makes this precise.

### Retrospective truth needs two indices

“The prediction was true when made” and “the prediction's content has now
been borne out” are different assertions under S. Re-evaluating the
earlier content using a later, smaller compatibility class is different
from reporting its earlier verdict with the earlier class held fixed.
The retrospective semantics must specify utterance and assessment
parameters rather than change them silently. With utterance u=b held
fixed, its earlier verdict uses (u=b,a=b); a later assessment uses
(u=b,a=c). The latter does not make the former verdict bivalent.

The smaller-class comparison is a structural analogy only. We have not
identified C_c(s*) with MacFarlane's histories through both utterance and
assessment moments, or supplied his full tense language and assertion
account. Contract S alone is not his two-context theory. See the primary
clause audit and REVIEW_PAIRED.md in the retrospection lane.

## What this comparison does and does not establish

It shows how the current-ground obstruction can be avoided in two
different ways without treating future concrete existence as already
proved: permit later grounds for bivalent truths, or allow current gaps
for unsettled contents. It also exposes shared costs: future reference,
the interpretation of admissibility, actual realization and genuine
grounding/passage remain to be supplied.

The decisive next philosophical question is whether truth must be fully
answerable to what exists *from the standpoint at which it is true*.
If that requirement is accepted, L needs a different base or fails the
criterion. If it is rejected, our sufficiency calculation does not
refute L. A successful defense needs reasons for the requirement beyond
restating a favored ontology.

The full original bivalent rival must be read before assigning it to
either simplified contract or claiming that these exhaust its options.
