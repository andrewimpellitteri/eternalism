# Red-team review of the paired contracts

27 October 2026. Review scope: `research/future-truth-rivals/coordinator/PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md`, especially Contract L, Contract S, the comparison's “same physical base” framing, and the representative/actual distinction. This is a conditional logical review, not a reconstruction of Correia–Rosenkranz. Their source gate remains open.

## Contract L: later grounding does not imply a present ground

Contract L is coherent as a proposal if it keeps three claims separate:

1. At b, Q receives a bivalent, outcome-matching truth value using the designated realization s*.
2. A later fact at c, at or after x, may ground the earlier truth of Q.
3. No complete ground for Q is required to exist at b.

There is no derivation from bivalence, truth-condition clauses, ordinary grounding necessitation, or transitivity alone to a ground that exists at b. A small consistency witness makes the point: take standpoints b<c, let x enter the stipulated event domain only at c, and let Q at b be true exactly when the realized field value at x exceeds q. Let the obtaining field-value fact at c ground Q-at-b. Grounding necessitation is satisfied; transitivity is satisfied (there need be no intermediate grounding links); and no ground for Q is in the domain E_b. This is only a model of the stated abstract clauses, not a theory of physical grounding.

A present-ground conclusion follows if one adds a premise such as “every truth at b has a complete ground among facts existing at b,” “all grounds of a b-indexed truth must exist no later than b,” or an equivalent current-ground completeness axiom. Those are substantive temporal principles. They are not consequences of transitivity. If a grounding theory requires every chain to terminate in an at-b ground, that termination condition likewise supplies the missing premise. Conversely, a later-ground account must defend the intelligibility and direction of later facts grounding earlier truth; this consistency check does not establish that such grounding is metaphysically acceptable.

The contract should keep temporal indices visible, e.g. `G_c grounds T_b(Q)`, and distinguish this from the fact that Q is true at b, from Q's truth-maker existing at b, and from the future event already existing at b. The field-value fact at x is a candidate witness only after a bridge principle connects that fact to the earlier truth. No grounding relation follows just from the shared content of the claim and the later fact.

## Contract S and “the same physical base”

The paired setup holds fixed M, the equation, the stipulated admissible solution class, the standpoint and target, and the metamodel realization s*. That supports a controlled comparison of verdicts over the same mathematical case. It does not by itself show that L and S use precisely the same *current grounds*.

L's truth clause consults s*'s actual outcome, while its own text says that the designation of s* is not derived from b's past. Treat that designation as a semantic/metamodel parameter or primitive actuality assignment, not silently as a current physical fact or ground. This lets L assign bivalent truth without claiming that the complete represented future currently exists. It also means the comparison must not describe s* as information in the current physical base.

S evaluates the whole compatibility class C_b(s*) under the fixed laws and admissibility policy. Its three-way verdict is extensionally determined by those inputs. That is a modal-semantic sufficiency claim. It becomes a claim about a current grounding base only if the account independently explains why the relevant laws and the complete admissibility/modal structure are among the grounds available from b. “Global admissibility conditions” must not covertly include the selected actual continuation; if they do, S would acquire an outcome selector and the advertised contrast would change. The coordinator's fixed all-smooth solution class and same-past fibre are a precise mathematical test case, but do not settle what a theory's physical current base contains.

Accordingly, “same physical base” is safe if it means the shared stipulated spacetime, equation, past restriction, law/admissibility policy, and realized model used to compare outcomes. It is too strong if it means L and S have already been shown to share identical ontological grounds at b. The latter is exactly part of the philosophical comparison, not a result of the common metamodel.

## Representatives and actuality

The selection result correctly separates a section ρ of the past-data map from the actual realization s*. A section returns one compatible solution by convention. On a non-singleton fibre it cannot return every member, so it cannot match all possible actual outcomes. Choosing ρ(R(s*))=s* for one designated s* requires that designation as an additional input or fact; it is not obtained from R alone.

This distinction also sharpens the paired text's claim that no physical selection mechanism is required merely to designate s* in a semantics. That is valid as a semantic stipulation. It does not answer what, in the target ontology, makes one realization actual, whether actualness is a primitive, or whether any such fact exists from b. Contract L can leave that question open, but cannot present the semantic designation itself as a physical explanation.

The symmetry discussion in the selection result is mathematical and must stay that way. Sign reversal fixes zero; translations act freely on the zero-past fibre. Thus no selector equivariant under the full translation action exists, while a non-equivariant representative can be chosen. This says nothing by itself about physical gauge, redundancy, or a symmetry of the full theory. Those require a separately justified physical action and an account of which observables and structures it preserves.

## Conditional verdict and limits

The pair has a genuine semantic contrast on the stated case: L is bivalent by the designated realization; S is gappy where its same-past class straddles the threshold. Neither verdict proves future concrete existence, passage, or a physical branch-selection process. L can avoid current-ground completeness by explicitly allowing later grounding or by leaving truth ungrounded at b; S can avoid actual-outcome matching by identifying current truth with settlement. Both carry extra semantic or metaphysical commitments.

No transitive logical principle inspected here forces an at-b future ground without a further current-ground or temporal-order premise. The robustness and plausibility of L's later-ground bridge remain open. S's modal sufficiency does not by itself establish grounding by currently existing facts. The “same base” claim should be limited to the shared model inputs until those bridge questions are resolved.

No Correia–Rosenkranz primary clauses were checked in this review. In particular, no claim is made that their full account adopts Contract L, permits later grounding in this exact sense, or faces this review's toy model. The paired file itself records the open source gate; that limit remains in force.
