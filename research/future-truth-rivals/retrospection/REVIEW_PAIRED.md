# Independent review of the paired contracts: retrospective section

Checked 27 October 2026. Scope: `research/future-truth-rivals/coordinator/PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md`, especially Contracts L/S and “Retrospective truth needs two indices.” This is a semantic audit, not a reconstruction of Correia–Rosenkranz or a physical-model audit.

## Findings

**L/S are distinct policies, and neither is MacFarlane's full account.** Contract L assigns a Boolean value by matching the designated complete solution `s*` (paired-contract lines 22–26, 34–39). Contract S assigns the current verdict by supervaluating the target across `C_b(s*)` (lines 66–81). S is therefore a current-assessment supervaluation/settlement clause for this fixed target. It is not MacFarlane's complete two-context semantics, which layers a postsemantic evaluation at utterance context `u` and assessment context `a` over truth-at-points, with an account of assertion. At `u=a=b`, MacFarlane's sea-battle example does yield a supervaluational gap; at a later `a`, his double-time-reference clause restricts the relevant histories through both `u` and `a`, yielding true or false in his example. That overlap at `u=a` does not identify the theories.

**The retrospective paragraph has the right distinction but needs the indices made explicit.** Under Contract S, the verdict for `Q` from `b` is computed using `C_b`. If that class straddles the threshold, its S-verdict at `b` remains gappy. A later assessment can ask a different question: re-evaluate the same earlier utterance/content with utterance index `u=b` and assessment index `a=c`. In MacFarlane's toy this can be true or false relative to the later assessor. It does not make the earlier `(u=b,a=b)` verdict bivalent. A report such as “it was true when made” is ambiguous between reporting that earlier verdict and saying, from `c`, that the prediction has been borne out. Under S the former must preserve the earlier gap; the latter needs an explicit later-assessment/report clause. The current sentence at lines 138–144 gestures at this distinction correctly, but should not treat the two reports as interchangeable or describe Contract S itself as MacFarlane's account.

The phrase “later, smaller compatibility class” at lines 139–141 is only a **structural analogy** to MacFarlane's later-assessment restriction. The paired contract has not established a map from the PDE model's `C_c(s*)` to the branching-time histories through both `u` and `a`; nor is the named-point proposition `Q` identical without further semantic clauses to MacFarlane's future-tense utterance. State that limitation wherever the analogy is used.

**The complete realization is explicitly mathematical, not an ontic commitment.** Lines 22–26 say `s*` is a complete mathematical realization used to compare outcomes and records, and explicitly deny that designating it entails that every represented event currently exists or that the designation follows from the past restriction. Lines 83–86 likewise deny that representing `s*` selects an outcome by current grounds. This is an adequate guard. Keep “designated realization `s*` in the metamodel” prominent in the retrospective discussion; do not shorten it to “the actual future/history” in a way that suggests a thin-red-line fact or a completed-history ontology. Any later grounding witness still depends on the separately stated proposal that the relevant event/fact exists at `c`.

## Author's later self-assessment of the 2003 argument

Verified the note on MacFarlane's current author-maintained [Papers page](https://johnmacfarlane.net/papers.html), entry 30, attached to “Truth in the Garden of Forking Paths” (listed as a chapter in Max Kölbel and Manuel García-Carpintero, eds., *Relative Truth*, Oxford University Press, 2008, pp. 81–102). The note says the core ideas come from the 2003 paper, but MacFarlane now believes that paper's **argument** inadequate; the later paper explains why and develops a more robust argument for the same conclusion.

The exact author-hosted source linked as the preprint is [Truth in the Garden of Forking Paths](https://johnmacfarlane.net/garden.pdf), a 26-page PDF dated 28 January 2007, prepared from a paper first presented in 2005. In §1, printed pp. 1–2, MacFarlane says he hopes to correct shortcomings in the 2003 paper and specifies them: its treatment of temporal modifiers as sentential operators; its focus on utterances/sentences rather than propositions and propositional truth (including a possible supervaluationist response to retrospective assessment); and its omission of an object-language truth predicate and an actuality operator. This pins the self-assessment to an identifiable author version and locators.

This is **MacFarlane's self-assessment of the 2003 argument and its formulation, not a refutation** of the 2003 paper's clauses, the paired contracts, or assessment sensitivity. The 2007/2008 chapter is itself a stronger, revised source that should be read before treating the 2003 paper as MacFarlane's final view. No broad survey was conducted here.
