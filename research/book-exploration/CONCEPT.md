# What Physics Leaves Open

## Status and priority

Exploratory book concept, 25 October 2026. Working title, not a publication
announcement. The papers remain the active priority. This folder parks a bounded
idea and two prose samples; it does not launch a book-writing program, commission
new research, or certify the papers' results or novelty.

## The idea

A philosophically serious book for an interested reader without specialist
training, moving from ordinary questions about time to carefully delimited
results about relativistic becoming and prediction. The organizing question is:
**What does physics establish about time, and what further assumptions turn
that into an account of reality?**

The reader should finish able to reconstruct an argument, identify its bridge
premises, and disagree intelligently. Understanding the problem is the promise;
agreement with local fragmentalism is not. The book should remain worthwhile
even if one of this project's proposed results is revised or turns out to have
an earlier predecessor.

Its distinctive opportunity is to make the passage from physical constraint to
philosophical conclusion visible. A light-cone diagram does not label events
“real.” A deterministic equation does not settle whether the future exists.
A geometric guarantee of determination does not necessarily exhaust everything
a particular dynamics fixes. These distinctions can become a sustained narrative
rather than a preliminary glossary readers are expected to remember unaided.

## Reader, form and voice

An intellectually ambitious general reader willing to follow an argument but
unfamiliar with modal logic or differential geometry. Assume patience rather
than background. Use ordinary cases, thought experiments, diagrams and extended
objections. Introduce a technical term only when a problem makes it useful.

The main narrative explains why a result holds and what would defeat its
interpretation. Appendices carry exact definitions, full proofs and source
locators. Metaphors must have stated limits: a completed novel can illustrate
the difference between existence and determination, but cannot establish the
metaphysics of an experienced life. No fictional conversations presented as
historical evidence; no claim of expert consensus from agent agreement.

## Provisional architecture

1. **Tomorrow's appointment.** Existence, inevitability, prediction and passage
   become separate questions through a single ordinary example.
2. **What belongs to reality?** Presentism, eternalism and growing blocks;
   the strongest motivation and difficulty for each, including truth about
   the past and knowledge of being at a growing edge.
3. **What would passage add?** Tense, change, McTaggart and the distinction
   between self-location and objective presentness.
4. **A world without one agreed now.** Relativity introduced through clocks,
   signals and simultaneity; constraints distinguished from ontological verdicts.
5. **Could becoming be local?** Stein's question, causal order, the limits of
   symmetry, and the proposed cross-spacetime locality hypothesis. Explain why
   this hypothesis is substantive and what happens when it is removed.
6. **What can the past fix?** Geometric guarantees, actual field determination,
   horizons and the closed-universe example. This chapter must incorporate the
   necessary/sufficient correction from the horizons review.
7. **What remains open?** Local fragmentalism as a candidate interpretation
   alongside its rivals, with outstanding questions about grounding, tense,
   physical restrictions and evidence.

The samples supplied now are chapter 1 and a trial chapter 6. They are a test
of the connection between foundations and results, not finished chapters of
a completed manuscript.

## Research and editorial contract

Every eventual chapter must distinguish established prior results, the current
papers' conditional claims, and our own reconstructions of philosophical
arguments. Give each rival a serious objection to the preferred conclusion and
a plausible reply. Do not imply that a classification of admissible relations
proves those relations represent objective becoming.

Existing models for structure are Barry Dainton's *Time and Space*, second
edition, and Richard Arthur's *The Reality of Time Flow*. Publisher descriptions
and contents were checked; neither whole book was read for this proposal.
Callender provides a useful contrasting research program, rather than a view
to dismiss on the way to our conclusion. Full comparative reading is deferred.

## When to return to it

Return after the active papers have stable statements, source audits and revised
circulation assessments. Then ask a reader unfamiliar with the project to
explain the two sample chapters back to us. Can they distinguish what exists,
what is fixed, and what can be known? Can they state the strongest objection?
If not, revise the explanation before expanding the book.

The next book step, when authorized, is an editorial test of these samples and
a source-backed argument map, not simultaneous drafting of all seven chapters.
