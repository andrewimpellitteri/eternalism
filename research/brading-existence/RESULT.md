# Local presents and unrestricted existence: the Brading coverage problem

Sole-worker analysis, 2026-10-21. This is the core argument for the reframed
article (`paper/REFRAME.md`). It is a philosophical argument, not a new physical
result.

## Verdict

Brading's system-relative present does **not** establish a restriction on
unrestricted concrete existence. Her own reformulation,

> (P1*) For each and every thing, that thing exists only presently, where it is
> something to be determined empirically (Brading 2013, p. 1110),

is not equivalent to the original presentist premise,

> (P1) All and only things that exist now are real (p. 1101).

P1* is best read as a **system-relative existence claim**: each thing exists in
the present of the system to which it belongs. That is compatible with every
event in the world history belonging to some system-present. The union of local
presents can cover the whole history, so no event is excluded from existence.
Brading's own gloss makes this explicit: "The ground of what is real is the
dynamics, and we belong to the same world as whatever we interact with, and the
rest of that world is as real as we are" (p. 1110). That is not a global
restriction on what exists; it is a relational account of existence within a
world that is all real.

The limited conclusion is therefore: **dynamical individuation alone is
insufficient for the intended existential restriction.** A presentist reading of
Brading's local presents needs an additional actuality/privilege premise, or a
revision of what "presentism" claims. The coverage objection is a burden-shift,
not a refutation of Brading and not a proof of eternalism.

## 1. The target: P1, P2 and the move to law-constitutive unity

Brading begins with the familiar tension between:

- (P1) All and only things that exist now are real.
- (P2) Special relativity is a complete account of spatiotemporal structure.

She argues that the tension arises from a shared assumption: that spacetime is
the **ontological principle of unity** of what exists (pp. 1104-1105). If
spacetime plays that role, then a present that is not spatially extended leaves
only a point-sized real, and P1 collapses into "pluralistic extreme solipsism."
Her alternative is to let the **dynamical laws** play the constitutive role:
the laws constitute genuine unities, their persistence and their change
(pp. 1105-1108). Spacetime is demoted to an **epistemic** principle of unity, the
framework in which we organize knowledge of the not-here and not-now
(pp. 1108-1109).

The present is then:

> a spatiotemporal region of whatever size is necessary to sustain the dynamical
> system in question (p. 1109).

It may be much smaller than the block, or it may be the size of the block. If
the only genuine system is the entire universe, Brading says, "this version of
presentism is defeated and the block universe triumphs" (p. 1109). The empirical
question is whether there are genuine subsystems whose sustaining regions are
proper parts of the whole.

This is a substantive and elegant proposal. The question is what it claims
about **existence**, as opposed to unity and presence.

## 2. The existential contrast

Let `W` be the set of concrete events in the world history. For each genuine
dynamical system `i`, let `U_i` be its system-sustaining present. Brading's
local present is a region, not a global slice; it need not be transitive across
systems.

The original presentist claim (P1) is a **global restriction**:

> there is a fact about what exists simpliciter, and not every event in `W`
> satisfies it; the future (and perhaps the past) is not real.

Brading's P1* is different in form:

> for each thing, it exists only presently, where "presently" is fixed by the
> system to which it belongs.

P1* quantifies over things and assigns each a present. It does not say that
there is a global domain of existence smaller than `W`. It is satisfied if each
thing has a system-relative present, even if every event in `W` belongs to some
such present.

The contrast can be put without choosing a tensed or tenseless reading of
"exists":

- **Existence:** the unrestricted claim that an event is part of what there is.
- **Presence:** an event's belonging to a system's sustaining region.
- **Unity:** the law-constituted integration that makes a system one thing.
- **Endurance:** the persistence of that unity through change.

Brading's argument moves from unity to presence. The further move from presence
to a restriction on existence is the one at issue.

## 3. The coverage objection

Local boundedness says, for every system `i`:

```
U_i is a proper subregion of W.
```

It does not imply:

```
the union of all U_i is a proper subregion of W.
```

If every event belongs to at least one system-present, the family `{U_i}`
covers `W`. The account then divides the history into dynamical unities; it does
not exclude any event from existence. The local present can be "local, not
global" (p. 1110) while the union of local presents is the whole history.

Two clarifications:

- **Overlap does not help.** Overlapping presents make coverage easier, not
  harder. If two systems' presents overlap, their union is larger.
- **Disjointness does not help either.** A partition of `W` into disjoint proper
  parts still covers `W`. Local boundedness is compatible with a cover in both
  cases.

The objection is exact and does not assume that Brading's view is false. It
shows that a premise she needs — that the family of actual system-presents does
not cover the history, or that coverage does not count as existence — is not
supplied by the local-boundedness claim.

## 4. Brading's text already points to system-relative existence

The coverage objection is not an external imposition. Brading's own gloss
supports the system-relative reading.

First, P1* itself: "For each and every thing, that thing exists only presently,
where it is something to be determined empirically" (p. 1110). The quantifier is
over things; the present is assigned per thing/system. This is not the global
"all and only things that exist now are real" of P1.

Second, the interaction passage:

> The ground of what is real is the dynamics, and we belong to the same world as
> whatever we interact with, and the rest of that world is as real as we are.
> ... Does my son exist relative to me when he is in London and I am here? Of
> course. There are plenty of interactions going on that link us. Is there a
> determinate fact of the matter about what he is doing right now? No. (p. 1110)

This is a **relational** existence claim: the son exists relative to the
speaker because of interaction; there is no determinate global fact about his
present. That is precisely the "system-relative existence" repair (repair 1
below), not the original global restriction. The rest of the world is "as real
as we are" — no event is excluded merely because it lies outside a local
present.

Third, the empirical defeat condition: if the only genuine system is the whole
universe, the view is defeated and the block triumphs (p. 1109). This is a
conditional about which systems exist, not a global actuality principle.

The textual evidence therefore supports a reading on which Brading has already
moved to a relativized existence claim. Whether that view should still be called
"presentism" is partly verbal; she herself worries that "labeling it
'presentism' dooms the view" (p. 1101). The substantive point is that the
global restriction is not established.

## 5. Four repairs and their costs

If one wants the original global restriction, at least one repair is needed.

1. **System-relative existence.** Replace a single unrestricted existence
   predicate with "exists-for-system-i." This blocks the coverage objection by
   changing the question. Cost: there is no system-independent fact that future
   events do not exist; the view is a relativized ontology, not P1. Brading's
   interaction passage is naturally read this way.
2. **Privileged actual systems.** Only some systems' presents are actual. This
   restores a restriction, but the selection principle is not derived from the
   physics or from dynamical unity. Cost: an independent actuality premise is
   needed; without it the choice is arbitrary, and with it the view may
   reintroduce a privileged perspective.
3. **Restrictions on coexistence.** Not all local presents can coexist: a
   maximal consistent set, a single actual present, or a dynamics of
   present-selection. Cost: this risks reintroducing a global present or a
   privileged foliation, and it needs a rule the physics does not supply.
4. **Revised presentism.** Give up global exclusion and accept local or
   functional becoming. Cost: this is coherent, but it is a different thesis. It
   may be an eternalist history with a local becoming, not presentism as the
   restriction on existence.

The paper does not select among these. It argues that at least one is needed,
and that each has a cost not settled by the physics. That is the limited
verdict.

## 6. Objections and replies

**Objection: P1* is a restriction because each thing exists only in its own
present, not at all times.** Reply: that is a restriction on each thing's
temporal extent, not on the domain of existence. If every event belongs to some
thing's present, every event exists. The union point is the missing step.

**Objection: the dynamics grounds unity, so the present is not arbitrary; the
coverage problem is merely set-theoretic.** Reply: the set-theoretic point is
exactly the issue. Unity can be local and law-constituted while the union of
unities covers the history. The dynamics explains why each unity has the extent
it does; it does not explain why the union must be proper.

**Objection: Brading's "rest of the world is as real as we are" is a claim
about interaction, not about existence.** Reply: that is the system-relative
reading. If interaction fixes existence relative to a system, the global
restriction is gone. If it does not, the passage does not supply the missing
actuality premise.

**Objection: the view is defeated if the only system is the universe; that is a
genuine empirical vulnerability.** Reply: yes, but it is a vulnerability about
which systems exist, not a derivation of global exclusion. The coverage problem
arises even when genuine subsystems exist.

**Objection: this is a verbal dispute about the label "presentism."** Reply:
partly. The paper's conclusion is not "Brading is not a presentist"; it is that
the existential claim she needs does not follow from dynamical unity. The label
can be retained; the missing premise cannot.

## 7. What would change the verdict

- A proof that the family of actual system-presents cannot cover `W` (a
  no-coverage theorem) would restore the restriction without an actuality
  premise.
- A physically motivated principle selecting privileged actual systems would
  supply the missing premise, though its cost would still need defense.
- A revised P1* that explicitly restricts the domain of existence, rather than
  assigning each thing a present, would change the target.
- A demonstration that "exists relative to a system" is not a weaker existence
  claim but the same global claim under another notation would defeat the
  system-relative reading.

## 8. Scope and limits

- Read depth: Brading 2013 full text, public published-layout copy, pp. 1101-1111
  (OCR checked against the page images for the quoted passages); Brading 2015
  abstract/indexed text only, not full text. The 2015 follow-up is used only for
  the system-sustaining-region formulation, which is already in the 2013 text.
- The argument is philosophical. It does not claim that Brading's view is false,
  that eternalism is true, or that every presentism fails. It does not supply an
  empirical discriminator.
- The coverage objection is elementary set theory. The contribution is the
  focused reconstruction and the cost analysis, not a new mathematical theorem.
- The paper's non-claims are in `paper/REFRAME.md`. No global priority or
  novelty claim.
