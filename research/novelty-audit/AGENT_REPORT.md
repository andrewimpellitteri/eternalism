**Role:** independent skeptic; candidates were supplied by the theory-map lane.  
**Verdict:** **no selection**. None of RC-1–RC-5 currently satisfies the six-part claim-contract gate with a defensible novelty delta. This is a gate decision, not a claim that no publishable result exists anywhere in these subjects. Search misses below are not priority evidence.

I read the current main-checkout instructions and contracts, the five RCs and defeat-search materials, plus the current axiomatic-relativity ablation table. I independently checked primary texts and exact versions listed in `source-manifest.md`; the strongest direct checks were the argument sections of Malament, Besnard, Rideout–Sorkin, Brightwell et al., Höhn–Smith–Lock, Hosseini–Lock, Hausmann et al., Ludescher et al., and Milz et al. Those limits prevent a positive priority conclusion.

## Contract-by-contract decisions

### RC-1 — Interactions, isometric reductions, and autonomous dynamics

**1. Exact question/result.** The proposal asks for necessary and sufficient conditions on a constraint, clock POVM, interaction, and common reduction domain that make the induced relational evolution a well-defined isometry generated by one time-independent self-adjoint operator, or for a counterexample family. The mathematical target is intelligible, but its “natural class” and observable operational criterion are still underspecified.

**2. Three nearest predecessors, compared at assumptions/result level.**

* Smith–Ahmadi, arXiv:1712.00081v3, assumes a clock/system tensor split and a chosen physical inner product, permits an interaction term, and derives a time-nonlocal conditional Schrödinger equation with an integral-operator kernel; it treats example interactions. This occupies the generic claim that interactions can alter relational dynamics, but does not by itself supply the proposed reduction-map classification. Body-level paper record was already available in the clock defeat-search; this run checked the primary arXiv PDF metadata and abstract, not its derivation anew.
* Höhn–Smith–Lock, arXiv:1912.00033v3, proves the Dirac/Page–Wootters/relational-Heisenberg “trinity” through reduction maps and covariant clock POVMs, but explicitly conditions equivalence on a well-behaved clock that does not couple to the system (Introduction and Appendix A). It establishes the non-interacting baseline; it leaves interactions outside that theorem.
* Hausmann–Schmidhuber–Castro-Ruiz, arXiv:2308.10967v2, compares twirled-observable and purified-measurement implementations. For finite-resource clocks, the purified measurement model can be time-nonlocal and nonunitary while the twirled model remains unitary; these are treated as operationally distinct measurement setups, not as a failure of equivalence among the trinity’s three pictures (Secs. 3–5). This occupies finite-resolution effects, but not the requested same-instrument three-picture comparison.
* A still closer 2026 predecessor is Ludescher et al., arXiv:2607.24976v1. It generalizes perspective-neutral QRFs to covariant instruments and explicitly obtains exact Schrödinger dynamics in a center-of-mass clock for a model where subsystem clocks interact; in its subsystem frame the same model instead has a time-dependent effective Hamiltonian, while its Appendix B.1 recovers the Smith–Ahmadi kernel. This defeats a broad “interacting clocks lack an exact relational frame” claim, though it does not state the proposed necessary/sufficient classification for arbitrary instruments and interactions.

**3. Delta.** The potentially distinct delta is a classification for a *fixed, finite-dimensional periodic clock; a specified non-ideal covariant POVM; and a non-commuting interaction*, comparing the three trinity reductions on a declared reachable domain. The 2026 instrument framework and 2024 periodic-clock trinity make even this a narrow question, not a verified gap. A change of reduction or clock frame alone is not an ontological equivalence or a measurable effect.

**4. Smallest example and independent check.** RC-1 proposes the finite-group center-of-mass model and exact arithmetic family checks. Ludescher et al. already works the center-of-mass frame for the interaction (K(X_1-X_2)^2). Reusing it can validate correspondence, but cannot be the new result. A new example would need the specified POVM, non-commuting coupling, physical inner product/domain, and a second derivation route (direct matrix calculation versus reduction-map construction). No such example is supplied yet.

**5. Falsifiers.** Already met in part: the covariant-instrument and periodic-clock papers defeat broad versions; the recorded Smith–Ahmadi and Hausmann results occupy kernel and finite-resolution phenomena. Remaining decisive falsifiers: inspect Ludescher’s arbitrary-clock/instrument scope and assumptions against the exact finite model; inspect forward citations and original code/math for any same-model three-picture calculation; check if the proposed “isometry” is guaranteed by a change of representation or by the chosen inner product. The current contract itself names these tests but does not resolve them.

**6. Novelty separation and unmet items.** A theorem or new counterexample could be mathematical novelty; implementing it in Lean would be formalization novelty only if the formalized content is genuinely missing and source correspondence/dependencies are audited. A physical claim requires an accessible instrument yielding different probabilities. No philosophical consequence follows from clock autonomy alone. Unmet: sharpened class and operational stakes; exact comparison with Ludescher and 2409.06479; reproducible new example; proof or explicit counterexample; falsifiers/forward search. **Disposition: not selected; broad framing partly occupied.**

### RC-2 — Premise dependencies for simultaneity and definiteness

**1. Exact question/result.** The proposed deliverable combines a context-preserving reconstruction of Stein’s causal-past characterization, premise ablations/countermodels, and a comparison to Malament simultaneity. This combines two different relation types and two different axiom settings; the contract needs to keep those domains separate.

**2. Three nearest predecessors.**

* Malament (1977), Noûs 11(3), pp. 293–300, proves that Einstein simultaneity relative to an inertial observer is explicitly first-order definable from causal connectibility plus that observer, then proves uniqueness under the stated equivalence, invariance, and nontriviality conditions (Props. 1–2). It already supplies the core simultaneity uniqueness result. Sarkar–Stachel’s 1999 primary critique constructs null-cone alternatives when temporal-reflection invariance is removed, demonstrating that premise variation has substantive prior art.
* Stein (1991), *On Relativity Theory and Openness of the Future*, pp. 148–150, is available in this audit only through a third-party OCR/transcription, not a scan of the printed primary. Its contextual argument is readable and appears to require an event-relative reflexive/transitive relation, invariance under time-orientation-preserving automorphisms, inclusion of the causal past, and unsettled events. The boxed theorem as rendered omits non-universality, and the strict/reflexive qualification is not secure. My reconstruction that a non-past pair, invariance and transitivity force universality is an inference from the surrounding discussion, not a resolved correspondence audit of Stein’s exact printed theorem. This remains a blocker before claiming an exact reconstruction or premise ablation.
* Besnard (arXiv:1104.1263v1; 2018 manuscript date shown in PDF) extends Malament’s classification by adding observer ordering/clock structure: Theorem 3.1 gives the conic family with ordered worldline; Theorem 4.1 classifies relations with a proper-time clock as arbitrary radial-profile classes; Theorem 5.1 treats observer-local light-cone data. The paper explicitly maps how conclusions change as available structure changes. This substantially occupies a general “assumption-dependency table for simultaneity.”

The current project’s `knowledge/axiomatic-relativity/ablations-and-temporal-ontology.md` also records established ablations from Giulini, Mamone-Capria, Ben-Yami, Besnard and axiomatic-relativity sources, and explicitly marks most of the proposed simultaneity lattice as already settled. This repository record is a synthesis, not an independent primary source.

**3. Delta.** A narrowly scoped machine-checked necessity table for the *particular contextual Stein theorem* might be useful. The mathematically new portion is not identified: the base theorem is Stein’s; the simultaneity premise ladder is already classified in primary literature. The proposed finite n≤6 certificate proves only a finite fragment and cannot establish the Minkowski theorem.

**4. Smallest example and independent check.** Existing project countermodels for Stein premise removal and a finite first-order structure could serve as a formalization check. They must be compared against Stein’s theorem and the existing ablation corpus; a finite certificate is not a general necessity proof. A suitable independent route would be a paper-to-formula correspondence audit followed by exhaustive enumeration with the finite-domain bound stated.

**5. Falsifiers.** The central theorem is already known; Besnard and the broader classification literature defeat a generic classification claim; the existing project ablation file reports specific settled axes. Remaining falsifiers include the full Clifton–Hogarth 1995 paper (not obtained in this run), a formal-repository/code search, and a proof audit of which Stein premises are actually necessary in the exact contextualized statement.

**6. Novelty separation and unmet items.** Any possible contribution is mathematical only if a genuinely new dependency result survives the literature comparison; formalization novelty would require a missing reusable formalization plus an audited repository search. Physics does not decide eternalism or determinism here. Stein’s relation is a mathematical definition/characterization, and the ontology bridge remains philosophical. Unmet: a specific non-preempted ablation; generalized rather than finite-domain proof route; formalization-prior-art audit; Clifton–Hogarth full text. **Disposition: reject current “new classification” framing; formalization-only possibility unproven.**

### RC-3 — Frequency-sector coherence and normalized conditioning

**1. Exact question/result.** The contract asks whether a coherent superposition of σ=± clock-frequency sectors can produce normalized, measurable conditional probabilities distinct from a sector mixture under specified effects. “Under the paper’s clock POVM effects” is key: adding an effect/reference that connects the sectors changes the operational model.

**2. Three nearest predecessors.**

* Höhn–Smith–Lock (arXiv:2007.00580v1), Sec. II.2 and Eqs. (13–14), derives a direct-sum split for (C_H=s p_t^2/2+H_S). Its text says physical expectation values are insensitive to cross-sector coherence and the σ sectors are superselected (around Eqs. 32–33). This directly occupies the proposed no-go under the stated gauge-invariant observables. Its Sec. VI normalization discussion concerns conditioning on non-covariant Minkowski time and the Klein–Gordon amplitude; do not misattribute the superselection result to that section.
* Höhn–Smith–Lock, arXiv:1912.00033v3, supplies the covariant-POVM conditioning and reduction-map construction for the trinity under its non-interacting, well-behaved-clock assumptions. It gives the correct operational baseline against which any alternative clock effect must be defined.
* Hosseini–Lock (arXiv:2604.00092v1), Secs. II–III and App. A, explicitly states that physical expectation values are insensitive to coherence between the two sectors; the naive position-conditioned reduction coherently combines symmetry-forbidden sector phase information, while an isometric reduction is possible sector by sector and is therefore a partial isometry. The authors also state that an observation of counter-propagating wavepacket interference would falsify their model. This is a close follow-up that occupies the normalization issue in a concrete time-of-arrival setting.

**3. Delta.** A model with an external phase reference and effects not twirled/block-diagonal in the sector label could yield cross terms, but it enlarges the system/instrument and no longer tests the original physical-observable algebra. The possible result is a straightforward instrument calculation with a changed admissibility premise; no distinct surviving delta is presently established.

**4. Smallest example and independent check.** The contract’s two-sector finite matrix model should be replaced or anchored to exact Eqs. (25–29) and the sector-wise reduction maps in 2007.00580/2604.00092. Compare (i) physical Dirac observables, (ii) sector-wise reduction, and (iii) any reference-assisted instrument. Positivity and normalization alone do not show operational distinguishability; explicitly derive an allowed measurement effect and its probability. None has been reproduced here.

**5. Falsifiers.** Falsifier (i) is met: the relativistic trinity paper states the superselection/no-coherence rule. Falsifier (iii) is largely met for gauge-invariant relational observables: the cross terms vanish due to direct-sum structure. Falsifier (ii) is strengthened by 2604.00092’s sector-wise normalization result. To pursue a different model, establish that the auxiliary phase reference is constraint-compatible and not an unphysical violation of the superselection rule.

**6. Novelty separation and unmet items.** The superselection/no-go is existing mathematical content; a new reference-assisted observable could be physical only if its allowed coupling and measurable cross term are derived. It has no direct ontological implication. Unmet: exact instrument and reference-frame premises, a novel probability rule, derivation of the cross coefficient, experimental/access condition, comparison with the 2026 follow-up. **Disposition: reject current contract; candidate reformulation is a different enlarged model and is not selected.**

### RC-4 — Record equivalence in classical sequential growth

**1. Exact question/result.** Within a fixed discrete-general-covariant classical sequential-growth law, does bounded internal record access distinguish growth paths with the same completed unlabeled causal set? The answer depends entirely on whether “record” includes birth labels. If labels are gauge, label-dependent records are not internal covariant observables; if all records are unlabeled-poset invariants, their equality is built into the observable definition.

**2. Three nearest predecessors.**

* Rideout–Sorkin, arXiv:gr-qc/9904062v3 (2004 version; published PRD 61, 024002), defines growth by Markov transition probabilities, imposes discrete general covariance as path independence, and says naturally labeled births are fictitious/gauge. Sec. 3 states that every path to a causet has the same transition-probability product; Sec. 5 gives the finite-stage arrival probability as the number of inequivalent natural labelings times the label-independent formation weight. Under this premise, conditioned on a final unlabeled causal set, labelings/path records have a law determined by that covariant history, not an extra parameter-dependent history. This is an inference from the authors’ covariance condition, not a published observer-record theorem.
* Brightwell–Dowker–Garcia–Henson–Sorkin, arXiv:gr-qc/0210061v2, defines answerable covariant questions as measurable relabeling-invariant collections and identifies the unlabeled completed causal set as the quotient space (Secs. 2–3, Prop. 1). Thus, if a record is explicitly a measurable function of the shared covariant history, its distribution is fixed by that history’s measure. This conditional inference does not cover arbitrary physical observer records or show that two ontologies are equivalent.
* Surya–Zalel, arXiv:2003.11311v1 (the only version listed on the landing page), gives Theorem 2.1 for extension of a C-valued complex measure from the cylinder-event algebra to its generated sigma-algebra, with a bounded-variation criterion (Secs. 2–4). This is not a general Hilbert-valued extension theorem and does not specifically settle extension to a smaller covariant event algebra. Failure on the full generated sigma-algebra would not by itself rule out extension to a specified smaller algebra. It is a relevant warning and predecessor, but it does not resolve the contract’s quantum boundary without the actual target algebra and measure.
* Wüthrich–Callender, arXiv:1502.00018v1, Secs. 3–5, explicitly distinguish the mathematical covariant growth law from its temporal interpretation: they discuss both a tenseless history-space reading and a growth/becoming reading with additional metaphysical costs. This is the closest philosophical predecessor and blocks an inference from the stochastic law to either completed-history ontology or objective passage.

**3. Delta.** The only plausible delta is an explicit observer-access model whose record is not simply a measurable function of the shared covariant history, or a quantum-growth model in which the measure fails to extend to the specifically defined covariant event algebra of interest. Surya–Zalel’s full-cylinder-algebra result does not decide that smaller-algebra question. Either target leaves the contract’s fixed-covariance classical family. A bounded access constraint on covariant observables adds no distinction unless the access algebra is specified in a way not reducible to the final poset. Non-Markov dynamics alone is not an obstruction to a classical path-space measure.

**4. Smallest example and independent check.** The proposed n≤6 enumeration is useful only as a consistency check of the path-independence argument. For each finite (C), enumerate inequivalent natural labelings with multiplicity, verify all path weights agree, and calculate record distributions conditional on (C). It cannot establish the infinite completed-history result or rescue a theorem already implied by DGC. The current main checkout’s `csg_ancillarity_check.py` is reported as a finite n≤5 check; I did not rerun it.

**5. Falsifiers.** The core fixed-classical-model proposition appears to follow from Rideout–Sorkin path independence plus the covariant observable quotient, conditional on records being measurable functions of the same shared covariant history; this is my inference, not a published observer-record theorem. A counterexample under that definition would falsify the inference. Label-sensitive records would change the stated covariance premise. The infinite-measure and quantum cases require separate proofs, not extrapolation from finite enumeration or from Surya–Zalel’s full-algebra theorem.

**6. Novelty separation and unmet items.** Classical result as worded: no established novelty; it appears to be a corollary once “record” means a measurable function of the same covariant history. Surya–Zalel addresses a mathematical extension problem on its stated sigma-algebra but does not settle the proposed smaller-algebra boundary. A stochastic law does not by itself decide whether the represented history is completed, nor whether growth is ontological passage. Any such reading is a philosophical bridge premise. Unmet: a defined nontrivial observer-access algebra; a new model family; infinite-stage proof; a specified quantum covariant event algebra and extension result; any defensible physical or philosophical consequence. **Disposition: reject RC-4 as currently bounded.**

### RC-5 — Four inequivalent memory criteria for clock-conditioned dynamics

**1. Exact question/result.** The proposal wants one clock-conditioned model that separates (a) failure of a homogeneous semigroup, (b) CP indivisibility, (c) trace-distance information backflow, and (d) multi-time process-tensor memory, then applies it to Eq. (10) of Nandi–Petruccione (arXiv:2606.08595v1). These are different definitions and not all predicates apply to the same object. Normalized postselection can be nonlinear when its success probability depends on the input state, while the usual channel divisibility tests require linear CPTP maps. The Nandi paper’s body derivation and Eq. (10) have not been audited here, so I make no conclusion about whether that equation is nonlinear or CPTP. A map family and its normalization must be specified before asking whether the criteria come apart.

**2. Three nearest predecessors.**

* Chruściński–Kossakowski–Rivas (arXiv:1102.4318v2, §§I–II and IV), *On measures of non-Markovianity: divisibility vs. backflow of information*, constructs and analyzes the relation between CP-divisibility and BLP trace-distance backflow; their criteria are not interchangeable. This occupies a major part of the proposed generic criterion separation.
* Milz–Kim–Pollock–Modi (arXiv:1901.05223v2, §§I–III and Appendix B), *Completely positive divisibility does not mean Markovianity*, gives a finite-dimensional open-system example: CP-divisible dynamics can still carry temporal correlations/memory in the process tensor. Their qubit partial-swap construction also separates operational from inversion-based CP divisibility. This occupies criterion (d) versus (b) directly.
* Pollock et al. (arXiv:1801.09811v1, main text and Appendices A–B), *Operational Markov condition for quantum processes*, derives a necessary and sufficient multi-time condition for Markovianity, recovering the classical condition in the relevant limit. It provides an operational definition of the process-level criterion, distinct from a time-local equation or a kernel alone.
* The contract’s clock-conditioned predecessor Hausmann et al. (arXiv:2308.10967v2) yields finite-clock time-nonlocal/nonunitary conditioned evolution for a measurement-coupled setup, but does not classify the four channel criteria. It is an application domain, not evidence of a missing general separation.

**3. Delta.** A mathematically precise application to a *linear* clock-conditioned CPTP map family could still be useful, but the contract has not yet specified a model/map family or demonstrated that Nandi–Petruccione Eq. (10) supplies one. That gap is definitional before it is novel; no defect in Eq. (10) is presumed. The “homogeneous semigroup” comparison also needs a separate time-homogeneity assumption and should not be conflated with kernel memory.

**4. Smallest example and independent check.** The source family must first define a linear instrument-to-channel map (for example by keeping a fixed outcome normalization and specifying interventions). Then calculate semigroup composition, intermediate CPTP maps, trace-distance monotonicity for all pairs of input states, and the process tensor/Markov condition. Each criterion needs a separate certificate. The contract’s Gaussian dephasing example only separates CP-divisibility from homogeneous semigroup behavior; it does not establish a clock-conditioned four-way separation.

**5. Falsifiers.** Generic separation is already published by the sources above, so only a demonstrably new clock-constrained model/application could survive. Read Nandi–Petruccione’s complete derivation and supplement; test whether Eq. (10) defines linear maps at all; search further clock-conditioned examples and later memory-criterion work. Current project source notes explicitly say the supplement/TCL promotion and all four tests remain unaudited.

**6. Novelty separation and unmet items.** Mathematical novelty would require a new separation result in the bounded clock model; formalization would certify the exact linear maps only; physical novelty requires a realizable measurement/instrument; no philosophical conclusion follows and nothing here establishes a thermodynamic arrow. Unmet: full primary-text derivation audit; linear channel definition; precise criterion set; concrete new example; independent computation. **Disposition: reject current contract pending model definition and full-text read.**

## Selection decision

**Explicit no-selection.** RC-1 has the clearest surviving *test proposal*: a finite periodic clock with a specified covariant POVM and a non-commuting interaction, testing agreement among Dirac, Page–Wootters, and relational Heisenberg reductions on a precisely defined common domain. RC-2’s possible formalization contribution is the next most concrete, but existing premises and theorem structure are heavily occupied. This is not a claim that the proposed sliver is open.
