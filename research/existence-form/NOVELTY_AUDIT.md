# Novelty audit: P1\*\*, coverage, and the form-of-existence trilemma

Date: 2026-10-21. Bounded defeat search for the reframed article. This is not a
global priority certificate.

## The candidate claims

1. **P1\*\* retarget.** Brading 2015 p. 17 states P1\*\* and says: "In order to
   avoid the block universe, all we need is (a)." Clause (a) is each thing's
   present extent being less than the entire spatiotemporal evolution of the
   universe. That is local boundedness. The paper argues that (a) does not
   exclude the union of system-presents covering the whole history, so
   Brading's own stated sufficient condition is insufficient for the global
   existential restriction.
2. **Coverage non-implication.** Local boundedness of every `U_i` does not imply
   `union_i U_i` is proper. This is elementary set theory.
3. **System-relative / frame-relative existence.** Existence claims are relative
   to a system or frame, rather than a single unrestricted domain.
4. **Form-of-existence trilemma.** P-form (predicate presentism) → coverage;
   Q-form (quantifier-restricted presentism) → needs a global present/privilege;
   N-form (no common domain) → relational/deflationary debate.
5. **Deflationary upshot.** The local-present route either changes the subject
   or needs an actuality/eligibility premise.

## Closest prior work

### Brading 2013, 2015

Brading 2013 gives P1, P1\*, the interaction passage and the system-sustaining
present. Brading 2015 p. 17 gives P1\*\* and the "all we need is (a)" sentence.
This is the **target**, not a prior critique. Her own text also says "what
exists (what is real) are the genuine unities" (2015, p. 17), which supports the
reviewer's point that the domain is unities, not events.

### Lucy Mason, "Temporal Perspectives, Probabilities, and Openness" (2025 preprint; Synthese 2026)

Mason uses Brading's empirical presentism as the most useful local-present
account. She explicitly says that a major flaw in the presentism/eternalism
debate is assuming we can answer "what exists?" without specifying the domain,
and that facts at other dynamical levels are "not necessarily included in what
exists in any given perspective" (preprint pp. 17-18). This is close to the
N-form/domain-relativity idea. She does **not** state the P1\*\* coverage
objection or challenge "all we need is (a)." She treats Brading's local present
as a live option, not as failing a global restriction.

### Mark Balaguer, *How to Be a Presentist* (OUP 2026)

Balaguer's R-presentism says that what exists is relative to a frame of
reference, with no frame-independent fact about which 3-manifold is real
(chapter "How to Make Presentism Consistent with Special Relativity"). This is
a developed **relativized-existence** view. It is a nearby alternative to
Brading, not a critique of her (a). It shows that system/frame-relative
existence is not novel as a position; it also shows that the N-form is a
serious option, not a straw man.

### Local-present literature

Savitt (2009, 2011) and Rovelli (2019) define local presents via causal
diamonds, lightcones or local frames. They do not raise the union/coverage
objection to Brading's (a). Their local presents are candidate definitions, not
a critique of the global restriction.

### Structural/relativity challenges

Earman 2008, Saunders 2000 and Pooley 2013 raise structural-completeness,
equivalence-relation and "take passage seriously" challenges. They are not the
P1\*\* coverage point. Saunders's equivalence-relation requirement and Pooley's
two easy routes are prior art for the paper's objections section, not for its
headline.

### General set theory

The non-implication `∀i U_i ⊊ W ⊬ ⋃U_i ⊊ W` is elementary. It is very likely
known in some form in the local-presentism literature or in general
mereology/topology. The novelty cannot be the bare set-theoretic fact. It is
the **application to Brading's own stated sufficient condition** and the
source-grounded reconstruction of why P1\*\* does not secure the global
restriction.

## Verdict

**Modest, source-grounded novelty; not a new general theory.**

- The **P1\*\* coverage point** — that Brading's own (a) is local boundedness and
  does not exclude coverage, so "all we need is (a)" is insufficient — appears
  not to be stated in the prior work found. This is the paper's defensible
  contribution.
- The **coverage non-implication** is elementary and should not be sold as
  novel.
- **System-/frame-relative existence** is not novel: Balaguer 2026 and Mason
  2025 already develop versions. The paper must cite them and must not claim
  the idea.
- The **P/Q/N trilemma** is a useful framing, but Mason's domain/level point and
  Balaguer's R-presentism make it unlikely to be novel as a general thesis.
  Use it as a diagnostic, not the headline.
- The **deflationary upshot** belongs to a known family (Earman, Dorato and
  others); no novelty claim.

## Recommended claim in the paper

> Brading's strongest anti-block condition, P1\*\* (a), is local boundedness.
> Local boundedness does not exclude the union of system-presents covering the
> history. Therefore her own stated sufficient condition does not secure the
> global existential restriction. This is a source-grounded negative result
> about one published argument, not a new theory of time.

This is exactly the "modest, publishable contribution" the second referee
identified. It is defensible even if the broader trilemma is not novel.

## What would defeat the novelty

- A published or unpublished critique of Brading's (a) making the same union
  point.
- A source showing that Brading's "all we need is (a)" is not intended as a
  global-existence condition but only as a condition on the extent of each
  thing's present.
- A proof that the actual family of system-presents cannot cover the history,
  which would make the objection moot.
- A prior statement of the P1\*\* coverage point in Mason, Balaguer or the
  local-present literature that this search missed.

## Limits

This is a bounded search, not a priority certificate. The exact-phrase searches
are weak evidence of absence. The published versions of Mason and Balaguer were
not read in full. The paper should cite them as nearby positions and avoid any
global novelty claim.
