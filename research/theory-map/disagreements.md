## D01 — Is probabilistic openness compatible with special relativity? `[disagreement]`

- **Parties:** N04 (Stein, `found-stein-1991`, full-text) vs N04-context
  (Maxwell, `found-maxwell-1993`, full-text).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** Stein argues SR permits a reasonable
  event-relative becoming/open future (via the causal-past definiteness
  relation, N04) and rebuts an inference from mutual indefiniteness of
  spacelike events to self-indefiniteness (pp.151–152). Maxwell's reply
  (Sec. III, p.343) disputes Stein's characterization and maintains that his
  probabilistic view is *not* shown compatible with SR by Stein's argument.
  The two also disagree about who is committed to a privileged cosmic now.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** whether "openness" requires present
  alternative actualities (a bridge premise); whether a probabilistic
  account needs a global present slice; what each other's view commits them
  to (the reply partly argues about attribution).
- **What could decide it:** a formal model in which "openness at `a`" is a
  defined property of a stochastic history relative to `a` (e.g.,
  `R`-conditional outcome distributions), plus explicit bridge premises —
  then the compatibility question becomes a theorem/counterexample question.
  This is folded into RC-2's ablation program and candidate A of
  `knowledge/NOVELTY.md`.
- **Why not averageable:** the dispute is over bridge premises and
  attributions, not over any measurable quantity; a vote or score would
  manufacture agreement that does not exist.

## D02 — Is temporal-reflection invariance physically warranted? `[disagreement]`

- **Parties:** N02 (Malament's uniqueness result, as relayed) vs
  `found-sarkar-stachel-1999` (full-text critique).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** within the Malament framework, dropping
  temporal-reflection invariance admits future/past null-cone simultaneity
  alternatives (Sarkar–Stachel's construction); what remains distinctive of
  Einstein synchrony is independence from observer position within a frame.
  Whether the invariance premise is a physically motivated requirement or a
  disguised convention is contested.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** which invariances definability may presume.
- **What could decide it:** nothing empirical — this is settled only by
  explicit choice of premises; the precise content is preserved by RC-2's
  premise-dependency table (which premise forces which uniqueness).
- **Why not averageable:** both sides agree on the mathematics; the
  disagreement is normative about premises. Averaging would hide that the
  "uniqueness of simultaneity" slogan is premise-fragile.

## D03 — Putnam's reality propagation vs Stein's local becoming `[disagreement]`

- **Parties:** N03 (`found-putnam`, full-text partial; `found-rietdijk`,
  abstract) vs N04 (`found-stein-1991`, full-text).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** whether frame-relative simultaneity, plus
  observer-neutral reality propagation (principle III + transitive closure +
  enough observers), forces a block ontology — or whether the argument
  misapplies relative simultaneity as a global spatial present (Stein
  pp.151–152) and whether transitivity of "equally real" is legitimate.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** Putnam's reality-transfer bridge premises vs
  Stein's event-relative definiteness with an unsettled condition; note the
  audit's correction that Putnam's operative premise is principle III
  (observer neutrality), not geometric transitivity of synchrony.
- **What could decide it:** a formalization separating (i) Lorentz geometry,
  (ii) reality-propagation rules, (iii) abundance-of-observers premise —
  then block-conclusion dependence on each premise is checkable. RC-2.
- **Why not averageable:** different bridge premises yield different
  ontologies on the *same* geometry; that is the central preserved
  incompatibility of the foundations lane.

## D04 — Does thermal time presuppose the time it defines? `[disagreement]`

*(Graph edge X13: N24 ↔ N26 ↔ N27.)*

- **Parties:** N24/N26 (`frontier-thermal-time-original-1994`,
  `frontier-emergence-time-qg-2012`, full-text lane reads) vs N27
  (`frontier-thermal-time-concept-2024`, full-text).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** Chua argues the *strong* TTH reading is
  circular or lacks a justified dynamical interpretation (KMS/equilibrium
  presuppose temporal notions; modular flow is defined but not automatically
  physical time). Martinetti–Rovelli's examples show realization modes
  (geometrical / partially / non-geometrical) and leave the identification
  open — they do not claim the strong reading succeeds.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** whether "time" in the TTH claim is
  identified with the flow by definition (weaker reading, where the
  hypothesis loses content) or by an independent physical bridge (strong
  reading, where Chua's circularity objection bites).
- **What could decide it:** an operational protocol realizing a specific
  modular flow with independent calibration (N25's open question is exactly
  this); RC-5-adjacent but philosophical — the deciding artifact would be a
  protocol or a no-go, not a survey.
- **Why not averageable:** the two readings disagree about what the
  hypothesis *is*; no statistic can merge them.

## D05 — Do records force a fundamental time asymmetry? `[disagreement]`

*(Graph edge X15: N29 ↔ N30.)*

- **Parties:** N29 (`frontier-records-histories-2016`, full-text access) vs
  N30 (`frontier-records-time-asymmetry-1993`, full-text access).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** whether a records-based formulation of
  quantum mechanics carries an intrinsic arrow, or whether apparent
  asymmetry is contingent on boundary conditions/state choice (Page's
  challenge).
- **Incompatible assumptions:** formalism-first (asymmetric decoherence
  functional as primitive) vs state/boundary-contingency-first.
- **What could decide it:** an invariance analysis — which asymmetry
  statements survive reformulations preserving all record statistics
  (candidate A of NOVELTY.md is the bounded version of this question).
- **Why not averageable:** both can agree on all recorded statistics while
  disagreeing about what the formalism *is*; a middle "partly asymmetric"
  score represents neither.

## D06 — What is an event? TO vs PM measurement protocols `[disagreement, model-level]`

- **Parties:** N18's two protocols (`frontier-page-wootters-measurements-2023`,
  full-text).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** twirled-observable (TO) vs ancilla-
  triggered (PM) operational event notions agree on multi-time statistics
  for ideal clocks but differ for finite-spectrum clocks (nonlocal,
  possibly nonunitary PM conditioning).
- **Incompatible assumptions:** what counts as a clock-time measurement
  (external protocol vs internal trigger).
- **What could decide it:** an experiment realizing both protocols on a
  finite-resolution clock; no such test is recorded anywhere in the lanes —
  this is a genuine "empirically undecided, formally settled-in-models"
  entry.
- **Why not averageable:** the difference is operational, not metaphysical
  posturing; it admits a decisive (hard) experiment.

## D07 — Is causal-set growth "becoming"? `[disagreement]`

- **Parties:** N08's growth interpretation vs
  `frontier-causal-becoming-2015` (abstract) and the map's caution; cf. X21.
- **What is disputed, precisely:** whether stochastic birth of poset
  elements instantiates objective becoming, or is representation plus
  stochastic modeling (Wüthrich–Callender's analysis; also no consensus that
  causal sets empirically select an ontology).
- **Incompatible assumptions:** growth-as-passage bridge premise vs
  instrumental reading of the Markov process.
- **What could decide it:** a record-observable distinguishability theorem
  (RC-4): if no internal record distinguishes growth histories within a
  covariance class, the "becoming" reading is empirically idle *in that
  bounded family* — which would not settle the general question.
- **Why not averageable:** the bridge premise is exactly what is disputed;
  averaging would reify one reading.

## D09 — Frame-dependence vs fundamentality of indefinite causal order `[disagreement, live]`

- **Parties:** N21's parity thesis (`map-indefinite-order-quantum-coordinates-2025v3`,
  abstract) vs the map's preservation requirement
  (project standards) that representation change not be promoted to
  ontology; adjacent: N20 (`frontier-time-delocalization-2026`, abstract)
  and N22 (`seed-bell-temporal-order`, abstract).
- **What is disputed, precisely:** whether "indefinite causal order" labels
  (a) a physical process property realized equally on optical benches and in
  superposed gravitational fields (N21's thesis), or (b) partly a matter of
  description whose fundamental-status attribution requires more than an
  observable that is *insensitive* to implementation differences.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** what follows from invariance of an
  observable across implementations: parity of realization vs mere
  indistinguishability-so-far.
- **What could decide it:** extraction of N21's observable definition and
  its assumption set (what access, what background structure); a
  temporal-QRF transformation moving that observable (X10's falsifier) would
  sharpen (b) into a theorem-shaped disagreement.
- **Why not averageable:** the dispute is about an inference rule
  (indistinguishability → same ontological category), which must stay
  explicit rather than dissolved.
