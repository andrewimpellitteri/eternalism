# RC-3 reread: sector-coherent conditioning

Coordinator draft, 2026-10-20, written before the parent task can be claimed. It
should be compared with `kb-sector-conditioning` when that lane is submitted.
Primary texts fetched 2026-10-20: arXiv HTML of 2007.00580 (Höhn, Smith, Lock)
and 2604.00092v1 (Hosseini, Lock). Not a novelty claim.

## Verdict: SHRINK (first draft said KILL; see the update below)

The contract asks for either a normalized sector-coherent conditioning rule
with a measurable cross-sector term, or a no-go. Its narrowest remaining case
(defeat search) was "non-twirled conditioning instruments with an explicit
external phase reference". The primary text already addresses both routes:

1. **Sector coherence is inaccessible in the factorizable model.** 2007.00580,
   passage on Eq. (33): "such coherence is not physically accessible because it
   does not affect the expectation value of any relational Dirac observable on
   account of the decomposition in Eq. (33). In other words, superpositions and
   classical mixtures across the σ-frequency sectors are indistinguishable."
2. **An external reference frame is argued not to lift it.** The same discussion
   (cites ref. [117], Eqs. (13)–(14)): "if an appropriate reference frame is
   used the superselection rule can be lifted [117]. This interpretation seems
   unsuitable here. Firstly, lifting the superselection rule would entail
   undoing the group averaging, in violation of gauge invariance. Secondly, …
   the origin of the superselection of Dirac observables here is not the group
   generated by the constraint, but is a consequence of a property of the
   constraint, i.e. the group generator."
3. **Where the superselection disappears.** Same passage, main text: with a
   `t̂`-dependent term in the constraint, "no superselection rule across the
   σ-sectors would arise. The above superselection rule can thus not be
   associated with the lack of a shared physical reference frame." Footnote 14
   holds only the example, `C_H = p_t² − H²(q_i, p_i, t)` with `H² > 0`.
   (Corrected after `kb-sector-conditioning`: the first draft attributed the
   quoted clause to the footnote.)
4. **Normalization obstruction and remedy, published for a free particle.**
   2604.00092v1: "physical expectation values are insensitive to coherence
   between the σ=± components" (after Eq. 20); the naive reduction map
   "coherently combines the different superselection sectors" and the clock
   states "cannot be normalised in a state-independent manner"; the remedy is
   sector-wise reduction maps that are "partial isometries" (Sec. II).

So (b), the no-go, is stated in prose by 2007.00580 for the factorizable
constraint. The external-reference-frame route is explicitly discussed and
argued to break gauge invariance. The sector-wise normalization is in 2604.00092.

**Conditional residue (not a SHRINK yet).** For a non-factorizable constraint
with time-dependent `H²(t)`, 2007.00580 says the sector superselection does not
arise, but does not, in the passage read, give a normalized conditioning rule or
cross-sector term. Whether that is open depends on the time-dependent
Page–Wootters literature, which `kb-sector-conditioning` may cover. Keep it as
a question for that lane, not as a contract.

## Update after `kb-sector-conditioning` (merged 2026-10-20)

The lane confirms the passages above, finds no normalized sector-coherent rule
distinct from the sector-mixed one in any source read, and finds no treatment of
an internal reference carrying σ inside a Hamiltonian constraint.
Bartlett–Rudolph–Spekkens cover lifting for compact or finite groups only.

The verdict moves from KILL to **SHRINK**, because the lane names a residue that
needs no external reference (its conjecture, unchecked):

> The clock reflection `P_C ⊗ I_S` commutes with `C_H = s p_t²/2 + H_S` (since
> `p_t² ` is reflection-invariant) and swaps the σ-sectors. A two-outcome
> refinement `|τ, b⟩ = (|τ,+⟩ + b|τ,−⟩)/√2`, b = ±1, appears to give a
> normalized rule with interference terms whose denominator is τ-independent,
> and summing over b recovers the sector-mixed rule, HSL Eq. (62).

Remaining case in one sentence: *whether sector-mixing operators that commute
with the constraint, such as the clock reflection, are admissible physical
observables, and if so, what normalized conditional probabilities the refined
two-outcome conditioning gives.* HSL's "relational Dirac observables" may be a
narrower algebra than 2409.06479's Dirac observables (Example 14, as read by the
lane, unverified). The likeliest defeater is Marolf gr-qc/9508015, unread, whose
abstract ties superselection to "the choice of auxiliary structures". The next
step is to read it and compute the two-outcome rule for one finite model.

## What would reopen RC-3

A gauge-invariant model in which the sector label is carried by a physical
system inside the constraint (not an external frame), giving a normalized rule
that differs from the sector mixture. That would answer HSL's first objection
and could be in the quantum-reference-frame literature (Bartlett–Rudolph–Spekkens,
RMP 79, 555, unread here). It must be searched before any contract is written.

The parent task was claimed after this draft. Re-fetched and re-read:

  physical Hilbert space that exhibit coherence across the σ-frequency sectors
  ... such coherence is not physically accessible because it does not affect the
  expectation value of any relational Dirac observable on account of the
  decomposition in Eq. (33). In other words, superpositions and classical

  "if an appropriate reference frame is used the superselection rule can be

  only "E.g. when `C_H = p_t² − H²(q_i,p_i,t)` and `H²>0 ∀t∈R`"; the clause "no
  superselection rule across the σ-sectors would arise" is the next main-text
  sentence. The lane's correction is right.
- **Hosseini–Lock 2604.00092v1.** Verified: after Eq. (20) "physical expectation
  values are insensitive to coherence between the σ=± components"; Sec. III.1
  the naive map "coherently combines the different superselection sectors",
  "is not invertible", and the clock states "cannot be normalised in a
  state-independent manner"; Sec. II.2 the maps "must be defined sector-wise"
  and "are partial isometries"; Appendix A: the map "is an isometry if and only
  if" Eq. (42) holds. The paper's model is a free particle with quadratic clock
  Hamiltonian and position conditioning; it says its approach "does not
  incorporate detector dynamics, finite resolution, or interaction effects"
  (Sec. IV). It therefore does not address the non-twirled-instrument case.
- **The lane's two-outcome reflection refinement remains a conjecture.** No
  source read states it, and it was not checked here. It is a candidate for the
  remaining case, not a result.

## Conditional claim-contract draft (NOVELTY.md gate)

This contract is **conditional** on reading Marolf gr-qc/9508015 and
Bartlett–Rudolph–Spekkens (RMP 79, 555) at full text first.

- **Question.** In the relativistic-trinity model `C_H = s p_t²/2 + H_S` with a
  finite-dimensional clock and a `C_H`-commuting, `G`-twirled conditioning
  instrument that is off-diagonal in the σ sectors (e.g. the clock reflection
  `P_C ⊗ I_S` refined into a two-outcome POVM), do normalized conditional
  probabilities exist, and do they differ from the sector-mixed rule by a
  measurable cross-sector term?
- **Nearest predecessors.** HSL 2007.00580 (sector coherence inaccessible to
  relational Dirac observables; external-frame route rejected); Hosseini–Lock
  2604.00092 (normalization obstruction and sector-wise partial-isometry remedy,
  free particle); Bartlett–Rudolph–Spekkens (reference frames lift
  superselection for compact/finite groups, unread).
- **Delta.** A `C_H`-commuting, off-diagonal instrument inside the constraint,
  with an explicit normalized rule and cross term. Possibly an application, or
  an immediate consequence of the observable algebra; the reflection example may
  already be excluded.
- **Smallest example.** Two-level clock with `H_C = Ω σ_z` and a qubit system;
  compute the two-outcome reflection-refined POVM on a physical state and
  compare its `b`-marginal with HSL Eq. (62).
- **Falsifier.** Marolf's auxiliary-structure argument; BRS's non-compact-group
  limitations; a proof that `C_H`-commuting off-diagonal effects are not
  relational Dirac observables.
- **Novelty kind.** At most a small conditioning computation or a no-go
  clarification. No ontology claim.
