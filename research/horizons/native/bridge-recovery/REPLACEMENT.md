# Replacement philosophical contract

## Horizons paper: replace the inference from unreality to openness

Use the paper's three predicates without collapsing them. An event is **real
from** event b when it belongs to the stipulated becoming fragment. It is
**guaranteed from** b when every past-inextendible causal curve from it meets
the chronological past of b, equivalently the geometric domain-of-prediction condition
defined in the manuscript. It is **fixed from** (b) under a specified law
class Σ when any two solutions in Σ agreeing on the chronological past of b agree at that
event. The first is a proposed ontological/standpoint relation; the second is
geometric; the third is law-relative. The domain-of-dependence hypothesis
implies that guaranteed events are fixed under the relevant laws. The converse
does not hold in general: special field equations can fix additional events.

Accordingly, the mathematical distinction establishes no entailment from
unreality-from-b to either lawful nonfixing or ontic indeterminacy. In the closed dust
model after maximum expansion, the geometric guarantee reaches all events;
before that epoch, the conformal scalar example fixes some events outside the
guarantee, including part of (b)'s timelike future. These are claims about
the specified classical models and law classes. A deterministic growing-block
theorist can accept that future events are absent from the standpoint's real
fragment while their values are fixed by the laws and the fragment, and can
hold that the later addition of events makes the ontology open. Whether ontic
addition suffices for openness, or whether openness requires lawful nonfixing
or indeterminacy, is a philosophical and semantic question not settled here.
Conversely, an eternalist can accept the same determination results while
holding that all events exist. The mathematics does not adjudicate between
these ontologies.

The strongest growing-block reply need not deny determinism: it distinguishes
ontic addition of events from their prior law-relative fixity. The
strongest eternalist reply is that a standpoint-indexed fact about what fixes
what is compatible with a completed spacetime and does not by itself establish
a privileged metaphysical present. The paper establishes the distinction
between reality-from, geometric guarantee, and law-relative determination;
claims about objective passage or ontic openness require further bridge
premises and an account of future-tensed truth.

## Stein–GR paper: scope the locality result to its category

The theorem concerns one relation assigned to every globally hyperbolic
**metric spacetime**, including off-shell geometries, with the manuscript's
causally-convex isometric embeddings preserving orientation and time
orientation. Its locality condition is a biconditional: the becoming verdict
for a pair is preserved **and reflected** by every such embedding. The result
therefore depends on determination by every admissible region, not merely on
ordinary forward functoriality. State the conclusion as a classification or
constraint on relations satisfying these hypotheses. It is not a theorem that
physics alone selects an ontology, nor that every physically admissible
spacetime must use the metric-only category.

A strongest shared-present reply equips each spacetime with a preferred
temporal function or physical clock and permits only embeddings preserving
that structure. A clock-level relation can then be transitive, contain the
chronological past, and make distinct events on one level mutually co-present
while satisfying locality relative to that restricted category. This reply
changes the objects and morphisms, so it escapes the theorem's assumptions;
it does not refute the metric-only result. A metric-defined cosmological clock
may add no independent field, but restricting the domain to spacetimes where
that clock exists still relinquishes off-shell universality. A physically
preferred clock or foliation adds structure. Present these as costs and
scope choices, not as proof that every shared present violates locality.

The growing-block/standpoint-first reply can accept the relation theorem while
interpreting becoming as local, point- or worldline-relative addition. The
theorem's no-shared-present corollary applies only to a shared present defined
as mutual becoming of distinct events. It leaves other definitions of a
present open. The eternalist can accept every formal result and deny that
becoming marks literal coming-into-existence. Thus “the theorem concerns a
relation, not ontology” is a substantive limit, not a modesty formula.

## Bridge commitments that must be stated, not hidden in definitions

Transitivity is a substantive bridge premise: if “has happened by” is defined
to include transitivity, that is a proposed analysis, not an independent
physical finding. Worldline transitivity across all ideal inextendible
timelike curves is stronger than agreement among actual observers who meet;
the latter needs a cross-worldline composition premise to recover the same
result. Transitivity propagates fragments along becoming steps, including
chronological steps under causal past, but does not alone establish growth
along every null or causal step.

Persistence should apply to tenseless past-event claims (and any explicitly
specified geometric consequences), not to centred claims such as “b is
present now-from-b.” The latter requires a separate tense-shift account.
Likewise distinguish metric causal reachability (x lies in the causal past of b), operational
signalling, and spacelike dynamical dependence. Causal reachability is
geometric; not every reachable event is controllably signal-capable under a
given theory. Bell dependence without signalling does not itself entail
causal-sufficiency of becoming. Any premise that all dynamical contributors
must already be real from the target is stronger and needs its own defense.

Finally, the local-fragmentalist thesis that standpoint-relative reality is
fundamental is a grounding proposal. Given the formal relation and any
separately defended persistence and scope premises, it says what grounds the
tenseless event facts. Grounding adds an asymmetric explanatory/dependence
claim: standpoint facts are basic and the block-like tenseless structure is
grounded in them. It does not, without more, change truth conditions or
empirical predictions, establish objective passage, or make the future
indeterministic. An eternalist may accept the same event facts and predictions
while rejecting that grounding direction. The view should be presented as a
metaphysical thesis whose significance depends on whether that grounding
asymmetry is independently intelligible and non-idle.
