## The picture

In algebraic quantum field theory each open region has an algebra of observables. The
question "what does the past of an event fix" becomes "what is the algebra of its past
light cone". Three known results answer it for free fields on Minkowski spacetime.

| Result | Says | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Timelike tube theorem (Borchers 1961; Araki 1963; Strohmaier and Witten 2023) | The algebra of an open set equals the algebra of its timelike envelope. It holds for interacting theories. Its classical form is Holmgren uniqueness: a solution vanishing on the set vanishes on the envelope | Strohmaier–Witten, arXiv:2303.16380, in full |
| Timelike commutativity for massless free fields in even dimensions (Buchholz 1977) | The commutator vanishes off the light cone, so the algebras of the future and past cones of a point commute. They are independent systems | Through Olson–Ralph and Jiang–Yang, both in full. Buchholz not read |
| Vacuum entanglement across time (Olson and Ralph 2011) | For a massless field the vacuum is an entangled state of the future-cone and past-cone systems, mode for mode the same entanglement as between Rindler wedges. Restricted to one cone it is thermal in a conformal time | arXiv:1003.0720, in full |
| Cone algebra for massive fields (Sadowski and Woronowicz 1971) | The algebra of the open past light cone coincides with that of a larger set | Abstract-level snippet only. Whether the larger set is everything is **not verified** |

## What follows for an observer

The timelike envelope of a thin tube around the past half of an inertial worldline is
the past light cone of its endpoint. So by the first result, **everything measurable
along an observer's past worldline generates the algebra of their whole past cone**.
An observer's own history, as a body of possible measurements, already is their causal
past. This needs no assumption about the field's mass or interactions.

The classical remark left unexamined in the massive-Minkowski note is thereby
supported: by Holmgren uniqueness a field vanishing near the past of one inertial
worldline vanishes on the past cone, and for a massive field of finite energy it then
vanishes everywhere.

## The same dichotomy as in the classical notes

| | Classical, finite energy | Quantum, free |
|---|---|---|
| Massless, four dimensions | The past cone fixes nothing about the future cone. Free data arrive from null infinity | The two cone algebras commute. Past and future of an event are separate systems |
| Massive | The past cone fixes everything | Timelike commutativity fails. The past cone's algebra is that of a larger set |

Two things are new in the quantum column.

1. **Independence without ignorance.** For a massless field the future of an event is
   a separate system from its past in the strict algebraic sense, whatever the state.
   This is a state-independent, covariant mark of openness, and it is exactly what the
   classical "free incoming data" becomes.
2. **Independent yet correlated.** In the vacuum those two systems are entangled. The
   classical lattice count found that the two cones share nothing. The quantum vacuum
   correlates them fully. So what the past *fixes* about the future and how strongly
   the two are *correlated* come apart completely here: no fixing, maximal correlation.
   The reciprocity note's distinction between fixed quantities and statistical
   dependence is realised in the simplest physical system there is.

## What it does not show

- Nothing here is new mathematics, and the massive entry rests on a snippet.
- Free fields on Minkowski spacetime only, except the timelike tube theorem.
- Entanglement entropy across the cones is infinite and needs a regulator. No number
  is claimed. Jiang and Yang note, crediting Witten, that strong subadditivity fails
  in the timelike direction.
- A state-independent algebraic relation is not a present, and commuting algebras are
  not an ontology of an open future. The bridge from "separate system" to "not yet
  real" is a philosophical premise that this note does not supply.
- The [entanglement-present lane](../../longshots/entanglement-present/RESULT.md)
  asked for a state-defined present and found it world-relative. This note is about
  something else: a relation between past and future that does not depend on the
  state.

## Open questions

- Read Sadowski and Woronowicz and settle the massive entry.
- For a massive field the past-cone algebra is presumably everything. Is the same true
  of the algebra of a thin tube around a past worldline, by the two results together?
  If so, a single inertial observer's past measurements generate every observable of a
  massive free field.
- What replaces the dichotomy with a cosmological horizon, where the classical notes
  already find that massive matter can be news?
