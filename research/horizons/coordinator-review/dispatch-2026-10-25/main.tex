\title{Unreal Is Not Open:\\ prediction, horizons and the future in
relativistic becoming}
\author{Andrew Impellitteri}
\date{25 October 2026 (draft 3)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Local theories of relativistic becoming make what is real from an event its
causal past. Does the unreality of the future from an event make the future
open from it? We separate the two questions. Call an event guaranteed from
$b$ if every past-inextendible causal curve from it meets $b$'s chronological
past; then every deterministic law with the domain-of-dependence property
fixes it from what is real from $b$. The notion is essentially Geroch's
domain of prediction. We characterize it by a condition on the slices of any
Cauchy time function, show that it is conformally invariant, and compute it
for the standard cosmological models. In Minkowski spacetime, spatially flat
and open big-bang universes, the closed radiation universe and de Sitter
spacetime, nothing outside an event's past is guaranteed; in Minkowski
spacetime and the flat universes the bound is attained, since a conformally
coupled scalar field is fixed from an event exactly on its causal past. In
the closed dust model everything is guaranteed from every event after
maximum expansion, the known moment at which the particle horizon
disappears; before it, the same scalar field is already fixed on an
antipodal echo of the past. Other local laws fix more: one with a preferred
flow fixes everything even in Minkowski spacetime. So unreality does not
entail openness, and how much of the unreal future is fixed from an event
depends on global causal structure, which can change with cosmic epoch, and
on the fields, which relativity alone does not decide. A closing remark
records what the corresponding standpoint logic does and does not show about
cosmological horizons.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Stein's theorem says that in Minkowski spacetime a transitive,
symmetry-invariant relation of becoming that makes some past event definite
is the causal order or trivial \citep{Stein1968,Stein1991,CH1995}. A
companion paper \citep{SteinGR} extends this to general relativity under a
locality principle: what has become for an event $b$ always includes its
chronological past and never goes beyond its causal past. On views that read
``has become for $b$'' as ``is real from $b$'' \citep{Fine2005,Lipman2023},
the future of $b$ is not real from $b$.

It is tempting to infer that the future is \emph{open} from $b$. The
inference fails, and the way it fails is instructive. Openness in the sense
relevant here is a matter of what the real fixes, and that depends on the
laws and on the global causal structure. The point is old in outline: a
growing block \citep{Broad1923} combined with deterministic laws makes the
future unreal yet fixed, and Rak\'ic's sea-fight case turns on an event
outside an observer's past that a decision inside it appears to settle
\citep{Rakic1997}. What is missing is a precise account, for relativistic
spacetimes, of when what is real from an event fixes anything beyond itself.
Geroch's domain of prediction supplies the tool \citep{Geroch1977}, and
Manchak's work on prediction its limits \citep{Manchak2008}. We use them to
answer three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which events does every deterministic local law fix from what is real
from $b$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:char})?
\item In the standard cosmological models, how much is that, and how much
more can a particular field fix (Section~\ref{sec:examples})?
\item What follows for the relation between unreality and openness
(Section~\ref{sec:phil})?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Conventions.} Spacetimes are smooth, connected, time-oriented and
globally hyperbolic. $\Is(p)$ is the strict chronological past (via timelike
curves), $J^-(p)$ the causal past; $x\le y$ means $x\in J^-(y)$, and $x\ll y$
means $x\in\Is(y)$. We use that $\le$ is closed, that $J^-(x)\cap S$ is
compact for every Cauchy surface $S$, push-up ($y\le z\ll b$ implies $y\ll b$),
and that $M=\Is(S)\cup S\cup\Isp(S)$ for a Cauchy surface $S$ \citep{MS2008}.
A \emph{Cauchy time function} is a continuous surjection $t:M\to(\alpha,\beta)$,
$-\infty\le\alpha<\beta\le\infty$, strictly increasing along future-directed
causal curves, whose level sets $S_c=t^{-1}(c)$ are Cauchy surfaces
\citep{BS2005}.

\section{Determined from an event}\label{sec:det}

\begin{definition}
An event $x$ is \emph{guaranteed from} $b$ if every past-inextendible causal
curve from $x$ meets $\Is(b)$. Write $\Det(b)$ for the set of such events.
Clearly $\Is(b)\subseteq\Det(b)$.
\end{definition}

$\Det(b)$ is a property of the spacetime and the event. To connect it with
what the laws fix, let $\mathcal L$ be the set of solutions of some field
equations on $M$. Say that $x$ is \emph{fixed from $b$ under $\mathcal L$},
and write $x\in F_{\mathcal L}(b)$, if any two solutions in $\mathcal L$ that
agree on $\Is(b)$ agree at $x$. Say that $\mathcal L$ has the
\emph{domain-of-dependence property} if, for every open $U\subseteq M$, any two
solutions that agree on $U$ agree at every event all of whose
past-inextendible causal curves meet $U$. This is a locality assumption on
the dynamics: nothing propagates outside the light cones.

\begin{remark}[What is guaranteed and what is fixed]\label{rem:laws}
If $\mathcal L$ has the domain-of-dependence property and consists of
continuous fields, then
\[\Det(b)\cup J^-(b)\subseteq F_{\mathcal L}(b),\]
since $J^-(b)$ is the closure of $\Is(b)$ in a globally hyperbolic spacetime
\citep{MS2008}. So $\Det(b)$ is fixed under every such law: it is what the
causal structure guarantees, whatever the fields. The inclusion is in general
strict, and nothing about $F_{\mathcal L}(b)$ beyond it follows from $\Det(b)$
being small. For example, on Minkowski spacetime with the unit timelike
field $u=\partial_t$, the local equations $u\cdot\nabla Q=P$,
$u\cdot\nabla P=0$ have the solutions $Q=A(\vec X)+tB(\vec X)$, $P=B(\vec X)$
for arbitrary smooth $A,B$. Their characteristics are the integral curves of
$u$, so they have the domain-of-dependence property; and every integral curve
of $u$ enters $\Is(b)$, so $F_{\mathcal L}(b)=M$ for every $b$. A law of this
kind uses background structure beyond the metric, but nothing in the
domain-of-dependence property excludes it. Neither notion is Belnap's
settledness, on which a proposition is settled at a point event if it holds
in every possible history through it \citep{Belnap1992}; under
indeterministic laws they come apart from it, and we say nothing about such
laws. We use $\Is(b)$, which every local becoming theory counts as real from
$b$, rather than $J^-(b)$; the characterization below uses that $\Is(b)$ is
open.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Relation to Geroch.} Geroch's domain of prediction $P(q)$ is the set
of $x$ such that every past-directed timelike curve from $x$ without past
endpoint enters $I^-(q)$, but $I^-(x)\not\subseteq I^-(q)$ \citep[sec.~3]{Geroch1977}.
$\Det(b)$ is the causal-curve analogue of the union of $P(b)$ with the
events whose pasts lie in that of $b$; the two differ at most on null
boundaries. Geroch gives an equivalent form by achronal surfaces in $I^-(q)$
\citep[sec.~3]{Geroch1977}; Theorem~\ref{thm:char} is a slicing form for
globally hyperbolic spacetimes.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:char}
Let $t:M\to(\alpha,\beta)$ be a Cauchy time function. Then $x\in\Det(b)$ if
and only if there is $c\in(\alpha,t(x))$ with $J^-(x)\cap S_c\subseteq\Is(b)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) A past-inextendible causal curve from $x$ crosses the Cauchy
surface $S_c$, at a point of $J^-(x)\cap S_c\subseteq\Is(b)$.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose that for every $c\in(\alpha,t(x))$ the set
$K_c=(J^-(x)\cap S_c)\setminus\Is(b)$ is nonempty; it is compact, since
$\Is(b)$ is open. Fix $t(x)>c_1>c_2>\dots$ with $c_n\to\alpha$, and let
\[P_n=\{(z_1,\dots,z_n)\in K_{c_1}\times\dots\times K_{c_n}:
x\ge z_1\ge z_2\ge\dots\ge z_n\}.\]
$P_n$ is compact, since $\le$ is closed. It is nonempty: pick $y\in K_{c_n}$
and a causal curve from $x$ to $y$; it crosses each $S_{c_i}$, $i<n$, at some
$z_i\le x$, and $z_i\notin\Is(b)$, for otherwise $y\le z_i\ll b$ would give
$y\ll b$. The maps $P_{n+1}\to P_n$ forgetting the last coordinate are
continuous, so the inverse limit of the nonempty compact spaces $P_n$ is
nonempty, giving $x\ge z_1\ge z_2\ge\cdots$ with $z_n\in K_{c_n}$. Join
consecutive terms by causal curves and concatenate. Each segment avoids
$\Is(b)$ by push-up, as before. Along the concatenation $t\to\alpha$, so it has
no past endpoint in $M$: it is a past-inextendible causal curve from $x$
missing $\Is(b)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cauchy}
If $\Is(b)$ contains a Cauchy surface $S$, then $\Det(b)=M$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\Is(S)\cup S$, then $x\le s\ll b$ for some $s\in S$, so $x\in\Is(b)$.
If $x\in\Isp(S)$, extend a past-inextendible causal curve from $x$ to an
inextendible one; it meets $S$, and not to the future of $x$, since then
$s'\ll x\le s$ for some $s,s'\in S$, contradicting achronality. So the curve
from $x$ meets $S\subseteq\Is(b)$.
\end{proof}

Since $\Det(b)$ is defined by causal curves alone, it depends only on the
conformal class of the metric; conformal diagrams decide it.

\section{The standard models}\label{sec:examples}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:balls}
Let $(\Sigma,d)$ be $\mathbb R^3$, hyperbolic space $H^3$, or the unit sphere
$S^3$ with $r_2\le\pi$. Then the closed ball $\bar B(X,r_1)$ lies in the open
ball $B(Y,r_2)$ if and only if $d(X,Y)+r_1<r_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) is the triangle inequality. ($\Rightarrow$) Let $r_1\ge0$ and suppose
$d(X,Y)+r_1\ge r_2$. On $\mathbb R^3$ and $H^3$, where geodesics extend
indefinitely and minimize, or on $S^3$ when $d(X,Y)+r_1\le\pi$, follow a minimizing geodesic from $Y$ through $X$ (any geodesic from
$X$ if $X=Y$) a further distance $r_1$; the endpoint lies in $\bar B(X,r_1)$ at
distance $d(X,Y)+r_1\ge r_2$ from $Y$. If $d(X,Y)+r_1>\pi$ on $S^3$, the
antipode of $Y$ is at distance $\pi-d(X,Y)<r_1$ from $X$ and $\pi\ge r_2$ from
$Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conformal}
Let $M$ be conformal to $(\eta_-,\eta_+)\times\Sigma$ with metric
$-d\eta^2+d\Sigma^2$, where $\Sigma$ is $\mathbb R^3$, $H^3$, or the unit $S^3$
with $\eta_+-\eta_-\le\pi$. Then $\Det(b)=\Is(b)$ for every $b$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use $t=\eta$. For $x=(\eta_x,X)$, $b=(\eta_b,Y)$: $x\ll b$ iff
$d(X,Y)<\eta_b-\eta_x$ (on $S^3$, $\eta_b-\eta_x<\pi$). For $\eta_-<c<\eta_x$,
$J^-(x)\cap S_c=\bar B(X,\eta_x-c)$ and $\Is(b)\cap S_c=B(Y,\eta_b-c)$, with
$\eta_b-c<\pi$ on $S^3$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} the inclusion holds iff
$d(X,Y)<\eta_b-\eta_x$, i.e.\ iff $x\ll b$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:char}.
\end{proof}

This covers Minkowski spacetime, spatially flat and open FLRW universes with
a big bang (conformal to $\eta>0$), the closed radiation universe
($a\propto\sin\eta$, $\eta\in(0,\pi)$) and global de Sitter spacetime ($\Sigma=S^3$,
$\eta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$) \citep{HE1973}. In none of them is anything outside an
event's chronological past guaranteed from it. In Minkowski spacetime and the
spatially flat universes this bound is attained by a natural field.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sharp}
Let $M=(\eta_-,\eta_+)\times\mathbb R^3$ with $g=a(\eta)^2(-d\eta^2+d\vec X^2)$,
$a>0$ smooth, and let $\mathcal L$ be the smooth solutions of the conformally
coupled scalar equation $\Box_g\phi-\tfrac16R\phi=0$. Then
$F_{\mathcal L}(b)=J^-(b)$ for every $b$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$J^-(b)\subseteq F_{\mathcal L}(b)$ by continuity, as in
Remark~\ref{rem:laws}. Conversely, $\phi$ is a solution iff $\psi=a\phi$
solves the flat wave equation $\partial_\eta^2\psi=\Delta\psi$ on $M$
\citep[app.~D]{Wald1984}. Let $b=(\eta_b,\vec Y)$ and $x=(\eta_x,\vec X)\notin
J^-(b)$, so $\eta_x-\eta_b+|\vec X-\vec Y|>0$, since the causal curves of $M$
are those of the conformal Minkowski metric. Choose a unit vector $\hat k$
with $\hat k\cdot(\vec X-\vec Y)=-|\vec X-\vec Y|$, a smooth $f$ vanishing on
$(-\infty,0]$ and positive on $(0,\infty)$, and put $\psi=f(u)$ with
$u=\eta-\eta_b-\hat k\cdot(\vec Z-\vec Y)$ at $(\eta,\vec Z)$. This solves the
wave equation. On $\Is(b)$, $\eta-\eta_b<-|\vec Z-\vec Y|$, so $u<0$ and
$\psi=0$; at $x$, $u>0$ and $\psi\neq0$. So $\psi/a$ and $0$ are solutions that
agree on $\Is(b)$ but not at $x$.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dust}
Let $M$ be conformal to $(\eta_-,\eta_+)\times S^3$ with $\eta_+-\eta_->\pi$.
Then $\Det(b)=\Is(b)$ if $\eta_b\le\eta_-+\pi$, and $\Det(b)=M$ if
$\eta_b>\eta_-+\pi$. In the Einstein static universe ($\eta_\pm=\pm\infty$),
$\Det(b)=M$ for every $b$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\eta_b>\eta_-+\pi$, take $c\in(\eta_-,\eta_b-\pi)$: $\Is(b)\cap S_c$ is an open
ball of radius greater than $\pi$, hence all of $S_c$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:cauchy} applies. If $\eta_b\le\eta_-+\pi$, every $c>\eta_-$
has $\eta_b-c<\pi$, and the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conformal} applies.
\end{proof}

For the closed dust FLRW model, $a(\eta)=\tfrac12a_m(1-\cos\eta)$ with
$\eta\in(0,2\pi)$, the threshold is $\eta=\pi$, the moment of maximum
expansion. That the particle horizon of the closed dust model disappears at
maximum expansion, so that an observer can then in principle see the whole
universe, is known \citep{GronJohannesen2011,BolotinTanatarov2013}; the
Einstein static case is Geroch's \citep[Fig.~5]{Geroch1977}. What
Proposition~\ref{prop:dust} adds is the consequence for determination: with
Corollary~\ref{cor:cauchy}, the vanishing of the particle horizon is exactly
the condition under which everything is guaranteed from $b$, so that every
law with the domain-of-dependence property fixes everything from what is real
from $b$. This is consistent with Manchak's result that prediction of events
to the future of an observer requires a compact Cauchy surface
\citep{Manchak2008}.

Before maximum expansion a particular field can fix more than is guaranteed.
Light leaving a point of the closed model refocuses at its antipode after
conformal time $\pi$, and the conformally coupled scalar carries its value
there exactly.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:echo}
Let $M$ be the closed dust model, $g=a(\eta)^2(-d\eta^2+d\Omega_3^2)$ with
$a=\tfrac12a_m(1-\cos\eta)$, $\eta\in(0,2\pi)$, and $\mathcal L$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}. Let $A(\eta,X)=(\eta+\pi,-X)$, where $-X$ is the
antipode of $X$. Then $J^-(b)\cup\bigl(A(J^-(b))\cap M\bigr)\subseteq
F_{\mathcal L}(b)$. If $\eta_b<\pi$, the set $A(J^-(b))\cap M$ is nonempty and
disjoint from $\Det(b)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $\psi=a\phi$ solves the conformally invariant equation of the Einstein
static universe, $\partial_\eta^2\psi=\Delta\psi-\psi$ on the unit $S^3$
\citep[app.~D]{Wald1984}, and extends uniquely to $\mathbb R\times S^3$.
Expanding in spherical harmonics of degree $n$, which have frequency $n+1$
and parity $(-1)^n$ under the antipodal map, each mode, and so every smooth
solution, satisfies $\psi(\eta+\pi,-X)=-\psi(\eta,X)$. Hence two solutions
that agree on $J^-(b)$ agree on $A(J^-(b))\cap M$. If $\eta_b<\pi$, then
$\Det(b)=\Is(b)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:dust}, which lies in
$\{\eta<\eta_b\}$, while $A(J^-(b))$ lies in $\{\eta>\pi\}$ and meets $M$, for
instance at $A(\eta_b/2,Y)$ when $b=(\eta_b,Y)$.
\end{proof}

So the geometric threshold at maximum expansion marks when everything is
guaranteed, not when this field first fixes anything beyond the past.
Whether $F_{\mathcal L}(b)$ is exactly the set in Proposition~\ref{prop:echo}
for $\eta_b\le\pi$, and whether in the closed models some local law fixes no
more than $\Det(b)\cup J^-(b)$, we leave open.

\section{Unreality and openness}\label{sec:phil}

Put together with the companion theorem, these results separate three
properties of an event $x$ relative to a standpoint $b$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{real from $b$}: $x$ is in $b$'s fragment, which a local becoming
theory fixes to lie between $\Is(b)$ and $J^-(b)$;
\item \emph{guaranteed from $b$}: $x\in\Det(b)$, so that every law with the
domain-of-dependence property fixes $x$ from what is real from $b$;
\item \emph{fixed from $b$ under given laws}: $x\in F_{\mathcal L}(b)$.
\end{itemize}
Every real event is fixed under any continuous law with the
domain-of-dependence property, and every guaranteed event is fixed under
each such law. The standard models exhibit two geometric regimes: in the
flat, open, radiation and de Sitter models nothing beyond the fragment is
guaranteed; in the closed dust model after maximum expansion, and in the
Einstein static universe, everything is. So:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Unreality does not entail openness.} An event can be unreal from
$b$ and guaranteed from $b$: in the closed dust model, every future event is
so from every event after maximum expansion. For particular fields this
happens more widely: the conformal scalar in the closed dust model before
maximum expansion (Proposition~\ref{prop:echo}), and the flow law of
Remark~\ref{rem:laws} even in Minkowski spacetime. This is the relativistic
form of the growing block combined with determinism, and on our reading it is
also where Rak\'ic's intuition belongs \citep{Rakic1997}: her sea-fight is, at
most, fixed from the observer, not real from her.
\item \emph{Determinism does not entail fixedness from each event.} Under
deterministic laws the whole spacetime is fixed by data on any Cauchy
surface, but no single event's past contains a Cauchy surface in the flat,
open, radiation or de Sitter models. For the conformal scalar in Minkowski
spacetime and the flat universes, what is fixed from $b$ is then exactly
$J^-(b)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}): from each standpoint the future is
unfixed as well as unreal. In the open, closed radiation and de Sitter models
we have shown only that nothing beyond the past is guaranteed.
\item \emph{How much of the unreal future is fixed depends on two things.}
Global causal structure decides what is guaranteed, and that can change with
cosmic epoch, so that the answer to ``is the future open from here?'' can
differ between two events of the same spacetime. The fields decide what is
fixed beyond that. Relativity settles the first; it does not settle the
second.
\end{enumerate}

Three limits. First, this openness is relational and informational: an event
outside $\Det(b)$ is still fixed by its own past, and a determinist eternalist
can accept everything in this section. What the section shows is that the
standpoint theorist's claim that the future is unreal from $b$ neither
secures nor excludes its being fixed from $b$; an ontically open future needs
indeterministic laws, about which $\Det(b)$ and $F_{\mathcal L}(b)$ are
silent. Second, the closed
dust result concerns an idealized model with deterministic, classical
dynamics; it is not a claim about our universe, which on current evidence is
not of that kind. Third, Manchak shows that an observer who does not already
know the spacetime cannot verify such predictions \citep{Manchak2008};
$\Det(b)$ and $F_{\mathcal L}(b)$ are nomological notions given $M$, not
epistemic ones.

\section{A remark on standpoint logic}\label{sec:logic}

Read $\Box A$ at $b$ as ``at every event $x\le b$, $A$'' on the frame $(M,R)$
with $bRx$ iff $x\le b$. If $A$ is a tenseless claim about what occurs at $x$,
this is the reflexive past modality of Diodorean tense logic, and the
standpoint reading of Fine and Lipman \citep{Fine2005,Lipman2023} adds no
formal content. $R$ is reflexive and transitive, so S4 is valid, and in
Minkowski spacetime the valid formulas are exactly those of S4.2
\citep{Goldblatt1980}. The axiom .2, $\Diamond\Box A\to\Box\Diamond A$, is valid at a
world for all valuations iff any two of its successors have a common
successor \citep{BdRV2001}. So .2 is valid at $b$ iff the subframe generated
by $b$ is directed, that is, iff any two events in $J^-(b)$ have a common
causal past.

In a model conformal to $(\eta_-,\eta_+)\times\mathbb R^3$, two events have a
common past iff $|X-Y|<\eta_x+\eta_y-2\eta_-$, so if $\eta_->-\infty$ the
directedness fails in the past of every event and .2 is valid at no event. This is the particle-horizon structure of Rindler
\citep{Rindler1956} in modal form, and it holds equally with or without an
inflationary era of finite conformal duration. The horizon problem of
cosmology \citep{Guth1981} is therefore not the failure of .2, which occurs
with and without inflation, but the failure of particular instances: pairs of points
on our last-scattering surface, at comoving separation up to
$2(\eta_0-\eta_{\rm ls})$, have no common past unless
$\eta_0-\eta_{\rm ls}<\eta_{\rm ls}-\eta_-$, which fails without inflation and
holds with enough of it. Two cautions. Under the view's persistence principle,
on which what holds from an event holds from every later one, valuations are
constrained along the future direction, and the frame property that matters
may concern common futures, which these models have when $\eta_+=\infty$; the logic of persistent
valuations is a separate question. And Lewis and van der Schaaf study causal
modal logics of general spacetimes \citep{vdSchaaf2026}; we have not checked
how their results bear on the past-directed frames used here. Whether S4 is
exactly the logic of all past-directed globally hyperbolic frames we leave
open.

\section{Conclusion}

The unreality of the future from an event, which local theories of
relativistic becoming deliver, neither implies nor excludes its being fixed
from that event. What every local deterministic law fixes is decided by a
slicing condition on Geroch's domain of prediction (Theorem~\ref{thm:char}),
computable from conformal diagrams. In most standard models nothing beyond an
event's past is guaranteed, and in Minkowski spacetime and the flat universes
a conformal scalar field shows that nothing more need be fixed; in the closed
dust model everything is guaranteed from every event after maximum
expansion. Particular fields can fix more than is guaranteed, in the closed
dust model before maximum expansion and, with a preferred flow, even in
Minkowski spacetime. The question whether the future is open from a
standpoint thus divides into a question about global causal structure, which
relativity settles, and a question about the fields, which it does not.
