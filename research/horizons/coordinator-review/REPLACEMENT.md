## Abstract / introductory question

> We characterize the region geometrically guaranteed to be determined by an
> event's chronological past under a domain-of-dependence assumption on the
> laws. In globally hyperbolic spacetimes, membership is equivalent to the
> containment of a suitable earlier Cauchy-slice section of the target's causal
> past inside the standpoint's chronological past. In the standard models
> considered, this region either coincides with the strict chronological past
> or becomes the whole spacetime. In closed dust the latter occurs after maximum
> expansion. The geometric criterion is sufficient for field determination;
> its failure does not establish that a particular law leaves the fields
> undetermined. Conditional on a local becoming interpretation, the positive
> result illustrates how an unreal future can nevertheless be fixed by what
> is real from a standpoint.

## Philosophy: replace the three numbered conclusions

> after maximum expansion, the standpoint's chronological past contains a

## Conclusion

> The geometric slicing theorem identifies a sufficient domain of field
> determination from an event's chronological past. Its extent depends on
> global causal structure and, in closed dust, changes with cosmic epoch.
> When that past contains a Cauchy surface, deterministic dynamics with the
> assumed domain-of-dependence property fix fields throughout spacetime.
> Combined with the companion becoming assumptions, this supplies a case of
> an unreal yet fixed future. Outside the geometric domain, questions of
> law-relative determination remain open to analysis; causal geometry alone
> does not settle them. Neither result establishes metaphysical openness or
> an observer's practical ability to predict.
