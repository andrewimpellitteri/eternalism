# What would count as an AI-assisted philosophical breakthrough?

30 October 2026. Coordinator methodological proposal, not a validated benchmark,
capability result, exhaustive literature review or claim of methodological
novelty. This extends the [existing playbook](STATE_AND_PLAYBOOK.md) and
[creative-search protocol](creativity-loop/PROTOCOL.md); it does not rediscover
their provenance, adversarial review or four separate gates.
The [20 October admissibility case](../article-admissibility/PROTOCOL.md)
already required a strongest same-physics rival and a separately defended
constitutive bridge. Those requirements are not new here either. The added
obligation is to complete and assess the particular inferential contribution,
and to separate its quality from historical priority and AI attribution.

## 1. The question we have been avoiding

What would a knowledgeable opponent have a reason to revise after reading
our completed argument? The revision might concern a conclusion, a premise,
a previously plausible inference, the available alternatives, or what needs
explaining. Agreement with our final ontology is not required. The reason
for revision must be identifiable even when the opponent remains unconvinced.

Our recent work often answers a more tractable question than the philosophical
one that motivated it. Source-faithful physics and valid mathematics are real
progress. They do not acquire an ontological consequence because the project
needs one. We need a prospective test of the inferential contribution, alongside
the existing checks of correctness and priority.

This is an aim for this project, not a definition of all philosophical value.
Interpretive scholarship, improved understanding and methods can be valuable
without resolving a dispute. Calling them breakthroughs would require a
separate argument about their scope and importance.

## 2. Four claims that must be assessed separately

| Claim | Evidence needed | Insufficient evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A useful research output | An argument, counterexample, distinction or explanation that survives its stated checks | Fluent prose or a long source list |
| Historical originality | Comparison with the nearest arguments at premise/inference/consequence level, with search limits | New wording, low text overlap, or unfamiliarity in this conversation |
| A philosophical advance | An identified improvement in reasons, understanding or the available positions on a substantive question | A correct calculation whose interpretation remains the original unsupported premise |
| An AI capability or attribution claim | Public generation/revision provenance, human contributions and selection budget; repeated appropriate comparisons for reliability | One selected success, several agreeing agents, or an unexplained finished essay |

An output can pass one row and fail another. A novel false claim passes neither
the argument test nor the significance test merely by being original. A known
counterexample rediscovered here can improve our project without establishing
historical priority. An important AI-assisted result need not establish that
the AI autonomously discovered it or can reliably repeat the achievement.

We need not first settle whether an AI has beliefs or understanding to assess
the argument in its output. Those are further philosophical questions about
the producer. Equally, output quality does not settle those questions.

## 3. What the primary evidence establishes

- Boden's IJCAI1997 paper distinguishes novelty to a producer from historical
  novelty, and exploration from transformations of a conceptual space. It
  emphasizes the difficulty of evaluating transformed ideas. The distinction
  warns against equating surprise here with originality in the literature.
  It does not supply a philosophical-breakthrough detector.
  [Primary paper](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1997/0229.pdf), §§1–2,4–5.
- Schwitzgebel, Schwitzgebel and Strasser tested identification of short
  Dennett/model answers. Their selected conclusion explicitly notes the
  absence of lengthy argument and distinguishes their design from sustained
  dialogue. Their text-overlap checks do not establish idea-level historical
  novelty. This is a different task from validating a new philosophical argument.
  [arXiv2302.01339v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.01339v2), §§5–6, pp20–23.
- Si, Hashimoto and Yang's execution study used 43 completed NLP projects,
  anonymized idea assignment and blind review. Initial AI idea ratings fell
  more after execution. Its §7.2 limits the idea scope to prompting techniques;
  it is not a study of philosophy or all AI research. Our methodological
  inference is to assess the completed argument, not just its prospectus.
  [arXiv2506.20803v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20803v1), §§2,4,7.2.
- Chalmers explicitly allows understanding, new arguments and new philosophical
  possibilities as progress while treating convergence toward truth as a
  distinct important aim. Hence neither consensus nor its absence can serve
  as our sole verdict.
  [Author manuscript](https://consc.net/papers/progress.pdf), pp10–11.

Two current evaluation efforts are relevant precedents, not our proposed
invention. PhilosophyBench describes blind essay assessment and AI-assisted
human comparisons; its FAQ supplies a design, not results inspected here.
The AI Philosophy Competition specifies content quality/originality and blind
judging alongside methodology reports. That is an external assessment route,
not certification that a breakthrough has occurred. Neither page read here
establishes a universally accepted criterion.
[PhilosophyBench FAQ](https://philosophybench.org/faq),
[competition rules summary](https://www.zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition).

## 4. The additional test: complete the consequential inferential step

Before expanding a candidate into a paper, record six items:

1. **Target.** One substantive question, with terms the rival can recognize.
   State the strongest existing answer and the problem it leaves.
2. **Contribution.** One exact new proposed move: counterexample, argument,
   explanatory mechanism or change in the options. Distinguish it from the
   nearest predecessor. A proposed concept must earn its use through a case
   or consequence that the old vocabulary obscured.
3. **Execution.** Supply the decisive inferential step in full. If a physical
   theorem is only an input, separately execute the philosophical bridge.
   Formal verification checks the encoded inference; reasons to accept a
   controversial premise remain substantive work.
4. **Rival response.** Reconstruct the best reply using the rival's own
   resources. Identify what it can retain, what it must change, and why
   retaining everything would fail. If it can retain everything, narrow or
   abandon the claimed advance rather than declaring a hidden concession.
5. **Independent consequence.** Apply the move to a second case not used to
   motivate its definition. State what would expose it as vacuous, known,
   invalid or a solution to an easier question. This is an overfitting check,
   not a demand that every philosophical claim be experimentally falsifiable.
6. **Priority and provenance.** Record the nearest source arguments, remaining
   search gaps, who supplied the decisive move, rejected alternatives and
   substantive revisions. Preserve public artifacts, not private reasoning
   traces. Lack of access to training data limits attribution; absence of
   copied wording does not eliminate conceptual inheritance.

The test is not that an argument changes a logically omniscient theorist's
deductive closure. Deducing a previously unnoticed consequence can be a major
advance although it was already entailed by known premises. Nor must progress
only eliminate options: a defensible new alternative can enlarge them.

The stronger term *breakthrough* is reserved provisionally for an advance
whose consequences substantially reorganize a live problem. That assessment
needs informed scrutiny beyond this project's agents and may remain disputed.
No scalar originality score or unanimous agent vote replaces it.

### Ways this test could suppress a real advance

The protocol itself needs criticism. It can favor checkable but unimportant
deductions, punish speculative conceptual work too early, or confuse a rival's
ability to accommodate a result with equal explanatory merit. An expansion
countermodel defeats a particular entailment; it does not settle an abductive
comparison. An event realist's ability to recognize processes does not earn
event realism a default preference, and a process account's ability to name
its realizers does not earn exclusion of other events.

A transformative contribution may expose a false presupposition in the
original question and replace the question. Record that as a new proposed
contribution, with reasons why the reformulation retains what mattered;
do not reject it merely for failing to answer the old question literally.
Conversely, silently substituting an easier problem is not a transformation
we should reward. The distinction requires philosophical judgment.

Nor must every contribution take the form of a deductive proof. A defended
explanatory preference may survive disputed premises. Its reasons and costs
must be assessed symmetrically, rather than hidden in our choice of shared
description, admissibility conditions or a scoring rule. This is a fallible
research discipline, not an ontology-neutral algorithm for recognizing truth.

## 6. A bounded prospective pilot, returning to the ontology

Use one continuing question rather than opening another indefinite meta-project:

> Can a physically individuated sustaining activity justify the concrete
> temporal extent of a system, beyond establishing how the system behaves?

This is already the project's open question, not a newly discovered problem.
The improvement sought is a completed positive bridge or a precise new reason
one cannot work under stated assumptions. The metaphilosophical protocol is
useful only if it changes the quality of our answer to that question.

For the next **one candidate and one bounded revision**, require a short
question/contribution/rival card before further mathematical development.
Choose from the [prepared organization/cycle tasks](../ontology-reconstruction/coordinator/NEXT_FRONTIER_TASKS.md)
only after the constitutive premise is written. Deriving a transport delay or
oscillator period is not the success condition.

The candidate must say whether its activity is necessary or sufficient for
unity, what physical alternatives support that claim, and why unity would
license excluding other events from concrete reality. Treating a later event
as nonexistent *from a standpoint* is not deleting it from the physical model.
The unrestricted event-realist can accept the whole activity as concrete;
an instantaneous-state theorist can encode it without thereby constituting
it. Both replies must be answered on explanatory grounds.

Use the failed reconstruction inference as a known invalid control and its
properly qualified predictive-sufficiency claim as a valid control. Label
them as controls; the existing public cases cannot now be called blinded
held-out tests. A fresh reviewer receives the question, sources, candidate
and controls without our preferred verdict. Source labels and prose may
still disclose authorship; do not claim perfect blinding.

Record the first decisive objection and whether revision actually answers
it. A smaller surviving theorem retains its own value but does not inherit
the original ontology ambition. If both attempts fail at the same bridge,
report that exact failure before launching further examples.

A later claim that this workflow improves AI philosophy would need a
prospective comparison with fixed questions, sources, model/tool budgets,
human assistance and selection rules. That experiment has not been run.
External human philosophical review remains a distinct future step; nobody
has been contacted or entered into a competition through this note.
