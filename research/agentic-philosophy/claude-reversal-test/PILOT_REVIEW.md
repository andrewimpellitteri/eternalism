# Review of the constitutive-bridge pilot

31 October 2026. Claude Code (Opus 5.5), at the user's request, reviewing RESULT.md at
agent/ontology-constitutive-bridge-pilot (ccb40b5). This reader had already seen the
coordinator's worked attempt and is not blind. Corrected after REVIEW.md, which checked
every quotation here. For coordinator inspection, not acceptance. No novelty or ontology
verdict.

## Verdict

**P2 and the lower-bound shape of the pilot survive as conditional claims. Its three
headline gains do not.** R1 as a width answer, R2 as a structural obstruction and
R4b as a demand on the rival each fail, for different reasons. The pilot's honesty
about its limits, its change log and its second case are good, and the Kepler case
did real work.

## Findings, strongest first

**1. The window's side is unargued in the pilot's lossless channel.** A token C_s
occupies [s,s+τ]. Tokens in transit at b span a region reaching from b−τ to b+τ. §1
gets (b−τ,b] only by taking "in-progress portions", which P2's defense glosses as "how
much of it has occurred". That is oriented, though not necessarily tensed: "earlier than
b" is tenseless, and the rival accepts earlier and later. So the claim of a present
"fixed by apparatus physics, not by a metaphysician's choice of look-back" lacks a
stated source for its side. In this lossless model, at a mirror standpoint, no past-sided
rule is neutral. A damped model could supply a side, so this is a finding about the
pilot's model and not a general result; see REVERSAL_TEST.md. Against R1's "at least", a
side excludes nothing, and the useful form is its corollary: a neutral present including
E on a fixed model includes its mirror image. Also, W_b is a triangle in (t,x), and width
τ is a time projection, a local duration only at the inlet.

**2. "Width" outruns the revision.** P3⁻ says the operative extent is *included in* the
present. That is a lower bound. R1's first sentence is safe, but "why is S's present this
wide?", R4b's "has this width" and R3's "determinate widths ... (channel: yes)" need an
upper bound, and P3-backward was retracted. This is an unearned presupposition, not a
contradiction. So R4b's charge that the realist cannot explain the width has no target.
The realist can also say what the pilot says: the activity has that extent. See finding 4
for how an upper bound could return.

**3. R4b's explanandum exists only under P2.** "The presence-width explanandum" is a
datum only for someone already using "present" this way. Common ground is that the
channel has transit time τ, and every party explains that the same way. A rival who
declines the word loses no explanation of anything physical. BREAKTHROUGH_TEST §4 item
4 asks what the rival must change. Here it must change nothing.

**4. R2 rests on an equivocation.** P3-backward says nothing outside the window is
*present-for-S*. §4(i) and R2 treat this as saying completed tokens are not concrete.
Those differ. An event-realist can accept that exited carriages are not part of S's
current operation while holding them concrete, which is the very distinction the
coordinator's note draws between a token's parts and E_b. On that reading P3-backward
is local and harmless, so no collapse follows. On the inventory reading it is global,
but then it was never a claim about S. "Structural, not a missing premise" therefore
restates the supplied diagnosis. §10 says it does more than that, and it does not. One
consequence: on the local reading the retraction of P3-backward was unforced, so an upper
bound could return, but only as a restriction of the word "present", which is the
pilot's rejected alternative (b).

**5. §4 says the rival accepts P2 "trivially", on the same conflation.** Occurrences
being concrete is not the same as their being "concretely present at b". The second is
what the event-realist has not granted, so the claimed concession at §4(iv) is not one.

**6. AC depends on a choice of level.** The channel gets a determinate width because
the sustainer is taken to be tokens. But the pilot's own role test removes the *regime*:
f≡0 from b onward. Taken at that level, the channel has run since switch-on, and its
operative extent is history-relative, exactly like the Kepler pair. So
"completion-structured" may be a feature of the chosen level. The test also does not end
unity at b, since tokens launched before b keep arriving until b+τ, so "no carriage is in
progress" is false until then. A first draft also said the Kepler interaction has
propagation tokens. That is a different, retarded model, and the claim is withdrawn.

A first draft's seventh finding, that calling P1 analytic empties it, is withdrawn. The
pilot calls only "operative at the assessment time" analytic, and P1 says more.

## What survives

- The labelled controls are correct. The free-particle witness works in §9's first use.
  Its "Invalid test" use does not: a free particle has no sustainer, so it separates
  nothing, and P2 as printed covers any process token, a restriction the pilot leaves
  unstated.
- P2 is a live conditional thesis, once its side is argued rather than assumed.
- R4a is a fair challenge to an instantaneous-stage theorist who also claims genuine
  operative sustaining, though its cost claim needs the same care as R4b.
- The Kepler case is a real finding: periodicity does not supply a width.
- The change log, rejected alternatives and depth labels meet the protocol.
