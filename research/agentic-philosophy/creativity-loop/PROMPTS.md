# Reusable short prompts

Use with a coordinator-owned task and disjoint write scopes. Supply the frozen
question and actual evidence files. Public proof sketches are appropriate;
do not request or archive private thinking traces.

## Propose a different mechanism

“Keep the question and fixed assumptions exactly as given. Give two mechanisms
that differ in mathematical object or access model, not in wording. For each:
state the reformulation, object to construct, first cheap discriminating test,
strongest kill, required physical bridge, and known predecessor. One may be
high risk. Label conjecture and missing information; do not pretend an unknown
gate passed. Do not see or copy another proposer's conclusion.”

## Repair a failed idea

“Here is a candidate and the specific failing test. Preserve the failure.
Propose one child that changes exactly one representation or assumption.
What remains invariant? Does the repair answer the original question or a
different one? Give the smallest test of the repaired mechanism. Do not fix a
failure by silently weakening the claim.”

## Invent a useful auxiliary object

“Which auxiliary object would turn this obstruction into a standard problem?
Try a dual object, common dominating measure, accessible observable algebra,
or a minimal countermodel. Derive the map and the condition for its existence.
Find an example where that condition fails. Familiarity of the construction
is acceptable; originality and physical meaning will be judged separately.”

## Review the submitted artifact

“Judge this statement against the frozen question and stated evidence. Try to
produce one explicit counterexample before accepting it. Separate mathematical
validity, source correspondence, physical access and philosophical bridge.
Report a failing assumption or a proof obligation, not an overall persuasion
score. Check both the seeded wrong example and its clean counterpart. Do not
infer novelty from passing a checker.”

## Recombine branches

“Choose two different mechanisms from the archive, including a failed one if
useful. Name the exact fragment from each that survives. State one combined
candidate, which earlier failure it avoids and which new risk it introduces.
Retain the original cards and attribute the new mechanism to its actual
parents. Stop after one bounded test or state the next unresolved test.”
