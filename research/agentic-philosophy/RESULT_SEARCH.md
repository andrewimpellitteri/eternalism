# A bounded search for one philosophical result

31 October 2026. Operating plan for this project, not a validated discovery
method. Reuses the [creative-search protocol](creativity-loop/PROTOCOL.md)
and the [contribution criteria](BREAKTHROUGH_TEST.md). No new runner or
benchmark is needed. The immediate deliverable is a completed, reviewable
argument with a defensible delta, not another methodology essay.

## What changes in the workflow

The previous attempt asked for a positive bridge before establishing which
premise could plausibly bear it. Its [handback review](PILOT_REVIEW.md)
finds that the answer imported the disputed premise into its common ground,
changed the physical entity under discussion, and overstated a general
obstruction. That is useful feedback for choosing the next search unit.

The search unit is now a **live inferential hinge**: a specific published
claim or independently motivated premise whose success would change a
recognized problem. Each hinge must have a cheapest decisive test and a
named strongest alternative. We do not ask agents to produce a breakthrough
or reward them for calling a result novel.

Three result types are admissible: a new argument or counterexample bearing
on a live philosophical position; a defended explanatory preference under
explicit premises; or a useful conceptual revision with a consequence the
old formulation missed. A mathematical result is welcome when it carries
the philosophical burden, but is not mandatory. An exposition, familiar
corollary or repeated missing-premise diagnosis remains background work.

## The sequence

| Step | Work and stopping condition | Concrete output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Select a hinge | Read the actual primary argument. Name the gap, nearest three predecessors and why repairing or defeating it matters. Unknown priority stays unknown. | One question card, at most a page of core claims |
| 2. Generate distinct mechanisms | Up to three routes: defend the premise; construct a charitable countermodel; change the conceptual formulation. Two workers plus coordinator at most. | One candidate per route, decisive inference and first test |
| 3. Execute cheaply | Test source correspondence, quantifiers, limiting cases and entity identity before extra mathematics or broad literature. A definition that assumes the conclusion fails this test. | Exact witness, argument or named failure |
| 4. Challenge the survivor | Give a fresh reviewer the evidence and strongest rival without a preferred verdict. Check inference and source/priority separately. Mere rival compatibility does not decide explanatory merit. | One decisive objection or bounded surviving claim |
| 5. Make one substantive revision | Answer the objection by changing a named premise/mechanism and preserving the original. If the question changes, explain why the new question matters. | Parent/child argument and change log |
| 6. Require a second consequence | Apply it outside the motivating case. Check it survives and does more than rename a known fact. A wider result must prove the wider quantifiers. | Second case and scope limits |
| 7. Produce a referee packet | Only if the claim survives: exact claim, full argument, nearest-predecessor delta, rival reply, second case, falsifiers and provenance. | A 3–5 page core suitable for external criticism |

The coordinator selects by explicit reasons, not a weighted score or agent
vote. A promising route needs an identifiable consequence, a plausible
noncircular premise and a feasible defeating test. If one of those is absent,
state which, rather than compensating with impressive supporting calculations.

## Limits that keep the search honest

Round1: one candidate per mechanism. Round2: at most one targeted revision
for a survivor. No third round of relabelling the same missing premise.
Before starting another batch, produce a disposition for every route:
retain, narrow, known, false, or unresolved—with evidence. No numerical
grid unless it tests a specified mathematical question; no dependency installs.

Stop immediately on a known exact predecessor, a counterexample to the
central inference, or a stipulated conclusion. Retain any smaller useful
claim under its own description. Failure to settle temporal ontology does
not make a bounded result worthless; a bounded result does not inherit the
ontology ambition merely because it was motivated by it.

One round is a research attempt, not evidence of a discovery rate. Preserve
all candidates, failed tests, selection reasons, model versions and human
contributions. External human review and any publication or correspondence
remain later steps requiring a concrete packet and the user's authorization.

## First round now instantiated

The [round1 question card](result-search-round1/QUESTION.md) asks what
warrants a temporally extended present. It separates two plausible mechanisms
and a coordinator countermodel route. This is a bounded search across
different inferential hinges, not an instruction to combine incompatible
ontologies into one account.

**Occurrence route:** Does an ongoing process's genuine diachronic unity
require its elapsed parts to be concretely co-present? Test the disputed
premise against a primary process ontology that distinguishes temporally
extended constitution from phases that are no longer present. Produce an
independent defense or a substantive new counterexample, not another restatement
that a premise is missing.

**Modal route:** Does the published branching-time account guarantee a
nontrivial extended present under its written assumptions? Test dense
branching against the inference from a non-choice moment to a non-singleton
co-presentness class. A possible counterexample is only a candidate until
all axioms and prior work are checked. A branching-time finding is not
automatically a result about physically realistic relativistic histories.

**Coordinator route:** Review the returned pilot, fix the target entity
(apparatus, active operation or individual token), and construct the strongest
source-faithful rival. Track three different predicates: temporally constituting
a process, being concrete, and being present from a standpoint. A translation
that collapses them by definition does not resolve the disagreement.

First-round success is one surviving claim worth a referee packet. If none
survives, the output is the exact obstruction and the next changed question.
This process raises the standard for useful search; it cannot promise a result.

**Candidate ancestry matters.** The occurrence route is a continuation of
the failed pilot's P2, now facing a directly read competing process account.
It is not a fresh independent discovery. This is its one targeted recovery
attempt; if it repeats the same unsupported premise, stop that family.
The modal-density route comes from a different primary argument. Renaming
a branch never resets its revision allowance or erases a failed gate.
