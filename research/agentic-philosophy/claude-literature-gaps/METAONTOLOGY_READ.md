31 October 2026. Claude Code (Opus 5.5), at the user's request. The first version claimed
that E_b has three readings carrying three diagnoses, so the bridge's repeated failures
need not be one failure. **That claim is withdrawn.** REVIEW_METAONTOLOGY.md, a same-model
red-team, found it does not stand, and its project citations were rechecked here. This
note keeps what survives. No ontology verdict and no novelty claim.

**Later correction, from TOTALITY_READ.md.** Item 1 below needs a qualification: the
local-fragmentalism theorem derives an upper bound from a stipulated negative, so "every attempt"
holds for the pilot attempts only, and the supply of a first negative stays open. The event
realist's gap is a stronger lower bound where H is unique. The candidate list under "What this

## What was wrong

- **"The formalism presupposes H" fails.** A subset of a fixed outer set is also how
  variable-domain semantics represents the tensed view, and Sider tests presentist
  translations with such a model. Semantics for a view need not import its domain. The
  project's red-team recorded this reply on 25 October (05_logic_metametaphysics.md,
  model theory versus content). I read that report and did not use it.
- **"The project has not said what E_b is" is false.** RIVAL_PROFILE_AND_REGULARITY_BRIDGE.md
  §2 calls it a metalinguistic assignment, translating Correia and Rosenkranz's clauses,
  and PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md calls x a common mathematical label, and says E_b is not a
  completed unrestricted quantifier domain. That usage fits none of my three rows.
- **The argument was inconsistent.** It said the restricted party rejects sentences over H,
  then proved a result for both views using a structure on H.
- **The lemma pointed the other way.** The truth of an R-relativized sentence depends only
  on the reduct to R, on every reading. So it explains one failure, not several.
- **The table was not a partition.** "Predicate" and "domain" are logical forms, while
  "primitive" is a status. Fine, Williamson, the moving spotlight, Deasy, Gołosz,
  fragmentalism and TRILEMMA's N-form do not fit it.
- **Note 17 was over-read.** Sider reports what a skeptic would claim, and says it cannot be
  refuted on logical grounds alone. That is not support for a translation.
- **"Do not count further examples" did not follow.** It also conflicts with
  BREAKTHROUGH_TEST.md §4 item 5.
- **"No deductive argument from premises about R" was too broad,** as the next section shows.

## What survives

**1. One failure, on the evidence shown.** Every attempt so far supplies a lower bound on
E_b. An upper bound is missing, on any account of E_b. The accounts differ over what a repair
would be, not over why the attempts failed.

**2. A narrow logical point, with conditions.** If R is nonempty and closed under the
function symbols, parameters lie in R, and the language is first-order with no
index-shifting operators, then the truth of φ^R depends only on the reduct to R. So a
relativized premise cannot bound what lies outside R. This is close to Russell's point that
atomic truths leave open whether those are all the things. It is standard and nearly
trivial. It excludes only relativized premises.

**3. Premises the restricted view can accept are not excluded.** Unrelativized does not mean
stated over H. Example, as a form and not an endorsement, from the reviewer: P1, any two
events concrete from b interact; P2, whatever interacts with R is in R. With a shared member,
these entail that the concrete events lie in R, and P2 alone does not. So the target is a
**stated upper-bound principle**, and those can be examined one at a time.

**4. A narrower demand, which is the useful output.** Before an E_b sentence is used for an
existence conclusion, state:

- (a) whether it is a pure metalinguistic assignment or an applied claim;
- (b) its object-language form: constant domain with a predicate, tensed quantifier, operator,
  fragment-relative, or a claim across standpoints;
- (c) the status of the contrast: stipulated, defined with a role, or primitive.

An applied reading owes a reference clause, which PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md already records as a
shared debt. This is a refinement of existing practice, not a new finding.

**5. Gołosz changes which premise is at issue.** He extends Meyer's objection to eternalism,
which is a useful symmetry. His positive move, following Prior, treats "only the present
exists" as analytic and moves the thesis to primitive becoming, or dynamic existence. For
such a view exhaustiveness is not the dispute, and the contested premise is passage. So the
project's inventory framing fits some rivals better than others.

**6. Savitt and Dorato are nearby.** Savitt restates both views over one event set: an event
exists iff it occurs, against an event Exists iff it belongs to the set. On his account these
are compatible relative to a background spacetime, and he declines to call the matter merely
verbal. Dorato presses tensed and tenseless readings into a triviality-or-contradiction
dilemma, and asks what "more fundamental" could mean between them.

**7. Sider's limit.** Multiple indexing may lapse into eternalism, as he allows, but his
concern is object-language machinery. A metalanguage indexed by all times does not trigger it,
since a conservative presentist's Kripke model has that shape too.

## What this changes

Little for the coordinator. The demand in item 4 is cheap, and the project already mostly
meets it. The real next question is which stated upper-bound principle deserves examination.
Candidates are Armstrong-style totality, an interaction-closure principle like the Brading
line TRILEMMA.md quotes, Gołosz's becoming, and maximality-style principles. An example then
counts for or against a stated principle, never as evidence for exhaustiveness alone.

PILOT_QUESTION.md says a result must not merely repeat that more premises are needed. Items
1–4 largely do. Only an examination of a particular principle could do better.
