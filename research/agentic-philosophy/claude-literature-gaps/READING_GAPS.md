## Why these gaps matter

Four attempts stopped at one step: Codex's worked attempt, the GLM pilot, the unity test
and the cycle test. Each established how a system behaves or is organized, and none
reached a claim about what exists. That step has three parts, and each has a literature
the project has not used.

### 1. Which activity unifies? Composition and life

**van Inwagen, *Material Beings*, Cornell UP 1990.** As secondary sources report: the xs
compose y iff the activity of the xs constitutes a life, with a life an event that is
well-individuated, self-maintaining and jealous, and persistence following the life.

- Bears on: this looks like the project's "physically individuated sustaining activity",
  already developed, already criticized, and with an existing answer on what counts.
- "Jealous" may speak to the coordination-unity worry that weak couplings merge modules.
- Note a limit: it answers composition, not temporal inventory, and may not touch the
  exhaustiveness gap at all. The criticism of it should be read too, including Sider's
  gunk objection in *Analysis* 53(4).

### 2. "Nothing else is": totality and negative existentials

**Armstrong, *Truth and Truthmakers*, CUP 2004**, on totality states, with the reply
literature on negative truths, including Molnar 2000 and Merricks on minimality.

- Bears on: R⊆E_b gives a lower bound, and E_b=R adds "and that's all". That has the form
  of a totality claim. If so, the missing exhaustiveness principle is a known problem with
  known costs, for every party: E_b=H needs a totality claim too.
- This could turn "a premise is missing" into a costed comparison of which totality
  claim each ontology owes. That is a hypothesis, and the priced comparison is unbuilt.

### 3. What is "concrete from b"? Metaontology

- **Sider, "Quantifiers and Temporal Ontology", *Mind* 2006**, on whether the dispute is
  merely verbal. A PDF is on the author's site.
- **Fine, "The Question of Ontology", in *Metametaphysics*, OUP 2009**, as reported:
  ontology concerns what is real rather than what there is, and reality is primitive.
- **Chalmers, "Verbal Disputes", *Philosophical Review* 2011.**
- **Thomasson, *Ontology Made Easy*, OUP**, and Hirsch on quantifier variance.
- **Williamson, *Modal Logic as Metaphysics*, OUP 2013**, on permanentism, which may
  change what the dispute even is.

Bears on: the project's E_b has no independent definition. CONSTITUTIVE_GROUNDING_TEST.md
says so itself: "not an ontologically neutral definition". This is the most serious
gap. Repeated failure at the same bridge has at least three explanations, and the
project has only tested the first:

1. A premise is missing, and could be supplied.
2. A premise is missing, and no physical argument could supply it.
3. The contrast has no determinate content, so there is nothing for a bridge to reach.

Option 3 is live in the literature the project already cites, through Meyer, Savitt and
Dorato. It needs a direct test before more example systems are run. This is a
hypothesis, not a claim that the debate is verbal. Sider, as reported, argues it is not.

## Also relevant

**Stout, "The Category of Occurrent Continuants", *Mind* 125 (2016)**, arguing processes
endure, and **Steward, "Processes, Continuants, and Individuals", *Mind* 122 (2013)**,
opposing that. Baratella's reply is in *Dialectica* 74(3), 2020. These bear directly on the
pilot's P2 and its "occurrence-presence" category view, which the pilot supports only with
abstract-level process-ontology leads. "Fully present at each time it happens" is at issue
in that exchange, so P2 may be a contested position rather than a neutral category point.

**Schaffer, "Monism: The Priority of the Whole", *Philosophical Review* 119 (2010).** May
bear on whether a whole is prior to its temporal parts. A memory that it has a covering
constraint usable as an exhaustiveness principle could not be confirmed. Do not rely on it.

## What is not needed

More method. BREAKTHROUGH_TEST.md already covers progress and attribution, and adding to
it would be displacement activity. A broad survey is also unnecessary. Each read should
answer one question below.

## Proposed bounded reads, in order

1. **Metaontology first.** Sider 2006, then Meyer and the project's existing Savitt and
   Dorato readings. Question: can E_b be given content that makes option 3 false, and
   what does each answer imply for the pilot question?
2. **Totality.** Armstrong's totality chapter and two replies. Question: is exhaustiveness
   an instance of the totality problem, and what does each ontology owe?
3. **Composition.** van Inwagen on lives. Question: does the pilot's sustaining criterion
   add anything to it, and does it survive the known objections?
4. **P2.** Stout and Steward. Question: is occurrence-presence a neutral premise?

Each should report what a rival must revise, under the existing test. Full-text reads
require accessible copies; some of these are books and may be gated. If a read is only
possible at abstract depth, it must be labelled so.
