## Five strongest problems

**1. Earman's p.144 was halved.** After the denial that a growing-block theorist can deny the future
absolutely, he gives two claims true in every stage and necessarily true. The first is "The sufficiently
distant future is literally unreal", glossed with a shifting "now" (Earman 2008, p.144). The note reports
only the second, on the sum total. The first is a stage-invariant negative existential, which is the
form an upper bound takes. So p.144 distinguishes a rigid denial, which growth excludes, from a shifting
one, which it does not. The note's persistence observation then rediscovers the first half and
misdescribes the second. See items 1 and 3.

**2. "Indexed" does not mean "acceptable to both sides".** Tooley 2010, a source the note read, says that
on static views no sense can be given to a temporally indexed notion of actuality, and only the
absolute notions apply. RIVAL_PROFILE §5 says eternalism can reject the shared clause (P2R), which is
indexed. P1's reality-from-a-standpoint is indexed and is what the rival denies. Both sides accept the
*sentence* only because the rival reads the index as a defined restriction. The real contrast is
defined index against primitive index.

**3. §5 runs Loss's puzzle and Fine's puzzle together.** Rows 1–4 reject members of Loss's tetrad, or the
truth-value change it needs. Rows 5–6 are stances on Fine's quartet, and reject no tetrad member. A
standard realist who keeps maximalism, necessitation and permanence is still inconsistent. Relativizing
each clause to standpoints reproduces the contradiction at b′ (item 4 proof below).

**4. The exhaustiveness claim is not obviously the tetrad's target.** The tetrad fires only on a proposition
that changes truth value. "Everything is past or present" never does. Given that sentence, the rigid
claim at b is equivalent to "b is present", so the totality case adds nothing to Loss's original.

**5. Setting aside indeterminism hid the strongest candidate, and §3's asymmetry.** Earman reports the
Broadhead reply that if completion is not unique there is no fact about which block obtains (pp.144, 154,
159). That attacks H directly. It is in the note's main source, and §7 sets the topic aside.

**Is "absolute" faithful, and does Earman conflict with Fine's standard realism?** The note uses three
senses without saying so.

- Earman's "absolute" (p.144) means a denial tied to one truncated model taken as reality. His

- The note's §4 "absolute" is "'everything lies in R' said from b", which carries an index.

On its face Earman's claim conflicts with standard realism, which is absolute and tensed and holds that

parity-between-stages construal, and is not a general result. Deng §2 adds that his perspectivality is
unexplained. The note cites Earman in §2 as settled, and offers standard realism as exit 6 in §5, without
noticing that exit 6 is what Earman says is unavailable. If Earman is right as reported, Result 4's

## 2. The §3 table: REPAIR

**E_b⊆H is built into the frame. The note moves in one sentence from that to its being common ground.** It is
shared only under conditions, and not uniformly.

- **Pointy restricted view (E_b⊆J^-(b)).** Holds for every completion through b, by past-closure. This
  theorist needs no H at all, and accepts the row for all candidates at once.
- **Bow-tie or slice restricted view.** E_b includes spacelike events, and completions agreeing on b's
  causal past differ there (PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md; RIVAL_PROFILE §2 (BO)). So this theorist owes selection on
  that part, and "restricted views" do not share one row.
- **Branching, no thin red line.** Pointy views are fine: E_b lies in every history through b. There is no
  single H for the row to name.
- **McCall.** Direct counterexample, in two sources the note read (Earman p.136; Tooley 2010). All future
  branches are concrete and later attrit. E_b lies in no single history, and it shrinks.
- **Non-unique extension past a Cauchy horizon.** Maximal *globally hyperbolic* development is unique, and
  extension past a horizon is not. Earman's p.139 inextendibility is a different condition. If H is the
  MGHD, then "nothing concrete lies outside H" is a claim that the world is globally hyperbolic. The
  note's "where a maximal development is unique" hedges the sentence and not the table.
- **Rejecting H's reference.** If H's later labels denote nothing, then "nothing concrete lies outside H"
  says nothing concrete lies outside R_b. That is this theorist's thesis, not a weaker shared claim. The
  sentence is shared and the proposition is not. Earman p.144 and pp.154, 159 give the Broadhead this
  reply: block models are mythological, or exist only as ideal completions.
- **Fragmentalists.** Non-standard fragments need not cohere, so there is no one H (Deng §4;
  REVIEW_METAONTOLOGY's table). The official P1 avoids this. The fragmentalist does not.
- **Brute endings.** E_b⊆H still holds, but H is no longer the ever-existing history. The sentence "the
  later events are not rival to it" fails, since those events never occur.
- **Correia–Rosenkranz.** On the project's report (maximality note §7), later growth can fail. The row
  survives with the same fault as brute endings.

**"More will come, or the stage is an ended world."** REPAIR. As a disjunction it is close to excluded
middle, so it says nothing the event realist need deny. The coordinator's distinction is a demand to say
which, and a theorist may hold neither is settled. Three further failures:

- STRONGEST_VIEW §2b gives future-tensed claims outside the fragment no truth value from b, unless settled.
- A presentist also owes a claim about what lay earlier, so the row is not only about the future.
- CGT's R is one activity region, and may include events later than b. The note's "R_b" appears silently in
  §4. The two are different things, and §3 needs a definition.

**"Nothing further on this count."** REPAIR. Unfair outside unique H. The event realist owes which
completion is concrete, and why not a truncation. PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md records that designating the actual
realization is not derived from b's past. In the indeterministic and non-unique cases E_b⊆H is a negative
selection claim that the pointy restricted theorist does not owe, because that theorist does not need H.
So READING_GAPS.md §2 is not simply wrong. It fails as a differential claim only where H is unique and
accepted by both. "Withdrawn" is too strong.

Result 3 ("the two sides do not owe the same kind of claim") survives only on that condition.

## 3. The persistence observation: REPAIR

**Valid core.** Let a claim persist from b if holding at b implies holding at every later b′. Persisting
truths are closed under index-wise entailment. So a non-persisting claim has no persisting entailers. That
is the whole argument: one line, and the note's six sentences add only an instance.

**Hidden premises.**

1. **Rigidity of R_b. This decides the scope.** The conclusion must keep its content between b and b′, or x
   refutes nothing. Take the shifting form C*: everything is past or present, with "now" shifting. C* holds at
   every stage on a growing block and persists as a sentence, so it is entailed by itself. Earman p.144 and
   p.159 assert exactly this, and call it necessary. So "no persisting premises entail the absolute claim" is
   false of the claim a growing-block theorist actually makes. It is true only of the rigid instance.
2. **The domain at b′ includes x.** Stated, and fine. But "growth" is the wrong word. Replacement suffices,
   so the argument covers presentism, where nothing grows. It does not apply to an ended stage.
3. **The class of persisting premises.** "Tenseless physical facts and facts about what constituted a
   system persist" is too quick, and "on the restricted view's own terms" fails.
   - **Presentism.** Many presentists deny tenseless facts about non-present events. What survives is a
     past-tensed successor, which is a different claim from the present-tensed premise used at b. This is
     the tense shift COORDINATOR_AUDIT.md §1 distinguishes from persistence. So the observation does not
     exclude a presentist's present-tensed constitution premise.
   - **Totality-form constitution.** "R constituted S" persists. "R is all that constituted S" fails if the
     activity continues. So the sentence holds only of positive constitution facts.

**Decomposition worth keeping.** Let L_b be "b is now". C* and L_b together give the rigid claim. C* persists as
a sentence, and L_b does not. So the only non-persisting ingredient needed is self-location, which the block
theorist also accepts as an indexical truth. What the block theorist rejects is C* with an unrestricted
quantifier. This relocates the debt from "a tensed premise" to C*, and is a stronger result than the note's.

**Further scope the note omits.**

- Past-tensed conclusions persist. "As of b, x did not exist" is an upper bound on an earlier stage, stated
  later. It is Loss's (GB1) and Earman's "was smaller". The note's "leaves open" list omits it.
- Non-deductive support is untouched. A defeasible inference from persisting evidence can be defeated at b′.

**Is it just tenseless-to-tensed?** Not quite. The class includes past-tensed truths, so it is wider, and that
is its only residue. It is the same shape as a familiar feature of persistent Kripke semantics, where a
universal holds at a point only if it holds in all extensions, and of closed-world reasoning, which is
non-monotonic. Those two comparisons are from memory, unread here, and need checking.

**Worth stating?** In two lines, as the reason for Earman p.144's first half. Not as a contribution. Nobody
is asked to revise anything except someone arguing to the rigid claim from tenseless or past-tensed premises
alone, and the note names no such person. BREAKTHROUGH_TEST.md §4 item 4 says to narrow in that case.

## 5. The relativistic collapse: REJECT the reason, REPAIR the claim

**"Ceases has no frame-independent date": does not follow.** Ceasing needs a strict partial order, not a date.
Say a fact ceases at s if it holds at s and fails at every later stage. Tot(b) then ceases at b, in every chain
through b. That is an event, so it is frame-independent, and no global moment is needed. Each worldline gives a
chain with no foliation, which is how Earman builds worldline models (p.151). Row 1's "needs a linear order of
stages" is false for the same reason.

**Poset witness.** Take the stems of two unrelated elements: ∅, {a}, {b}, {a,b}. Tot({a}) holds at {a} and
fails at {a,b}. {a} and {b} are incomparable. No chain and no foliation enter.

**Partial-order growth.** Classical sequential growth is a chain of causets in labelled form (Earman p.155),
but stages are down-sets, not slices. Earman p.156 knows no proof that stage boundaries follow one time function.
Its proponents treat birth order as unphysical, which yields equivalence classes and an indeterminacy of
becoming (pp.156–158). So there are two cases. One actual chain (p.158) gives row 1 and row 6 with no foliation, at
the price of label-invariance. Equivalence classes give parity, and then external relativism or indeterminacy.

**"The sixth needs a foliation or a single event": too strong.** A single privileged past set suffices for the
present stage, and is neither. Its history needs a chain of past sets. In the continuum that is a weak foliation,
though it need not be smooth or spacelike. In the discrete case it is a linear extension. Single-event
privilege is one case (Hinchliff's point and cone models).

**Why the claim looks right, and why the collapse is not relativistic.** A cessation theorist must say where
"exists" is evaluated. One privileged point gives a Neutrality violation. No privileged point gives relative
evaluation. But that is true in Newtonian spacetime as well, once parity is assumed. Exit 1 is on Loss's axis
and exits 5–6 are on Fine's. They are orthogonal, so one cannot fall into another. A relativist still has
to choose: keep totality facts and cease them, index them, deny maximalism, or deny temporal necessitation.

**Relativizing does not exit the tetrad.** Let p be rigid. From b: p. Maximalism from b: some M makes p true.
Persistence: M exists from b′. Necessitation across standpoints: if M makes p true from b, then p holds from any
standpoint where M exists. Hence p from b′. Growth: not p from b′. All of this is at b′, so relativism gives no relief.
The standard realist's case is the same with tense operators. Each must deny a member.

**What survives.** Absent a privileged chain of stages, parity pushes a view to relativism. That is Deng's
tension between privilege and parity, not a finding about relativity.

## 6. Earman's "cheating" and identity across chips: REPAIR

**Fair as a flag. Aimed at the wrong target.**

- CGT's frame is a countermodel device. Parasitism on a block is harmless for showing an invalidity. Earman's charge
  concerns a block supplying the order, or identity, of a growing block's *own* model. Even he says only that this
  arouses suspicion and reduces attractiveness (p.145), and shows no incoherence.
- PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md's label x runs across *solutions* at fixed M. It does not run across stages, so it is not
  Earman's trans-n identity. The analogy is loose.
- The real site is inclusion across standpoints in one M: b≤c ⇒ E_b⊆E_c (RIVAL_PROFILE §2), and R3 and P5 of

## What survives

Totality gives no deductive premise for exhaustiveness, on any account of truthmakers, with Forrest as an unread
exception to "no other work". Where H is unique and accepted by both sides, the event realist's extra claim is a
stronger lower bound and not a second totality. Where it is not unique, the debt is the event realist's too. The
unindexed causal criterion does not restrict the future. The local-fragmentalism theorem turns an assumed bare negative
into a sharp bound on what has happened by b, and the indexed causal bound is that same bound. A rigid "that's all" cannot
persist under change of inventory, and for that reason cannot follow from persisting premises alone, which is half of
Earman p.144. The shifting form can persist, and the rigid instance needs only self-location beyond it. The growing-block
tetrad covers exhaustiveness only as Loss's own case. What does not survive is the claim that "indexed" sorts candidates, the
collapse of cessation into relativism as a relativistic result, and any suggestion that the note goes beyond PILOT_QUESTION.md.
The useful output is one diagnostic, defined index against primitive index, plus a shorter candidate list that now includes
Tooley's causal principle, the no-fact principle and a least-base argument. None of this favours any ontology.
