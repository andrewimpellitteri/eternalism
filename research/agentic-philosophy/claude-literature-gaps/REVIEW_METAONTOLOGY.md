## Strongest problem

§2 says writing E_b as a subset of a fixed H "is the first form", Meyer's untensed one.
§4(a) and §6 depend on that. It is false, and §4(b) needs it to be false.

1. **The shape does not select the form.** A subset of a fixed set in a metalanguage is
   how varying-domain semantics represents *tensed* quantifiers: each world gets a subset
   of an overall domain (SEP, Possibilism–Actualism, §4.1.1). Sider himself, in the target's
   main source, refutes Skeptic 2 with a Kripke model containing an object that exists at a
   past time but not the present, under actualist quantifiers (pp. 14–15). He does not
   thereby assert eternalism. Meyer's two forms differ in the *object-language* quantifier,
   and CONSTITUTIVE_GROUNDING_TEST.md (CGT) states no object language.
2. **The project has said.** RIVAL_PROFILE_AND_REGULARITY_BRIDGE.md §2: E_b is "a
   metalinguistic assignment", which "does not identify the mathematical domain with the
   presently concrete inventory". PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md: "It is not a completed unrestricted
   quantifier domain", and spacetime labels are "not an existential quantifier over
   currently real concrete events". ROADMAP.md check 1 forbids inferring eternalism from a
   model's domain. This matches none of the three rows, and it is the project's stated use.
3. **The target contradicts itself.** §4(a) says H is outside the first party's domain and
   "R⊊H" is unavailable to it. §4(b) then proves its result in a structure on H and
   concludes about "both views". If model theory is neutral, §2, §4(a) and §6's "not neutral
   as formalized" fall. If it is not, §4(b) is an H-party theorem the first party need not
   accept. The analysis needs both.

**Second headline-level problem.** Truth of a relativized sentence depends only on the
reduct to R. That one fact explains the failure on all three rows, so "three diagnoses"
describes three repairs to one failure (item 7).

## What survives

1. The model does not supply the content of "concrete from b". That is the target's real
   point, and the project owes it: PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md records the reference clause as a debt.
2. Repaired headline: *E_b is a metalinguistic assignment. What it represents is
   unspecified along three independent axes, and the diagnosis depends on the second.*

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Object-language form | Fixed domain with predicate; varying domain; operator; no single coherent structure |
| Status of the contrast | Stipulated; physically defined with a role; primitive or privileged |
| What is disputed | Extent at b; change of inventory across b |

3. A modest generalization: no set of R-relativized first-order premises entails an upper
   bound. This extends CGT's single inventory constraint to a syntactic class.
4. §4(a) holds conditionally, if H is literally a set of non-present events (item 2).
5. Direct reading of Sider and Meyer is a real depth gain over the red-team's memory reports.

### 2. Meyer's form and the neutral metalanguage: GAP

The "exact form" claim fails beyond the strongest problem above, because E_b cannot be
Meyer's E!. His eternalist accepts ¬E!c for Caesar (argument line 2), so an eternalist does
not hold that all of H satisfies E! at b. In Meyer's language the first party's claim sits
near (P1) and the event realist's near the temporal-existence disjunction. These are two
different predicates, not two extensions of one.

**Does the disclaimer do more than the target allows? Yes.** CGT calls E_b "not an
ontologically neutral definition of existence for every theory" and the test "a restricted
countermodel to the displayed inference". A countermodel needs only that a structure
exists. It does not assert its domain. "The quantification over H is what does the work"
confuses using a model with asserting its domain.

**The set-theoretic objection lands, with a fork.**

| H is | Then |
|---|---|
| Pure: labels or abstract structure | Neutral for validity and countermodels. §2 and §4(a) fail, and §4(b) is available to everyone |
| Impure: a set of the events | §2 and §4(a) have force for a theorist who accepts set dependence. §4(b) is unavailable to the first party, and this is not what the project says H is |

The target's best rejoinder is Sider §5: if impure sets depend on their members, a presentist
cannot have a set of non-present events. That principle is contested. Savitt note 38 and
Dorato §5 (pp. 103–104) both reject it. Labels avoid it entirely.

**Neutrality has a limit, and this is what survives.** It covers pure semantics, not an
intended interpretation. SEP's analysis (§4.1.4) is that an intended Kripke interpretation
may keep the commitment, leaving actualists a non-instrumental account or an instrumentalist
reading. CGT's H is "the represented history", so it is applied. The first party then needs
an instrumental reading or ersatz times (Bourne; Crisp 2007, via SEP Presentism §6.1).
Meyer's §6, within the target's read range, argues that saying which abstract presents are
times requires tense operators. So the debt is real, but it is an interpretive debt of the
comparison's application. It is not an error in its formalism, and it does not touch the
invalidity result.

The red-team §7b already gave this reply ("Distinguish *model theory* from *content*"). The
target cites §7b and omits the reply.

### 4. Reading 1: REPAIRABLE

"Not a dispute about existence" does not follow.

- **The step assumes the conclusion.** "Two extensions of one predicate", with each claim
  "true by the choice of extension", is unstable. For proper R the two claims conflict if the
  predicate has one meaning, and then one is false, not chosen. If each is true there are two
  meanings, and by Sider's §2 definition the dispute is verbal. Shared domain decides neither.
- **"Existence" is reserved by stipulation.** Fine reverses it. Dorato treats concrete,
  spatiotemporal existence as a mode of existence (pp. 99–101). Meyer says E! expresses a
  tensed notion of existence (§4). RIVAL_PROFILE defines E_b by "x exists from standpoint b".
- **"Concrete" may be substantive.** On Williamson's view, as Cresswell's review reports it,
  concreteness takes over the robust notion of existence, so a dispute over its extent is
  what remains of the old one. Cameron, as reported by Tallant and Ingram, holds that a
  presentist can accept permanentism.
- **The table's hedge vanishes.** "Unless the label has a role" is dropped by §3, and the
  role test is then filed under Reading 3.

The remark on conceptual engineering is sound. Sider's §2 allows a lingering dispute over
what it would be best to mean.

**Symmetric claim.** "Does not test event realism": VERIFIED but not new (item 8).
"Presupposes it": fails, since a countermodel uses its domain without asserting it. "That
claim is that the domain is H" also misstates the project's event realism, which
ROLE_OF_EXISTENCE §1 states as actual events belonging to concrete reality, a predicate-form
thesis. Its premise 2 concerns actually occurring events that may not exist from a standpoint.

### 5. Reading 2: REPAIRABLE

**The lemma.** Sound under conditions the target leaves implicit: R nonempty; closed under
function symbols; parameters in R; first-order, with no index-shifting operator; atomic
extensions inherited; one coherent 𝔄. The closure condition matters physically. A total
flow or successor map is not closed on a proper interval, so 𝔄|R is not a substructure.
Relationalizing fixes that, but then "every state has a successor" becomes an unrelativized
∀∃ sentence outside the lemma. Laws are not covered. By the standard modal analogue, truth is
preserved for generated submodels, and a bounded R is not closed under F (standard fact, not
source-read).

**More than the inclusion test?** Slightly. The inclusion test is an instance only after
restating the lemma as "φ^R depends only on the reduct to R", since CGT varies a predicate on
a fixed domain and §4(b) varies the domain. grounding-inclusion-review already recorded the
underdetermination, with the caveat that maximality, tense or grounding axioms could
constrain the assignments.

**"No deductive argument from premises about the activity in R can reach exhaustiveness."**
This turns on "about R".

- **Syntactic sense** (relativized sentences): true and nearly trivial. Sentences that cannot
  mention the outside do not bound it. That is Russell's general-facts point (item 8).
- **Subject-matter sense:** false. A quantifier-free T(r), "this region is everything
  concrete from b", is unchanged by relativization and entails the bound given its meaning.
  The lemma survives only because the signature is uninterpreted.
- **Question-begging element:** the partition "about R alone" is drawn from H. On Reading 2
  the first party's unrestricted sentences are about everything. "The first party rejects
  those stated over H" is a false dichotomy, because an unrestricted premise is not stated
  over H.

**Can a non-relativized premise be acceptable to the restricted view? Yes.** A form witness,
not endorsed, loosely modelled on the interaction criterion TRILEMMA.md quotes from Brading:

- (P1) Any two distinct events concrete from b interact.
- (P2) R is isolated: whatever interacts with a member of R is in R.
- (P3) Some r in R is concrete from b.
- Conclusion: everything concrete from b is in R.

The argument is valid (brute-force check over all 4,233 finite structures to size 4, in about
0.01 s, no counterexample; P2 alone fails on a two-element witness). P2 is an ordinary physics
premise about R and is not relativized. P1 mentions no H. P1 is implausible as stated, which
is where the philosophy belongs. Modal, nomic and totality premises have the same form. The
target half-concedes this by exempting tensed premises, and then forgets it in §6.

**Dilemma: fails.** The first horn needs every event to be a standpoint, which is the
fragment frame's assumption (red-team §7c frame error 2), not CGT's. Genuine quantification
over standpoints gives standpoints in the domain, not H. Standpoints may be system states or
abstract indices. The second horn only repeats §4(a), and the red-team's hybrid object
language answers it. Schematic generality is also available. PILOT_REVIEW.md §3 warns against
the neighbouring step, since applying a rule to all systems does not remove its index.

**"Resembles that liberal apparatus."** The analogy fails. Sider's conservative presentist
also has D(t) indexed by every t in one framework, in the semantics he uses at pp. 14–15, and
note 5 exempts her. Multiple indexing is an object-language device. §4(c)'s collapse is not
shown.

### 6. Reading 3: REPAIRABLE

The claim is true only as an analytic remark: a primitive is not defined. Three faults:

- It conflates content with extension. A link law can fix extension without fixing meaning.
- The collapse target is wrong. Definability from physics does not yield a label. A reduction
  still disputes whether the physical relation occupies the existence role, and
  ROLE_OF_EXISTENCE §7 frames exactly this as explanatory priority.
- If the physics is itself stated as growth, fixing yields a varying domain, not Reading 1.

**Repair:** "A primitive cannot be defined from physics. If a physical definition is accepted
the view is reductive, and the dispute becomes whether that relation deserves the existence
role."

### 10. Gołosz's move: GAP in scope, no row refuted

Gołosz grants that "only the present exists" can be treated as analytic, because the present
is identified with what exists. He moves the main thesis to Becoming, or to Dynamic Reality:
tensed, primitive, unanalysable (pp. 735–737).

- **Rows.** Row 3 by status and row 2 by form, since the thesis is tensed with no H. Another
  case of one view on two rows. Row 3's diagnosis still holds: physics needs a link principle.
- **Table's scope.** Here it breaks. Its column is "Diagnosis of E_b=R versus E_b=H", a contrast
  at one b. The same assignment b ↦ E_b could be read statically or dynamically, so this
  dispute does not supervene on E_b. This is a further application of his view by this
  reviewer, not his claim: his present is global, and relativity stands on his open list (p. 746).
- **§4(b).** "What exhaustiveness adds" misdescribes this presentist, for whom exhaustiveness
  of the present is definitional. But that holds for *the present*, not for R. The bridge
  becomes showing that an extended activity is what is coming to pass, and the reduct argument
  applies again. His move relocates the upper bound and does not remove it. This supports item 7.
- **Option 3.** Triviality of the exclusion thesis does not imply nothing to reach.

**Caution: his own non-triviality argument is weak.** He says DR is non-trivial because
"present" does not occur in it. But he defines the present as the totality of dynamic
existents, so by substitution DR says all objects are in the present. What carries content is
"coming into being", which is what Deasy states with tense operators and no new primitive.
That leaves a real question the table cannot express: is becoming's irreducibility needed
beyond transience? His MLP/MLE debate over truthmakers (p. 744) is a further axis.

**Repair:** the third axis under "What survives".

### 11. Savitt's CP5 and CE5: REPAIRABLE

Over one event set G, Savitt's presentist ties existence to occurrence (CP5), and his
eternalist ties detensed Existence to membership in G (CE5). The two are compatible.

**Supports.** Shared structure, both true, neither side revises. This is the core of §3, and it
is prior art for it.

**Undercuts.**

- **Two predicates, not one.** His lowercase and capitalised verbs are different verbs, and his
  case rests on temporal equivocation. Compatibility is impossible for "two extensions of one
  predicate". He gives Reading 1 its correct form.
- **Not a label.** He warns against concluding the difference is merely verbal. It is one of
  perspective, and physics seems to need the external one (p. 124). The "unless" clause fires.
- **He reconstructs.** Compatibility is conditional on the debate coming to CP5 versus CE5. His
  detensed verb is a disjunction of tensed ones, the shape of Sider's Skeptic 2. A Siderian
  eternalist would deny that CE5 states her thesis. So Savitt cannot show that no rival about
  existence must revise.
- **No fit to thick R.** CP5 uses instantaneous events and a simultaneity hyperplane in Galilean
  spacetime, and he defers the Minkowski case (notes 39–40). An extended R is not what occurs
  now, so the first party's claim is not CP5 and inherits no compatibility. CGT also uses
  "occur" tenselessly, separate from existence from b.

**Bearing on the strongest problem.** His *presentist* states her view over a fixed event set
(CP1), and note 38 denies that sets need coexisting members. So a fixed set does not mark
Meyer's untensed form. Arthur p. 133 adds that representing an event as existing does not make
it exist at the time of representing.

**Repair for Reading 1:** "Two predicates over one shared structure. Compatible if the dispute
reduces to them. That reduction is itself contested."
