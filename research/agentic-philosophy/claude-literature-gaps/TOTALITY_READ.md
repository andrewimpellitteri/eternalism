# Totality and the upper bound: a bounded read

31 October 2026. Claude Code (Opus 5.5), at the user's request. First item in
LITERATURE_AUDIT.md's order. Question: is exhaustiveness an instance of the totality problem,
and what does each ontology owe? No novelty claim and no ontology verdict. Sources are
paraphrased, with locators. This version applies REVIEW_TOTALITY.md, which refuted several claims
in the first draft (see Provenance). Armstrong was read only through reports of him.

## Result

**This note does not meet PILOT_QUESTION.md's bar.** It defends no bridge, and no strongest rival
gets a new reason to revise anything. It is a bounded read, and its output is corrections to
project notes plus one diagnostic.

1. **Totality supplies no premise here.** It is a slot for what would make a negative truth true,
   and gives no reason to accept that truth. Filled with "concrete from b", it restates the claim.
   Forrest 2006 uses general facts for other work, and argues from them against the block view
   (abstract and a review only). Until that chapter is read, the family stays on the list.
2. **Neighbouring points are already in the literature.** Earman 2008 p.144 says the denial of the
   future cannot be rigid and absolute under growth. Deng 2017 §6 says the totality of what is
   actual as of now looks tensed. Loss §V treats presentness on a growing block as being at its
   edge. None discusses the inference from constitution to inventory, so these sources do not state
   the project's gap. The project never claimed that gap as a discovery.
3. **My working hypothesis failed, conditionally.** I expected a costed comparison of two totality
   claims. Where H is unique and accepted, the two sides do not owe the same kind of claim. Where
   completions are not unique, the event realist's bound is a selection the other side does not owe.
4. **What survives is a diagnostic, for stated candidates only.** An index on "concrete from b" can
   be defined or primitive. Defined as a causal or temporal restriction, the claim is a triviality
   every party accepts. Primitive, it is a thesis the event realist rejects, indexed or not. So
   "indexed" sorts nothing, and the missing argument lies between the defined and primitive
   readings. Three candidates fall outside the simple sorting (§4).

## 1. What totality does

Armstrong's totality fact relates an aggregate to a property when the aggregate comprises all
the things with that property. The largest such fact totals the first-order states of affairs,
and it serves as truthmaker for negative and general truths (SEP "Truthmakers" §§2.2–2.3).
Molnar 2000 §VII argues that this is a negative fact under another name, and that it needs a
type hierarchy. Armstrong grants that it involves negation and sets a limit (as reported in
SEP §2.3).

The rival accounts disagree about the truthmaker: absences (Martin), the world with its contents
essential (Cameron 2008), the whole positive fact (Cheyne and Pigden 2006), or none, with
negatives true for lack of falsemakers (optimalism and Bigelow's supervenience of truth on being;
SEP §§2.4–2.5; Molnar §III reports Lewis on falsemakers). All of them start from an accepted
negative truth and ask what grounds it. None of them is a reason to accept it.

Applied here, E_b=R fills the slot as T(R, concrete from b). If the property is membership in R,
the totality is trivial. So the content is in "concrete from b". Molnar adds that the one big
totality fact could not be stated or checked by anyone. That is an epistemic remark about the one
big fact. A quantified premise need not enumerate it, so it does not bear on the premise's use here.

**So LITERATURE_AUDIT.md's "a stated upper-bound principle already exists here" was wrong.**

## 2. Neighbouring work

- **Earman 2008, pp.144–145, n.19, §8 p.159.** A growing-block theorist, he says, cannot coherently
  deny the future's reality absolutely, since that fixes one truncated model and gives up growth.
  The denial can only be perspectival, and a block theorist can say the perspectival version too, as
  a triviality. He then gives two claims true in every stage and necessary: that far enough beyond
  now nothing exists, with "now" shifting, and that the sum total was smaller and will be larger.
  At p.159 these fail in block models, which is why he calls the difference ontological. So p.144
  separates a rigid denial from a shifting one. n.19 argues Broad's quantifier is not "at present".
  His "absolute" is not Fine's: the standard realist holds a tensed denial absolutely and keeps
  growth through what will be, and Deng §4 rejects the step from that view to one fixed block.
  Deng §2 asks what this perspectivality comes to. Earman treats disputes over labelling an agreed
  block as idle (p.145, p.159).
- **Deng 2017 §6.** Against Tooley: what is actual as of now includes that those facts are all the
  tenseless facts. "And that's a tensed fact." She hedges that the objection may misconstrue
  actuality as of a time.
- **Tooley 2010.** A dynamic view needs the totality of what is actual as of a time, and on the
  growing block, presentness is structural: the latest within that totality. Per Deng §6, Tooley
  1997 also makes whatever is actual as of some time actual simpliciter.
- **Fine, as Deng §3 reports.** Realism, Neutrality, Absolutism and Coherence are jointly
  inconsistent. A rigid "nothing lies beyond R_b" is a tensed fact in this sense.
- **Loss, "How the block grows".** Permanence, A-change, truthmaker maximalism and the temporal form
  of necessitation are inconsistent (§II). The proposition he uses is that T is present. His §V
  treats this as an exhaustiveness claim: on the block, being present is being at the edge, and the
  truthmaker is such that every other time is earlier. Tooley's structural reading agrees.
- **Broad 1923 p.66, via Earman p.144.** A present event's essence is that nothing lies later than it.

Project status: Earman §§5–7 were read for local-fragmentalism, and the p.144 passage was not used
in the pilot. This Deng paper is not in the registers, though redteam 02 reports Deng 2013 at
abstract depth. STRONGEST_VIEW.md O2 states the real-simpliciter or real-from-b dilemma, from the
25 October red team. No novelty is claimed, and no priority audit was run for §§3–5.

## 3. The two sides do not owe the same thing, on a condition

R_b below is what a restricted view counts as concrete from b. It differs from
CONSTITUTIVE_GROUNDING_TEST.md's R, a single activity region that may include events later than b.

That file defines E_b as a subset of H, so E_b⊆H is built into the frame. Read as an applied claim
it says nothing concrete lies outside the represented history. Parties accept that on different
terms. MAXIMALITY_AND_EXISTENTIAL_OPENNESS.md §3's admissibility excludes shorter complete
histories, and with MGHD uniqueness fixes H, within the stipulated class.

| | Pointy restricted | Slice or bow-tie restricted | Event realist |
|---|---|---|---|
| E_b⊆H | Holds for every completion through b, by past-closure | Holds only for completions agreeing on the slice or elsewhere | Holds of one H |
| Selection owed | None | For that part | Which completion, where they differ |
| Extra claim | E_b⊆R_b: negative, differs by b | The same | H⊆E_b: positive, the same for every b |
| Must say | Whether more will come, or why that is unsettled | The same | Why H and not a truncation |
| Supported by R⊆E_b? | No | No | No |

Where H is unique in a class all parties accept, the event realist's extra claim is a stronger
lower bound, and READING_GAPS.md §2 fails as a differential claim. Where completions are not unique,
by indeterminism or past a Cauchy horizon, it stands: the event realist's E_b⊆H is a selection a
pointy theorist does not owe. Earman reports the Broadhead reply that without uniqueness no fact
selects a block (pp.144, 154, 159). Three views fall outside the table. On McCall's view E_b lies in
no single history. Fragmentalists have no one H. For a theorist whose H has no referent beyond R_b,
"nothing concrete lies outside H" is the thesis itself, so the sentence is shared and the proposition
is not. The positive claim carries more events and the negative one a changing limit, and this read
gives no way to price that.

Gołosz sits outside the table: exhaustiveness is analytic for him, and the thesis moves to
primitive becoming. C&R's (P2R) and (PO) in RIVAL_PROFILE_AND_REGULARITY_BRIDGE.md §2 are stated
upper-bound clauses of the thesis, indexed and rejected, which is why "indexed" sorts nothing.
Forrest is unread and could change the first row.

**Why "indexed" fails as a sort.** Tooley 2010 says static views can give no sense to temporally
indexed actuality. Both sides accept the *sentence* only because the rival reads the index as a
defined restriction. For Fine's relativist, "everything lies in R" said from b still carries the
index, so "absolute" is not available as a contrast, and the operative step is from a defined index
to anything stronger. Earman's two tensed claims show another form: a shifting denial that holds at
every stage, which the event realist rejects only with an unrestricted quantifier.

**The theorem is an upper-bound derivation of a conditional kind.** Given (CB), (T) and (LC) over
all globally hyperbolic spacetimes, it says the relation is either universal, or inside the causal
past in every spacetime (research/stein-gr/main.tex, thm:main). So it amplifies an assumed failure
of universality, P4, into a sharp bound. It does not supply the first negative: P4 has no argument
beyond its gloss, and the event realist rejects it when "real" is read through P2. It also needs the
off-shell premise (O4). The bound is on what has happened by b, which STRONGEST_VIEW.md §1 says the
eternalist must accept, given P3w. Existence enters at P2.

**The causal criterion.** The Eleatic principle admits only what has causal power (Colyvan 1998 §1,
framed as a test for justified belief; Plato, *Sophist* 247d–e, in Jowett). Read tenselessly it
passes every later event with causes or effects. Only a standpoint index gives a bound at the
future: "concrete from b only if it can affect b". That is the converse of P6, and given P2–P4 it is
the theorem's bound, so it is no separate candidate. P6's rationale does not support it. The Eleatic
sources give it little. Cowling's Temporal Eleaticism says what exists at a time manifests causal
power at that time, which is a different claim: as a condition on what is concrete from b it would
exclude b's past too. He reports it as settling restricted questions for an eternalist, and absolute
ones too for a presentist, then rejects it for its consequences for persistence. Colyvan §3 says the
epistemic motivation gives no reason to believe in future objects, or in regions light could not have
reached us from (n.7). He counts the latter against the motivation. Project precedent:
research/horizons/PHIL_REVIEW.md already calls such a restriction Eleatic or verificationist.

**Interaction closure.** Two principles are in play. Brading's passage, per TRILEMMA.md, is
inclusive, so as an upper bound it needs its converse, and TRILEMMA.md already records that a
universal unity yields the block. REVIEW_METAONTOLOGY.md's valid form has two premises: concrete
events interact, and R is isolated. Read two-way, an event realist denies isolation. Read one-way,
isolation is past-closure, which R=J^-(b) satisfies, and the burden falls on the first premise, which
becomes the indexed criterion. Neither premise has independent support so far.

**One elementary observation.** Persisting truths are closed under entailment, so a claim that
fails to persist has no persisting entailers. "Everything lies in R_b", with R_b rigid, fails to
persist wherever the inventory changes, on a growing block or on presentism. That is the reason
behind Earman p.144's rejection of a rigid denial. It does not touch the shifting form "everything
is past or present", which holds at every stage and which Earman calls necessary for the view. The
rigid instance follows from the shifting form plus self-location, so the only non-persisting
ingredient needed is locational. The observation leaves open past-tensed conclusions, which persist,
and non-deductive support, which b′ can defeat. It covers positive tenseless and past-tensed premises,
and not totality-form constitution claims or a presentist's present-tensed premises. It says nothing
against the event realist, and I have not audited its history.

## 5. What each party has a reason to revise

**A growing-block theorist who accepts maximalism, temporal necessitation and permanence** cannot
also hold a rigid "that's all" truth at b which growth falsifies. This instantiates Loss's argument,
with growth in place of A-change, and his §III covers any proposition that ceases to be true. It does
not fire on the shifting form, which never ceases to be true. Given that form, the rigid claim is
equivalent to "b is present", which is Loss's own case (§V). It does not fire against presentism,
which lacks permanence, or the block view, which lacks change.

| Member given up | Exit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Permanence | Totality facts cease, or permanence is restricted to events | A fact ceases at each step. Broad's text covers whatever has become, and Loss's (GB2) is unrestricted, so restriction departs from both |
| Temporal necessitation | Truthmaker intrinsicalism (Loss, after Parsons) | The block is the truthmaker and gains proper parts. Mereological universalism goes (§§V–VI). Armstrongian states of affairs go as truthmakers (§IV), so no totality fact remains. Molnar §IX rejects a primitive contingent relation; whether that reaches this version is unargued |
| Maximalism | Negative and general truths lack truthmakers (optimalism; Bigelow; Lewis, per SEP §§2.4–2.5) | Growth stays additive, and the upper bound is no fact at the fundamental level. Cameron and Cheyne–Pigden argue against the restriction. Neither addresses Bigelow's principle |
| Truth-value change | Index the proposition (Tooley) | Actuality as of a time is primitive. Deng §6 argues the totality as of now looks tensed, against Tooley's tenseless facts |

Separately, each view takes a stance on Fine's quartet, via Deng §3. These stances are not exits
from the table above: relativizing every clause to standpoints reproduces the contradiction at b′,
and a standard realist faces it with tense operators.

| Stance | Cost |
|---|---|
| Deny Neutrality (standard realism) | Deng §7: other stages' tensed facts are left out |
| Deny Absolutism (external relativism) | Deng §4: hard to give literal content. The official core of STRONGEST_VIEW.md P1 |
| Deny Coherence (fragmentalism) | Deng §4: harder still. Optional in P1 |
| Deny Realism (block view; Tooley's aim) | No tensed fundamentals. Deng §6 presses this against Tooley |

**Relativity.** Nothing above needs a foliation. A fact ceases at s if it holds at s and fails at
every later stage, which needs only a partial order, and along each worldline through b the totality
at b ceases at b. What relativity removes is a privileged chain of stages. Standard realism needs one
present stage and, for its history, a chain of past sets. That chain need not follow a time function:
in classical sequential growth it is a linear extension (Earman pp.155–158), at the price of treating
birth order as physical. Without a privileged chain, parity pushes a view to relativism, but it would
do so in a Newtonian setting too. Causal sets are at discovery depth here.

**STRONGEST_VIEW.md.** P5 still says every fact holding from b holds from later b′.
COORDINATOR_AUDIT.md §1's repair scopes P5 to tenseless occurrence truths, which already covers
exhaustiveness: "nothing lies beyond F(b)" is then a centred truth from b that need not persist. This
is no second objection. What remains is whether that centred truth is fundamental, which takes a row
of the first table, or the defined claim, which is common ground, as O2 grants.

**The event realist.** Nothing from totality. Where completions are not unique, §3's selection debt
applies, and it was already in the project.

**The project's frame, to check.** Earman's charge is that a block supplies the order or identity
of a growing block's own model (pp.140, 143), and that this is suspicious rather than incoherent
(p.145). The use of H as a countermodel is not touched. PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md's label x runs across
solutions, not stages. The charge reaches inclusion across standpoints in one M: E_b⊆E_c in
RIVAL_PROFILE_AND_REGULARITY_BRIDGE.md §2, with R3 and P5. Tensed clauses that quantify in assume the
same identity. This is the reference clause PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md records. The variable-domain reply
answers a semantic charge, not this one. Earman's worst cases need symmetric or non-unique embeddings,
so the cost here may be small. This is a flag, not a shown defect.

## 7. Limits

- The sorting covers stated candidates. It is not a theorem about all upper-bound principles.
- It repeats, more sharply, that premises are missing. PILOT_QUESTION.md asks for a defended bridge,
  and this note gives none. Its tables rearrange Loss, Deng and Earman.
- A truthmaker argument binds only those who accept truthmaker theory. Many in this debate do not.
- Causal notions are used informally. Bell correlations are set aside. Indeterminism is set aside
  except where §3 and §4 name it.
