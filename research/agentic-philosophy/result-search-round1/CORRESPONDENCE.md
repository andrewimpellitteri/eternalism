# Coordinator test: hold the physical and philosophical questions fixed

31 October 2026. First-round coordinator work, separate from the two GLM
candidate routes. This is an analytic correspondence check, not a new
causal theorem or independent priority claim.

## 1. Specify the entity before claiming that it ceases

The transport apparatus, an ongoing transmission and one carried token are
different possible targets. Switching the source off can finish the
transmission after a transit delay while leaving a functional apparatus.
Its signal state can change while the boundary law and response capacity
remain. A unity criterion must say which target it identifies and preserve
that target in its counterfactuals and conclusions.

The first [pilot review](../PILOT_REVIEW.md) gives an explicit test:
for input1 before b and0 after, y(b+s)=1−s/τ for0<s<τ. There are still
in-flight signals. Neither their later exit nor the source switch proves
that the material apparatus ceases to exist.
The exact linear expression is the ideal-step/weak-solution case. A smooth
nonnegative switch beginning at b still gives y(b+s)≥1−s/τ on that interval,
so the delayed-cessation point also holds for continuous source histories.

## 2. Preserve the intervention/access contract, not just one formula

For q_t+cq_x=0 on[0,L], put τ=L/c, inlet f(t), outlet g(t)=f(t−τ).
The regional average has both exact descriptions

    y(t)=τ⁻¹∫₀^τ f(t−s)ds=τ⁻¹∫₀^τ g(t+s)ds.

These identities do not make independently manipulable inlet histories
equivalent to independently manipulable future outlet histories under the
same physical intervention contract. With an inlet drive and passive outlet,
changing g at a later time while holding its required earlier f fixed is
not an admitted change of this source problem. Fixing the entire outlet
history instead is a different conditioning problem.

Thus equal expressions on a complete solution do not by themselves preserve
causal access, control or explanatory dependence. The existing response
benchmark already restricts realization invariance to fixed physical
inputs, outputs, clock and gain; this test applies that restriction.

A time-reversal argument must likewise distinguish symmetry of an equation,
symmetry of a particular full solution, and symmetry preserving the drive,
readout and admissible interventions. An equivariant rule has a symmetric
output on a symmetry-fixed full model; equivariance alone does not make
its output symmetric on every model. Boundary conditions and experimental
roles can provide physical asymmetries even when the equation is reversible.

This is relevant to Claude's concurrent reversal candidate, inspected in
the shared checkout. Its conditional symmetry lemma and algebraic identity
are useful, but a claim about what the *whole physics* fixes must include
the boundary and role assignments. No wholesale acceptance or historical
novelty is inferred here; its files remain under Claude's ownership.

## 3. Keep three philosophical predicates separate

- A phase temporally constitutes the process.
- That phase is concrete in the proposed ontology.
- That phase is present from the specified standpoint.

An argument may connect them, but must state and defend the connection.
For instance, an ongoing process can be identified using earlier phases
without this alone showing that they are concretely present together.
Conversely a complete-history realist can admit the whole process as
concrete without accepting a privileged extended present. Neither example
settles which explanation is better.

The required candidate must therefore preserve the target entity, physical
variation/access contract and disputed predicate across its decisive
inference. These are acceptance checks for the argument, not another
definition of a preferred ontology.
