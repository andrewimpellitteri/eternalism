# Grounding and inclusion review

31 October 2026. Bounded analytic review of
`research/agentic-philosophy/CONSTITUTIVE_GROUNDING_TEST.md`; no external
source survey or ontology verdict.

## Finding

The strongest gap is exactly at Candidate 1's step 5. Premises 1–4, even if
granted, yield that the occurrences constituting the activity are concrete
from `b`. They do not yield either that no other events are concrete from `b`
or that the activity exhausts the temporal extent of a system-token. “R is the
extent constituting S” supports a claim about what realizes or composes `S`;
it is not by itself an exhaustiveness principle for `E_b`. The document
correctly treats the missing move as an additional premise rather than a
consequence of factivity.

The inclusion comparison is a valid restricted countermodel to an inference
from `R ⊆ E_b` to `E_b = R`: with nonempty proper `R ⊂ H`, both assignments
`E_b=R` and `E_b=H` include all constituting occurrences. This establishes
logical underdetermination under the stated inventory constraint. It does not
show that every complete theory admits both assignments; independent
maximality, tense, grounding, or other axioms could constrain them.

The scope distinction is preserved. If `E_b` means all events concrete from
the standpoint, enlarging it is a counterexample to global exhaustiveness.
If the conclusion concerns only the parts of this particular `S`-token, events
outside the token can be concrete without being its parts; the expansion is
then no counterexample to that mereological claim. The revision earns a
possible process-mereological proposal. “Just is the concrete organization …
in R” could be read as making the token's parts exhaustive, but even on that
strong reading it restricts that token's composition, not the total
`E_b`-inventory. The text should continue to mark this reading as system
relative.

## Oscillator check

For any smooth cutoff `χ` that is zero on `t ≤ π/4` and one on `t ≥ π/2`,
`χ′` is supported in `[π/4,π/2]` and has finite supremum norm. A cutoff with
these properties exists. For example, set `ρ(s)=0` for `s≤0`,
`ρ(s)=exp(−1/s)` for `s>0`, and
`χ(t)=ρ(t−π/4)/(ρ(t−π/4)+ρ(π/2−t))`. The denominator is positive for every
`t`; this gives the stated constant regions and a smooth transition.

Choose `ε>0` such that `ε<1` and
`ε ||χ′||∞ < sin(π/4)` (if the norm is zero, the second condition is
automatic). Such an `ε` exists. Since `sin(t) ≥ sin(π/4)` on the transition
interval, `q′_B(t)=−sin(t)+εχ′(t)<0` there. Off that interval `χ′=0`, so
`q′_B=−sin(t)`. Thus on `0<t≤2π`, the only zeros of `q′_B` are `π` and
`2π`. At those times `q_B(π)=−1+ε` and `q_B(2π)=1+ε`, neither of which is
the initial position `1`. So `B` has no positive-time return to `(1,0)` by
`2π`, while `A` returns at `2π`. The equations hold by construction:
`q″_A+q_A=0` and `q″_B+q_B=ε(χ″+χ)=u_B`.

The two histories, states, and input functions agree on a neighborhood of
`b=0`; their later forcing differs. This is a valid counterexample to
determining the completed-return property from the local state and equation
form alone when future input is not fixed. It does not compare solutions
under identical complete driving data, challenge uniqueness, or establish
that either return is a present disposition. A useful precision edit is to
state explicitly that return candidates are considered only on `(0,2π]` and
to give a cutoff construction such as the one above. The current existential
cutoff and epsilon conditions are already jointly satisfiable.

## Attribution and limits

Brading's 2015 §5 / P1** connection, and the C&R later-grounding comparison,
are inherited provenance recorded by the coordinator. I did not reread either
primary source for this audit. The test explicitly declines to identify
Brading's system-relative proposal with global `E_b=R`, and characterizes C&R
as a warning about a disputed bridge, not as a settled account of physical
constitution. Those are appropriately limited formulations on the record
available here. No claim is made that the test reconstructs either view
completely, that its distinction or method is historically novel, or that
either ontology is established.

The first unresolved inference is the substantive bridge from a system's
constituting activity (or its parts) to an exhaustive claim about what exists
from a standpoint. To advance it requires an independently defended
exhaustiveness/maximality principle and an account of whether the target is
the token's composition or the standpoint's full concrete inventory.
