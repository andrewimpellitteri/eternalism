# Bounded review: controlled-process explanatory comparison

## Recommendation

**Accept the bounded negative verdict, with one scope clarification.** The initial argument does not establish an explanatory advantage for a growing concrete inventory: its interruption inference requires essential membership of a rigid aggregate, and process individuation does not itself entail inventory growth. The feedback example also gives an event realist a concrete, causally organized rival account for the specified operation. This is enough to rebut an exclusive advantage argued from the stated explanandum. It does **not** establish equal explanatory adequacy of complete process and event metaphysics, or show that the physical organization exhausts metaphysical unity.

## Exact check

The feedback equations are mutually consistent for the stated ideal device. With q=1 on [0,1/2), h(1/2)=1/2; for constant later rate r>0, first target attainment solves 1/2+r(T-1/2)=1, giving T=1/2+1/(2r) and dT/dr=-1/(2r^2). Thus A completes at 1, B at 3/2, and cancellation at 1/2 leaves h=1/2 and prevents completion. The almost-everywhere flow indicator correctly stops inlet flow at target; endpoint conventions do not affect these integrals. Controller execution and positive liquid flow are appropriately kept as distinct predicates.

The replay equation is also correct for its stated illustration. With q=1 and no cancellation, closing the timed valve at 1 matches the feedback height and flow histories, since feedback reaches its target at 1. Under B, replay closes at 1 with height 3/4 while feedback continues to 3/2. The contrast demonstrates that a shared output trace alone does not identify a shared organizing mechanism. It does not hold the complete physical event/field facts fixed, as the test itself notes.

For the full advertised input family, the feedback account has a well-defined completion time as the first target-hitting time, if any, before cancellation. Nonnegative piecewise-continuous q is locally integrable on bounded intervals under the ordinary convention, so Q is defined there. The mechanism and equations can be retained for arbitrary such q, including stalls and noncompletion. No error was found in the quantified claim when it is read this way. To make the convention explicit, describe q as locally integrable (piecewise-continuous is a sufficient stipulated class), define completion as first attainment before cancellation, and treat “never” as no finite cancellation. The closed-loop account should not be conflated with the replay device: replay is a single counterexample about output matching, not a universal substitute for feedback.

## Identity, unity, and modal scope

The participation rule is physically substantive within this engineered example: a common start latch, level comparison, target and actuator path causally select controlled inlet activity; a reset marks a new execution. It gives a proposed cross-protocol counterpart criterion. It does not demonstrate that counterparts are numerically identical across worlds, nor establish that this causal organization is the uniquely correct metaphysical criterion of process unity. The text expressly leaves those as further questions, which is the right scope.

The modal lemma is valid as stated: if P is identical to rigid F, F exists wherever P does, a member of F is essential to F, and P exists in a world without that member, the assumptions conflict. Necessary identity alone does not supply essential membership; premise 3 does that work. The lemma therefore refutes only the package, not temporal parts, event realism, or aggregate accounts that reject essential membership. Steward's source claims and locators, including the newly added §3 diagnosis, are inherited from the coordinator's selected reading; I did not inspect the originals, consistent with the assigned division of work.

The “whole process active at b” to “all temporal parts concrete at b” inference is not licensed by the example or lemma. The new Steward provenance note may support the historical attribution, but it does not change this logical point. No occurrence-to-exhaustive-inventory route is needed or revived here.

## First remaining inference

The first substantive unresolved inference is from causal organization to **sufficient process unity**: why this latch/sensor/actuator causal structure constitutes one operation, and why its persistence conditions are the right ones across changes, rather than merely furnishing a useful operational grouping. The example supports the grouping through physical coupling and intervention response, but it does not prove a unique or fundamental individuation. The event realist's answer is therefore a plausible bounded replacement, not a demonstration that every metaphysical demand about unity has been met.

The further step from that unresolved unity question to a need for a particular temporal inventory is also unargued. A proponent of growth would need to show why the additional grounding or identity fact requires a changing or restricted inventory, and specify the indexed existence claim at issue. Requiring future constituents to be concrete from b could instead favor a rival block interpretation, so it does not establish the growth bridge. Nothing in the feedback equations or the interruption lemma establishes that bridge.
