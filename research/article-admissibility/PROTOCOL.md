# Bounded admissibility comparison: assessment protocol

Recorded before the case assessment, 2026-10-20. Prior knowledge includes the
accepted complete Brading 2013 reading and the article's argument review; this
is not a blind preregistration or a claim that those sources were unknown.
Subsequent source-driven changes must be identified rather than rewritten into
this protocol after seeing their effects.

## Exactly one dispute

Compare Brading's 2013 law-constitutive account of local presents with the
spacetime-constitutive unity argument she challenges. The comparison is about
which account explains the unity/extent of physical systems within a shared
physical description. Do not equate every block interpretation with that
particular unity argument, or define the rival as denying laws or local systems.
Reconstruct a block advocate who can accept dynamical systems and law-based
explanation while denying that their extent determines what exists.

Brading's constitutive commitment to test: laws ground a system's unity and
the spatiotemporal extent needed for its dynamics fixes that system's local
present. Check the primary wording and whether this is a completed model or a
conditional program. The rival's minimum commitment: events can belong to a
spacetime history without their ontological unity requiring one global present;
it may also accept subsystem dynamics. Neither package may simply redefine the
other's disputed ontological predicate as its own.

## Common physical/access assumptions and auxiliary freedoms

Choose one physical description actually used in the primary discussion. State
the laws, model/solution class, what counts as a physical system, the specified
records/measurements and their probabilities if the source supplies them. If no
shared concrete model/access regime is supplied, identify that failure; do not
invent probabilities to complete the comparison. A conditional matched-model
exercise may assume identical empirical likelihoods, labelled as a project
stipulation rather than a source-established equivalence theorem.

Both sides may use the same empirical dynamics, decompositions, boundary
conditions and explanations of local behavior. Both must state any extra
bridge from that description to ontology. Changing those common dynamics is a
new physical package and must be evaluated separately. Unstated systems,
arbitrary exception clauses, contradiction with the fixed physical description,
and an undefined operational claim are public reasons to withhold assessment.
Merely failing to share the other side's ontological bridge is not.

## Two profiles fixed before the case verdict

1. **Empirical and structural profile.** Admit a consistent package with the
   specified common physical model, empirical adequacy and an explicit bridge.
   Do not exclude it merely because its ontology is not uniquely identified by
   the records. Under matched likelihoods, the evidence supplies no empirical
   preference. Assess explanatory claims only if an independent criterion is
   articulated; do not assume a winner from representational parsimony.
2. **Constitutive explanation profile.** Retain the same conditions, and ask
   whether each package supplies an independently motivated account of system
   unity and the scope of its ontology, with stated auxiliary freedoms. Credit
   law-based explanation equally when either side can use it. Treat a disputed
   bridge as an argument requiring defense, not an empirical fact or an
   automatic disqualifier. If this profile cannot supply a verdict without
   assuming the contested constitutive premise, report the circularity or
   underdetermination instead of assigning a cost/score.

The second profile is a proposed explanatory standard, not a demonstrated
neutral rule. Compare sensitivity of the inference to both profiles and to the
strongest rival reconstruction. Distinguish admission, explanatory preference,
and empirical likelihood discrimination throughout.

## Primary evidence and defeat conditions

Start with Brading 2013 and seek the closest full-text follow-up/criticism,
including Brading 2015 and the Brown/Pooley–Balashov/Janssen explanatory
dispute if directly relevant. Use at least three actually inspected primary
philosophical sources, naming exact sections/editions and partial versus full
reading. Different disputes may supply methodological comparisons only; they
must not be presented as direct rebuttals to Brading. If another predecessor is
closer, explain the substitution. No metadata/abstract upgrade counts as a
primary argument read.

Try to defeat the method's novelty: locate existing assumption-sensitive
explanatory/underdetermination analysis doing the same work. Try to defeat any
putative advantage by allowing the strongest rival the same law-based
explanation. A decisive failure is a conclusion that follows only by building
one side's constitutive bridge into admissibility. A decisive positive case
would need a separately defended criterion that discriminates packages without
restating their rival ontological definitions. A known restatement, an
exposition-only result or a circularity diagnosis is an acceptable output and
must not be described as a new physical prediction.

Outputs stay under `research/article-admissibility/`. Proposed manuscript edits
are proposals here only; no paper, source-register or selection-gate changes.
No weighted scores, priors, installations or paid runs.
