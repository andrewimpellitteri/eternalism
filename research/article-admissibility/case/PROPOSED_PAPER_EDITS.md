# Proposed edits to the working manuscript

These are proposals only. No manuscript file was changed.

1. Add a short worked case after the discussion of admissibility profiles in “What is being compared?” Brading’s case is a good test because law-based system unity can be granted on both sides while the inference from a system’s dynamical extent to what exists remains contested.
2. State Brading’s 2015 P1** accurately: clause (a), smaller extent than the universe’s full history, is required to avoid the block; clause (b), smaller extent than the system’s own complete history, is optional for that narrower aim. Note that she leaves empirical satisfaction of the conditions and further elaboration open.
3. Label the strongest block-compatible rival as a composite reconstruction for this case. It can accept law-based dynamical unity while denying that a system’s law-sustaining region exhausts that system’s existence; neither cited source jointly develops this reply to Brading. Do not characterize the rival as rejecting laws or local systems.
4. Make clear that Brading accepts special relativity as a complete account of spatiotemporal structure in the relevant regime, not as a complete matter-dynamics theory. She supplies no shared concrete solution/access protocol or probabilities. If matched likelihoods are used for illustration, label the equality as a project stipulation; do not describe either realization as actually admitted or verified.
5. Report the profile outcomes as conditional on consistent, adequate realizations for a common completed model. Under matched likelihoods there is no empirical preference; under the constitutive profile, no established independent reason in the cited texts decides between Brading’s restricted-existence bridge and the rival’s full-history bridge. Present the result as underdetermination/exposition, not novelty.
6. Describe Brading’s extent as system-relative and potentially nontransitive; do not claim that every other system outside a selected system’s present is unreal. Use Balashov and Janssen §§9–11 as the specific spacetime-explanatory argument, while noting their target is Craig’s neo-Lorentzian interpretation. Cite Brown and Pooley arXiv v1 §§3–5 as the methodological dispute over explanatory direction, not as a direct argument about Brading’s presentist ontology.
