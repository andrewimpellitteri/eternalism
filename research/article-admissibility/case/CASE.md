# A bounded admissibility case: law-constituted unity and the present

## Scope and result

This case compares Katherine Brading’s law-constitutive proposal for locating a local present with a block-compatible rival that grants dynamical laws an explanatory role in physical-system unity. The target is one question: does the law-based explanation of a system’s unity also establish that only its law-sustaining spatiotemporal region exists? It is not a comparison of every presentist and eternalist theory, and it does not test a new physical prediction.

The result is conditional under both preregistered profiles; the source comparison is incomplete because no common concrete model or fully specified realizations have been selected. If consistent, empirically adequate realizations of both packages can be supplied for one common model and access regime, and if their likelihoods are stipulated equal, neither package has an empirical advantage in that regime. Under a constitutive-explanation profile, Brading identifies a substantive explanatory route, but her 2015 version makes the anti-block condition explicit and leaves its empirical realization and further elaboration open. Our strongest block-compatible rival is a composite reconstruction: it shares the law-based explanation of system unity while denying that the region dynamically needed to model or sustain a system exhausts what exists. Neither Balashov and Janssen nor Brown and Pooley jointly defend this exact reply to Brading. The cited sources do not supply an independently defended criterion that decides between the bridges. The outcome is conditional underdetermination and an exposition-only application of an existing dispute, not a novel result or a verdict for either ontology.

## Packages and common comparison domain

**Brading’s package.** Brading rejects the assumption that spacetime must be the ontological principle unifying everything that exists. Brading accepts special relativity as a complete account of spatiotemporal structure in the relevant regime and treats Minkowski spacetime as the framework for organizing knowledge and predictions, while physical systems are genuine unities in virtue of dynamical laws. The “present” for a unity is the spatiotemporal region dynamically needed to sustain that system. On the 2015 formulation, this extent is system-relative and need not be transitive across systems (p. 17); the view does not thereby assert that every other genuine system outside one chosen system’s present is unreal. The 2013 paper leaves open that this region could extend across the whole universe; in that case, Brading says, the proposal has not escaped the block. In the 2015 formulation, avoiding the block requires condition (a): each system’s present is smaller than the universe’s full represented evolution. Condition (b), that it is also smaller than that system’s entire history, is expressly optional if the aim is only to avoid the block; Brading recommends including it if a finite present for each system is part of the desired presentist intuition. These are the author’s stated commitments, not consequences established by special relativity alone (Brading 2013, §§4–7, pp. 1104–1111; Brading 2015, §5, pp. 17–18).

**Strongest block-compatible rival.** The rival keeps the same relativistic dynamics, dynamical laws, system decompositions, and law-based explanations of how subsystems behave and cohere. It can grant that laws help constitute the unity and identity of a physical system. It retains the spacetime-constitutive block argument as one account of global unity, while also granting that laws explain the behavior and organization of local systems. As alternative accounts of the global explanatory direction, it may take the Balashov–Janssen route, where spacetime structure explains why distinct laws share Lorentz covariance, or the Brown–Pooley route, where Lorentz-covariant laws underwrite the Minkowski geometry. These are distinct explanatory variants, not premises combined in one package; the block reconstruction does not depend on choosing between them. In either variant, it denies the further exclusivity bridge: a region sufficient or necessary for a system’s dynamical treatment need not be the full domain of that system’s existence, and law-based unity need not make other temporal stages unreal. It treats the system’s complete history as real and its temporally restricted “present” as a useful or explanatorily relevant region within that history. This rival does not reject systemhood, dynamical explanation, or the empirical authority of special relativity. It declines the inference from law-constituted unity to the claim that only a law-sustaining extent exists. That is the disputed bridge, stated symmetrically rather than hidden in an admissibility rule.

**Shared physical description and access.** The common background is special relativity, which Brading accepts as a complete account of spatiotemporal structure in the relevant regime. This does not assume that special relativity supplies a complete matter-dynamics theory. For a completed comparison, both packages would need to use the same selected solution, laws, boundary and initial data, subsystem criteria, preparations, and accessible records. Brading does not present one fully specified relativistic solution with an operational test for identifying the relevant genuine unity, computing its sustaining region, or assigning probabilities to a defined observation protocol. Her Newtonian collision discussion is an explanatory illustration, not a relativistic likelihood model. The source therefore gives no common concrete access regime or numerical likelihoods from which to calculate an empirical comparison.

For the sensitivity exercise only, stipulate that a selected common model and access regime are fixed and that the two interpretations assign the same likelihood to every allowed record. This is a project stipulation, not an equivalence theorem or a fact established by Brading. It grants no model priors and makes no claim about future interventions outside that stipulated access class. A result obtained by changing dynamics, system boundaries, or observations would be a different comparison.

**Exclusion rule and auxiliary freedom.** Exclude an implementation from this comparison if it changes the common laws or solution, adds an unshared preferred structure that changes observable predictions, relies on a system boundary or exception introduced only after seeing the result, or leaves “dynamically needed to sustain” without any stated application to the fixed system. Such an implementation may define a different package, but it cannot count as a cost-free rescue within this comparison. Permit both sides the same changes to initial/boundary data, subsystem decomposition, and record-access model only when those changes are fixed before assessing the ontological bridge. The cited papers do not provide such a fully operationalized case, so these are protocol constraints, not claims that Brading has already failed a specified physical test.

## Sensitivity to the two registered profiles

| Profile | Admission and evaluation | Result for this case |
|---|---|---|
| Empirical and structural | Admit each internally consistent package that respects the fixed physical description, is empirically adequate in the declared regime, and states its ontological bridge. Do not require evidence to select one ontology when the evidence does not identify it. | Conditional eligibility follows only if consistent, empirically adequate realizations of both packages exist for the same completed model and access class. Equal likelihoods, if stipulated for those realizations, yield no empirical preference. The source comparison does not verify either completed realization. |
| Constitutive explanation | Ask whether each package gives an independently motivated account of system unity and ontological scope. Credit law-based explanation to either side when both can use it. Treat the inference from dynamical scope to restricted existence as a premise needing defense. | Brading offers a law-constitutive account of unity; the rival can retain that account of unity while taking its scope to include the system’s complete history. The sources do not establish an independent criterion that turns the former explanatory contribution into a verdict for restricted existence. The outcome is underdetermination, not a tie in explanatory merit proved for all possible models. |

This distinction matters: **admission** of both packages does not establish equal explanatory value; **equal likelihoods** do not establish metaphysical identity; and an explanatory preference, if one could be defended, would not be empirical likelihood discrimination. No numerical weight or prior is warranted by these sources.

## What the primary sources establish

Brading’s 2013 article first rejects the shared spacetime-unity assumption and sketches law-constitutive unity from Newtonian examples. In §4 (pp. 1104–1105) she explicitly presents the law-constitutive account without defending it there. Sections 5–7 (pp. 1105–1109) connect laws, persistence and the possible local present, while allowing that a system may require the full universe’s history. The conclusion (pp. 1110–1111) calls for further elaboration. The paper motivates a program; it does not provide a worked relativistic system model and an independent operational rule for selecting its ontology.

Brading’s 2015 follow-up develops the proposal rather than closing that gap. Section 5 (pp. 16–18) reverses the order of explanation: identify genuine law-constituted unities first, then identify their present with the dynamically required region. It makes the anti-block requirement more explicit in P1** clause (a), while treating clause (b) as optional for avoiding the block alone. The author says whether the relevant dynamical conditions hold in the actual world remains an empirical question, and concedes that the approach needs substantial elaboration (p. 18). This is a real constitutive proposal, not merely an unspecified supervenience claim, but its key extension from unity to what exists remains to be argued and applied.

Balashov and Janssen’s 2003 paper is the closest source for the spacetime-constitutive side, though it addresses Craig’s neo-Lorentzian defense of presentism rather than Brading’s proposal. In §§9–11 of their full archive preprint, they argue that spacetime structure explains shared Lorentz-covariant behavior and prefer the spacetime interpretation on explanatory grounds. They also acknowledge a relationist can reverse the explanatory direction. This provides a specific spacetime-unity argument for comparison; it does not establish that spacetime must be the only source of system unity or settle Brading’s bridge.

Brown and Pooley’s 2004 draft is a further methodological comparison, not a direct reply to Brading or a defense of eternalism. In §§3–5 of arXiv v1, they dispute whether Minkowski geometry genuinely explains the Lorentz covariance of matter laws. They argue for the reverse explanatory direction, with dynamical laws grounding the geometrical regularities. This shows that debate over explanatory direction predates the present case; the rival reconstruction treats it as an alternative to the Balashov–Janssen explanatory direction, not as a claim that both directions hold together. It does not show that a block ontology is true, nor that the law-constitutive present follows. Their analysis supports allowing a block advocate to accept dynamical explanation without accepting Brading’s ontological restriction.

The closest sources and exact read limits are recorded in the companion report. The Brading articles were read in full at argument level from complete journal-paginated reproductions. Balashov and Janssen’s archive preprint was read in full at argument level; it is a longer preprint of their forthcoming journal article, so locators are to that preprint. Brown and Pooley was read in full at argument level in arXiv v1; it identifies itself as a draft for an edited volume, and no comparison against the later published chapter was performed.

## Failure mode, falsifier, and novelty check

Requiring Brading’s own clause (a) to call a realization an instance of her anti-block program is not by itself circular; it is a fidelity condition on her stated view. Circularity would arise if that disputed bridge were exported as an independent admissibility veto against rival packages, or if the definition that a present is restricted to the law-sustaining region were treated as evidence that only that region exists. Conversely, declaring the proposal empirically empty just because its ontology is not uniquely identifiable would use the empirical profile to settle an explanatory question it does not address. Neither move is justified here.

A result favoring Brading would require more than the fact that laws explain subsystem behavior. It would need an independently defended account of genuine systemhood and of why the dynamically required extent exhausts the existence of that system, plus an argument that a block rival accepting the same law-based explanation cannot preserve the same explanatory success. Because the account is system-relative and potentially nontransitive, the argument should not be expanded into a claim that all other systems outside one chosen system’s present are unreal. A result favoring the rival would likewise need to show that full-history existence is independently motivated and that the finite or sub-universe regions Brading identifies do not do the explanatory work she claims. An empirical discriminator would require a specified shared solution, observable/access protocol, and different likelihoods under the two packages.

The present case can be materially changed by a fully worked relativistic model that operationalizes a specified genuine system and its dynamically sustaining extent, or by a source argument establishing an independent bridge from such an extent to restricted existence for that system. It would also change if a fixed physical/access regime produced different testable probabilities. Until then, Brading’s own papers frame the question as open and empirical in the relevant sense, and Brown–Pooley already illustrate the contested explanatory direction. The admissibility exercise is therefore best presented as an exposition of assumption sensitivity and underdetermination, not as a new method or physical result.
