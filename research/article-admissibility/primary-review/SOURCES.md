## Katherine Brading, “Presentism as an Empirical Hypothesis” (2013)

Complete 11-page published-layout copy, *Philosophy of Science* 80(5), 1101–1111 (December 2013). Public copy URL: <https://a7ad7762-bf80-40ab-9c06-f74d25597b55.filesusr.com/ugd/766654_811c433ef66645b68a25658f93370442.pdf>. Local temporary copy SHA-256: `c93ca072e14c4fd0f1c638ff960db17847231479ec9bf168cf0d69f9644bb054`. Read full text (all 11 pages); no proof reconstruction claimed. The file is published-layout, but I did not collate it against the publisher's version.

Sections 1–3 (pp. 1101–1104) define the initial P1/P2 clash and identify spacetime as a shared ontological unity assumption. Section 4 (pp. 1104–1105) states, but explicitly does not argue for, law-constitution in Newton; the billiard-ball collision illustrates conservation/redistribution as unity. Section 5 (pp. 1105–1108) argues this account of persistence favors endurance over perdurance and perhaps presentism. Sections 6–8 (pp. 1108–1110) shift spacetime to an epistemic role and propose P1*: each thing exists only presently, with “present” extent dependent on dynamics and to be determined empirically. The article explicitly says the dynamical region may be as large as all spacetime; if no genuine subsystems exist, the proposal loses. It takes genuine subsystems as a starting position for the local-present illustration, then says scope needs much further work. No concrete subsystem criterion, common measurement/record protocol, probability model, or worked likelihood comparison is provided.

## Katherine Brading, “Physically Locating the Present: A Case of Reading Physics as a Contribution to Philosophy” (2015)

Complete 7-page published-layout copy, *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A* 50, 13–19 (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2014.09.007. Public copy URL: <https://a7ad7762-bf80-40ab-9c06-f74d25597b55.filesusr.com/ugd/766654_f45ceb55279c45f7b7e7c522d1c92c51.pdf>. Local temporary copy SHA-256: `7caceae74aebe28d1775561cd307dce50f912570eb1a7f9a709d70c699b08f6b`. Read full text (all seven pages); no reconstruction of Newton scholarship or SR proofs claimed. The file is published-layout, but I did not collate it against the publisher's version.

Sections 3–5 (journal pp. 15–18) restate the SR/P1-P2 conflict, present Newtonian law-constitutive unity, and apply it to a law-grounded alternative. Section 5 (pp. 17–18) distinguishes the epistemic role of spacetime from the ontological ground supplied by dynamics. On p. 18 Brading concedes that substantial elaboration remains and that whether there are genuine dynamical subsystems smaller than all spacetime is the empirical question on which the proposal depends. She also explicitly connects the dynamical-priority idea to Harvey Brown's *Physical Relativity* (2005), as a possible starting point, while leaving subsystem existence unresolved. The text advances a philosophical program, not a completed physical subsystem model or a demonstrated likelihood discriminator.

## Yuri Balashov and Michel Janssen, “Presentism and Relativity” (2003 preprint)

Complete public 30-page archive preprint, 13 sections, at <https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/525/1/presentism_and_relativity.pdf>. The file's own title page identifies it as a Pittsburgh Philosophy of Science Preprint Archive version and says a shorter version was forthcoming in *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*. The inspected text is this 30-page preprint, not a collated or verified BJPS edition; its section count/pages must not be transferred to the shorter published version. Local temporary copy SHA-256: `0f40d4e0c60b95f3275d6e934e58ca0babab58776d2882102db1cb4e25458011`. Read the full preprint, with claims assessed principally in §§4–11, especially §§9–11 (archive PDF pp. 17–24; §9 begins p.17, §10 p.21, §11 p.23); no rederivation of SR or Earman's symmetry principles claimed.

This is a direct philosophical rival on the explanatory role of spacetime, though it predates and does not answer Brading. Section 11 argues that spacetime interpretation unifies Lorentz invariance across matter laws, while a neo-Lorentzian dynamical account leaves their common Lorentz invariance unexplained; it appeals to explanatory common cause and symmetry principles. The authors acknowledge that relationists can reverse the direction and treat effective Lorentz invariance in dynamics as explaining the spacetime structure, and that their explanatory argument is not ironclad. Sections 9–10 analyze neo-Lorentzian auxiliary costs and explanatory payoffs. Their comparison concerns SR interpretations and a preferred frame, not Brading's law-constitutive identity or empirical subsystem boundaries. The text offers a strong opponent who allows dynamical laws, not evidence that Brading's ontology is false.

## Harvey Brown and Oliver Pooley, “Minkowski Space-Time: A Glorious Non-Entity” (2004 arXiv v1)

Complete arXiv v1, submitted 17 March 2004: <https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0403088v1>; official HTML full text: <https://arxiv.org/html/physics/0403088v1>. It is the 2004 draft, not collation against the 2006 published chapter “Minkowski Space-Time: A Glorious Non-Entity,” *Philosophy and Foundations of Physics* 1, 67–89. Local temporary PDF SHA-256: `8a68ac00cf91a2e80eca37e93f4734e114a78fcf530c1f222988a5563435d75d`. Read the full arXiv HTML; bounded argument use is §§3–5 (official HTML section locators; no PDF page locator asserted). No proof/derivation audit claimed.

This is methodological prior art only, not a direct Brading rebuttal (it predates her 2013 paper and focuses on the explanation of inertial structure and length contraction). Sections 3–5 distinguish principle explanations from constructive models, compare spacetime-geometric and dynamical explanations of relativistic effects, and argue about the direction of explanation; §4 concedes that geometric explanations can be acceptable in some contexts while questioning whether they count as constructive. This provides an earlier example of assumption-sensitive explanatory comparison. It neither argues for Brading's local-present bridge nor establishes a presentist/block likelihood difference.
