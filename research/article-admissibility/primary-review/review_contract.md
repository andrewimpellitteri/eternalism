# Bounded review contract

This is the one case fixed by `research/article-admissibility/PROTOCOL.md`: Katherine Brading's law-constitutive account of the unity and local present of physical systems, compared with the spacetime-constitutive unity interpretation she criticizes. The candidate result under test is explanatory preference, not a new physical prediction.

The sides are fixed as follows. Brading's proposal treats laws as constitutive of physical individuals, parts, and composites; the dynamical region sufficient to sustain a system is proposed to delimit that system's present. The strongest block rival is reconstructed for this test: it may accept the same subsystem decompositions, laws, and law-based explanation of local behavior and identity through change, while treating the system as a temporally extended part of a spacetime history and denying that its dynamical extent determines what exists. It may also argue that spacetime structure explains why diverse matter laws share Lorentz symmetry. Balashov and Janssen provide a nearby source for the latter explanatory argument; they do not jointly defend this composite rival to Brading. Neither side gets to define its disputed ontological predicate as an admissibility condition.

The sources do not supply a shared worked physical model with a common solution class, system boundary rule, record/measurement protocol, or likelihoods. Brading uses Newtonian free bodies and billiard-ball collisions to illustrate law-constitutive unity, then applies the proposal to special relativity's spacetime structure; she explicitly makes the existence and extent of genuine subsystems an open empirical issue. Balashov and Janssen discuss SR interpretation and explanatory direction (including rods and clocks), but do not test Brading's subsystem criterion. I therefore do not synthesize these into a model or claim that the packages have actually passed the protocol's eligibility checks. For the sensitivity test only, conditional eligibility and identical records/likelihoods are stipulated, not derived from those texts.

**Profile 1 — empirical/structural.** If both packages are conditionally eligible under the same SR and subsystem dynamics and record likelihoods are stipulated equal, neither ontology is empirically preferred. Brading's P1* is a conditional empirical hypothesis about subsystem extent, but no record set or likelihood is worked out in these sources. This conditional comparison does not establish actual eligibility and does not settle ontology.

**Profile 2 — constitutive explanation.** Credit both accounts for law-based explanations of local system behavior. Brading supplies a principled candidate for system unity, but the move from a law-constituted system to the claim that its dynamical extent is the extent of what presently exists needs an additional bridge. The rival can accept the unity explanation and deny that the explanatory/individuation role fixes ontological scope; it may also offer a competing spacetime-unity/common-cause explanation of Lorentz invariance. Brading explicitly states that she does not argue for the Newtonian law-constitutive account in the 2013 paper and concedes major elaboration remains. The profile yields underdetermination unless an independent, non-question-begging bridge criterion is defended. It does not license a score or an automatic block-universe victory.

**Verdict and novelty challenge.** The worked case is an underdetermination/circularity diagnosis, with an exposition-only result: under equal likelihoods and symmetric access, the result depends on which constitutive bridge one accepts. Brading herself presents the proposal as conditional and empirically open, while Balashov and Janssen already supply an assumption-sensitive explanatory comparison in which geometric and dynamical descriptions trade explanatory roles and acknowledge a relationist's reversal. Brown and Pooley provide a related earlier analysis of constructive and principle explanations, but not a direct reply to Brading. The article may accurately present this as a clarified case study or method demonstration; the case does not establish an ontology-neutral explanatory discriminator, a novel physical result, or an original refutation of Brading.

**Concrete falsifier / reopening condition.** This diagnosis would need revision if a specified model and operational record regime, common to both sides, showed unequal empirical likelihoods; or if an independently defended criterion mapped Brading's law-constitutive system boundaries to ontological scope while remaining acceptable to a block proponent who grants the same law-based local explanation. Either would move the dispute beyond the present conditional comparison.

**Paper-edit proposal only.** State that this is a conditional underdetermination case. Mark Brading's P1* and subsystem criterion as an argued bridge/program, not an already operationalized model. Present the block rival as accepting system dynamics and law explanation. Say explicitly that empirical likelihoods are matched only by stipulation because no shared access model is specified. Describe the result as exposition/diagnosis rather than a new discriminator or prediction.
