# Independent adversarial review: article-admissibility case

## Disposition

The case is a sound, bounded exposition of an underdetermination problem. I found no decisive logical error that reverses its result. It does not establish a new admissibility method, an empirical result, or a preference for either ontology. The result is appropriately limited to what the cited texts establish: they leave the bridge from law-constituted system unity to restricted existence without an independently defended discriminator.

Two wording safeguards would make the manuscript proposal harder to overread. These are precision revisions, not blockers to the case’s scoped conclusion.

## Adversarial checks

- **Does the likelihood tie follow from a model?** No. The cited papers provide no joint relativistic solution, operational subsystem rule, access protocol, or probability model for this comparison. The case says so, and labels equality as a project stipulation. That is consistent with the protocol’s conditional matched-model exercise. However, “both packages are admitted on the stipulated common model” could be mistaken for a completed admission test. No such model was actually selected or checked. In any paper version, call this *hypothetical conditional admission under a stipulated common physical/access model*, not admission established by the sources.
- **Does the rival get its strongest relevant commitments?** Yes, for the bounded question. It grants dynamical laws, local-system unity, and the same physical description, while rejecting only the inference that dynamical extent exhausts existence. It also includes the spacetime common-cause explanation for Lorentz covariance. This is a synthesized rival, not a single jointly asserted view from Balashov–Janssen and Brown–Pooley; describe it as the case’s reconstruction wherever attribution could be ambiguous. The two sources address different explanatory targets and do not jointly establish the reconstruction.
- **Is the explanatory conclusion circular?** The case does not claim that law-based unity proves restricted existence. It requires an independent argument for that bridge and applies the same burden to the full-history rival. That matches the registered constitutive profile. The defensible conclusion is evidential: these sources do not settle the bridge. Avoid wording that makes this metaphysical underdetermination across all possible arguments or models.
- **Is Brading’s commitment represented faithfully?** Yes. The report distinguishes 2013’s possible whole-universe extent from the 2015 P1** refinement: clause (a) is required for avoiding the block; clause (b) is optional for that narrower aim. It also preserves Brading’s own open empirical and elaboration qualifications rather than treating the proposed system criterion as operationally completed.
- **Does the case demonstrate novelty?** No. Brown–Pooley predates the case and makes explanatory direction in relativity a live methodological dispute; Balashov–Janssen supplies a concrete spacetime-explanatory rival, while acknowledging reverse direction. These sources do not perform this exact preregistered comparison, but the case supplies no basis for claiming the method or diagnosis is new. The case’s explicit “exposition-only” disposition is warranted.
- **Falsifier and scope:** The proposed reopening conditions are appropriate: a worked relativistic model with operational system criteria and records, an independent defense of the scope-to-existence bridge, or a specified regime with unequal testable likelihoods. Such evidence could change the current comparison; it is not implied by the present source review.

## Recommended wording checks

1. Preserve the explicit caveat that no common model or likelihoods were found in the sources. Describe the profile-1 result as a conditional sensitivity exercise, not an empirical evaluation of two instantiated theories.
2. Label the block-compatible package as a reconstructed composite rival. Attribute the spacetime explanation to Balashov–Janssen’s target dispute and the explanatory-direction comparison to Brown–Pooley; do not attribute the whole composite to either source.
3. Keep “underdetermination” scoped to the evidence and arguments inspected. State that the sources do not provide a neutral criterion deciding the disputed bridge, rather than implying no such criterion can exist.
