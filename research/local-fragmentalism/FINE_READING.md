## What is actually on pp. 304–305 (internal 70–71)

The space/time-asymmetry discussion. Fine asks whether, under SR, the
non-standard realist can retain the distinction between the spatial and the
temporal:

> "However, one is not forced to treat space and time alike. SR embodies
> enough of an asymmetry between space and time that the difference between
> the spatial and temporal forms of indexicality can be retained. Indeed, one
> can simply take spatial indexicality to be relativity to a frame-place pair
> and temporal indexicality to be relativity to a frame-time pair." (p. 70)

and then the anti-chauvinism move:

> "Each (representative) standpoint will give rise to its own reality and no
> one can be singled out as being the standpoint of reality. If it is asked
> 'why is your standpoint not also a standpoint of reality?', then the answer
> is that it is, regardless of your world-line or location and regardless of
> what might be true at your standpoint." (p. 71)

So Lipman's report is right: this is where Fine answers the "distinguished
character" worry by making every standpoint a standpoint of reality, with the
space/time asymmetry carried by the frame-place/frame-time pair structure.
This is an objection to *locational and frame-indexed* standpoints' ability to
mark the space/time distinction in a privileged way — and it is the passage
local fragmentalism must answer. The answer stands: the causal-past fragment
makes "here" as objective as "now" (both relative to b), the asymmetry is
internal to the fragment (the timelike past is real, spacelike events are
not), and in GR the content is derived (R1), not stipulated by a frame.

## The finding that holds regardless of pagination: fn. 36

> "We cannot sensibly take such claims to be relative to an event or
> momentary object, even if our ontology allowed for such things." …
> "Though I was interested to discover that Sprigge [92] appears to hold such
> a view. He writes (p. 12), 'every event must be present from its own point
> of view, and as it really is' … But the notion of each event having its own
> 'perspective' on reality is decidedly odd; and I assume that there must be
> some more plausible way in which one might accommodate the universal
> presentness of events within our overall conception of what is real."
> (internal pp. 78–79)

Fine waves event-perspectives away without argument. Local fragmentalism is a
candidate for the "more plausible way" he assumes must exist, because it
specifies what an event's perspective contains (its causal past) and why
(locality across spacetimes, R1). This is the precise delta over Fine — it
does not depend on any page mapping.

Also confirmed: fn. 31, "This is essentially the framework of Clifton &
Hogarth [95]" (internal pp. 67–68) — the junction between Fine's framework
and the Stein/CH becoming literature is explicit in Fine's own text, and what
an event's standpoint *contains* is left open exactly there.

## Consequences for the ledger

- O11 (as updated in STRONGEST_VIEW.md): the concession stands. Fine's
  304–305 passage is a real objection to locational standpoints' space/time
  marking, and the view answers it with (a) the fragment-internal asymmetry
  and (b) the general-relativity derivation — not by claiming Fine never
  objected.
- O9: Fine said fragmentalism; he never said what an event's fragment
  contains, and set event-perspectives aside without argument (fn. 36). The
  derivation claim survives this reading, now documented at the textual
  level.
- The paper can cite: Fine 2005 pp. 304–305 (frame-place/frame-time asymmetry
  answer), p. 306 vicinity ("Newtonian in structure", per Savitt), §11
  (fragmentalism over relativism), and fn. 36 (the conceded gap).
