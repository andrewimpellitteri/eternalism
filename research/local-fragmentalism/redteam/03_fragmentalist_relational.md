## Headline findings before the sections

1. **Rakić 1997 (BJPS 48, 257-280) is the closest prior anticipation of the formal frame, and it is not in PRIOR_WORK as such.** She indexes ontological status to events: eRe' = "with respect to e, e' is realized". R is transitive, reflexive, contains the causal past, excludes the causal future, and she explicitly cites Stein's theorem (and Clifton-Hogarth) to show that if R were definable from Minkowski structure it would be exactly the causal past (her constraint (d)). She then **rejects** that outcome (the "seafight" argument, pp. 264-265) and makes R a primitive not definable from causal structure (Theorems 5(i)-(ii): some elsewhere events are realized, some not). Local fragmentalism's R1 is, in her vocabulary, constraint (d) derived from a locality principle. So the view is not novel in framing, and it directly contradicts a published rival result on the same frame. The paper cites Rakić 1997 only as a "point-based present"; that is a mischaracterisation (her presents are PRES = mutual realization, i.e. non-punctual).
2. **Dieks 2006 and Savitt 2005 both treat becoming/passage as local and survive Gödel universes.** Local fragmentalism's R7 (collapse to eternalism in totally vicious spacetimes) is a cost that these views avoid.
3. **Fine's relativism/fragmentalism distinction cuts the view in half**: the invariant "B(x,b) iff x in J^-(b)" is a tenseless fact stated from outside every fragment, and P7/R6 gives a coherent whole. That is the structure of external relativism, not fragmentalism.
4. **The Hofweber-Lange objection returns as a dilemma** (section 2).

## 1. Kit Fine: external relativism and fragmentalism

**Steelman.** Realism about tense (an objective, absolute distinction between present and non-present) conflicts with relativity only if reality must be a single coherent whole. Standard realism picks a privileged standpoint (chauvinism). External relativism makes tensed facts relative to standpoints, which concedes that there are no absolute tensed facts. Fragmentalism keeps tensed facts absolute but lets reality be incoherent as a whole: it divides into fragments, each internally coherent, with no coherent union. In the relativistic setting, each frame-time standpoint gives its own reality. Fine's point is that the interesting choice is between relativizing facts (relativism) and fragmenting reality (fragmentalism), and only the second preserves the metaphysical force of tense.

**Strongest attacks on local fragmentalism.**
(a) *Relativism in fragmentalist dress (targets P1, P5, R6, R6', O1, O13).* Fragmentalism is the thesis that the fragments are fundamental and there is no coherent whole. Local fragmentalism asserts a single global metatheory: the causal order of M, the map b -> F(b) = J^-(b), and (with P7) the totality F(M) = M, "the view from the whole is the block". All of this is stated from a standpoint that is not any event's standpoint and that is coherent. Fine's fragmentalist has no such standpoint; that is the whole point of the view. A theory with an invariant geometry, a derived map from events to subsets, and a coherent union is a relational theory: "x is real-from b" is the tenseless relation x <= b. The only residual "fragmentalism" is the idea that the sentence "x is present" is absolutely true in F(x), but then so is every indexical truth on any indexical semantics. The text says (O1) that the relativist reading "still yields R1-R8", which concedes that the theorems are neutral between relativism and fragmentalism. So the view's name advertises something the arguments do not use.
(b) *Fine's chauvinism and the symmetry between fragments.* Fine's fragments are incompatible with each other (a square object at rest in one, non-square in another). Local fragments are nested (F(b) subset of F(b') when b <= b') and spatially disjoint ones overlap. Nothing in the view is incoherent across fragments except the indexical "present"/"future" verdicts. Different pairs of fragments are incompatible only in what is tensed-present. So the view needs the incoherence only for the thin tense facts; it is not Fine's fragmentalism about spacetime facts (the Lorentz-related incompatibilities), which is what Fine's STR section is about.
(c) *The space/time asymmetry (Fine 2005 on locational standpoints, O11).* Already handled in the file; I add that the reply (a) concedes that "here" is as objective as "now", which is exactly Fine's stated reason for rejecting the locational view. The structural asymmetry (fragments grow along future-directed displacement) is a **tenseless** asymmetry of the invariant order; a B-theorist has it for free. So the reply does not restore what Fine thought presentists needed.
(d) *Bridge, not theorem.* Fine would say the entire argument rests on reading "x has become for b" as "x is real from b". Nothing in the Stein-GR result supports this identification versus "x is determined for b", "x is causally accessible to b", or "x is in b's domain of dependence". This is the bridge the file already tags THESIS; Fine's contribution is to show that the bridge is exactly what the dispute is about.

**Already answered?** O9, O11, O1, O2 address pieces. O1's remark that the relativist reading avoids non-standard realism but "still yields R1-R8" does not answer (a); it is the concession (a) exploits. The answer "the view replaces the absolute-existence question" (red team A/B in RED_TEAM.md, not yet in STRONGEST_VIEW) is the honest move. O11(b) (no global frames in GR) is a good reply to the frame-vs-event choice, not to (a).

**Severity.** Serious for the *name and the claim of resolving the divide*; manageable for the physics. If the view is relativism, the headline "presentists and eternalists are each right about the standpoints they recognise" loses force because the eternalist's "unprefixed existence" simply is the invariant tenseless discourse.

**Best reply.** Say plainly that there are two versions: a **relativist** local theory (a theory of determinacy/accessibility plus indexical semantics, strongly supported) and a **fragmentalist** one that adds that the fragments are fundamental and the invariant geometry is not a further fact "behind" them. Give the second a defined job: it must say why the geometry is not explanatorily prior (see H&L below). Cost: either concede the relativist reading as the main thesis (and retitle "resolution" as "reduction of the dispute to which relation tense talk tracks"), or accept the burden of a primitive-coherence story that H&L attack.

**Prior anticipation.** Fine 2006, p. 411-412 already has event-indexed standpoints each with "its own reality" (cited in the file). Fine n. 31 calls the world-line/location framework "essentially" Clifton-Hogarth's. See also Rakić below for the content.

**Sources.** Fine, "Tense and Reality", in *Modality and Tense* (OUP 2005), 261-320; "The Reality of Tense", *Synthese* 150 (2006) 399-414. Read depth: full-text per PRIOR_WORK (§§9-11 and 2006); I did not re-read.

## 2. Hofweber & Lange on Fine

**Steelman.** (*Noûs* 2016/17.) Fine's fragmentalism about STR makes frame-time fragments fundamental. But frame-relative facts are related by Lorentz transformations. The standard explanation is that these are projections of a single frame-invariant reality (the interval), so frame facts are appearances. The fragmentalist must treat the transformations as brute, or let an "über-reality" (the incoherent collection) explain them, which requires the division of facts into fragments to be explained by coherence, which Fine takes as primitive. Likelihood: cr(laws | fragmentalism) low, cr(laws | fragments are appearances) high. The point generalises (§IV): any fragmentalism needs harmony among fragments (e.g. every fragment with "e1 present, e1 earlier than e2" contains "e2 future").

**Strongest form of the objection against local fragmentalism (the coordination objection returns as a dilemma).**
Local fragments are *not* brute-coordinated: F(b) = J^-(b), F is monotone along causal order. O10 says the coordination is "the causal order itself". Now ask which is explanatorily prior.
- *Horn 1: the causal geometry is prior.* Then the invariant structure explains why the fragments are as they are (this is literally what R1 says: locality plus Stein-type constraints fix F(b)). H&L's explanatory order ("How things are explains how things appear from a perspective") then applies verbatim: the fragments are derived, determined by an invariant common reality. That is H&L's rival A ("fragments are mere appearances of a common underlying reality"), and cr(C|A) is high by construction. The fragmentalist's claim that fragments are *fundamental* is lost.
- *Horn 2: the fragments are prior.* Then the fact that they nest according to a Lorentzian partial order, and that the apparent invariant geometry is recoverable from them, is brute coordination; locality (P3) is then a brute constraint on the fragment-assignment, not a derivation.
The paper's own methodology is Horn 1: the theorem takes M's causal structure as input and outputs F. So the view as proved is Horn 1, and the "fragmentalist" gloss lives in the metaphysics section only.
Second, subtler form (§IV "harmony"): the view must explain why every fragment obeys "if x < b, x is past in F(b); x is not present in F(b)". Answer: locality plus causal sufficiency. Good, but again explained by the invariant structure.

**Already answered?** O10 gives the Horn-1 answer ("not brute: the causal order") and calls it "an advantage over frame fragmentalism". It does not notice that this is the concession H&L want: the fragment is not explanatorily basic. The STRONGEST_VIEW does not say the fragments are fundamental (P1 only says there is a fragment F(b)), so O10 is coherent but it forfeits the "fragmentalist" label (see Fine, (a)).

**Severity.** Serious (to the label "fragmentalism" and to the claim that O10 is an advantage); not fatal to the relativist reading, where H&L's objection is simply accepted: tense facts are appearances of an invariant causal structure.

**Best reply.** Embrace Horn 1 and the relativist reading; argue that the interesting content is the **identification of which relation of the invariant structure "has become" tracks**, not that fragments are fundamental. Alternatively argue (as Lipman 2023 does, from memory) that standpoints are a primitive structure of the world and invariant structure is what they have in common, and defend a symmetric (non-priority) relation between geometry and fragments. Cost: concede that the physics supports relativism, and that fragmentalism is an extra, independent, H&L-exposed commitment.

**Prior anticipation.** None of local fragmentalism. H&L target frame fragments; but note they say (§IV) that the problem is general to any fragmentalism requiring harmony. So the objection was stated in generalised form and was published in 2016.

**Sources.** Hofweber & Lange, "Fine's Fragmentalist Interpretation of Special Relativity", *Noûs* (2016, doi 10.1111/nous.12150), sections II-IV. Full-text (local copy in scratchpad audit-fine/hofweber_fine.txt). Page locators not available in the text I read; I cite by section.

## 3. Martin Lipman: standpoints

**Steelman.** (*J. Phil.* 2023; *Phil. Studies* 2018/2020; *Standpoints: Time and Subjectivity*, OUP 2026.) Reality contains many standpoints; facts obtain relative to standpoints, and a hybrid-logic framework models standpoint-shifting with a reflexive accessibility relation R and a domain function v(w). Lipman's pluralist theory of time reconciles A- and B-theorists by positing an atemporal standpoint plus temporal ones. In the 2018 paper he defends frame fragments and notes (n. 1) that other fragmentalist interpretations "stay much closer to the Minkowskian conception", such as Stein's single-event view, and that Fine (2005: 304-305) briefly argues against it.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Priority on §2b (target the "new classification" claim).* If the standpoint frame is Lipman's, what the view adds is only the physics-derived content of v(b). Lipman's own 2026 chapters ("A Fragmentalist Theory of Time", "Spacetime and the Lessons of Relativistic Physics", chs. 4 and 6 per PRIOR_WORK) are unread and plausibly give a relativistic version of his framework. If they argue that event-standpoints with causal-past domains are the right relativistic version, the novelty claim shrinks to the locality derivation. This is a **discovery-level** risk, not an established finding: publisher blurb says the book "helps explain the passage of time and special relativity" and a talk "Fragmentalism reconfigured" says his approach "bears strong resemblance to fragmentalism" but differs "in key respects" (web search, no full text).
(b) *Lipman's atemporal standpoint is a real standpoint, and P7 does not derive it.* R6'/O13 claim the totality's fragment F(M) = M is the atemporal standpoint, "derived rather than posited". But P7 is an optional posit ("a region R may count as a standpoint"); F(R) = union is a definition; the view then lists the totality as a standpoint by fiat. And Lipman's atemporal standpoint is one from which temporal standpoints are accessible; the union is a *standpoint with no tense* only if we add that no tensed fact obtains from it. That is a further premise.
(c) *Constraint "if w1 R w2 then w2 in v(w1)" with R(b,x) iff x in v(b).* Reflexivity and the constraint hold by construction, but R(b,x) holds for x in the past, so from b, **x is a standpoint**. For a past event x, "from x, x's causal past is real" is then accessible from b, which entails b can "occupy" x's standpoint. This interacts with transitivity (P2): what is real from x is real from b. Fine; but it means standpoints at spacelike separation are *inaccessible to each other*. Lipman's logic allows adopting standpoints (shifting); the view says no event can adopt the standpoint of a spacelike event. What happens to the semantics of "from over there, now"? That is a coverage gap, not a flaw.
(d) *Subjectivity.* Lipman's book is about time *and* subjectivity; standpoints are bound to subjects. An event is not a subject. If standpoint-hood requires experience or agency (as in his treatment of the first person, from memory), then "every event is a standpoint" is not Lipman's notion, and the identification in §2b ("Local fragmentalism is a class of such models") is formal only.

**Already answered?** §2b and O13 handle (b)-(c) at the level of formal embedding; they do not meet (d) or (a). Status of (a) is explicitly "must read" in PRIOR_WORK.

**Severity.** (a) potentially serious for novelty, unresolved until Lipman 2026 and I&T ch. 4 are read. (b)-(d) manageable.

**Best reply.** Read chs. 4 and 6 before any priority claim; reduce the claim to what the theorem delivers (content of v(b) and the locality derivation). For (d) say the model class is formal, and the experiential bridge goes via O7. Cost: concede "R6' derived" and describe it as definitional.

**Prior anticipation.** Lipman 2018 n. 1 (single-event Stein view as a fragmentalist option); Lipman 2023 (framework). Possible but unverified anticipation in Lipman 2026.

**Sources.** Lipman, "On the fragmentalist interpretation of special relativity", *Phil. Studies* 177 (2020; online 2018), doi 10.1007/s11098-018-1178-4 (local copy; note 1 per PRIOR_WORK); "Standpoints: a study of a metaphysical picture", *J. Phil.* 120(3) 2023, 117-138 (full-text per PRIOR_WORK); *Standpoints: Time and Subjectivity*, OUP 2026, ISBN 9780198921288 (discovery only: publisher and library listings found by web search).

## 4. Iaquinto & Torrengo (Flow Fragmentalism); Loss; Calosi

**Steelman.** Flow Fragmentalism (Torrengo & Iaquinto, *Theoria* 2019; *Fragmenting Reality*, Bloomsbury 2022) is a non-standard A-theory: each moment's tensed facts are absolute, but reality as a whole is fragmented so that incompatible tensed facts are never co-obtaining. The claim is that this secures real passage while the facts "supervene on being". The book extends it to relativity (ch. 4 per Andreoletti's review and publisher abstract), causation and time travel. Calosi, Iaquinto & Loss (*AJP* 2026) develop related issues (PRIOR_WORK: no relativity content seen).

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Anaemic passage (O12).* Andreoletti's review: from the global perspective, reality "features no passage at all". The view agrees (the totality is static). A flow fragmentalist would say the view's "passage" (growth along each worldline, R3) is a monotone relation among fragments, i.e. the B-relation "F(b) subset F(b')", which a B-theorist has too. The relation that makes F(b') *later* than F(b) is in the invariant order; the fragmentalist's passage is the thing the fragments disagree about, and in local fragmentalism they disagree only in tense verdicts that all follow from position in the invariant order.
(b) *Time travel / CTCs.* Flow Fragmentalism includes a treatment of time travel as a selling point. In totally vicious spacetimes the view collapses to eternalism (R7, Prop. 20). A defender of fragmentalism would say that a theory of passage that fails exactly where fragmentation is most needed (closed causal loops) is incomplete, and R7's "locality isolates such universes" is an evasion, since possible-world locality is exactly the question (see Dieks, Savitt below).
(c) *Truth supervenes on being (TSB).* Past-tensed truths supervene on past facts. In the view, a future-tensed truth about b, "b' will occur" (b' > b), is true in F(b)? F(b) does not contain b'. So future-tensed truths about events in the causal elsewhere and future of b lack truthmakers in F(b), fine for an open-future view; but the same holds for "b' is occurring now elsewhere" statements, which Flow Fragmentalism distinguishes. Nothing in the view says what the truth conditions of elsewhere-tensed sentences are. This is a gap rather than an attack.

**Already answered?** O12 only for (a), by conceding the static whole. R7 and O4 for (b), partially; they declare Gödel universes outside our world, which Dieks and Savitt (below) show is a cost others avoid. (c) not addressed.

**Severity.** (a) serious (a central target, as the file says); (b) manageable but a real asymmetry with rivals; (c) manageable.

**Best reply.** For (a) hold the line: passage is a relation of fragments, not a flow; cost: the strongest A-theorist ("real passage must be non-relational") is not satisfied. For (b) concede R7 as an explicit boundary of the view.

**Prior anticipation.** Possibly ch. 4 of I&T (unread). Treat as unresolved.

**Sources.** Iaquinto & Torrengo, *Fragmenting Reality: An Essay on Passage, Causality and Time Travel* (Bloomsbury 2022; abstract confirmed via UniUPO repository listing, discovery); Torrengo & Iaquinto, *Theoria* 2019 (discovery); Andreoletti, *Organon F* 32(4) 2025, 491-500 (full-text per PRIOR_WORK); Calosi, Iaquinto & Loss, *AJP* 104(2) 2026 (grep only). Loss: I did not find or read a Loss paper on relativistic fragmentalism; no claim made.

## 5. Dennis Dieks: local becoming; radical perspectivalism

**Steelman.** Dieks 2006 ("Becoming, relativity and locality", in Dieks (ed.), *The Ontology of Spacetime*, ch. 8, pp. 157-176). Becoming is local: the successive happening of events at their own spacetime locations. It is "a deflationary analysis": becoming is "nothing but the happening of events, in their temporal order", with the temporal order being the light-cone partial order; nothing is added to the block, and the worldline proper-time ordering gives becoming "with respect to an object or causal process". The view is explicitly designed to survive Gödel-type universes. Dieks 2025 (*Found. Phys.* 55:30) is a different project: quantum perspectives from Everett relative states, "independent of each other and remain so even when they make causal contact".

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Already in this literature? Dieks 2006 has the locality motivation and the anti-Gödel robustness, but not the content.* Dieks gives no "reality-from-b" relation and no causal-past fragments. He says becoming = happening, so every event "comes into being at its own location" and nothing is added to the block. So Dieks's becoming is eternalist in ontology. Local fragmentalism could be called Dieks's local becoming plus the claim that "has come into being for b" is a non-trivial restriction. But Dieks says that is not needed: he does not make the future non-existent from b. The relevant point is that **Dieks denies the key bridge (P2: becoming = reality-from-a-standpoint)**. For him becoming is not relative reality at all.
(b) *Gödel.* Dieks explicitly says local becoming "can be accommodated in all models of general relativity, even in universes with a complicated Gödel-like temporal ordering structure" and shows the "moving now" fails there (Reichenbach example). Local fragmentalism says in these universes F(b) = everything, no fragmentation. The Dieks-style critic: a theory of becoming that is silent exactly in the universe that Gödel used to argue against passage hands Gödel the argument, restricted to such worlds.
(c) *Radical perspectivalism undermines R3 (fragment inclusion) at the level of facts (targets O6, O10, R3).* O6 appeals to Dieks 2025 as the route that keeps P3 compatible with entanglement. But on Dieks 2025 perspectives are independent even after causal contact: Bob's later fact about Alice's outcome need not agree with Alice's own fact. Local fragmentalism's coordination (F(b) subset F(b') for b <= b') requires that what is real from b remain real from b' **with its content**. If Alice's outcome event a is in Bob's fragment F(b') (b' later, contacted), is the *fact that a had outcome "up"* in F(b')? In radical perspectivalism, not necessarily. So the coordination of O10 holds for events as bare points, not for what is the case at them; the real content of "has become for b" is exactly the contents. Either the inclusion is only of event-sets (then fragments are just subsets of M and carry no facts, the relativist reading), or inclusion of facts holds (then O6's route is not available). This is a real internal tension, specific to the R3/O6 pair.
(d) *Causal contact in EPR.* P5: facts at standpoints are invariant only if they hold from every standpoint; Alice's outcome at Alice's event is real only from standpoints in its causal future, so is not absolute. The RED_TEAM.md note C (EPR correlations are not absolute facts) is the same point; Dieks gives no help with Born-rule statistics across perspectives beyond "laws are valid in all perspectives" (his section on shared theories).

**Already answered?** O6 uses Dieks 2025 explicitly but does not address (c). R7 is the only answer to (b). Priority: PRIOR_WORK notes "no priority conflict" for Dieks 2025; I confirm none for 2025 and **partial anticipation of the motivation (not the content) in Dieks 2006**.

**Severity.** (c) serious (a hidden conflict between two claims the view needs). (b) manageable. (a) manageable for priority, but the "deflationary" alternative is a competing resolution of the same dispute worth stating as such.

**Best reply.** For (c): separate event-level coordination (what is the case at which events, the causal skeleton) from fact-level coordination, and restrict O6 to say that the view needs only event-level inclusion, with outcomes handled by whichever interpretation. Cost: the "fragments" now carry minimal content, and the quantum route no longer shows that facts accumulate along worldlines; that weakens R3's "reality accrues". For (a)-(b) cite Dieks as the deflationary rival and keep R7 explicit.

**Prior anticipation.** Dieks 2006 anticipates: locality as the reason to give up global nows; light-cone partial order as the structure of becoming; world-line becoming; robustness in Gödel universes. Does not anticipate: causal-past reality-for-an-event, a non-trivial notion of "not yet real for b", a theorem.

**Sources.** Dieks, "Becoming, relativity and locality" (Elsevier 2006, doi 10.1016/S1871-1774(06)01008-4), full-text, cited by section (§4-5 for the deflationary proposal and conclusion; Gödel discussion in §5); Dieks, "Radical Perspectivalism, Locality and Relativity", *Found. Phys.* 55:30 (2025), doi 10.1007/s10701-025-00844-3, abstract and the EPR/contact sections read (grep-level).

## 6. Carlo Rovelli: "Neither presentism nor eternalism"; relational QM

**Steelman.** (arXiv:1910.02474v2; *Found. Phys.* 2019.) "Fundamental becoming is real, but local and unoriented." The present is a spacetime point ("the 'here now'") or a finite region chosen terminologically ("a matter of convenience, not of ontology"). Temporal structure is the light-cone causal structure of the manifold; distinct local becomings are "weaved to one another" by the geometry; reality needs a "when" and a "where" ("There are facts that are real now on Earth, facts that were real in the past ... and also facts that are real on a distant galaxy at a time which is neither in our future, nor in our past, but nevertheless they are in the past of one another"). He also gives a reply to McTaggart: each event of a B-series determines a different "now", which locally promotes the B-series to an A-series, so no regress and no contradiction. Relational QM (from memory): facts about a system are relative to another system, with no view from nowhere.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *"What is real?" is an ill-defined question without "when"/"where".* Rovelli says the question is ill-defined without a location; the view, in effect, supplies the answer function for the sub-question "what is real from b". The Rovelli-style critic says: you have given the nonexistent unrestricted question a restricted answer, but you then proceed (R6, P7) to also answer the unrestricted question ("the totality's fragment is M"), which he declines to do. Thus P7 is not just optional but un-Rovellian.
(b) *Terminological present.* For Rovelli the choice among present notions is not ontological. If so, "J^-(b) is real from b" is one more terminological choice, so the theorem classifies definitions, not realities. This is a version of O2.
(c) *Relational QM and the independence of perspectives.* RQM says facts are relative to systems, and relative facts of different systems are compared only upon interaction (and then via the cross-perspective consistency principle in his formulation, from memory). That is a different coordination condition from F(b) subset F(b'). RQM can be read as local fragmentalism's quantum analogue, but its facts-at-contact condition is a quantum consistency axiom, not inclusion.
(d) *Direction.* Rovelli's becoming is *unoriented* at the fundamental level, with direction emerging from coarse-graining. Local fragmentalism uses the time orientation of J^- as a primitive input (Rakić's remark that without time orientation constraints (a) and (c) contradict). So the view's fragments presuppose an objective direction, which Rovelli says is thermodynamic/perspectival. That is a distinction among causal order, thermodynamic arrow and ontology the view should state: the theorem requires only a time orientation, which is a causal-order input; whether it is fundamental is an open independent question.

**Already answered?** Not addressed: (a), (d). (b) is O2's concession.

**Severity.** (a), (b) manageable (they say the view is more than Rovelli wants). (d) manageable but unnoticed; flag it.

**Best reply.** State that time orientation is an input (as in Stein and Clifton-Hogarth), distinguished from the arrow; accept that Rovelli's version is the more modest cousin (local becoming without claims of uniqueness).

**Prior anticipation.** Substantial in spirit, and I have to correct PRIOR_WORK's "close in spirit; no causal-past identification". Rovelli identifies the temporal structure with the light-cone structure, says facts are real "in the past of one another" at distant locations, and says each event promotes B to A. Not a derivation, not "reality from b = causal past" stated exactly, but "what is real is indexed by here-and-when and follows the cone structure" is held.

**Sources.** Rovelli, "Neither Presentism nor Eternalism", arXiv:1910.02474v2 (8 Feb 2020), full-text (local scratchpad copy); sections cited: Intro, §V "What is real now?", §VI "The mistake in McTaggart's argument". RQM: from memory (Rovelli 1996, *Int. J. Theor. Phys.*), not re-read.

## 7. Steven Savitt: the diamond present

**Steelman.** Savitt 2009 ("The transient nows", in *Quantum Reality, Relativistic Causality, and Closing the Epistemic Circle*), 2005 ("Time travel and becoming", *Monist* 88, 413-422) and 2015 ("I heart diamonds", *SHPMP* 50, 19-24). The present relative to two timelike-separated events p < q is the causal diamond I+(p) intersect I-(q). A series of such presents along a worldline represents passage, locally. Existence is two-tiered: all events exist "in the detensed sense" (they are events in the spacetime), while "only that point and its associated present exist (in the tensed sense)"; others disagree, "but we are used to such disagreements in relativity" (Monist 2005, p. 419). Passage is deflationary and does not require Cauchy surfaces; it is available in Gödel spacetimes with CTCs. Savitt (2015, fn 7) notes Gibson & Pooley's "Stein presents" are his diamonds, and that no region of spacetime is metaphysically privileged.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The two-tier existence structure is already this view's architecture.* Savitt 2005 has detensed existence (all events; the block) and tensed existence relative to a worldline segment. The view's invariant/tensed split (P5) and R6 (totality = block) are the same structure with a different relation for the tensed tier (causal past instead of a small diamond). The attack: the view is Savitt's two-tier ontology with the tensed tier enlarged from the diamond to J^-(b). The theorem decides between them: diamonds are not transitive/monotone under P2 (Dorato: Alexandroff regions are not closed under the relevant relation), and cannot have the "chronological inclusion" property, so the choice of P2 does real work.
(b) *Gödel again.* Savitt 2005's main point is that Gödel's argument fails because passage is local; local fragmentalism concedes it in vicious spacetimes (R7). Direct clash with Savitt's central thesis.
(c) *Why not diamonds?* The diamond present is exactly the mutual-influence relation (Stein 1991, quoted by Arthur: events "contemporaneous" if signals can pass both ways between them) and respects P3 (locality) because the diamond lies in a causally convex region. So P3 does not select J^- over diamonds. Selection is by P2 + P6 (inclusion and causal sufficiency), which the view imposes as meaning of "has become". Savitt's reply: a presentist wanting "reality" can have the diamond as its present; the past is real in the growing sense. Not an attack on the theorem but on the claim (R9) of uniqueness: the uniqueness holds only given (i)-(v), and the diamond view denies (ii) for the present.
(d) Dorato's truncated past light cone (Savitt 2015 section on Dorato, where the present of b is the part of the past cone within 30 ms of b): another "present" tied to the causal past. Any theory that takes the whole past cone as the present is dismissed (Savitt notes it "extends backwards in time almost to the Big Bang"). Local fragmentalism takes the whole past cone as *real*, not present, so this does not hit, but readers will conflate the two; explicit distinction needed (O7 does this).

**Already answered?** O7 answers experience/specious-present (diamonds as experiential windows, not co-reality). R7 and O4 partially for Gödel. Not answered: (a) the architecture parallel (the view's text claims Savitt only supplies a "diamond present" with no curved-spacetime classification); the parallel two-tier existence claim in Savitt 2005 is not acknowledged.

**Severity.** (a) manageable but a novelty-framing correction. (b) serious as an asymmetry (same as Dieks).

**Best reply.** Cite Savitt 2005 for the two-tier idea and say what's new is (1) the tensed tier's content derived from locality, and (2) the claim that no extended present exists at all. Cost: conceding that the architecture is not new.

**Prior anticipation.** Savitt 2005, p. 419 (detensed existence for all; tensed existence relative to location/present). Arthur and Savitt (2006, 2009): diamond presents. Gibson & Pooley 2006 (Savitt 2015 fn 7; not read).

**Sources.** Savitt, "Time travel and becoming", *Monist* 88 (2005) 413-422, full-text; "I heart diamonds", *SHPMP* 50 (2015) 19-24, full-text; "The transient nows" (Springer 2009, doi 10.1007/978-1-4020-9107-0_18): not re-read, relied on Savitt 2015 and Dorato 2011. Dorato, "The Alexandroff present and Minkowski spacetime" (Springer 2011), grep-level.

## 8. Richard Arthur: Minkowski spacetime and the dimensions of the present

**Steelman.** (Dieks (ed.), *The Ontology of Spacetime*, 2006, ch. 7, pp. 129-155.) Against the block universe: the four-dimensional universe "cannot be said to exist now, already, or indeed at any time at all", so "existence in the manifold" is not a sense of existence that can pre-empt becoming. Against the relativised present: time lapse is measured by proper time, not coordinate time (twin paradox). Becoming is a sequence along each worldline, "like water flowing through an uncorrelated, non-denumerable set of narrow creeks" (Dorato's image quoted by Arthur). He discusses the "punctual present" (Robb, Čapek, Stein): "what is present at a given spacetime point is constituted by that point alone". Following Stein, he glosses "real for" as "has become real for" and the content as the topological closure of the past (backward cone included): "what has occurred at a spacetime point is what lies in its absolute past", real "in the sense of 'having become determinate for'". But the punctual present is "decidedly harsh" and subject to Zeno-style objections, so he proposes the interactive/diamond present for extended events.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Local fragmentalism = Arthur's section 4 punctual present + presentist converse; and Arthur already rejects it.* Arthur reports Stein's remark that "present implies real" with its converse yields solipsism, treats the punctual present as "formally correct" but empirically and logically problematic: becoming cannot occur in an instant. The view's R2 (from b, b alone is present) is exactly the punctual present, and Arthur's Zenonian objection applies: if becoming takes place in the present and the present is a point, there is no becoming. The view's reply must be that becoming is not in the present but in the growth of fragments along a worldline (R3). OK, but that reply is just Arthur's own resolution (becoming over durations), with the interval's content being F(b'') minus F(b).
(b) *Existence-sense argument.* Arthur's claim that the block does not "exist" in any temporal sense, so "everything exists" is not a rival to "the present exists", threatens the view's table (§4, row 4): "The union of fragments is the spacetime" is the claim that the block exists from the whole, in a sense Arthur argues is not a temporal-existence sense. If so, the eternalist's unprefixed existence is simply atemporal, and there's no conflict to resolve: the view's O2 "each side is right about the standpoints it recognises" is a verbal reconciliation of Arthur's own diagnosis ("a non-sequitur"), so the dispute was never between rivals. This is stronger than a standard attack because it comes from a defender of becoming.
(c) *Real-for transitivity.* Arthur's presentation of Putnam: if "real for" is transitive, the future is real for a. His diagnosis: "x is real for y" should be "x has become real for y", for which transitivity holds without the Putnam consequence. That is P2's rationale (STRONGEST_VIEW already gives it via "has happened by"); but Arthur indicates this requires a **reinterpretation of "real"** as "determinate", so the view is a determinacy view (Stein). The bridge from "determinate for b" to "ontologically real from b" is where (b) bites.
(d) *Interactive present is the live alternative for the very object in dispute (shared present).* The interactive present is a co-reality class for extended events, which the view's R2 declares non-existent for points. So the view and Arthur disagree about whether any shared (extended) present exists; locality does not decide because the diamond is local (see Savitt (c)). R8's trilemma ("locality, extended present, non-trivial becoming cannot all be had") needs a careful scope: it applies to presents where each member has become for each other (mutual realization), not to diamonds whose members need not be mutually past (the paper's own caveat, "Other notions of a present ... are not addressed"). So R8 is narrower than §4's diagnosis suggests.

**Already answered?** O7 (diamonds as experiential windows) answers (d) for experience only; the paper's trilemma caveat covers the logical scope. (a)-(c): partly the conclusion of Arthur's own view; not addressed in STRONGEST_VIEW.

**Severity.** (b) serious (a becoming-realist argues the eternalist dispute is confused; the view's "resolution" restates it). (a) manageable. (d) manageable if R8 and §4 are scope-limited.

**Best reply.** Adopt Arthur's distinction: the block's existence is atemporal, tensed existence is relative to location; the view's contribution is the content of the tensed tier. This repositions the view as continuous with Arthur and Savitt rather than as a resolution of an eternalism/presentism rivalry. Cost: the table in §4 and the "resolves the divide" language should be retitled.

**Prior anticipation.** Arthur §4 reports and endorses as "formally correct" Stein's point-relative "has become real for" = closure of the past, and Robb's punctual present: this is the physics core. He also separately anticipates the two-tier structure (existence in the manifold vs. temporal existence).

**Sources.** Arthur 2006 (doi 10.1016/s1871-1774(06)01007-2): full-text, sections 2-5 read; quotations above from §1, §4 (pp. 144-145 as printed). Arthur 2019, "Relativity and the present" (*The Reality of Time Flow*, ch. 6): not read, local PDF only.

## 9. Rakić and Müller: point-based presents

**Steelman.** **Rakić 1997** (BJPS 48(2), 257-280): the open-future view (past and present real, future not) is compatible with STR if an extended spacetime (W, <=, R) is used, with R a primitive ontological relation on point events: eRe' means "with respect to e, e' is realized". R is transitive. Axioms require that the causal past of e is realized, e itself is realized and the causal future is not (constraints (a)-(c)). If R were definable from the causal structure, Stein's theorem makes R_e = {e} union causal past (constraint (d): the elsewhere is unrealized). She argues constraint (d) is **unacceptable** (seafight example: the admiral's decision e2 is in the causal past of observer e1, the fight e3 is in e2's future and e1's elsewhere; "it is hard to see why it should be impossible that e3 has already become"), and constraint (e) (the elsewhere is realized) contradicts (a) by transitivity. Her Theorem 5 shows that for every e some elsewhere events are realized and some not; PRES(e,e') iff mutual realization, giving a non-punctual present. The main result is a logical conservativity theorem. She concludes that ontological notions are not temporal notions: R is "not a temporal but an ontological relation". **Müller 2006** ("On the problem of defining the present in special relativity: a challenge for tense logic", in *Time and History*, De Gruyter, 441-457): accepts Gödel's claim that a frame-relative notion of the real is unacceptable, requires a frame-independent equivalence "ontologically present with", notes van Benthem's theorem leaves only identity and the universal relation, and defines PRES via sameness of causae causantes in branching space-times (indeterminism needed; in a deterministic Minkowski universe every event is present with every other).

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Rakić's seafight argument targets R1 directly (target: R1, P3, P6, R4, R9).* Local fragmentalism's theorem delivers F(b) subset of J^-(b): the seafight e3 in b's elsewhere, even though its decisive cause e2 is in b's past, is not real from b. Rakić's intuition: if e2 settles e3, then from b the fight has already happened-by-b in the sense that matters. The view's P3 (locality) is the premise she denies by making R a primitive not definable from causal structure. In the view's formulation: whether e3 has become for b is settled by data on a thin acausal slab containing e3 and b (P3w) and so cannot depend on e2 being in b's past. The view must say: "determined by past data" is not "real". But if determinism holds, domain-of-dependence supervenience (the P3w justification, "D(Sigma) is exactly the region whose physics is fixed by data on Sigma") yields that the verdict on (e3, b) supervenes on D(Sigma), and e3 and b are both in D(Sigma) for a slab Sigma through e2's future? Not so: to contain both e3 and b in its interior the slab has to lie near them, and Cauchy data on it determine e3 (e3 in D(Sigma)). So the physics of that region fixes e3's occurrence, and the decision is derived data on Sigma itself. Under determinism, then, "e3 is determined by data in b's causal past" is a fact about D^+ of a slab in b's past, not about a slab containing e3. The view says becoming should not turn on that. The attack stands as a challenge to P6: causal sufficiency counts only influence from x to b, ignoring **settledness of x by b's past**, i.e. b's past determines x (in deterministic worlds, J^+ of b's past cone data covers D^+). This is the "known by then / settled by then" reading of "has become", versus Stein's "causal access". The view must tell us why settledness is not enough, since in deterministic theories it makes (nearly) everything in the domain of dependence real for b, which for a b in a globally hyperbolic deterministic spacetime includes the whole future of any Cauchy surface in b's past. That collapses toward eternalism and is exactly why Stein and Clifton-Hogarth add the open-future premise. The view declares itself independent of determinism/open future (my distinction) but P4 (non-triviality) and P6 are chosen to avoid this collapse. This should be stated.
(b) *R is primitive in Rakić; the view's R is derived. That is the substantive disagreement.* Whether "realization" is definable from geometry is the whole question: Rakić says no (she finds the definable option unacceptable and so adds R), the view says that locality forces it. P3 begs the question for her: "In the view, becoming relation must be locally covariant" assumes becoming is a geometric-kinematic notion; for her it is an ontological primitive that "introduces absolute time" in the extended structure. So the view's central premise excludes the whole Rakić/Maxwell family of views by definition. That's the same as the "Monton's move" horn (R8, second horn). It is a fair treatment, but Rakić's R need not violate locality in the sense of P3w (it could be a locally defined extension).
(c) *Transitivity and time-orientation as inputs.* Rakić notes (a) and (c) are contradictory without an objective time orientation; the view needs it too.
(d) *Müller: Gödel's premise and determinism.* Müller accepts Gödel's "existence by its nature is something absolute" and concludes a frame-relative reality is not acceptable. That is the generic absolute-existence attack on P1. And his result is that in a deterministic universe, presentness is universal, so there is no non-trivial presentism without indeterminism. Local fragmentalism takes the opposite line: non-trivial becoming without indeterminism (kinematic). This puts the view in the position of defending that becoming need not track branching/indeterminism, the very thing Müller thinks distinguishes presentism from the block. A critic: then what makes the future "not real" from b in a deterministic world, other than the stipulated J^-? This is O2 again.

**Already answered?** O5 (transitivity/inclusion are adopted openly), O3 (locality), and R8's second horn gesture at (b) but never mention Rakić's argument; the Stein-GR paper cites Rakić 1997 only as a "point-based present". The seafight argument is the best published case against constraint (d) and appears nowhere in the view's replies. Not answered.

**Severity.** (a) serious: this is the one published argument against the exact conclusion R1 (that the elsewhere is unreal for b). Note that it is an argument about the *plausibility* of the outcome, and it rests on determinism/settledness, a different notion from causal access; it does not touch the theorem. (b) serious for the uniqueness claim R9, manageable otherwise. (d) manageable.

**Best reply.** Distinguish "settled for b" (determined by b's past, theory-dependent, collapsing toward eternalism in deterministic worlds) from "has become for b" (causal access; kinematic); say the view is about the second and that the first is an epistemic/nomological notion. Accept as a cost that someone who identifies reality with settledness gets different fragments. State that R9's uniqueness is relative to (i)-(v), and note that Rakić's R is a violation of (iii) (or (iv)/(v)), which makes the disagreement explicit rather than refuted.

**Prior anticipation.** **Strong.** Rakić 1997 has event-indexed realization set R_e, transitive, reflexive, containing causal past and excluding causal future, the Stein-derived "definable implies causal past" result in her framework (Section 5.2), and PRES(e,e') iff R_e = R_e'. That is §2b's frame, in 1997. What she lacks: a derivation of the content in curved spacetime, the locality principle; she rejects the content. Müller 2006 anticipates the extended present and the dependence of non-trivial presentness on indeterminism (not the fragment view).

**Sources.** Rakić, "Past, present, future, and special relativity", *BJPS* 48(2) (1997) 257-280, doi 10.1093/bjps/48.2.257: full-text (local PDF); sections 5.1-5.3 (pp. 262-266), 6 (pp. 266-270) and 7 (p. 275-276) quoted/paraphrased. Müller, "On the problem of defining the present in special relativity: a challenge for tense logic", in *Time and History* (De Gruyter 2006, doi 10.1515/9783110333213.441; pages 441-457 per the text I read, pp. 449-456 for the argument): full-text via scratchpad copy. Page locators for Rakić are the printed BJPS pages visible in the extracted text.

## Top threats from this group

1. **Relativism in fragmentalist dress, via H&L's explanatory-priority dilemma and Fine's relativism/fragmentalism line (sections 1-2).** The theorem takes the invariant causal structure as input and derives the fragments; the metaphysics (P7/R6) then states a coherent totality. That is relativism with H&L's "common underlying reality" as explanans. O10's "not brute: coordinated by the causal order" is a concession, not a reply. This concerns temporal ontology and what "fragment" means; it does not touch the theorem.
2. **Rakić's seafight argument against R1, and the broader "settledness vs causal access" bridge (section 9; touches P6, P3, R9).** A published event-indexed, transitive, Stein-aware rival that denies exactly the output (constraint (d)) and makes R primitive. The view must say why locality (not determinism-based settledness) is the right standard, and that the disagreement is about premises (iii)-(v). This concerns the open-future/determinism question and causal order together.
3. **Priority/architecture: Savitt 2005, Dieks 2006, Arthur 2006, Rovelli 2020 hold a two-tier, local-becoming structure and all survive Gödel; the view concedes Gödel (R7).** Plus the unreplied tension that Dieks 2025's perspective independence undercuts fact-level inclusion (R3/O10/O6). The view's novelty is narrower than "fragments of causal pasts as a resolution"; it is the locality derivation of the content.

**Single repair that does the most good:** state two explicit versions of the view and fix which one the claims belong to. (R) **Relativist local theory**: the theorem classifies which relation "has become for b" can be, tense facts are indexical, H&L is accepted, the "resolution" claim is retitled as a reduction of the dispute to the choice of relation. (F) **Fragmentalist add-on**: fragments fundamental, with an explicit account of why the invariant geometry is not prior (or an admission that none is available). Then attach R1-R9 to (R), where they are proven or conditional, and attach O2, O12, O13 and the table in section 4 to the version that can bear them.
