## 1. The "merely verbal debate" deflation (Meyer, Lombard, Dorr, Savitt, Sider)

**Steelman.** The slogans "only the present exists" and "past and future things exist too" are not obviously a dispute. If "exists" is tensed, presentism is trivially true for everyone (everything that exists now is present). If "exists" is tenseless, presentism is trivially false for everyone who admits dinosaurs. The substantive form needs an operator-free, unrestricted, non-tensed quantifier, and the deflationist doubts anyone has a unique grip on it. Meyer 2005 pushes this as a dilemma for the presentist: trivial or untenable depending on how the thesis is read (**discovery**: bibliographic data and a secondary summary only; Gołosz 2019, *Logic and Logical Philosophy* 28, is a reply that says Meyer ignored some readings). Dorr's "What we disagree about when we disagree about ontology" (2005, in Kalderon, ed., *Fictionalism in Metaphysics*; **from memory**) and Hirsch's quantifier-variance work (2002 and later) are the general verbal-dispute apparatus. Savitt 2006 ("Presentism and eternalism in perspective", in Dieks, ed., *The Ontology of Spacetime* I, Elsevier; **discovery**: abstract only) argues that the slogans are unclear because of copula ambiguity, that once spacetime structure is in view a family of meaningful but mutually compatible presentisms and eternalisms emerges, and that these can be complementary parts of a naturalistic philosophy of time. Sider (*Writing the Book of the World*, 2011, and "Quantifiers and temporal ontology", *Mind* 2006; **from memory**) is the realist counter: there is a joint-carving unrestricted quantifier, so the dispute is substantive.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view's headline is the deflationist's thesis, with physics added.* §1 says the dispute "looked unresolvable because both assumed a shared present" and O2 says "each side is right about the standpoints it recognizes". That is exactly Savitt's "complementary" move (and Dorr/Hirsch's "both are right in their own language"). A deflationist will say: you have not discovered a resolution, you have restated verbal-dispute diagnosis with a causal-geometry gloss. The theorem fixes the *content* of a standpoint, but nothing in the theorem decides whether the talk of "real from b" is an ontological claim or a restricted-quantifier convention. In the language of §2b, `v(b)` is just a **restricted domain**, and restricted quantification is the textbook case of non-ontological variance. (This attacks P1, P2 and the O2 reply. It concerns ONT only.)
(b) *Sider's horn, aimed at the view.* If there is a joint-carving unrestricted quantifier, then either it ranges over the whole of M (the eternalist wins and the fragments are restricted quantifiers) or over J^-(b) for each b (then the view is one presentist-flavoured thesis per standpoint and the metalanguage quantifier is the cheating one). The view's attempt to refuse the choice (R6: "the universe has no standpoint") is exactly a refusal to say what the unrestricted quantifier is. A Sider-style realist will say the view has not stated a position, only refused to.
(c) *The view cannot be both deflationary and substantive.* O2's reply says the difference is "statable": which standpoints exist and what they contain. But the statement is made in a quantifier ranging over all events and all standpoints (the model in §2b). That is the eternalist's unrestricted quantifier. See 7b.

**Already answered?** Partly. RED_TEAM.md (section B) already says "relativity plus locality derives the fragment geometry; nothing derives fragmentalism". O2 concedes empirical equivalence and concedes that someone who denies that standpoints are part of reality sees no difference. That concession is the deflationist's whole point; it is not an answer to it. R6/R6′ do not help against (b), they make it worse (7b).

**Severity: serious.** Not fatal because the view can retreat to "we replace the question", which is a respectable position, but then "resolves the divide" has to be dropped from §1, §4 and the table, and the view becomes a proposal about how to reformulate the debate.

**Best reply.** Embrace the deflation selectively: claim that the *substantive residue* of the debate is not the quantifier but two structural questions with determinate answers: (i) is there any local, covariant relation of reality-for that goes beyond the causal past (answer: no, R1/R9), and (ii) is any co-presence relation spacelike and local (answer: no, R2). These are non-verbal and the paper settles them under (LC). Cost: concede that "presentism v. eternalism" as a quantifier dispute is not settled and may not be a dispute.

**Prior anticipation.** Savitt 2006 (complementarity claim, spacetime-structure-based) is a prior anticipation of the *diagnosis* in §1 and O2. The view should cite and distinguish it explicitly. PRIOR_WORK.md lists Savitt 2016 on Fine but not Savitt 2006 on this point. Arthur 2006 (causal-diamond presents) is nearby. **From memory / discovery; read the Savitt chapter before claiming any difference.**

**Sources.** Meyer, "The Presentist's Dilemma", *Philosophical Studies* 122(3) (2005), 213–225 (discovery). Savitt, "Presentism and eternalism in perspective", in Dieks (ed.), *The Ontology of Spacetime* I (Elsevier, 2006) (discovery, abstract). Dorr 2005 (from memory). Lombard: I cannot locate the specific text the brief intends; **unverified, do not cite** until located (candidate: a Lombard paper on whether presentism is trivially true; from memory only). Sider, *Writing the Book of the World* (OUP, 2011) and *Mind* 115 (2006) (from memory). Gołosz, *Logic and Logical Philosophy* 28 (2019) (discovery).

## 2. Williamson's permanentism vs temporaryism; Deasy

**Steelman.** In *Modal Logic as Metaphysics* (OUP, 2013; **from memory**, not re-read; a web search returned only secondary mentions, **discovery**) Williamson argues that the best-behaved quantified temporal logic is the one where necessarily always everything always exists (permanentism, the temporal analogue of necessitism) and that the presentism/eternalism contrast, as normally stated with "exists", is muddled. The real contrast is permanentism v. temporaryism (some things exist only temporarily), and the A/B contrast is a separate matter. Presentists can be permanentists: past things exist but are not concrete or present. Deasy ("What is presentism?", *Noûs* 2017, and later work including a defence of Priorianism; **discovery**) goes further: there is no satisfactory way to define presentism via "is present", and he recommends a debate about *transientism* (things are temporary; "is present" expresses a temporary property) and A/B. The upshot is that "x is real" has been the wrong predicate all along, and the right ones are *existence*, which is not tensed, and *presentness/concreteness*, which is.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view is not defined until it says which predicate "real from b" is.* Under permanentism, D in the Lipman frame (all entities) is the permanent domain and `v(b)` is concreteness-from-b or presentness-from-b, not existence. Then "x is real from b" does not say x exists from b; everything exists from everywhere. Reality-from is just a standpoint-relative sortal. That makes the view *consistent with eternalism about existence plus a perspectival concreteness predicate*, which is the O2 collapse by another route. If instead "real from b" is existence, the view is temporaryist in a strong sense (some events do not exist from b) and must deny the permanentist's modal-logic argument (Williamson's argument is about the simplicity and strength of the logic with Barcan-type principles). Williamson's argument strongly favours permanentism *in the frame model*: the domain function is constant, since D is a fixed set and `v(b)` varies only the restriction. That is exactly the §2b frame: D = all events constant. So **the view's own frame is permanentist**; what varies is `v`.
(b) *"Is present" is not definable in the view without importing a foliation or a location.* Deasy's scepticism about defining "present" applies directly: R2 says "from b, b alone is present", which defines presentness as identity with the standpoint. That is the Lombard/Deasy-style "location" definition, and Deasy's critique is that it makes presentness relative to a location, so "presentism" becomes "everything is located at the standpoint" which is trivially true. This is also the Meyer triviality point from section 1. (ONT.)
(c) *Williamson's logic.* The reality-from relation is reflexive and transitive and not symmetric (S4, not S5; see 7c). The view's totality (P7) is the S5 universal standpoint. Williamson's thesis that metaphysics should be done with the strongest, simplest logic would choose S5 and unrestricted domain, i.e., treat the S4 structure as a restriction of the S5 structure. So the logic itself recommends the eternalist's totality as the base.

**Already answered?** No. STRONGEST_VIEW never mentions permanentism. O2 gets close but is formulated with "exists", the very word Williamson/Deasy call muddled.

**Severity: serious** (not fatal). It forces a precise statement of what is relativised. It does not refute any theorem.

**Best reply.** State the view with two predicates: *existence* (permanent, unrelativised, in D) and *reality/concreteness-from-b* (relativised, `v(b)`). Then say openly the view is a thesis about the second predicate only and is agnostic about permanentism. Cost: concede that it is silent on the traditional presentist's "temporary existence" claim, and that "local fragmentalism resolves presentism v. eternalism" is true only as "resolves A/B-style questions about what is concrete/settled".

**Prior anticipation.** Nothing close to the fragment content. The relevant anticipation is Williamson/Deasy on the *form* of the question. The view should adopt their vocabulary.

**Sources.** Williamson, *Modal Logic as Metaphysics* (OUP, 2013) (from memory). Deasy, "What is presentism?", *Noûs* 51(2) (2017) (discovery, abstract-level; page range not confirmed). Correia & Rosenkranz (2015), Cameron (2016), Deasy (2019) in the same exchange (discovery, via a search summary).

## 3. Truthmaker objections and grounding

**Steelman.** Truths about the past and future need truthmakers (Armstrong, *Truth and Truthmakers*, 2004; Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism*, 2001; Cameron, "Truthmaking for presentists", *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 6, 2011; all **from memory**). If only the present exists, "there were dinosaurs" has no truthmaker. The sophisticated version (Cameron) accepts ersatz or property-based truthmakers. A related issue is grounding: if F(b) is a mere subset of the block (the view's F(b) ⊂ M), then the tensed truth "x is past" is grounded in what? In the tenseless fact `x < b`? Or does the tensed fact ground the tenseless one? Which direction the grounding runs decides the A/B question (this is the usual "does F(b) ground tensed truths?" question).

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view solves the old truthmaker problem for the past but creates a worse one for the future and for the spacelike.* Past-tense truths about J^-(b) are made true by real events. But: (i) future-directed truths ("the sun will rise") have no truthmaker in F(b), same as for the presentist; (ii) **truths about events spacelike to b** (the Sun "now", the galaxy beyond our horizon, the far side of the universe at the same cosmic time) have no truthmaker from b at all. Presentists with a foliation have those; the view loses them. In an accelerating universe, events beyond the future event horizon are in **no** fragment F(b) for any b on our worldline ever. So no standpoint of ours can ever truthfully assert what cosmology asserts about them. That is a stronger truthmaker deficit than presentism has. (ONT/semantics.)
(b) *Reference failure.* From b, a name or description for an event outside J^-(b) has no referent in F(b). Then a large part of physics (spacelike correlations, EPR, cosmology beyond horizon) is either truth-valueless from every standpoint of the observer or evaluated from P7's totality only. This is the stating problem (7b) in its truthmaker guise.
(c) *Grounding direction.* §2b makes `v(b) = J^-(b)`, a function of the causal order. So the tenseless causal relations ground which events are real from b: "x ∈ F(b) because x ≤ b". That is B-grounds-A. The A-theorist's central claim is the reverse. The view then cannot be an A-theory in the grounding sense, only a *relativised reading of the B-facts*. This is the Sider/Cameron form of "eternalism with tensed vocabulary" (O2) and the grounding framing makes it sharper, because the theorem shows the A-extension is fixed by the B-structure. Fine's own fragmentalism avoids this by taking tensed facts as primitive; local fragmentalism's content is *derived* from B-geometry, which looks like a reduction.

**Already answered?** O8 and R6 treat spacelike/universal facts as a prediction, not as a truthmaker problem. O2 says the parties are empirically equivalent. Nothing addresses (a)'s event-horizon case or (c)'s grounding direction. (c) is not answered: the reply "the theorem fixes content" *is* the objection.

**Severity: serious**, with (c) possibly fatal to the "A-theory" credentials and (a) fatal to any claim that the view preserves *scientific realism about the unobserved universe* without P7.

**Best reply.** For (a)/(b): scientific assertions about spacelike or super-horizon regions are made from the totality (P7) or are standpoint-invariant tenseless generalisations; each local standpoint speaks only about its fragment. Cost: P7 becomes mandatory, and the view concedes that physics is formulated from the block standpoint (7b). For (c): take the grounding to be *mutual* or to be explained by the standpoint, not by the causal order alone: the causal order is the *constraint*, not the ground. Cost: need an independent primitive reality-from relation and a story about why the constraint is exceptionless (the theorem under P1–P6 supplies it, but P2/P3/P6 are theses).

**Prior anticipation.** Rovelli (2019/20) and Slavov (2020) both discuss perspectival truthmaking for "now" (PRIOR_WORK.md); Slavov's is a perspectival *eternalist* truthmaker account, and Slavov rejects fragmentalism. For the "world of an occasion is its causal past" idea, Whitehead (*Process and Reality*, 1929, from memory) is the classic anticipation: an actual occasion's data are its causal past and contemporaries are causally independent. The view should say where it differs (Whitehead has no theorem, and no relativised reality; PRIOR_WORK.md does not list him).

**Sources.** Armstrong, *Truth and Truthmakers* (CUP, 2004); Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (OUP, 2001); Cameron, "Truthmaking for presentists", *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 6 (2011) (all from memory). Whitehead 1929 (from memory).

## 4. Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics; persistence

**Steelman.** Lewis (*On the Plurality of Worlds*, 1986, §4.2, from memory) argues that endurantism cannot account for change in intrinsic shape without relativising intrinsics to times (which makes them relations, not intrinsics) or making them properties of temporal parts. Perdurantism handles change by temporal parts. Haslanger (*Analysis* 1989) and Lowe (1988) press adverbialism and tensed-property replies. The lesson: any view that makes what is *real* vary with a standpoint threatens intrinsicality.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The temporary-intrinsics problem returns at the level of events.* Take an object o and its shape. Fragments are sets of events (existence-domains). A property of o at x is "intrinsic to x" and that is a fact in F(x) and in every F(b) with b ≥ x. From b ≥ x, o's shape at x is part of F(b). The **tensed** form "o is bent" is true from x and false from b. Lewis's problem is that this is either relativisation (making "is bent" a relation to a standpoint: not intrinsic) or incompatibility (bent and straight "simpliciter"). Fine's fragmentalism is precisely the tool for taking the second horn. The view's P1 offers it but also offers the relativist reading, so on that reading the view has Lewis's first horn.
(b) *Persistence of objects across fragments.* An enduring object o that exists from x and from b is "wholly present" in both fragments. But the fragments differ in what else is real. Is o's intrinsic character the same? The view gives no account of what makes the o in F(x) the same as the o in F(b): no persistence theory is stated. Events are the primitive; objects are not. If objects are sums of events/worldlines then o in F(x) is a *proper part* of o in F(b) (the worldline up to x versus up to b). Then an object "grows"; o in F(x) is an initial segment. That is a growing-block perdurantism, with Lewis's problem solved by parts in the usual way. But then *the object is different in the two fragments* (different segment), so "the same o is bent then straight" becomes "an initial segment vs a longer segment", i.e., Lewis's solution, and the view is B-theoretic about persistence.
(c) *Dependence on the fragment boundary.* An object that is spatially extended has parts spacelike to b (a rod: most of its body is outside J^-(b), since a rod at "now" is spacelike). So from b the rod is not wholly real, only its past-cone segment is. Extended objects are never fully real at any standpoint, except as segments of their past cones: *no ordinary spatially extended object is wholly present from any standpoint*. This is the Lewis-style "temporary intrinsics" problem transposed to space, and it is much more severe: the intrinsic shape of a rod from b is determined by a null-cone cross-section, not a spacelike slice. The shape facts (length, bend) are not facts of F(b) for any b except via Lorentz-covariant reconstruction. (Concerns ONT and physical object ontology.)

**Already answered?** No. O7 deals with experience; no objection about persistence is listed.

**Severity: serious** for (c) (ordinary extended objects), **manageable** for (a)/(b) (standard options available).

**Best reply.** Adopt worldline-/spacetime-region perdurantism for objects (objects are fusions of events), accept that an extended object is real from b only as its past-cone part, and treat shape as a fact of the *region*, available in F(b') for b' later than the whole region. Cost: nothing is ever "wholly present"; the view is incompatible with endurantism and with the intuitive claim that I am wholly here now.

**Prior anticipation.** None for fragments. The "past-cone slice of an extended object" point is standard in the light-cone-presentism literature (Wüthrich 2012, in PRIOR_WORK.md; **snippet only**).

**Sources.** Lewis 1986; Haslanger, *Analysis* 49 (1989); Lowe 1988 (all from memory).

## 5. Open-future views (Łukasiewicz, Thomason, Barnes & Cameron, MacFarlane)

**Steelman.** Łukasiewicz (1920/1930, three-valued logic; from memory) treats future contingents as neither true nor false. Thomason (1970, "Indeterminist time and truth-value gaps", *Theoria* 36; and 1984 in the *Handbook of Philosophical Logic*; from memory) gives a supervaluationist semantics on a branching-time tree: a future-tensed sentence is true iff true on all histories through the moment, false iff false on all, otherwise gappy. Barnes & Cameron ("The open future: bivalence, determinism and ontology", *Philosophical Studies* 2009, and "Back to the open future", 2011; from memory) argue that the open future is an ontological claim (the future is metaphysically unsettled) which does not require rejecting bivalence, and that it is distinct from indeterminism. MacFarlane (*Assessment Sensitivity*, OUP 2014, ch. 8 on future contingents; also "Future contingents and relative truth", *Phil. Quarterly* 2003; from memory) gives a **relativist** semantics: the truth of "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is relative to a context of use *and* a context of assessment, with the assessor's moment fixing which histories remain open; the claim is retroactively assessable as true or false once the future is settled.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view has no future-tense semantics, and every natural choice causes a problem.* From b the future is not real. Options: (i) all `F φ` are false from b: then `G ⊥` ("it will always be that falsum" or, depending on the dual definition, the world ends) comes out true. This is the standard presentist trap. (ii) `F φ` is evaluated over completions of F(b) to full spacetimes: that is Thomason/Belnap, and the completions live in a space of possible spacetimes outside F(b), which brings back a modal totality the view has not defined and the quantification over which is exactly a view from outside every standpoint. (iii) Gappy: Łukasiewicz-style, losing classical logic from b for future-directed sentences while keeping it for the past. Each needs to be stated, and none is in the document.
(b) *Where the openness comes from.* This is an interesting positive point and a danger. In a globally hyperbolic spacetime the future of b is not determined by data on J^-(b) alone, even under deterministic dynamics, because the causal past of future events extends outside J^-(b) (domain-of-dependence geometry). So F(b)-openness is **not indeterminism**; it is the absence of spacelike facts. That makes the view's open future *different from Barnes & Cameron's* (where the unsettledness is indeterministic branching or ontological incompleteness at a moment). Barnes & Cameron's distinction (open future v. indeterminism) is relevant but the view needs to say which fact the openness is. The danger: Thomason/Barnes-Cameron semantics assume a *moment* or history structure; the view has points with causal pasts. The correct tool is Belnap's **Branching Space-Times** (Belnap 1992, *Synthese* 92; Müller, Placek and collaborators; from memory), in which propositions are evaluated at point events with causal pasts and histories are maximal directed sets. See prior anticipation below.
(c) *MacFarlane's relativism is not local fragmentalism's relativism.* MacFarlane's relativism is **semantic** (truth relative to a context of assessment), explicitly compatible with a non-metaphysical open future, and needs a distinction between context of use and context of assessment, with norms of assertion and retraction doing the work. The view's "standpoint-relative reality" is **metaphysical**, with no use/assessment distinction. Three readings of "tensed facts are standpoint-relative" must be distinguished and the document uses all of them: (R-ctx) indexical contextualism: "real" means "real-from-the-speaker's-event" (propositions are eternal; the view is the eternalism it denies); (R-circ) temporalism-style circumstance relativism: truth is relative to an index (an event), the standard Prior/Kaplan-style temporalism; (R-assess) MacFarlane assessment sensitivity. Under (R-ctx) there is no metaphysical content; under (R-circ) the view is "event-temporalism", a known position with a known critique (the "retraction" data, MacFarlane's own argument, and the problem that circumstance-relative truth lacks an account of disagreement); under (R-assess) the view needs an assessor, a norm for retraction, and an account of faultless disagreement between assessors at **spacelike** events: b and c each assess the other's event as non-real. Is that a disagreement? Each says "c is not real" / "b is not real" and each is right by its own standard. This is a faultless-disagreement structure with no shared fact about which is right, a stranger case than MacFarlane's taste or future-contingent cases.
(d) *Retroactive truth.* On MacFarlane's view, the assessor at the later event can assess an earlier future-contingent as true. In the view, b' ≥ b has b in its fragment, and the outcome too. So b' may assess b's assertion. Fine, and a strength: MacFarlane's mechanism has a natural relativistic home (assessor's causal past = settled). But the view should *notice* this: MacFarlane's mechanism is a prior semantic anticipation, minus relativity and minus the metaphysics.

**Already answered?** No. STRONGEST_VIEW never addresses future-tensed sentences, open-future semantics, or branching. O6 touches indeterminism only through collapse.

**Severity: serious.** The missing future-tense semantics is a hole, not a refutation; the missing choice among (R-ctx)/(R-circ)/(R-assess) is a clarity failure that recurs in O1.

**Best reply.** Adopt a Belnap-style point-event semantics: `F φ` at b is true iff φ holds in all spacetime completions of F(b) compatible with the laws; this defines settledness from b, treats openness as lack of spacelike data (not indeterminism), and gives MacFarlane-style retroactive assessability for free. Say which of the three relativisms P1 is. Cost: introduces a space of completions, which is a modal structure the document does not currently contain; and the "completions" are quantified over by a standpoint that does not exist from b (the stating problem again).

**Prior anticipation (specific).** Belnap's Branching Space-Times (1992) evaluates propositions at point events and takes a point's causal past as settled; this is the nearest existing formal framework to "a standpoint is a point event; its causal past is what is settled". It is indeterminist, not a theory of what is real. Müller's later BST work (including with Belnap on relativistic quantum correlations) is relevant to O6. **Not in PRIOR_WORK.md; check.** From memory only. Whitehead (above) is the metaphysical anticipation. MacFarlane (above) is the semantic anticipation.

**Sources.** Łukasiewicz 1920; Thomason 1970 and 1984; Barnes & Cameron 2009 and 2011; MacFarlane 2003 and 2014; Belnap 1992 (all from memory, locators not confirmed).

## 6. Dummett on the past (anti-realism)

**Steelman.** Dummett ("The Reality of the Past", *Proc. Aristotelian Soc.* 1969; the Dewey Lectures "Truth and the Past", published 2003/2004; from memory, dates unconfirmed) argues that realism about the past (determinate truth-value for every past-tense statement whether or not any evidence survives) cannot be manifested in use. The anti-realist replaces truth-conditions by verification conditions; a past-tense statement is true only if there is (present) evidence or a way of verifying it; logic goes intuitionistic for the past. Its significance here: it makes the past's reality depend on *present* standpoint, which is structurally standpoint-relative reality.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view is realist about the past and its justification is causal, not epistemic.* F(b) = J^-(b) includes every event in the past cone, including those from which no record, trace, or signal survives to b. Dummett's challenge, "what in your practice manifests belief in this unrecorded fact?", is aimed straight at P6 and R1 (J^-(b) ⊆ F(b)). P6 is a *causal* sufficiency principle ("whatever can influence b is real from b"); it does not distinguish influence that leaves a trace from influence that does not. A Dummettian says the cut-off is right in shape (a past-cone structure!) but wrong in kind: reality-from-b should be *what b can in principle verify*, and that is the set of events from which a signal can reach b **and be decoded**, a smaller set. In particular the view treats **bivalence for past-cone facts** as given; Dummett denies it.
(b) *Kripke semantics for intuitionistic logic has the same shape.* The frame ⟨events, ≤, domains growing along ≤⟩ is a Kripke model; persistence along the order is the intuitionistic persistence clause. An anti-realist can say: this is *our* structure, and the correct logic from b is intuitionistic, not classical, even for the past cone. The view uses classical logic within F(b) without argument. (Concerns ONT and logic.)
(c) *The reverse use is a weapon for the view.* Dummett's anti-realism about the *future* is the open-future thesis in epistemic dress, and his argument for the reality of the present as the only truth-maker gives a motive for "events outside J^-(b) are not real from b". The view could use Dummett as a motivation for P6 (conceptual explanation of "what has become": what is available to b), but only by moving from a causal to an epistemic-availability criterion and giving up P6's role in forcing the null cone.
(d) Also: the **past-cone** is in practice not epistemically available as a whole (most of J^-(b) has left no usable trace); Dummett's objection therefore bites hard in the relativistic setting.

**Already answered?** No.

**Severity: manageable.** It attacks only the classical-logic assumption inside fragments and the causal criterion in P6; the geometry is untouched. If the view wants to say that P6 is a "causal sufficiency" and not an "epistemic availability" principle, it should say so and reject Dummett explicitly.

**Best reply.** State that fragments are classical and realist about the past cone, justified by the same principle as Clifton and Hogarth's (causal contribution, not evidence); note that anti-realist inflection is compatible with the geometry (one can run intuitionistic logic on the same frame). Cost: P6 becomes a metaphysical, not an epistemic, thesis, and Dummett's manifestation challenge is simply refused.

**Prior anticipation.** None known for relativistic Dummettian anti-realism. Whether anyone has proposed "Kripke models over the causal order of Minkowski spacetime for intuitionistic logic of the past cone" I do not know; a search is worth doing before claiming novelty for the frame.

**Sources.** Dummett 1969 (*PAS*); Dummett, *Truth and the Past* (Columbia UP, 2004; Dewey Lectures); from memory.

## 7. Logical coherence of fragmentalism; the §2b frame

This is the most dangerous section. Seven specific points.

### 7a. P5 ("absolute facts are those that hold from every standpoint") is incoherent as literally stated

**Steelman of the position.** Standpoint logic (Lipman, *J. Phil.* 2023, hybrid logic; **read in full per PRIOR_WORK.md; not re-read here**) models truth from a standpoint. A fact is "invariant" if it is true from all standpoints. The view says tenseless causal geometry is invariant.

**Attack.** In the §2b frame, from standpoint c only events in J^-(c) exist. A fact about events x, y holds from c only if x, y ∈ J^-(c). So "x ≤ y" holds from c only if c is in the common future of x and y. A fact holds **from every standpoint** only if its subject events lie in **every** J^-(c), that is, in ∩_c J^-(c). In Minkowski spacetime, and in any cosmology without a unique earliest event in M, this intersection is empty (for any x there is a b not in J^+(x)). In FRW spacetime with the big bang singularity removed from M, again empty. So **under the literal definition of P5 there are no absolute facts about any event**, and the §1/§4/R6 claim that "the standpoint-invariant facts are the tenseless causal geometry" is false.

The repair is *not* cosmetic. Invariance has to be replaced by one of:
- **Persistence**: p holds from c and c ≤ c' implies p holds from c' (this is the Kripke persistence clause; tenseless facts about the past are persistent); or
- **Agreement on overlap**: whenever a fact is expressible from both b and c, it has the same value (a sheaf/gluing condition on the Alexandrov topology of the causal order, with fragments as down-set sections).
Either way, "absolute" no longer means "holds from everywhere". It means "never changes as one moves to later standpoints, or across standpoints that share the subject matter". Then: **there are no facts about the whole causal geometry from any event-standpoint**, and the view that the causal geometry is "the invariant content" is true only from the P7 totality, which is a non-event standpoint (7b). In other words the geometry that the theorem uses to define the fragments is a **global** structure that is real from no local standpoint.

**Already answered?** No. P5 is stated once and used throughout (O10: "single shared causal geometry, which is itself the standpoint-invariant content (P5)"; §4 table).

**Severity: serious**, formally an error rather than a conceptual objection. It is repairable by the persistence/overlap reformulation, but the repair has a price: the claim in O10 that coordination is "not brute" depends on the shared geometry being real from each standpoint, and it is only real *up to the overlap*.

**Best reply.** Replace P5 by "the absolute facts are those that are persistent along ≤ and agree across overlapping fragments" and say so. State the consequence: the causal order on F(b)∩F(c) is the same from b and c, and the global order exists only from the totality. Cost: concedes that the whole-geometry fact is a P7/block fact, not a standpoint-invariant one.

### 7b. The stating problem (Fine's "can you state it?"; Lipman's own motivation for an atemporal standpoint)

**Attack.** Every theorem is stated with a quantifier over all events of M and a function `b ↦ F(b)` defined on all of M simultaneously. The §2b frame's D is the set of all events: a permanentist/eternalist domain. From which standpoint is this assertion made? Not from any event b, since M ⊄ J^-(b). The view says the totality "has no standpoint" (R6, without P7). Then the statements "R1 holds", "F(b) = J^-(b) for all b" are **true from no standpoint**, so by the view's own account they are not facts at all, or are facts of a kind (metatheoretic) the view does not recognise as part of reality. With P7, they are true from the totality, and then the totality is a (the) privileged standpoint from which the theory is stated, which re-introduces what the diagnosis said was an artefact: an unrelativised vantage with a view of everything. Lipman's pluralist theory posits an atemporal standpoint exactly to avoid this; local fragmentalism says it gets one for free (R6′). But then:
- the totality standpoint is S5-like, the event standpoints are S4-like (7c), and the *theory* lives in the former, so the "fragmentalist" account of reality is a theory whose home is the eternalist standpoint;
- R6 (no P7) and R6′ (P7) are the two horns of one dilemma: **either the theory is not assertable by its own lights, or the eternalist's totality is the stating standpoint.** That is stronger than O2: it says the eternalist is right *about the status of the theory*.

**Already answered?** O13 and R6′ address this by choosing P7 and saying "derived, not posited". But "derived" is a derivation in the metalanguage that assumed D. That the totality's fragment equals the union of fragments is a theorem about a structure presupposed. So the reply is partly circular: the totality is not derived from the fragments, it is *assumed* in order to define them.

**Severity: serious, verging on fatal for the stronger claims** ("resolves", "not a relabelling", and R6 as a prediction). Survivable if the view adopts P7 and says openly: the physical theory is stated from the totality standpoint, local standpoints see the part, and the dispute reduces to "which standpoint is explanatorily or ontologically prior" (O2), which the view then says nothing about.

**Best reply.** Distinguish *model theory* from *content*. The frame is a representation device, as in tense logic where Kripke semantics does not make times real for the presentist. Give an **object-language** version: a hybrid language with `[b]φ` ("from b, φ") and a reality predicate, and state R1 as a derivable principle of that language. Then check that R1 is not trivial from b (from b, "everything real is in my causal past" is true almost by definition because the quantifier is restricted; the non-trivial content is cross-standpoint: if x is real from b and y from x then y from b, and the cross-spacetime uniformity). Cost: the theorem's content is not an internal truth of any fragment; it is a constraint on the *family* of standpoints, available only from a vantage that sees several. That concedes that the family has a vantage.

### 7c. The logic of "x is real from b": factivity, iteration, R6

Take `P φ` to mean "φ holds at some x real from b" (i.e., x ≤ b). Then:
- **Reflexive (T)**: b is real from b. True under J^-; fails under I^- unless reflexivity is stipulated (the paper stipulates it).
- **Transitive (4)**: P2. Fine.
- **Not symmetric (not 5, not B)**: otherwise the relation is an equivalence and the theorem's non-triviality fails. So the logic is **S4** (S4.2 if the frame is directed, as in Minkowski, per Goldblatt 1980, "Diodorean modality in Minkowski spacetime", *Studia Logica* 39; **from memory**, a known result that the modal logic of the causal order of Minkowski spacetime is S4.2). The block standpoint (P7) is the S5 cluster. This is a clean characterisation and should be in the document, and it **is a prior anticipation of the formal skeleton** (Goldblatt for the modal logic of the causal order; Shehtman and Shapirovsky for related spacetime logics, from memory, unverified).
- **Factivity** of "x is real from b": if it holds then x exists in v(b) by Lipman's constraint, by construction. But the statement "x is real from b" is itself a fact about b's fragment. From which standpoints is it true? From c ≥ b (persistence), not from c elsewhere (b not real from c). So it is persistent, not invariant (7a).
- **Iteration**: `[b][x]φ` for x ∈ F(b). Is `[x]φ → [b]φ`? No for tensed φ ("x is present"). Is `[b][x]φ` true when `[x]φ`? It must be (b can report "x was present") and this is the tensed fact "x was present". So the nested operator is the **past tense operator**, and the iteration principle needed is `[x]φ → [b]P-shifted φ`, a shift principle that is not in the document. Without it, "fragments grow" (R3) is true for **events** but false for **facts**: F(x) ⊆ F(b) as event sets, but "x is present" is a fact of F(x) and is not a fact of F(b) (where x is past). So `F(b)` as a **set of facts** is not monotone, and F(b) is not just the domain J^-(b): it is that domain **with a centre** (b present, the rest past by various degrees). The theorem fixes the domain; it does not fix the centred fact structure, and the identification "fragment = J^-(b)" conflates the two.
- **R6 and the top element**: with P7, M is a standpoint with R(M, b) for all b and not R(b, M). So the frame has a greatest element. The question "is M real from b?" is answered no, but "M" here is the mereological sum of all events, which presupposes the sum exists in D (the permanentist domain). Fine, but then *M exists* and is in D. So P7 does admit a global entity; R6's "the universe has no standpoint" holds only without P7.

**Frame errors found in §2b (itemised).**
1. v(b) is described as the set of **events** real from b, but with P7 W includes regions, so D contains regions, and v(b) must contain at least the sub-regions of J^-(b). The statement "v(b) = F(b) = events" is then false; v(b) is events plus regions or has to be restricted. Minor.
2. "R(b, x) iff x ∈ v(b)" makes **every** real thing a standpoint. Lipman's constraint only gives the inclusion R ⊆ ∈v. Identifying them is a substantive choice (it makes standpoint-hood equivalent to existence-from) and makes the Lipman model degenerate: R and v carry the same information. Then "classification of locally covariant standpoint frames" is a classification of one relation, not of Lipman-style frames. Overstated, not wrong.
3. The Loc category (main.tex Definition 1) has **open** causally convex embeddings. J^-(b) is closed and contains b, and I^-(b) is open but excludes b. So the fragment F(b) is **not an object of the category** (J^- is not open; I^- does not contain b, so b is not a standpoint of its own open past). The fragment cannot be treated as "a world in the category" without leaving the category. Manageable.
4. **With P7, regions as standpoints readmit extended presents.** For a spacelike hypersurface Σ, F(Σ) = J^-(Σ), and Σ is the natural "present" of the standpoint Σ. Then Σ is a standpoint with a shared, extended present, local (Σ is covariantly defined) and non-privileged. This is precisely a Fine-style frame-time standpoint, recovered inside P7. R2 ("no shared present at all") is then true only of **point** standpoints, and the corollary used in the "trilemma" defines co-presence as mutual becoming between distinct events, which is narrower than "both present from a standpoint". The statement "there is no shared present at all" in §1/R2/§4 is overstated once P7 is on. The O11(b) argument against Fine ("frame-times need a foliation") then fails against P7's own region-standpoints, which need no foliation: each hypersurface is a standpoint, none privileged. Either P7 excludes hypersurfaces (ad hoc) or it readmits Fine's fragments. **This is the sharpest single internal inconsistency found.** Severity: serious.

**Already answered?** No for 7c.4; partly for the rest (RED_TEAM.md section E noted P7's physicalism but not the hypersurface case).

**Best reply for 7c.4.** Restrict P7 to causally convex, *bounded-below* regions or to regions that are the past of a point (so every region standpoint is of the form J^-(S) with S a set of events and the only presents are those of points); or accept that region-standpoints have co-presence and that this is harmless because none is privileged and none is a "shared now of the universe". Cost: the claim "no shared present at all" must be replaced by "no privileged shared present" (which Fine's frame fragmentalism already gives), and the distinctiveness of the view versus Fine shrinks to the content of point standpoints.

### 7d. Is "reality is standpoint-relative but tenseless facts are standpoint-invariant" coherent?

Yes, if "tenseless" means "persistent and overlap-agreeing" (7a) and "invariant" is read that way. No, with literal invariance. And it is incoherent as a *combination with P1 (fragmentalism)*: if facts in different fragments can conflict, then invariance cannot be "agreement on overlap", because agreement on overlap is a coherence requirement across fragments, which is exactly what the fragmentalist drops. So the **fragmentalist** reading of P1 and the **invariance** of P5 pull in different directions: P1 allows cross-fragment incoherence, P5 demands agreement on the tenseless sector. They are compatible only if the incoherence is confined to the tensed sector and the tenseless sector is a coherent common core. This is a coherent proposal but it has the shape "a tenseless base plus a tensed superstructure that can be incoherent across fragments", which is the standard structure of **Fine's own response to McTaggart and the standard objection to him**: the tensed facts then float on a B-theoretic base. Combined with 3(c) (B grounds A), the view is Fine's fragmentalism with the A-facts reduced to centred B-facts. In which case P1's fragmentalist reading adds nothing: since the centred facts are definable, no conflict ever arises and the relativist reading is correct (O1).

**Severity of 7 overall: serious.** 7a is a straightforward error; 7b is a deep structural problem; 7c.4 is an inconsistency between P7 and R2/O11(b); 7d is a dilemma between P1 and P5.

**Prior anticipation.** Goldblatt 1980 for the modal logic of Minkowski causal order (formal skeleton). Lipman 2023 for the frame. Fine 2005/2006 for non-standard realism and the "can you state it" problem (Fine, "How to be a nonstandard realist" is, as I recall, a section of the 2005 essay; locator unconfirmed). Kripke 1965 semantics for intuitionistic logic for the persistence structure.

**Sources.** Lipman, *J. Phil.* 120(3) (2023) (full text per PRIOR_WORK.md); Fine 2005/2006 (full text per PRIOR_WORK.md); Goldblatt 1980 (from memory).

## 8. McTaggart's paradox applied to fragments

**Steelman.** McTaggart ("The Unreality of Time", *Mind* 1908; from memory) argues the A-series is contradictory: each event is past, present and future, which are incompatible; the escape (it *is* present, *will be* past, *was* future) generates a vicious regress or circle. Fine (2005) reconstructs the argument as a trilemma over Realism, Neutrality, Absolutism and Coherence; fragmentalism drops Coherence, relativism drops Absolutism, presentism drops Neutrality, B-theory drops Realism.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The paradox is avoided only by relativisation to a standpoint, and the standpoints are themselves in a static order.* An event x is present in F(x) and past in F(b). The contradiction is avoided because no single fragment holds both. But "was present" in F(b) is a nested fact `[b]P[x]present(x)`, and the truth conditions for it are fixed by the causal order (7c). So the A-determinations are **definable from the B-relation plus a standpoint index**: past-from-b iff x < b, present-from-b iff x = b, future-from-b iff x > b (with the future unreal from b, see below). That is a tenseless (token-reflexive) analysis of tense à la Mellor, and McTaggart's argument is classically what drives one to B-theory. The view avoids contradiction by turning the A-series into a B-series with a perspective.
(b) *"Future" is not available.* From b, x ∈ J^+(b) is not in F(b), so the fact "x is future" cannot be expressed in F(b) about x. McTaggart's trichotomy past/present/future is not a trichotomy in the view: for b, events are past (x < b), present (b), or **non-existent** (all else including spacelike ones). The correct A-series is "past/present/nothing". The view can say that, but then "x was future" (from b' later than x) has to be read as P(there was y with y < x) where y is real from b' and x ∈ J^+(y): a B-fact about two events, both real from b'. No regress. So the McTaggart second-order regress is blocked, but by definability, which again is the O2 reduction.
(c) *The regress returns at the level of "which standpoint is occupied/actual".* If the totality of standpoints is the static poset (P7), then to say "this thought occurs at b" is indexical self-location. The A-theorist's "it is now b" is then a fact about the indexical, nothing more. This is O12 (anaemic passage). It is not a new problem for the view relative to Fine's fragmentalism (which has the same cost), but the view's claim that passage is "the growth of fragments (R3)" is a *comparison between standpoints*, available from the totality or from a later standpoint, not an event internal to any fragment. McTaggart's own conclusion ("no change in the B-series") is then reapplied: the sequence F(b) ⊆ F(b') is a B-relation among fragments.
(d) *Direction of passage versus direction of the order.* The growth F(b) ⊆ F(b') is a feature of the causal order, which exists in the fundamental laws with time-orientation as input (ARROW is not involved; this is CAUSAL). The view therefore provides *directed* but not *dynamic* becoming; passage as a dynamic, "the present moves" claim is not in the formal content. This is the standard McTaggartian complaint against Stein-style "becoming as a relation" (Stein's becoming is a tenseless relation).

**Already answered?** Partly. O12 (anaemic passage) acknowledges (c) and (d) and concedes the block is static. It does not address (a)/(b) as McTaggart arguments, and the document nowhere says that Fine's four-way trilemma applies to its own P1. (b) is a novelty for this view: it should be stated, since it shows a precise sense in which the future is not an A-determination.

**Severity: manageable.** McTaggart's argument is a challenge to *any* A-flavoured view and the view has Fine's standard escape plus a relativist reading; the cost is that it is not an A-theory in McTaggart's sense, which O1/O2 already admit.

**Best reply.** Say plainly that the view is the fragmentalist (or relativist) solution to McTaggart's trilemma, locate it in Fine's four-way taxonomy (P1 dropping Coherence or Absolutism), and add the observation that its A-series is "past/present/nothing", not "past/present/future", which is a precise structural difference from the McTaggart series. Cost: concede that dynamic passage is not captured and that tense is reducible to centred B-facts.

**Prior anticipation.** Fine 2005/2006 (taxonomy; full text per PRIOR_WORK.md). Stein 1968 for the "past/present/nothing" structure (Stein's becoming is "what has become" and the future is simply not yet determinate; PRIOR_WORK.md lists Stein as full text).

**Sources.** McTaggart, "The Unreality of Time", *Mind* 17 (1908) (from memory). Fine 2005 (full text per PRIOR_WORK.md).

## Top threats from this group

1. **The stating problem (7b), compounded by P7's readmission of extended presents (7c.4).** The theory is asserted in a quantifier over the whole of M, which is real from no event-standpoint; adding P7 supplies a vantage but makes the eternalist's totality the home of the theory and (with regions as standpoints) readmits Fine-style shared presents that R2/O11(b) said were excluded. This makes O2 sharper than the document's reply allows and puts R6 and R6′ on the two horns of one dilemma. Severity: serious, fatal to the strong rhetoric ("resolves", "no shared present at all", "prediction").
2. **P5 is incoherent as stated (7a), and the combination of P1 (fragmentalism) with P5 (invariance) is unstable (7d).** There are no events real from every standpoint, so no absolute facts about events; the repair (persistence/overlap) leaves the whole causal geometry real only from the totality, and makes the fragmentalist reading collapse into the relativist one. Severity: serious; a clean fix exists.
3. **Truthmaker/grounding direction and the missing semantics for the future (3(c), 3(a), 5(a)).** The fragment content is derived from B-facts, so B grounds A; truths about spacelike or super-horizon events and all future-tensed sentences lack truth conditions from any event-standpoint. Without a Belnap-style completion semantics the view has no account of "will". Severity: serious.

**Single repair that does the most good.** Give the view an **object-language and an explicit logic**: a hybrid S4 language with `[b]φ`, a persistence-based notion of absolute fact (replacing P5), a stated shift principle for nested standpoints, a stated future-tense semantics (settledness from b by completions, Belnap-style), and an explicit decision on P7 (adopt it, restrict it to exclude hypersurfaces or accept them, and say that the theory is stated from the totality). That one move (a) repairs P5, (b) turns O2 into a precise comparison between logics (S4 families v. S5 totality), (c) answers the stating problem honestly, (d) fixes which relativism P1 is, and (e) gives MacFarlane/Belnap their place. It costs the claim that the view "resolves" the dispute.
