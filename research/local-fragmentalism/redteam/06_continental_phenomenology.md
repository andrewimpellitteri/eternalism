Locators for specific passages are not given and should be fetched before anything is cited.

**Which notion each attack concerns.** The view's claims concern temporal ontology (what is real from where), causal order (the theorem), and a restricted notion of passage (R3). It disclaims clock time, the thermodynamic arrow and determinism. The continental attacks mostly concern **time-consciousness** and **the framing**, and only secondarily ontology. I flag this for each, because many of them are misfires if the view stays inside physical ontology and are serious if it claims to "resolve the divide" for experienced time.

**Cross-cutting point.** All seven thinkers distrust the same two features of the view: (a) the point-event as the unit of time, and (b) the "view from the whole" (P7, R6, R6′, O13). Four of them (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Bergson, Levinas) would read (b) as the totalizing overview they reject. The view already has a version without P7 (R6, O13: "without P7 the view instead recovers the eternalist's truths as the facts invariant across all standpoints"). That version is the safer one against this group.

## 1. Husserl

### Steelman
Time is first of all the form in which anything is given to consciousness, and only derivatively a parameter of objects. In the lectures on internal time-consciousness (1893–1917; Husserliana X; Brough's English translation 1991), a temporal object such as a melody is intended in a "primal impression" (the now-phase), a "retention" (the just-past, still held, not remembered) and a "protention" (the about-to-be). Retention is not recollection (*Wiedererinnerung*), which is a separate re-presenting act. The pure now-point is an ideal limit, an abstract moment that cannot exist alone, and the concrete living present is the whole structure. Retentions are nested (retention of a retention) and fade without a sharp cutoff. Beneath constituted objective time lies an "absolute flow" that is not itself in objective time and whose own structure cannot be described in the vocabulary of temporal objects. Later manuscripts (the C-manuscripts) speak of a "standing-streaming" present. Objective time, including the time of physics, is a constituted unity, and intersubjective time is built through empathy (*Einfühlung*) and the co-presence of other living presents (Husserliana XIII–XV).

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Order of constitution (framing attack; concerns time-consciousness and the status of the whole project).** The view takes objective causal order as basic and derives "tense" as standpoint-relative reality. Husserl says this reverses the order: causal-objective time is a constituted achievement, and a theory of what "present", "past" and "becoming" mean must start from the constituting flow. "Standpoint of b" for an event b with no experiencer is, from this angle, a formal index and not a standpoint. Then P2 ("x has become for b" means "x is real in F(b)") uses the phenomenological words for a relation (x ≤ b) that contains nothing phenomenological. This is a sharper form of O2 (eternalism with tensed vocabulary): the equivocation is on "present", whose lived sense the view does not touch.

**(b) The point-like present is an abstraction (targets R2).** R2 says that from b, b alone is present. Husserl would agree that a now-point is not a concrete present, but he would also say that a point present with a past-set attached still fails to capture retention. The retained past is given in the present with intentional structure (fading, perspectival modification), not as a set of equally real events. F(b) = J^-(b) is reality-of-everything-in-the-past, whereas the retentional horizon is a graded thing that gives way to recollection. The view's O7 reply (the specious present is a duration along a worldline, "the Arthur–Savitt diamond read as an experiential window") quietly gives up the identification of b's fragment with the living present. Husserl would accept that, but then the phenomenological content of "tense" is entirely missing from the fragment.

**(c) Intersubjective co-presence (targets R2; concerns time-consciousness and shared experienced time).** For Husserl my living present and the Other's are given as co-present through empathy; the common now is part of how a shared world is constituted. The view says there is no co-presence, so shared experienced now has no ontological counterpart. This is the phenomenological face of "locally definable spacelike co-reality" (§8, item 3).

### Already answered?
O7 answers (b) only partly: it relocates the specious present, but does not say what makes the apex-plus-past a *present for someone*. O2 anticipates the equivocation worry but does not engage the claim that the sense of "present" is constituted. Nothing in the view addresses (c). O8 and R2 concede that no extended shared present exists but say nothing about the experiential one. So the view's reply is incomplete against this version.

### Severity
**Manageable** for the physics and the theorem; **serious** for the sentence in §4 that the view "resolves the divide" if "present" there is meant to carry its lived sense. The attack is a misfire if the view is stated explicitly as a thesis about physical ontology only.

### Best reply
State a scope restriction: the view is a thesis about *what is real from where* in a causal structure, and the words "present" and "past" label structural positions (apex, interior). It does not identify F(b) with any lived horizon. For (c): shared experienced now is a coarse-grained feature of regions small relative to the light-travel time between participants (the diamond of O7 for conversations on Earth differs from a point by nanoseconds to milliseconds). Concession: the view then says nothing about why experience has the structure it does, so it cannot claim to dissolve the phenomenological side of the tense debate.

### Prior anticipation
No anticipation of the causal-past content. A structural *echo* worth flagging: Husserl's retention is iterable and cumulative (the retention of a retention is still a retention of the earlier phase), which is the phenomenological analogue of P2's transitivity. This is an analogy, not a priority claim. Retention as a model of the specious present is, in the analytic literature, one of the standard options (retentionalism, versus extensionalism and cinematic views; see Dainton on temporal consciousness).

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
The analytic author should (i) stop using "specious present" loosely as if it were a physical duration (O7), and say whether retention (content about the past, at a moment) or extension (a real duration of experience) is meant. Retentionalism is compatible with a point-like objective present, which would make O7 unnecessary. (ii) Concede that "tense" in the view is structural and not the lived sense.

### Sources
Husserl, *Vorlesungen zur Phänomenologie des inneren Zeitbewusstseins* (ed. Heidegger 1928; Husserliana X, ed. Boehm 1966); *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time (1893–1917)*, tr. J. B. Brough, Kluwer 1991. Hua XIII–XV, *Zur Phänomenologie der Intersubjektivität*. *Die C-Manuskripte* (Hua Materialien VIII, 2006). Read depth: **from memory**.

## 2. Heidegger

### Steelman
In *Sein und Zeit* (1927), time is not a series of nows but the horizon on which Dasein's being is intelligible. Temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) is the unity of three ecstases: the future (being-ahead-of-itself, anticipation of death), having-been (*Gewesenheit*, which is not *Vergangenheit*: my past is something I am), and the making-present (*Gegenwärtigen*). The future has primacy. "World-time" (*Weltzeit*) is the public time in which things are encountered: datable ("now, when..."), spanned, significant and public. "Within-time-ness" (*Innerzeitigkeit*) is the mode in which beings are in time, and measurement by clocks derives from it. The "ordinary" or "vulgar" concept of time (§§81–82), which Heidegger traces through Aristotle's *Physics* IV to Hegel, is a levelled-down world-time: a homogeneous, infinite sequence of nows, each present-at-hand (*vorhanden*). It conceals the finitude of originary temporality. Being as presence-at-hand is the guiding horizon of the tradition.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Whole project = metaphysics of *Vorhandenheit* (framing attack; concerns temporal ontology as such).** Heidegger would say yes, with a nuance. Events, fragments, reality as a totality of facts, reality-from-a-standpoint and the question "what exists" are all categories of the present-at-hand. The presentism/eternalism/growing-block debate is an argument about how nows are distributed: which are real. The view's "resolution" is therefore a re-arrangement inside the now-sequence conception (each event is the apex of its own cone), not an escape from it. The shared-present trilemma (§4 of the view, §6 of the paper) is a problem *interior* to that conception.

**(b) Inverted priority (targets R1 and R3; concerns temporal ontology).** The view makes the past what is real from b and the future what is absent. Heidegger's priority runs the other way: the future is the primary ecstasis, and the past (*Gewesenheit*) is temporalized *from* it. "Not real from b" for the future treats the not-yet as mere absence, a deficiency of the present-at-hand, when it is the horizon from which anything is a "something". R3 (reality accrues, F(b) ⊆ F(b′)) models time as sedimentation of settled facts, which is the structure of a record, not of existence.

**(c) The index is not Dasein (targets P1).** A fragment F(b) is "from b" only as a formal relation. For Dasein, perspective is not located at a point; Dasein's spatiality is itself derived from its temporality (§70), and a "point of view" is not a place in a container. The "standpoint" talk imports the *language* of perspective into a structure that has no one for whom things stand.

### Already answered?
Not directly. O2 is the closest (relabelling). The view never says it is a regional ontology of the present-at-hand. In STRONGEST_VIEW the phrase "Tense lives in fragments" (table, §4) suggests tense is thereby accounted for, which is exactly what Heidegger would deny.

### Severity
**Misfire** against the theorem and the physical claims, since Heidegger himself allows that the sciences uncover nature as present-at-hand, and a regional ontology can be legitimate. **Serious** against the rhetorical claim that the view resolves the *tense* dispute. (a) is a framing attack: it does not refute any labelled claim but it says what the claims are about.

### Best reply
Accept the classification: the view is a theory of the present-at-hand, namely of what is real from a position in a causal structure, and it makes no claim about originary temporality. The positive claim that survives is that within that regional ontology the dispute between presentists and eternalists can be located precisely, and that Heidegger's own ranking (derivative world-time rests on lived temporality) is not contradicted. Concession: drop the language that the view "resolves" the divide in any sense wider than this regional one, and drop "tense lives in fragments" unless "tense" is flagged as structural.

### Prior anticipation
None of the causal-past content. Two surface similarities worth flagging, not anticipation: (1) Heidegger's *world-time* is not a single cosmic now but time as disclosed from a Dasein, with publicness derived; (2) *Gewesenheit* ("I am my having-been") is an agent-relative past that grows. I recall that Heidegger's 1924 lecture "Der Begriff der Zeit" (GA 64) comments on Einstein's theory as presupposing the observer and the clock, but I have not verified this and would not cite it without checking.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That a debate which assumes time is a series of positions that are (or are not) real is making a substantive assumption, not a neutral one. The view could say, more modestly, that it studies what remains of the "series of nows" assumption when relativity is taken seriously. The point about future priority is worth a sentence in the answer to O12 ("anaemic passage"): the view treats the future only negatively.

### Sources
Heidegger, *Sein und Zeit* (1927), esp. §§65–71 (temporality), §§78–81 (within-time-ness, world-time, the ordinary concept of time) from memory; Macquarrie–Robinson (1962) and Stambaugh (1996) translations. *Der Begriff der Zeit* (1924; GA 64, 2004). Read depth: **from memory**.

## 3. Bergson (including the 1922 debate)

### Steelman
Real time is *durée*: an indivisible, qualitatively heterogeneous flow in which the past is conserved and interpenetrates the present, and the future is genuinely created, not unrolled (*Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience*, 1889; *Matière et mémoire*, 1896; *L'évolution créatrice*, 1907). The intellect spatializes time: it cuts it into instants, lays them side by side, and represents change by positions. That is useful for action and for measurement but falsifies duration (the "cinematographic" mechanism of thought). In *Durée et simultanéité* (1922), Bergson argues that relativity's multiplicity of times is a multiplicity of *measures* attached to fictitious observers. There is one real time, namely the lived duration of an actual observer. Simultaneity at a distance for him is grounded in the simultaneity of flows perceived in one consciousness, so the relativistic reading cannot abolish a single real time. In the debate of 6 April 1922 at the Société française de philosophie, Einstein replied that there is no "time of the philosophers", only the psychological and the physical. Modern assessments (Canales 2015, and the physics-side literature) generally find that Bergson's technical argument about reciprocity and the twin was mistaken. His wider claim, that physical time is measured, spatialized time and not duration, is a separate matter.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Spatialized time and the possible (targets R3, R6′, O12; concerns passage).** F(b) ⊆ F(b′) is accrual represented as set inclusion on an ordered family of sets. Bergson would say it is the model of becoming as a ratio of positions: the totality is a completed whole (R6′: the union is the block), and the parts "come into reality" by being revealed. That is the "retrograde movement of the true" and the "possible preceding the real" that he criticizes (the essay "Le possible et le réel", 1930, from memory). If the future events exist in the totality, novelty is only apparent. The view stays neutral on determinism, but its own claim to capture *passage* reduces to a monotone family of sets, which is what a cinematographic model would supply.

**(b) Instants and points (targets P1 and the unit of the theorem).** Durée attaches to a continuing process, not to a point. An event-standpoint is an instantaneous section. The theorem and R1 treat the standpoint as a point and its past as a set of points. Bergson would say these are mental cuts.

**(c) Relativity specifically (framing attack; concerns clock time versus lived duration).** The 1922 position is that relativity, correctly read, yields many *measured* times and one *lived* time. Local fragmentalism is, from Bergson's side, the opposite error: it ontologizes the many. Bergson's alternative is empirically equivalent in the sense that it leaves the physics alone (his "real observer" is the one whose frame we are in). It trades the uniform treatment of observers for a privileged real one. That has the costs of neo-Lorentzian views (R8: preferred structure), and in spirit it is the frame-based perspectivalism of Fine rather than anything event-indexed.

### Already answered?
O12 is the closest: passage is growth along a worldline, a standpoint-internal fact. It does not touch the "possible precedes the real" form of (a), which is aimed at the totality. R6 and R6′ say the totality is the block; that is the eternalist's truth. Bergson needs the claim that the whole is not a given. (b) is not addressed anywhere; Clifton and Hogarth's Theorem 2 and the paper's worldline corollary (Corollary on worldline-dependent becoming) give an available fix. On (c), R8 says shared-present views pay in locality or structure, which is a reply in kind but does not engage the point about measured versus lived time.

### Severity
(a) **serious** for the "passage is real" claim, **manageable** for the physical claims, because the theorem is silent on determinism and on the openness of the future. (b) **manageable**: the view can take the worldline as the unit. (c) **misfire** as an objection to the physics (the technical 1922 arguments about reciprocity are generally judged to have failed), **manageable** as a statement that the view says nothing about lived duration.

### Best reply
(a) Make the openness question explicit: the view takes no stand on whether the totality is determinate in advance of its parts. Passage in the view is growth of what is real from each standpoint, and nothing in the theorem says the future is fixed in a stronger sense than the block says. The cost is that R6′ should be presented as optional and as a view *from* the whole, not as a thesis that the whole is "already" there. (b) Take the unit to be the pair (worldline, event on it), with F(b) as the cross-section; the paper's worldline-dependent theorem supports this.

### Prior anticipation
Two flags. (1) The *cone* diagram of *Matière et mémoire* (an inverted cone with its apex at the present, the whole past conserved in the widening sections) looks like a past cone with an apex at the present, and the past accumulates. This is an iconic resemblance: Bergson's cone is a cone of memory within one consciousness and has nothing to do with light cones. I flag it only because a reader will notice, and it should not be cited as an anticipation. (2) Bergson's "real observer versus fictitious observers" reading of relativity is a perspectival view of real time, closer to Fine's frame-based fragments than to event-indexing. Neither shows a causal-past identification.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That model-theoretic "growth" does not by itself capture the qualitative novelty that tense realists often want, and that the claim "the view captures passage" should be put at the weaker level: it captures *directed accumulation of what is real from a standpoint*. Also that the 1922 episode is a cautionary tale about arguing from lived time against the physics: the view should avoid the symmetric error of arguing from the physics to a claim about lived time.

### Sources
Bergson, *Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience* (1889; *Time and Free Will*, Pogson 1910); *Matière et mémoire* (1896; *Matter and Memory*, Paul and Palmer 1911); *Durée et simultanéité* (1922; English: Jacobson 1965, and Lewis and Durie 1999, from memory); "Le possible et le réel" (1930, in *La pensée et le mouvant*). Jimena Canales, *The Physicist and the Philosopher: Einstein, Bergson, and the Debate That Changed Our Understanding of Time* (Princeton UP, 2015; date of the debate, 6 April 1922, confirmed by search). Read depth: **from memory**; Canales **discovery** (publisher and reviews only).

## 4. Merleau-Ponty

### Steelman
In *Phénoménologie de la perception* (1945), Part III ch. 3 ("La temporalité"), time is not a real process that I merely record. It is born of my relation to things, and the subject *is* temporality and not something in time. Past, present and future are not points on a line but a network of intentionalities (Husserl's longitudinal and transverse intentionality, reread as the subject's bodily openness to a field). The present has thickness: it is a field of presence with horizons of the just-past and the about-to-come, which are not "contained" in it but held in its grip. The past is sedimented in the body's habits. There is no time in the things themselves taken apart from a perspective that supports it. A view from nowhere ("*pensée de survol*", overflight thought) is the abstraction of objective thought, which forgets the situated subject it comes from. The many subjective temporalities communicate through a shared world; there is one time because there is one world.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Overflight (targets P5, R6, R6′, O13).** The standpoint-invariant facts and the view-from-the-whole are objective thought in the strict sense he criticizes: a perspective purporting to be none. The view's own gloss that the whole has "no present" is the objectivist's way of saying that time is not in the world as seen from nowhere, which MP would accept, but the view then reinstates the block as the content of that overview.

**(b) Thickness and horizon (targets R2; concerns time-consciousness).** A point-event with a set of earlier events is not a field with horizons. The relation between b and its past is, for MP, internal (the past is held in the present), and the view's relation is external (membership in a set). This overlaps with Husserl (a) and (b).

**(c) Perspective is not a position in space or in a causal order (framing attack).** MP's perspective is embodied and inhabited, not located by coordinates. The idea that a standpoint is determined by an event in a Lorentzian manifold makes the world a container in which perspectives are placed, the very picture the book argues against. A lived perspective cannot be derived from an event's causal past.

### Already answered?
O7 and O8 touch on experience; none touches the objectivism charge. The view does not claim to derive perspective, it uses the word. The charge in (a) is only half answerable through "the view from the whole is not privileged".

### Severity
**Manageable.** The claims (a)–(c) are aimed at the claim that the view captures what time is for a perceiver, which is not asserted. They would become **serious** only if the view were extended to experience.

### Best reply
Same as for Husserl: scope restriction. For (a): the view already declines to make the overview privileged (P5 is about invariance, not about a God's-eye truth), and the version without P7 does not even posit a standpoint of the whole. Concession: say that "standpoint" in the view is a technical index, and do not borrow the lived connotation of "perspective" in the exposition. The sentence "Presentists are right about how reality is from every standpoint" is the one most exposed.

### Prior anticipation
None on causal pasts. A structural echo: MP's claim that the past is *held in* the present rather than left behind resembles inclusion F(b) ⊆ F(b′), but again as analogy. MP's later *Nature* lectures at the Collège de France (1956–60) discuss physics, including relativity, as I recall; I could not verify what they say about simultaneity and it should be checked before the school is claimed to have a view on this.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That "perspective" and "standpoint" have a lived sense the exposition leans on, and that the claim "no view from nowhere" is a thesis the view shares at the level of tense but not at the level of the invariant facts.

### Sources
Merleau-Ponty, *Phénoménologie de la perception* (Gallimard 1945), Part III ch. 3 (Smith tr. 1962; Landes tr. 2012). *La Nature: notes, cours du Collège de France* (Seuil 1995; English, Vallier tr., Northwestern UP 2003). Read depth: **from memory**.

## 5. Derrida

### Steelman
In *La voix et le phénomène* (1967; *Speech and Phenomena*, Allison 1973; *Voice and Phenomenon*, Lawlor 2011), Derrida reads Husserl's first Logical Investigation and the time-consciousness lectures. The "living present" is supposed to be the source of meaning's self-presence. But the retention it needs is not a presence modified: it is the trace of what is no longer present, and so the present is constituted by non-presence. Self-presence is possible only through a repetition that already divides it (*différance*). Hence the metaphysics of presence, the determination of being as presence (*ousia* as *parousia*), is not escaped by moving from the objective now to the living present. In "Ousia et grammē: note sur une note de *Sein und Zeit*" (delivered 1968; *Marges de la philosophie*, 1972; *Margins of Philosophy*, Bass 1982), he reads Heidegger's footnote on Aristotle and Hegel. The tradition thinks time through the *now* (*stigmē*, point) and the *line* (*grammē*), and Aristotle's aporia of the now (it is and is not the same; it divides and joins past and future) is never solved by the tradition, including Heidegger's critique of it. There is no outside of this conceptual system from which "vulgar time" can simply be left behind. Deconstruction is not an alternative theory of time but a reading of how these oppositions (presence/absence, origin/derivation) are unstable.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) The view is the point-now made rigorous (framing attack).** An event is exactly the Aristotelian now: an indivisible limit. R2 ("from b, b alone is present") makes presence point-like, and the view's definition of the real from b as the set J^-(b) with b as apex makes *being* a matter of belonging to the domain of what stands in relation to a presence. "Real" is defined as "present to a standpoint (or having been)": being as presence. The view therefore sits inside the metaphysics Derrida analyzes, in its geometric form.

**(b) The apex is not self-identical (turns the view's own structure against its rhetoric).** The point b is defined by its past cone: it has no content except through what it is the limit of. That is the Derridean point about the living present (it is constituted by non-presence). So the view's structure *exemplifies* the trace structure that Derrida finds in the present, yet states the structure as the presence of a self-contained point with a set attached. The view contains deconstruction's insight but not the self-understanding.

**(c) The light cone is the aporia of the now.** The choice J^- versus I^- (R4, P6, P6′) is the question whether the boundary of the past belongs to it. This is a mathematical rerun of Aristotle's problem of the limit-instant (does the now belong to the past or the future?). The view resolves it by P6/P6′ (closure), a decision, not a derivation. Derrida would read the resolution as the tradition's usual gesture: a decision that conceals an undecidable.

**(d) Decentring.** Derrida's remark in "La structure, le signe et le jeu" (1966; from memory, with the Sokal controversy as a reminder of the risk) that the Einsteinian constant is not a center, but the concept of variability, is about the absence of a privileged center. The view agrees: no privileged standpoint, no present for the universe. But it replaces one center by a plurality of local ones, each with full presence of its apex (a monadology). Decentring that multiplies centres is not decentring of the logic of the centre.

### Already answered?
No. O2 and O12 touch the hollow-relabelling worry, but nothing in STRONGEST_VIEW addresses presence as such. R4's closure choice is justified (P6, P6′) as a physical or topological regularity, not as a decision about a limit, and in that sense it is answered for the theorem and unanswered for the philosophical gloss.

### Severity
(a) **manageable**: it is a classification, not a refutation, and Derrida does not claim to be able to provide a better theory of time. It misfires if read as "therefore the view is false". (b) is not an attack but an *opportunity*. (c) **manageable**: an observation about structure. Overall the Derrida question the brief poses ("is it the very metaphysics he deconstructs, or does it deconstruct presence itself?") has the answer: **both, in different respects**. It deconstructs the *shared*, universal present (the centre that organizes the block-versus-flow dispute), which is a real displacement, and it retains presence as the criterion of the real from each standpoint.

### Best reply
Accept (a) as a description and point to (b): the view's formal structure makes presence relational and derivative of the cone, so the "presence" of the apex is not primitive. A sentence to that effect is more honest than the phrase "present is the apex of reality". For (c), say that the closure choice is a decision, motivated twice (P6, P6′), and that the view lets the reader see that the alternative (I^-) is also coherent (R4). Concession: the view is not a break with the tradition of presence but a regional re-working of it.

### Prior anticipation
No anticipation of the causal-past content. The trace-structure parallel (the present as constituted by its past) is an interpretive resource, not an anticipation.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That "the present is real from its own standpoint" can be restated without presence as primitive: as a relation of dependence. The Derridean point and the Stein-style point converge on the claim that the present has no content apart from what has happened. Analytically, this can be put as the thesis that "presentness" in the view is definable from the order alone (apex of J^-), not an extra property. That is a modest gain in clarity for the view's exposition.

### Sources
Derrida, *La voix et le phénomène* (PUF 1967); *Speech and Phenomena*, tr. D. B. Allison (Northwestern UP 1973); *Voice and Phenomenon*, tr. L. Lawlor (Northwestern UP 2011). "Ousia et grammè", in *Marges de la philosophie* (Minuit 1972); *Margins of Philosophy*, tr. A. Bass (Chicago 1982); the page range 29–67 comes from one library record found by search. "La structure, le signe et le jeu" (1966) in *L'écriture et la différence* (1967). Read depth: **from memory**; bibliographic details partly confirmed by search (discovery).

## 6. Levinas

### Steelman
In *Le Temps et l'Autre* (lectures 1946–47; published 1948; Cohen tr. 1987), *Totalité et Infini* (1961) and *Autrement qu'être ou au-delà de l'essence* (1974), time is not the achievement of an isolated subject (against Husserl's constitution, Heidegger's being-toward-death, and Bergson's duration) but the relation to the Other. The future is the absolutely other: what cannot be grasped or anticipated, which "falls on" the subject. The present is achieved through "hypostasis" (*De l'existence à l'existant*, 1947), and the alterity of time is the alterity of persons. *Diachrony* is non-coincidence: a time of the Other that cannot be gathered into the synchrony of a shared present or of a representation, including an "immemorial" past that was never present. Synchrony and totality (the gathering of the many into one system, the *same* absorbing the *other*) are the ontological violence of Western philosophy. Ethics, not ontology, is first philosophy.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Totality (targets R6, R6′, P7, O13).** The union of fragments equal to the block, viewed from the whole, is a model of synchrony: every standpoint's time recovered into one representation. Levinas would call this the totalizing gesture, where diachrony is recuperated by thematization.

**(b) An immemorial past (targets R1 and R2; concerns temporal ontology).** The view says every event in the past of b was itself the apex of its own present (each event is its own standpoint). Levinas's immemorial past was never present, and so by definition cannot be a member of anyone's fragment as an event that "was once present". The view cannot represent a past that precedes all presence. This is a clear structural disagreement, but the view could respond that no event in the physical past is immemorial in that sense.

**(c) Framing.** Time is a relation among events, says the view. Levinas says time is a relation of persons, and that the relation to the Other is irreducible to a relation between terms in a system.

### Already answered?
O12 answers part of (a) by granting that the view from the whole is static and that passage is not a fact from the whole. No answer to (b)–(c) because they do not arise in the view's vocabulary.

### Severity
**Misfire** against the theorem and physical claims; **manageable** as a challenge to P7/R6′/O13. The attack is partly an *ally* of R2: non-synchronizability of distinct presents is Levinas's thesis, said in a different key.

### Best reply
Present the version without P7 as the main view. Then the totalizing standpoint is not part of the ontology. Concede that the block, as union, is a representation produced by a theorist and not a standpoint. (This is the same repair recommended for Bergson, Merleau-Ponty and Husserl.)

### Prior anticipation
Levinas's non-synchronizability anticipates R2 only in the loose sense that the Other's present is not co-present with mine. It is not a physical thesis and has no causal-past content.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That the "no shared present" result has a long non-physical pedigree and that it is not self-evidently a cost: for Levinas, the failure of synchrony is where ethics begins. The analytic author can also concede that the neutral, system-level description ("the totality of fragments") is a stance with a history.

### Sources
Levinas, *Le Temps et l'Autre* (Fata Morgana 1979; first published 1948; *Time and the Other*, tr. R. Cohen, Duquesne 1987); *Totalité et Infini* (1961); *Autrement qu'être* (1974); *De l'existence à l'existant* (1947). Read depth: **from memory**.

## 7. Ricoeur

### Steelman
*Temps et récit* (3 vols., 1983–85; Blamey and Pellauer, Chicago 1984–88) argues that time becomes human to the extent that it is organized in a narrative mode. In vol. 3, Part IV, the aporetics of temporality sets the phenomenological time of Augustine, Husserl and Heidegger against the cosmological time of Aristotle and Kant. Each tries to derive or occlude the other: phenomenology cannot constitute objective time without presupposing it, and the cosmological view cannot account for the "now" of someone who dates events. The aporia is not resolved by theory. Narrative, through its poetics, offers a mediating "third time", most clearly in historical time with three connectors: the calendar (an axial event, a direction of reckoning, units of measure), the succession of generations, and the *trace* (documents, archives, vestiges), which is both an effect in the physical world and a sign of the past. The aporia is "productive" and remains open.

### Strongest attacks
**(a) Does it bridge or restate the aporia?** The view lives on the cosmological side: its entities are events in a causal order, and "standpoint" is defined from that order. It offers a structural counterpart to the lived "now" (apex) and the lived "having-been" (J^-). It does not derive the phenomenological side, so it reinstates the aporia as the question why F(b) should correspond to lived time at all. If the view says "it doesn't", then it is simply cosmological time; if "it does", then objective time is presupposed in defining the standpoint, which is Ricoeur's circularity (objective time presupposed in constituting objective time).

**(b) The calendar (targets R2, R8).** Ricoeur's calendar time is an institutionally constituted shared now: an axial moment, a numbering of days. It is how shared dating is possible without cosmic simultaneity. If the view says "no shared present" and leaves shared dating as conventional, it supports Ricoeur's claim that common time is instituted. If it presents the absence of a shared present as a deep fact about reality, it must say how shared dating is possible, which Ricoeur treats as a creative act. In GR the point is stronger: there is no canonical global time to choose.

**(c) Trace.** J^-(b) is the causal reading of the trace. Ricoeur says the trace has two readings, causal and semiotic (the vestige is both an effect and a sign that stands for what is absent). The view has the first and not the second. A past that is real from b is, on the view, simply *there*; for Ricoeur the past is accessible only through traces that need interpretation. This presses on the epistemic status of "real from b".

### Already answered?
O7 and O8 touch the experience question and nothing else does. R2's cost to shared experience is not discussed. The view's physics-first strategy is not defended against the circularity charge.

### Severity
**Serious for the "resolves the divide" rhetoric, manageable for the physics.** The aporia is not a refutation. The *good* version of the attack is also the one the view can accept: it does not bridge, it relocates the cosmological side, and it makes one specific claim about it.

### Best reply
Say that the view bridges nothing and does not try to: it is a thesis within cosmological time, with a precise answer to "what is present, past and future for an event". The phenomenological aporia is untouched, and the view's contribution to it is a negative one (a shared objective now is unavailable, so whatever mediates the common time of persons cannot be cosmological simultaneity). Ricoeur's calendar and trace then become the account of how common dating is possible, and the physical trace relation is the causal half of his trace. Concession: concede the aporia in so many words.

### Prior anticipation
None. The causal trace relation is not a causal-past ontology. I did not find (nor recall) a sustained discussion of relativity in *Temps et récit*, but I have not checked, so I make no claim.

### What an analytic author can learn or concede
That the epistemology of the past (how b's past is accessible to anyone) is a separate question from its reality-from-b, and that tying them together would require a theory of traces. Also that "common time without cosmic simultaneity" is a good question the analytic literature underplays: the answer (conventional, constructed, local approximations) is already implied by R8, and Ricoeur gives it a vocabulary.

### Sources
Ricoeur, *Temps et récit* I–III (Seuil 1983, 1984, 1985); *Time and Narrative*, vols. 1–3, tr. K. McLaughlin (Blamey) and D. Pellauer (Chicago 1984, 1985, 1988). Read depth: **from memory**; vol. 3 bibliographic details confirmed by search (discovery).

## Top threats from this group

1. **The "equivocation on present" charge (Husserl a/b, Merleau-Ponty c, Heidegger a, Ricoeur a).** The view's tensed vocabulary is carried by a structural relation (apex of a cone) with no connection to what "present" means in experience. It is the phenomenological face of O2, and none of the replies in STRONGEST_VIEW meets it as such. Scope: temporal ontology versus time-consciousness. It is not fatal to any labelled claim, but it limits what §4 ("Why this resolves the divide") and the table row "Tense lives in fragments" can honestly say.
2. **The totalizing overview (Bergson a, Levinas a, Merleau-Ponty a, and in part Derrida).** P7, R6, R6′ and O13 give a standpoint of the whole, which several schools read as the problem and not as the solution, and which Bergson reads as "the possible preceding the real". Scope: passage. This is the same weakness that O12 flags from the analytic side.
3. **Event-points as the unit (Bergson b, Husserl b, Merleau-Ponty b, Derrida a/c).** The view and the theorem take punctual events as standpoints. The schools say processes or fields are basic. The theorem has a worldline-dependent form, which gives a fix.

**The single repair that would do the most good:** (i) present the version **without P7** as the main view, with the totality's fragment demoted to a remark; and (ii) add an explicit **scope paragraph**: the view is a thesis about what is real from where in a causal structure; "standpoint", "present" and "past" are technical positions in that structure and not claims about lived time; it takes no stand on the openness of the future and does not claim to bridge phenomenological and cosmological time. This costs the clean reply to O12/O13 (the Lipman-style atemporal standpoint) and the phrase "resolves the divide" in its widest sense. It gains protection against the whole group at once.

## Prior anticipations found

No anticipation of the central content (fragment = causal past, derived by a locality theorem) in this group. Analogies only, each flagged above and none to be cited as priority:

- Bergson, *Matière et mémoire*: the inverted cone with apex at the present (iconic resemblance only; a cone of memory in a single consciousness).
- Bergson 1922: "real observer versus fictitious observers" as a perspectival reading of relativity (closer to Fine's frame fragments than to event-indexing).
- Husserl: nested retention as the analogue of P2 transitivity; Merleau-Ponty: the past "held in" the present as the analogue of fragment inclusion.
- Levinas: non-synchronizability of distinct presents, in an ethical and not physical key, as the analogue of R2.
- Derrida: the present constituted by its trace, as an interpretive reading of the apex-and-cone structure.
- Outside this group, flagged for whoever has process philosophy: Whitehead's "actual world" of an occasion as its causal past, and the causal independence of contemporary occasions (*Process and Reality*, 1929), which from memory is the nearest prior art to the content of the fragments. I did not check it here.
