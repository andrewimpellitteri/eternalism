## 1. Whitehead

**Steelman.** Reality is process: the ultimate units are actual occasions, each a concrescence in which a many (the already-actual world) is gathered into a novel one. Every occasion prehends only occasions already complete, so its "actual world" is a relative notion, specific to each occasion (from memory; the standard locator is PR Part I, ch. 2, around the first-edition p. 28, secondary-confirmed only in sense). Occasions that are neither in each other's past nor future are "contemporary" and are causally independent of one another (secondary: study-guide and Lango-based summaries point to PR pp. 123–26; the exact wording "contemporary events happen in causal independence of each other" I recall but could not locate in a fetched page, so treat the quote and page as unverified). Whitehead explicitly takes relativity to require this: there is no unique cosmic simultaneity, only a "unison of becoming" of contemporaries that carries no causal or ontological weight (secondary). Becoming is the creative advance, an epochal acquisition of one occasion at a time, not a flow of a time-parameter. In The Principle of Relativity (CUP 1922; from memory) he offers a gravitational theory built on a uniform, flat-type spacetime structure that he preferred to Einstein's variable curvature. That theory was later refuted empirically (Will 1971, from memory; the sign of the effect on Earth tides) and does not generalise.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *Priority (the main threat; concerns objective passage and causal order).* Secondary sources in my search say outright that in Whitehead the actual world of an occasion is the set of occasions in its backward light cone (Stapp 1977, quoted via religion-online: "The actual world of a given event is the set of all actual events" with the backward light-cone clause; Process Studies 7(3), pp. 173–182, secondary read depth, snippet only). If that is accurate, P1 + R1 + R2 (standpoint-relative reality, fragment = causal past, co-presence trivial) are Whitehead's metaphysical position as read by Stapp and Hättich, 70 years before Stein. The "relativity forces the point-relative cone" step is also Whitehead's own motive, not merely Stein's.
(B) *Ontological direction is reversed.* Whitehead's actual world is what an occasion *prehends*; it is a stock of *settled* facts feeding a *concrescing* subject. The concrescing occasion itself is not in its own actual world (its satisfaction is not yet a datum). On the view, F(b) includes b (reflexive R, b present from b). A Whiteheadian will say that the view mislabels the apex as a fact "as it stands" when the whole point of becoming is that the apex is the not-yet-determinate. This is an attack on R2/R3 as an account of passage: growth of fragments F(b) ⊆ F(b') is block-style accumulation, whereas Whitehead's creative advance is generation of the thing that grows.
(C) *Extended occasions.* Whitehead's occasions are finite regions, not points. The cone for an extended region has no unique apex, and the causal past of a region is the union of J^-(p) over its points, which is exactly P7's F(R). Hence for Whitehead P7 is the basic case, not the optional extra, and the pointwise fragment is a limit idealisation. This bears on O7/P7, and on whether the "event" in the theorem is the right ontological unit.
(D) *Bridge to GR.* Whitehead himself did not accept general-relativistic curvature, and his "congruence" and "extensive connection" machinery (PR Part IV, from memory) presupposes more uniform structure than a generic Lorentzian manifold. So his tradition has no purchase on the GR case; this cuts both ways: it shows that your GR extension is not anticipated, but also that the process tradition might insist on a uniform background (a foliation or conformal structure) which the view's P3 excludes by fiat.
(E) *God.* Whitehead's consequent nature prehends all actual occasions; the function of this "standpoint of the whole" is close to P7/R6. But it is a temporal, growing whole (creative advance continues), which conflicts with R6 ("the view from the whole is the static block").

**Already answered?** Partly. STRONGEST_VIEW's priority section names Stein, Fine, Lipman, Rovelli, Slavov but not Whitehead, so the claim "the content of the fragments is not new in the metaphysical literature" is not yet addressed. O9 ("Fine already said this") has the right form but the wrong target. Nothing answers B or E. C is partly answered by P7 but only as optional.

**Severity.** (A) is *serious for novelty, not for truth*: it takes away "what an event's perspective contains" as a new idea and leaves the derivation (theorem, classification, GR, causal sets). (B) is *manageable*. (C) *manageable*. (D) *manageable*. (E) *manageable*.

**Best reply.** Concede the thesis-level anticipation and recast the contribution as: a theorem showing the Whiteheadian identification is *forced* by locality in all spacetimes, together with J^- versus I^- (R4), causal sets (R5), and Gödel isolation (R7), none of which Whitehead has. To meet (B), split F(b) into the settled part J^-(b)\{b} and the apex {b} (the concrescing event) and say that R2's "present from b" names the apex only; this costs nothing in the theorems since they concern the set, and it recovers Whitehead's asymmetry between subject and datum. To meet (C), promote P7 from optional to part of the basic statement.

**Prior anticipation.** Yes, strong at the level of the philosophical content "an occasion's actual world is its causal past; contemporaries are causally independent", on secondary evidence. Hättich (see 4) and Stapp read Whitehead this way. Whether *Whitehead himself* used the light cone in PR is a matter of interpretation: PR works with extensive connection and "contemporary" defined by non-connection, which in a Minkowski setting coincides with spacelike separation (from memory). Do not claim that Whitehead derived anything; but do not claim the identification is new.

Whitehead, *The Principle of Relativity* (CUP 1922), from memory. ## 2. Hartshorne and process theology

**Steelman.** Hartshorne developed a neoclassical metaphysics in which becoming is the fundamental category: the past is settled and "objective", the future is genuinely open (objective modalities), and God is the supreme, ever-growing cosmic experience who is affected by every event (dipolar theism). On relativity he held (from memory) that the physical facts permit a cosmic, God-given "now" or that the relativistic insight is just that *causally independent events are not ordered*, but that this is compatible with God's omnipresent prehension of the whole of the settled past; the divine perspective gives the "cosmic present" that physics leaves unmarked. He also pressed (from memory) that the "past" is the only settled domain, and that this is why physics' "block" is an abstraction from process.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *R6 and the "no standpoint of the whole".* If there is a God, there is a standpoint that encompasses all that is settled at any stage of the creative advance; this standpoint has a present, a growing one. On P7 the totality's fragment is the whole block (R6'). Hartshorne would say that this is the one error the view cannot avoid: the whole is not a block; it is a growing totality whose "present" is the boundary of the settled. If the objector gives the divine standpoint a preferred foliation, then P3 fails at the divine standpoint, and the view must say whether it permits such a standpoint (concerns clock time and objective passage).
(B) *Open future.* The view says the future is "not real from b". Hartshorne distinguishes unreality from indeterminacy. The theorem is silent on whether the future of b is open or merely not real from b. If spacetime is deterministic, then J^+(b) is fixed by data on Σ, and what is "not real from b" is nevertheless fixed. So the view yields a non-existence claim but not an openness claim, and Hartshorne's tradition wants the latter. This concerns determinism/open future, not causal order, and it is a real gap. O6 mentions Clifton–Hogarth but not this.
(C) *Eternalism-with-tense, O2 again.* Process theologians give O2 an independent form: if the subject's "being real for b" is merely a feature of a block, then real novelty is lost; novelty is what makes creativity ultimate.

**Already answered?** O2 and O12 gesture at (C), but there is no answer on (A) and (B). R6' is the exact opposite of Hartshorne's claim, and nothing in the document addresses a *temporal* whole.

**Severity.** (A) *serious* if the view is advertised as comprehensive (it says "no standpoint of the whole is privileged"); *manageable* if the view stays neutral on theology. (B) *serious* for a presentist/growing-block audience, since "not real" is not "open". (C) *manageable* (same as O2).

**Best reply.** To (A): state explicitly that the view is a view about physical standpoints and that any divine or cosmic standpoint with a present would be extra structure violating P3 (R8, second horn). Cost: the view cannot absorb panentheism. To (B): add an explicit "no claim about openness" disclaimer, or add a premise (P8) that fragments are determined only by their own causal past (a dynamical openness claim). The latter is a new physical commitment (and conflicts with deterministic dynamics).

**Prior anticipation.** Partial. Hartshorne held (from memory) that the "elsewhere" region is causally unrelated and not ordered, and that only the causal past is settled for an event. He did not produce a classification.

**Sources.** Hartshorne, *Creative Synthesis and Philosophic Method* (1970) and *Whitehead's Philosophy: Selected Essays 1935–1970* (1972), *Reality as Social Process* (1953), from memory, no locators, not read. Treat everything above as a lead to verify. Next step: search Hartshorne on "cosmic simultaneity" and "Einstein" in the 1953–70 essays.

## 3. Čapek

**Steelman.** Čapek, a Bergsonian physicist-philosopher, argued over decades that relativity and quantum physics rehabilitate becoming against the "myth of frozen passage". His core move (from memory of the 1961 book, ch. on relativity, and the 1966 and 1975 papers) is that special relativity destroys *cosmic* simultaneity and therefore a cosmic "now", but it preserves an invariant causal order between events connected by signals; what becomes in the world is therefore a plurality of causally linked "local" processes, and the light-cone "elsewhere" is a region where no temporal ordering, and so no determinate "already" or "not yet", obtains. In the 1975 paper (whose last section is titled "The Physical Emptiness of the Future", confirmed in search snippets from Čapek's own reference list; *Foundations of Physics* 5, pp. 607–616 per one citation, another gives IV, 610–17: verify) he replies to Grünbaum's claim that relativity eliminates becoming. His line (from memory) is that the Minkowski diagram is a geometrisation that suppresses the causal, genetic asymmetry between the past cone and the future cone, and that the future is "physically empty" at the point-event, not yet determinate.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *Priority (the single most dangerous attack in this group; concerns objective passage and causal order).* If Čapek says that becoming is *local*, with the absolute past cone settled and the future cone and elsewhere empty or indeterminate, then P1, P2, P4 and R1–R3 are in his philosophy, possibly stated more directly than Stein's, and with a Bergsonian motivation for the "why". I cannot verify what he literally wrote. I recall that Čapek explicitly rejects "universal becoming" and talks of a "causal net" in which "local" becomings are woven; and an article in *Diogenes* titled "Timespace rather than spacetime" is in my search results under his name (title only, discovery). A reviewer who knows Čapek will see "becoming relative to the light-cone, no cosmic now, the elsewhere has no temporal status" as old.
(B) *Čapek would reject the theorem's "kinematic" framing.* For a Bergsonian, becoming is duration, a qualitative interpenetration, not a binary reality-membership relation. Defining "has become for b" as "x ∈ F(b)" is the spatialisation of time that Bergson attacked. Attack on P2 (the bridge), concerning objective passage.
(C) *Past and future are not symmetric in reality.* Čapek's "emptiness of the future" claim is stronger than the view's: the future is not "real from b" but may still be determinate (R3 is silent). Same as Hartshorne (B).
(D) *The Putnam/Rietdijk reading.* He argued that relativity does not force the block, so the interpretive neutrality that STRONGEST_VIEW claims in O2 is Čapek's own position (relativity and eternalism are not equivalent), and he gave this reading before Stein's own 1968 paper (which was Stein's reply to Putnam).

**Already answered?** Not at all; Čapek is not mentioned in the document or in PRIOR_WORK (grep). O9's pattern is the right template.

**Severity.** (A) *serious*, possibly *fatal to the novelty claim at the philosophical level* if his texts say it plainly; no effect on the theorem. (B) *manageable*. (C) *manageable*. (D) *misfire* as an attack, but relevant as context.

**Best reply.** Read Čapek 1961 ch. on relativity, "Time in Relativity Theory: Arguments for a Philosophy of Becoming" (in J. T. Fraser, ed., *The Voices of Time*, 1966; chapter and page range from memory, verify), and 1975 in full before the next draft. Then restate the claim as: the *derivation* of fragment = J^- under locality and covariance, its extension to GR and causal sets, the fine structure at the cone, and Gödel isolation. Cost: concede that "becoming is causal-past-relative and local" is a standing philosophical thesis.

**Prior anticipation.** Probably yes, philosophically (highest risk), unverified. Specific claims to check in Čapek: (i) becoming local, no cosmic now; (ii) past cone is what has become for an event; (iii) explicit denial that the "elsewhere" is co-real.

**Sources.** Čapek, *The Philosophical Impact of Contemporary Physics* (Van Nostrand, 1961; xvii+414 pp., confirmed by catalogue), discovery plus from-memory. Čapek, "Relativity and the Status of Becoming," *Foundations of Physics* 5 (1975) 607–616 (volume and pages inconsistent across citations; verify). Čapek, "The Myth of Frozen Passage," *Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 2 (1965) 441–453 (from Čapek's own citation seen in search). Čapek, "Timespace rather than spacetime," *Diogenes* (title seen in search only). No full text read.

## 4. Shimony and Stapp (Whiteheadian quantum events); also Hättich

**Steelman.** Shimony ("Quantum Physics and the Philosophy of Whitehead," 1965, reprinted in *Search for a Naturalistic World View* vol. 2; "Events and Processes in the Quantum World," 1986; and the essay on "The Transient Now", from memory) argued that quantum state reduction is a physical "actualization" event that supplies the becoming Whitehead describes, and wrestled with relativity: he took the "passage" literature seriously and tried to preserve a local, point-event structure of passage compatible with the light cone. Stapp (1977 *Process Studies* paper above; later "Bell's Theorem and Stapp's Revised View of Space-Time", *Process Studies* 1996, and *Mind, Matter and Quantum Mechanics*) first held Whitehead's constraint (an occasion depends only on its backward cone), then concluded Bell's theorem refutes it and revised the Whiteheadian picture to allow nonlocal, frame-dependent ordering of actualizations (Tomonaga–Schwinger style). Hättich (*Quantum Processes: A Whiteheadian Interpretation of Quantum Field Theory*, Agenda Verlag 2004; secondary: reviewed by Bain, SHPMP) reads QFT with occasions as fundamental and defines the actual world of an occasion as its causal past (secondary); Stapp's review says Hättich's conclusion that Whitehead's doctrine is ruled out does not follow, and suggests an ordered sequence of occasions (secondary, via search snippet).

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *O6, but sharper: the tradition closest to your view abandoned it because of Bell.* Stapp took the view "an event's reality is its backward light cone" seriously, found it inconsistent with a generalised Bell's theorem, and replaced it. Your document treats nonlocality as an "open" pocket and cites Dieks. The Stapp–Shimony literature is the best-developed *prior* discussion of exactly this trade-off, and it went the other way. Concerns causal order and determinism.
(B) *Becoming as collapse.* If becoming is physical actualization (collapse), then becoming is not a relation of "reality from b" but an event with a location, and collapse in a relativistic setting needs either a foliation (Tomonaga–Schwinger / hypersurface-dependent) or nonlocal correlations. Then (P3w) fails for the process that does the work of "becoming".
(C) *The ordered-sequence rescue* (Stapp on Hättich): impose a sequence of occasions; this is a global structure, exactly what R8 calls costly. It is Monton-style.
(D) *Where the view is silent.* The view claims compatibility via Dieks and says it is "hostage". Shimony would say it is a failure to address what becoming *is*.

**Already answered?** O6 and §8 point 2 name the problem and call it open; nothing engages Stapp's or Shimony's specific proposals.

**Severity.** (A) *serious* (the same exposure as §8 item 2, now with a precedent). (B) *serious*, interpretation-dependent. (C), (D) *manageable*.

**Best reply.** Add the Stapp 1977 → 1996 trajectory to the O6 reply as a documented case of the trade-off, and state that the view takes the other horn (no-collapse or perspectival readings). Cost: the view is tied to Everettian or Dieks-style readings, which many process thinkers reject.

**Prior anticipation.** Yes: Hättich and Stapp 1977 hold "an occasion's actual world is the set of events in its backward cone" (secondary, snippets, discovery depth). Shimony's own stated view is unverified.

**Sources.** Stapp 1977 (bibliography confirmed; snippet). Stapp, "Whiteheadian Process and Quantum Theory of Mind," LBNL-42143 (1998), abstract fetched (no relevant passage). Shimony 1965, 1986, 1993 (from memory). Hättich 2004 (secondary). Next step: read Stapp 1977 and 1996, Hättich ch. on causal structure.

## 5. Deleuze (with Bergson)

**Steelman.** In *Difference and Repetition* (1968; Patton tr. 1994, ch. II "Repetition for Itself") the three syntheses of time are: (1) the passive synthesis of habit, which constitutes a living present by contracting instants; (2) the synthesis of Memory (Mnemosyne), in which the pure past is the ground of passing presents and is not a former present but coexists with each present ("the past is contemporaneous with the present that it was"); (3) the empty form of time (Aion, Hölderlin's caesura), in which the future as eternal return selects and makes difference return. In *Bergsonism* (1966) the ontological virtual past coexists as a whole and each present is a contraction of it at some degree of tension. Bergson himself (*Durée et simultanéité*, 1922; from memory) argued that the relativistic "multiple times" are measurement artefacts and there is one real duration; Čapek is the Bergsonian who disagreed.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *Past is not a set of former presents.* F(b) = J^-(b) treats the past as a set of events that were present and now are settled. Deleuze says that the past is pure virtuality that coexists with the present. On that view, "growth of fragments" (R3) is a picture of the passive synthesis only (the habit-time of the living present, ordering by succession), and the foundation of time is not in succession at all. Concerns the metaphysics of time-consciousness and passage, not causal order.
(B) *No standpoint ontology.* Deleuze rejects the subject and the perspectival centre, but also rejects a view from nowhere; what exists is a field of impersonal singularities. "Standpoint" in P1 imports the individuated point of view that the second synthesis dissolves.
(C) *The future.* The third synthesis is the future as the power of the new; a theory in which the future is simply "not real from b" has the wrong ontology of the future, since the future is the real condition of repetition. This overlaps with Hartshorne (B).
(D) *Bergsonian simultaneity.* If Bergson is right that there is one duration, then P3 (locality) and R2 are false; but relativity has confirmed the opposite, and Deleuze himself does not defend Bergson's physics.

**Already answered?** No. O7 (specious present) does not touch Deleuzean time. No objection of this type is addressed.

**Severity.** (A) *misfire at the level of the physics and the theorem*, but *manageable* as a challenge to the claim that the view captures "becoming". (B) *manageable*. (C) *manageable*. (D) *misfire* (physics of relativity of simultaneity is settled).

**Best reply.** State plainly that the view concerns temporal ontology and causal order, not the phenomenology or the metaphysics of duration; say what the view is silent on (what makes the past "pure"). Cost: it gives up claiming to be a theory of time-consciousness.

**Prior anticipation.** None for the causal-past content. Deleuze's virtual past is an altogether different structure.

**Sources.** Deleuze, *Différence et répétition* (PUF 1968), Patton tr. (Columbia UP 1994), ch. II; *Le bergsonisme* (PUF 1966), Tomlinson–Habberjam tr. (1988); Bergson, *Durée et simultanéité* (1922). All from memory.

## 6. Meillassoux

**Steelman.** In *Après la finitude* (2006; Brassier tr., *After Finitude*, 2008) Meillassoux names "correlationism" the thesis that we have access only to the correlation of thought and being, never to either term alone. Ancestral statements (about the "arche-fossil", e.g. the accretion of the Earth 4.56 billion years ago, before any givenness) challenge it: science speaks literally of a time anterior to all relation to a subject. He argues that the only way to take such statements at face value is to absolutise the facticity of the correlation, arriving at the necessity of contingency, "hyper-chaos": no law, including physical law and time's order, is necessary. Time is thus not a form of intuition but the capacity for anything to come into being or perish without reason.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *Correlationism by reality-for-a-standpoint.* P1 and P2 define reality as "from b". Meillassoux will say this is correlationism with events replacing subjects: what is "real" is indexed to a standpoint. The ancestral statement "the Earth formed 4.56 Gyr ago" is, in the view, either tenselessly invariant (P5, hence fine) or tensed and standpoint-relative, in which case it is true "for b" and the world as such is reached only via the totality (P7). If the view says the fundamental facts are standpoint-relative (the relativist reading of P1), it is a correlationism of events. Concerns temporal ontology.
(B) *Ancestrality and the earlier fragment.* There is a sharper form: for an ancestral event e (no observer in J^-(e)), the fragment F(e) contains no life and no thought. The view therefore treats such an e as a perfectly good standpoint, which *rebuts* the subject-correlationist reading; but the tensed statement "the future of e does not exist from e" then makes the existence of the thinker-containing events relative to later standpoints, which is what the arche-fossil denies.
(C) *Hyper-chaos against the off-shell premise and the uniqueness result.* O4 and R9 assume one rule for every geometrically possible spacetime and that the dynamics (causal structure) is stable. Meillassoux would say that this is a covert principle of sufficient reason: the stability of the cone structure and the laws across all possibilities. A contingent-lawed world gives no ground to make the rule universal. This is an attack on the bridge premises (P3, the off-shell premise), concerning causal order.
(D) *The inaccessible future.* For Meillassoux the future's novelty is absolute: any lawful constraint on what will be real from b is contingent, so "F(b) ⊆ J^-(b) as an invariant theorem" overstates.

**Already answered?** No. O4 notes Gödel's modal premise but no response to hyper-chaos.

**Severity.** (A) *manageable*: the view's standpoints are events, not subjects, so the arche-fossil is accommodated (B shows the ancestral event is a legitimate standpoint). I flag a risk, though: the relativist P1 reading is the weak spot. (C) *manageable* to *serious* for anyone who accepts Meillassoux's premises; it applies to any physics-based argument and not specifically to this one. (D) *misfire*: the theorem is conditional on stated premises.

**Best reply.** For (A): the correlate is the causal order, which is not a correlation with mind; the invariant content (P5) is exactly what holds from every standpoint, and ancestral statements are of this tenseless kind. Say so in the document. Cost: concede that tensed claims are not absolute. For (C): concede that the uniform-law premise is a metaphysical commitment, and that the result is conditional on it.

**Prior anticipation.** None.

**Sources.** Meillassoux, *Après la finitude: essai sur la nécessité de la contingence* (Seuil 2006); *After Finitude*, tr. R. Brassier (Continuum 2008). From memory, no locators.

## 7. Stengers and Prigogine

**Steelman.** Prigogine and Stengers (*La nouvelle alliance*, 1979; *Order out of Chaos*, 1984) and Prigogine (*From Being to Becoming*, 1980; *La fin des certitudes*, 1996, English *The End of Certainty*, 1997) argue that irreversibility is a fundamental physical fact, not a coarse-graining artefact. The time-reversible laws of classical and quantum dynamics describe an idealised, integrable fragment of nature; for unstable, non-integrable systems the Poincaré resonances break time-symmetry at the level of the dynamics, and the statistical description (not trajectories) is fundamental. Prigogine introduces an "internal time" operator T for the statistical state and says the arrow is constitutive of the order we find. They see the Einsteinian geometrisation (the "time is an illusion" attitude) as a loss and relativity as a theory of idealised, reversible dynamics. Stengers (e.g. *Thinking with Whitehead*, 2002/2011) joins Whitehead's process view with this physical case.

**Strongest attacks.**
(A) *Wrong arrow (a category objection).* The view's becoming is defined by causal order, which is entropy-neutral. The thermodynamic arrow and irreversibility are not the same as causal order, and Prigogine's claim is that the latter is not what makes time. The view says nothing about why growth of fragments goes the direction of entropy increase, and a time-reversed copy of the spacetime has a time-reversed causal structure with the same theorem. Concerns the thermodynamic arrow.
(B) *Internal time as global.* Prigogine's T is defined on distributions over phase space, and a cosmological statistical state defines a global time-like parameter. If this is the real physical time, then it provides a physically privileged foliation, which is §8 item 1 ("A physically privileged foliation") arrived at from statistical mechanics. This would undermine P3's motivation. (I do not know of an actual relativistic treatment in Prigogine's work that delivers this, and its absence is also a reply.)
(C) *Time is not a relation among events.* For the Prigogine–Stengers tradition, time is constitutive of events by irreversible processes rather than a geometric relation (the "event" as the unit comes from processes). The view's use of J^- as primary treats geometry as basic.
(D) *Open future as physical.* The unpredictability of unstable systems is, for them, what gives the future its openness. Same gap as Hartshorne (B).

**Already answered?** The document does not address the arrow at all. It treats causal order as basic, and it would be worth a paragraph. §8 item 1 covers (B) in form.

**Severity.** (A) *manageable*: it is a request for scope clarity, not a refutation; the view makes no thermodynamic claim. (B) *manageable* (a hypothetical; no relativistic version exists in what I know). (C), (D) *misfire* against the theorem; *manageable* as scope limits.

**Best reply.** Add an explicit scope statement: the view concerns temporal ontology and causal order, and is neutral on the arrow. Cost: the view loses the claim that "becoming is real and directed" in the thermodynamic sense; "directed" means the causal orientation of spacetime only.

**Prior anticipation.** None.

**Sources.** Prigogine and Stengers, *Order Out of Chaos* (1984); Prigogine, *From Being to Becoming* (1980); Prigogine, *The End of Certainty* (Free Press 1997); Stengers, *Thinking with Whitehead* (Harvard UP 2011). All from memory.

## 8. Nietzsche's eternal recurrence and the Gödel result (curiosity)

**Steelman.** Nietzsche (*Gay Science* §341; *Zarathustra*, "On the Vision and the Riddle"; from memory) poses recurrence as a test: could you will this life again, eternally? Cosmologically (Nachlass notes, finite energy and infinite time), every configuration recurs. Amor fati is willing the past as it was; "the will cannot will backwards" is the ressentiment against "it was" (*Zarathustra*, "On Redemption").

**Attack and observations.**
(A) In a Gödel-type or otherwise totally vicious spacetime (R7) every fragment is everything, so a literally cyclic or closed-time cosmos is the one case where the view *coincides* with eternalism. Nietzsche's doctrine is a stance, not a spacetime theory, and numerical recurrence ("the same event again") is not the same as the identity of events in a periodic time (which is what totally vicious spacetimes realise). This is a pun, not an objection.
(B) A more serious Nietzschean point: the doctrine tests whether the past is willed. The view makes the entire past real and fixed from every later standpoint (R3), and no later standpoint can lack it.
