## The central argument, stated once (the "translation argument" against O2)

All positions below press O2, so here is the sharpest general form, which the sections then use.

1. Let L_F be the fragmentalist's language: "x is real from b", "x has become for b", F(b), P1-P7. Let L_B be the tenseless language of an eternalist with Lorentzian causal structure: events, the order <=, and ordinary quantification.
2. Translation T: "x is real from b" becomes "x <= b" (or "x in J^-(b)"); "reality grows along a worldline" becomes "b <= b' implies J^-(b) is contained in J^-(b')"; "fragment of the totality is M" becomes "the union of all causal pasts is M"; "absolute facts" (P5) become "facts true at every index".
3. Every theorem R1-R9 is a theorem about locally covariant relations on the tenseless structure (M, g, time-orientation). Stein-GR's own Limits paragraph says it "concerns relations of becoming, not ontology; it does not decide between presentism, the growing block and eternalism."
4. So the theorem is neutral and the whole metaphysical weight is carried by one premise: that the relation B *is* "x is real from b" in a sense stronger than "x lies in the causal past of b". SV §6 labels this the bridge premise. The B-theorist's claim is that the premise has no content that survives translation, and that the "stronger sense" is either (a) a restricted quantifier, which every eternalist accepts, or (b) an unexplained primitive.

Dilemma for O2's reply (SV O2, "both kinds of standpoint exist"):
- *Horn A (unrestricted reality exists).* If "real from M" (P7) is just "real", then from the whole everything is real simpliciter, and the event-standpoints give restricted domains of that. This is eternalism plus event-indexed domain restriction, exactly what Sider and Mellor already allow ("from b, the real = what is not later than or spacelike to b").
- *Horn B (only standpoint-relative reality).* Then "real from b" is an index-relative predicate. Index-relative truth over events is the standard semantics of tense for the token-reflexive B-theorist (Mellor) and the centred-worlds theorist (Lewis). What is left is the fragmentalist's claim that the relativity is *constitutive* of reality, not a semantic feature. That claim is untested by the theorem and has no empirical, modal or explanatory signature (SV O2 itself concedes empirical equivalence).

SV O2's present reply ("the presentist says the future exists from none of them; the eternalist says some standpoint sees everything; the view says both") works only if "exists from b" is not itself reducible to "is in J^-(b)". Nothing in SV or Stein-GR blocks the reduction; P2 *defines* becoming by a relation on events that is characterised entirely in the theorem by order-theoretic conditions (CB, T, LC).

## 1. Classical B-theory (Smart; Mellor, *Real Time II*)

**Steelman.** There are no irreducible tensed facts. Tensed sentences have tenseless truth conditions (Smart 1949/1963, "river of time" regress objection; Mellor 1981/1998: a tensed token is true iff the event it concerns bears the right earlier/later relation to the token itself, "token-reflexive" truth conditions). The "now" is an indexical, no more metaphysically special than "here". Temporal passage is either a confused metaphor (the rate-of-change regress) or the B-series succession itself. Tenseless causal and temporal order explains everything the A-theorist wants explained, including tensed belief and action (Mellor's account of why tensed beliefs are needed for action).

**Strongest attacks.**
- *Translation argument above (against O2, P1, P2).* "x has become for b" translates as "x <= b" with a token-reflexive reading with b as the token. Local fragmentalism is the observation that, in relativity, token-reflexive "earlier than" relative to token b has to be taken as the causal order, which Mellor's causal theory of time order already supplies (Mellor, *Real Time*, causal theory of temporal order; from memory) and Mellor already accepts that relativity makes "present" a property of the token. The new content is the label "reality".
- *The Smart regress (R3, O12).* "Fragments grow along worldlines" is passage-talk. Ask: at what rate does F(b) grow? Either the answer is "one fragment-inclusion per unit proper time", which is B-series succession, or it uses a second time, which is the regress. SV O12 retreats to "passage is a standpoint-internal fact", which is the B-theorist's "passage is the fact that later events have more in their causal past."
- *P5 is the B-theory.* "Absolute = holds from every standpoint" with standpoints being events is precisely "tenseless = index-invariant" (Mellor's tenseless facts). SV Table (§4) assigns "no standpoint privileged" to eternalism and "present special from each standpoint" to presentism; the second is, for the B-theorist, the trivial truth that "now" refers to its own location.

**Already answered?** SV O2 (statable thesis about which standpoints exist) and O12 (passage is not a fact from the whole). O2's answer does not defeat this version. It restates the disagreement as "which standpoints exist", but with Horn A/B above, the B-theorist grants every standpoint of the form "event b with domain J^-(b)" exists as an index; what he denies is that the index confers reality. SV does not give a test that distinguishes "confers reality" from "indexes a domain". O12 and the R3 reply concede the regress point partly ("the block is indeed static: that is the eternalist's truth, and the view grants it").

**Severity.** **Serious, close to fatal for the framing "resolves the divide"** (not for the theorem). If the translation holds, the view's claim to be a third option collapses into a presentation of eternalism plus a rediscovery of causal-past domain restriction. It remains a manageable result for the claim "this is what any local becoming relation must be".

**Best reply.** Adopt a *truthmaker/grounding test* explicitly: fragmentalism holds that tense facts about b are *grounded in* b's standpoint (nothing else), whereas B-theory grounds tense facts in the global tenseless order. This is a hyperintensional difference: different grounds, same truth conditions. Then argue (a) that the locality principle P3 is itself a grounding principle (the verdict on (x,b) is grounded in D(Sigma), not in the whole manifold), giving a *non-translation-invariant* commitment, and (b) say so openly. Cost: the view becomes a thesis about grounding/fundamentality, not about what exists, and must concede that it is empirically and truth-conditionally equivalent to eternalism.

**Prior anticipation.** Mellor's token-reflexive truth conditions and causal theory of time order: tensed truth relative to a token event, and "earlier" analysed causally. Also Grunbaum's "mind-dependent becoming" (Grunbaum, essays 1960s, e.g. "The status of temporal becoming" in Gale ed. *The Philosophy of Time*, 1967; from memory, locator unchecked): an event "becomes" relative to the awareness of a percipient. A web search found it described as mind-dependence, not tied to causal pasts in what I could read, so *do not claim* Grunbaum anticipated J^-; flag as a lead to check. Also Reichenbach/Mehlberg-style causal theories of "has happened". No checked source gives "the world of an occasion is its causal past" with locality derivation; this is a lead only.

**Sources.** Smart, "The River of Time", *Mind* 58 (1949); Smart, *Philosophy and Scientific Realism* (1963); Mellor, *Real Time* (CUP 1981) and *Real Time II* (Routledge 1998). All from memory.

## 2. Four-dimensionalism: perdurance and stage theory (Sider; Hawley)

**Steelman.** Ordinary objects are spacetime-worms (perdurantism, Sider 2001; Hawley 2001) or instantaneous stages with temporal counterpart relations (stage theory, Sider 1996/2001). Eternalism follows from the best account of persistence and from the argument that the A-theorist's non-eternalist ontology is incompatible with relativity (Sider's relativity argument: "present" must be relativized to frames, so the A-theory is committed to a privileged frame; Sider 2001, ch. 2). Temporal location is just like spatial location; the "problem of change" is solved by parthood.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *Persistence (new, specific).* Local fragmentalism says what is real from b is J^-(b). But persisting objects are not instantaneous: a perduring worm extends into b's future. What is the standpoint-relative reality of an *object* that is partly in F(b) and partly not? The view has no account of persistence: either the object exists from b only as its initial segment (then "the same object exists from b and b'" is a counterpart or segment relation, i.e. stage-theory machinery) or the object is a whole worm and is thus real from b only if wholly in J^-(b), meaning ordinary people exist from b only when they are dead. Sider's "temporal parts" argument shows that A-theories without temporal parts of objects face a mismatch, and fragmentalism is a partial-block view, so it needs a theory of what the "object" is. SV does not address persistence anywhere. **This is a real gap.**
- *Sider's vagueness argument against restricted composition/existence.* Sider (2001, ch. 4, from memory) argues unrestricted composition from vagueness: any restriction of the quantifier must be non-vague. The domain F(b) = J^-(b) is sharp at the light cone only if P6/P6' is imposed (R4 admits the Weyl-gated rules). Without P6' the "boundary" of reality could be vague or curvature-sensitive. SV R4 closes this by P6, which the B-theorist reads as an ad hoc closure stipulation.
- *Relativity argument for eternalism.* Sider's argument that A-theories need absolute simultaneity applies to presentism, but local fragmentalism is an A-theory that survives it; so the 4D-ist concedes that point. Remaining attack: the conclusion "the future is not real from b" is a restricted quantifier over a perdurantist's worms, which is trivial.

**Already answered?** Not answered at all. SV is silent on persistence; O2 addresses the generic translation. The vagueness point is half-answered by R4/P6.

**Severity.** **Serious** (persistence) and **manageable** (vagueness). Persistence is a genuine theoretical lacuna but fixable.

**Best reply.** State an explicit account of material objects: objects are worms; "o is real from b" iff some part of o is in J^-(b); "o exists *wholly* from b" iff all of o is. Persisting objects are then partially real from most standpoints. Alternatively adopt stage theory: only stages (events) are fundamental. Cost: the view becomes a four-dimensionalist view with a family of domain restrictions, which strengthens the O2 translation (the metaphysics of objects is fully eternalist).

**Prior anticipation.** Hawley and Sider do not hold fragmentalism. However, Sider's own "relativized quantification" move (a restricted domain index by a spacetime point) is the semantic core of the O2 translation. From memory.

**Sources.** Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (OUP 2001); Sider, "All the world's a stage", *Australasian J. Phil.* 74 (1996); Hawley, *How Things Persist* (OUP 2001). From memory.

## 3. Putnam-Rietdijk-Maxwell block-universe argument

**Steelman.** Putnam (1967), Rietdijk (1966), and Maxwell (1985): if "real for x" is (i) reflexive, (ii) transitive, (iii) not privileging any observer (Lorentz-invariant), and (iv) includes whatever is simultaneous with x in x's rest frame, then relativity of simultaneity yields that all events, including those in the future cone, are real for x. Hence the future is already real and the world is a block; or, Maxwell's version, probabilistic laws are incompatible with special relativity because the outcome of a future event would already be fixed from some observer's present. Stein 1968 resisted by rejecting (iv); his own result is that the only invariant, transitive alternatives are the causal past or the trivial relation. Stein-GR extends Stein.

**Strongest attacks.** This position is the *argument local fragmentalism is built to escape*, so the attacks come from the argument's descendants:
- *Rejecting (iv) is the whole game, and it is a refusal, not a refutation.* Putnam's proponent says: "co-presence" in (iv) is the sole link between a standpoint and anything non-causally related. The view's R2 sets co-presence to the identity. That is Stein's "peculiarly extreme (but pluralistic!) form of solipsism", which SV accepts. Putnam's descendants say the cost is that "x is real for b" cannot even relate to the lightcone-external structure that physics treats as real (e.g. correlated outcomes of entangled measurements, spacelike-separated degrees of freedom entering the initial data on a Cauchy slice). SV P3w itself says the physics of D(Sigma) fixes b and x. For a Cauchy slice Sigma containing x, spacelike to b, physics treats x and b as co-determined parts of one state. The Putnam-minded reader says: "the Cauchy-data picture already treats all of Sigma as one state, so the sensible reading of 'real at the time of b' includes x." The view's reply must deny that spacelike-separated data on a slice is co-real.
- *Maxwell/open-future.* The view says F(b) excludes the future, but an event e in b's future that is spacelike to a third event c has c-real-fragment containing e's cause but not e. Facts "indefinite from b" are definite from c' in b's future. So *indefiniteness is standpoint-relative and later fragments fix it.* The Putnam descendant says: this makes the open future a statement about b's access (an epistemic or causal-access domain), not about the world. Distinguish: determinism/open future (a claim about dynamics) versus ontology. For an indeterministic dynamics, the B-theorist already gets "the future is open from b's data" as underdetermination by data on J^-(b). The view's reality-language adds nothing. Point (c) of the Putnam descendant: R6/P7 admits the union is the block, so Putnam's conclusion (all events are real) is returned at the level of F(M). So the argument is *accepted at the totality level and refused at event level*, which is a retreat to O2.
- *Symmetry of (iv).* Time-reversed Stein: the same locality theorem with the opposite orientation yields F(b) = J^+(b). The "reality from b" being the past rather than the future is therefore an input, not a result. (See Section 5, Price: R3's direction is orientation, not a theorem.)

**Already answered?** Partly. SV §4 diagnosis (trilemma) and R8; Stein-GR phil. section states the trilemma. The answer is that (iv) is what is given up. It does not address the Cauchy-slice point or the "retreat to O2" point.

**Severity.** **Manageable** as an attack on the theorem's use (it is exactly the Stein/Clifton-Hogarth dialectic); **serious** as a challenge that the view's escape is a verbal one.

**Best reply.** Say plainly that the view does not refute Putnam; it shows that the conclusion "the future is real" is not forced *at the event level* without (iv), and that Putnam's own premises (reflexive, transitive, invariant) have a locally covariant solution besides the trivial one. That is Stein's point, now general. Cost: concede that the block remains the totality's view and that nothing about physics favours "the event standpoint" over "the whole" as the one bearing ontological weight.

**Prior anticipation.** Stein 1968 and 1991 (the causal-past relation as the nontrivial option); Clifton and Hogarth 1995; Dieks 2006 "Becoming, relativity and locality" (local, point-like becoming; from memory); Arthur 2006 and Savitt 2000 (local "present" regions). All are cited or close to SV's sources; the new element is locality-covariance in GR.

**Sources.** Putnam, "Time and Physical Geometry", *J. Phil.* 64 (1967); Rietdijk, *Phil. Sci.* 33 (1966); Maxwell, "Are probabilism and special relativity incompatible?", *Phil. Sci.* 52 (1985); Stein, "On Einstein-Minkowski space-time", *J. Phil.* 65 (1968) (p. 18 as cited in SV). From memory except as cited by SV.

## 4. Callender, *What Makes Time Special?*

**Steelman.** Callender (OUP 2017) asks what, if anything, distinguishes time from space, and argues that the features that seem to make time special (passage, the present, direction, the A-series, our attitudes to past and future) come from the human temporal perspective together with the asymmetries of thermodynamics and agency, not from fundamental physics. Physics is tenseless. Nothing in physics requires a metaphysically privileged present; the proper explanatory task is to explain *why we represent the world in tensed terms* (an error-theory-flavoured account of the specialness). Callender also (with Wuthrich, BJPS 2017) showed that the Stein-style argument fails for invariance within a single causal set, which Stein-GR cites.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *The specialness is ours (P2, R3, SV §4 table).* Every row of SV's "where vindicated" table is explainable by the agent's temporal perspective: the "present as apex of reality" is the observer's causal access; "reality accrues" is memory/records accrual. A view that *identifies* the explanans (causal past accrual) with the explanandum (becoming) has no explanatory advantage and runs against Callender's methodological rule that one should not reify features that are projections of the human perspective.
- *Orientation is imported.* Fundamental GR is time-reversal invariant. The theorem needs a time orientation (what is "the past" lobe), and "directed" in R3 comes from that input, not from locality. Callender (and Price) would say the labelling of one lobe as "past" is made by the thermodynamic arrow or by us. Hence "becoming is real and directed" (R3) is not proved; only "F(b) is one of the two lobes" is proved. (Distinguish: causal order, thermodynamic arrow, objective passage.)
- *The locality premise is a physicist's methodological preference.* P3 is motivated by locally covariant QFT. Callender would say that is evidence about a *representation convention in a particular theoretical framework*, not about metaphysics: LCQFT's functoriality is a constraint on algebra assignments to regions, and transferring it to "what has become" is the bridge premise at work with no independent warrant. This overlaps the question 3 in SV §7.
- *Causal-set caveat.* Callender-Wuthrich's own result (cited in Stein-GR) says invariance within a single causal set fails to give Stein; the view needs covariance across *all* causal sets (R5). Callender would press that quantifying over all possible causal sets / all spacetimes is a modal premise (the off-shell premise, O4), not physics.

**Already answered?** O7 (specious present as worldline duration) deals only with experience. The orientation point is not answered anywhere in SV. O3/O4 partly answer locality and off-shell, but not the "convention" version.

**Severity.** Orientation: **serious** (R3 is overclaimed as "proved"; at minimum a wording defect). Projection point: **serious**, same class as O2. Locality as convention: **manageable**.

**Best reply.** Reword R3: "given a time orientation, reality accrues along the future-pointing direction"; state that the orientation is an input and that the thermodynamic/causal-order questions are left open. For projection, concede that the explanation of why we experience passage is B-theoretic (O7 already uses B-theoretic machinery) and claim only that the *ontology of becoming* is what the theorem constrains. Cost: R3's claim "becoming is real and directed" is weakened to "if there is becoming, it is local, causal-past-shaped and aligned with the given orientation."

**Prior anticipation.** Callender holds no fragmentalism. But Callender's treatment of "the present as perspectival" is close to the view's P5 distinction. From memory.

**Sources.** Callender, *What Makes Time Special?* (OUP 2017); Wuthrich and Callender, "What becomes of a causal set?", *BJPS* 68 (2017). The latter is cited in Stein-GR. From memory.

## 5. Price (perspectival arrow; *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point*)

**Steelman.** Price (OUP 1996) argues that the asymmetry of time is a feature of our perspective as temporally asymmetric agents and observers, not of the world; the correct stance is the "view from nowhen" (an Archimedean standpoint outside time) from which the block is symmetric. The "flow" of time is to be explained as a projection of perspectival asymmetries (agency, causation as seen from the deliberative standpoint, entropy gradient). We should avoid "double standards": a temporal asymmetry in explanation (e.g. treating initial conditions as needing no explanation but final conditions as needing one) is a bias arising from the temporal viewpoint.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *The standpoint-relative facts are Price's exactly, and Price denies them ontological weight.* Price accepts that tensed facts hold relative to a standpoint. The view says: tense facts are standpoint-relative *and real*. Price: "real" adds nothing; and P5 + R6 (the view from the whole is the block) concedes Price's Archimedean point *is* the view from the whole. So the view *contains* the view from nowhen as the totality's standpoint (R6') and adds a family of local standpoints that Price already explains as perspectives. O2, restated.
- *"Now is like here" (SV O11a).* SV concedes the fragment is centred on b so "here" is as objective as "now". That is Price's, Smart's and Mellor's central analogy (temporal indexicality parallels spatial indexicality). Fine's complaint (O11) is that this destroys the A-theorist's space/time distinction; the view's concession therefore *accepts the B-theorist's analogy*. The only residual asymmetry is the inclusion F(b) in F(b'), which is the causal order again, which Price says is grounded in the entropic arrow or the agent's perspective.
- *Double standard.* The theorem treats the past lobe asymmetrically by input (CB: chronological *past* included). Price would call this the standard time-asymmetry bias: assuming the past as "settled" and the future as "open" is an input from the temporal viewpoint. Applying the same theorem with reversed orientation yields J^+(b) as the "fragment": fully symmetric. So R3's directed becoming is projected into the premises (cf. Callender).
- *Explanatory idleness.* The explanations of why events seem to "become" (memory, records, causal access, deliberation) are all available given the tenseless J^-(b). F(b) does no explanatory work beyond them.

**Already answered?** O11a concedes the analogy without recognising it as Price's; O12 accepts that nothing moves from the whole. Neither answers the idleness or double-standard point. P2's chronological-past axiom is not defended as non-conventional.

**Severity.** **Serious**, because the view's rhetorical gain over eternalism depends on the claim that the asymmetry is more than perspectival, and the concession in O11a gives that away. Not fatal as an account of what a local becoming relation must be.

**Best reply.** Embrace it: say that the view *is* the claim that the right metaphysics of becoming is perspectival in Price's sense, with the theorem showing the perspectives are rigid (causal pasts) and the totality is the block. Then reframe the novelty claim: not "a third option between presentism and eternalism" but "a precise form of perspectival B-theory, together with a proof that perspective-relative becoming has only one local form". Cost: abandon the claim to vindicate the presentist, and the O2 slogan "each side right about the standpoints it recognises".

**Prior anticipation.** Price's view from nowhen plus agent-perspectival time is close to the combined P5/R6' structure. Rovelli (2019/20, cited in PRIOR_WORK) is the nearest in physics. No causal-past derivation in Price (from memory).

**Sources.** Price, *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point: New Directions for the Physics of Time* (OUP 1996); Price, "The flow of time", in Callender ed., *Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time* (2011). From memory.

## 6. Ismael (perspectival / agent-centred time)

**Steelman.** Ismael argues that the temporal perspective, including the present, flow and openness of the future, is the standpoint of an information-gathering-and-utilising system embedded in the block (*The Situated Self*, OUP 2007; "Temporal experience" in the Oxford Handbook, 2011; later essays on passage as a feature of the logic of temporal perspectives). Such a system has records of its past but not its future and it acts on its environment; its self-model has a "now" that updates. Tensed claims are true relative to perspectives; the perspectives are objective features of the block, namely local regions with an information-flow asymmetry. There is no further question about "the real" perspective.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *Exactly the view, minus the word "reality".* An IGUS's accessible domain at b is, up to thermodynamic details, J^-(b). Ismael can adopt R1-R3 as a theorem about the structure of any physically possible perspective, and decline the label "reality". She is therefore the clearest case of "same structure, no bridge premise": evidence that P1-P2's identification of perspective-relative accessibility with *becoming* is optional.
- *Perspectives are physical, so their number is fixed by the physics of agents, not by events.* If perspectives belong to systems, then not every event is a standpoint (P1 says "for each event b"). Most events have no IGUS. SV treats every event as a standpoint, which is a metaphysical generalisation with no physical motivation in her picture: event-standpoints are not agents' standpoints. If standpoints are agent-based, the theorem's classification (events as the domain) is the wrong index set, and P7's regions (agents' extended worldlines) might fit better.
- *The Archimedean point is available.* Ismael's block has an external description; P5 and R6 grant it. So the view for her is not a rival.

**Already answered?** Not directly. O7 and O8 deal with experience and the totality. The "every event is a standpoint" point is not discussed in SV; the closest is P7 (regions), which is a different issue.

**Severity.** Event-versus-agent index set: **manageable** (add a premise that standpoint-hood is independent of agency, or note the theorem needs only the index set of events as bare positions). Collapsing into perspectival B-theory: **serious**, same as Price.

**Best reply.** Distinguish *positions* (every event is a standpoint, a thin notion of "location from which facts are indexed") from *perspectives* (agents' models). Fragmentalism concerns the former; Ismael's concerns the latter. Cost: the thin notion makes the metaphysical claim bear even more on the unargued bridge premise, because there is no agent-based motivation for an inanimate event having a "view".

**Prior anticipation.** Ismael's account of perspective as a region of the block with information flow is the closest B-theoretic structure to "world of an observer is its causal past" (from memory; the exact statement is unchecked, so treat as lead). Do not cite as a prior derivation.

**Sources.** Ismael, *The Situated Self* (OUP 2007); Ismael, "Temporal experience", in Callender ed., *Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time* (2011). From memory.

## 7. L. A. Paul (temporal experience without passage)

**Steelman.** Paul, "Temporal experience", *J. Phil.* 107 (2010), argues that an experience as of passage need not be an experience *of* passage, and that the phenomenology of temporal flow can be explained by the B-theorist: the content of experience includes representation of change and succession (via the "Tensed Content" / "Prediction" mechanisms), and the experience of dynamism is a feature of how a brain models temporal order rather than a perception of the world's dynamic character. (Her stance is that the A-theorist's appeal to phenomenology does not favour A over B; whether she holds a full "illusion" thesis is not something I can verify from memory.)

**Strongest attacks.**
- *O7 concedes the explanatory ground.* SV O7 handles the specious present using a B-theoretic diamond-along-worldline account. If experience is explained without passage, then phenomenology offers no support for F(b) or R3, and the only reason left to adopt local fragmentalism is the metaphysical aim of securing a becoming relation. That aim is not needed by Paul.
- *Phenomenology is not a standpoint index.* There are experiences with a single temporal extent in many incompatible spacetime locations (extended conscious states). Paul's account makes experience of "now" a feature of an extended representation, which does not require its content to be a fragment. SV's claim that a "diamond is not a fragment" (O7) is correct but then everything about experience is done by something else.
- *The illusion alternative.* Even if fragmentalism is true, why believe we have *access* to our own fragment rather than just a representation of it? Time-consciousness is a distinct topic from ontology and the view does not use it. Fine.

**Already answered?** O7 shows awareness of the issue; it is the right move. It does not address the point that O7 deletes any evidential role for experience.

**Severity.** **Manageable.** SV does not rest on experience. The only cost is that one motivation (SV §4 table, "becoming is real") loses its support from phenomenology.

**Best reply.** State clearly that the view's support is not phenomenological: it rests on the physics-plus-bridge structure, and phenomenology is explained B-theoretically. Cost: lose "the present is metaphysically special" as a datum.

**Prior anticipation.** None (about time-consciousness, not ontology).

**Sources.** Paul, "Temporal experience", *J. Phil.* 107(7) (2010). From memory, locator unchecked.

## 8. Natalja Deng (minimal A-theory; deflation of the A/B debate)

**Steelman.** Deng argues that the A-versus-B debate is subtler than usually presented: she defends Fine's "argument from passage" in part, agrees that existing A-theoretic accounts of passage do no better than the B-theory at capturing the intuitive dynamism, and concludes that a B-theoretic account identifying passage with the succession of times remains a serious option ("Fine's McTaggart, temporal passage, and the A versus B debate", *Ratio* 26(1), 19-34, published 2013 per most bibliographies, PhilArchive dates it 2012; abstract confirmed by web search). She explicitly dismisses Fine's non-standard realism as more of a conceptual gesture than a workable theory (same abstract). Elsewhere (from memory, partly unchecked) she argues that explanations of why time seems to pass are available to both sides, and that a minimal A-theory with tensed truths but no robust passage is a stable position.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *Aimed directly at P1.* If non-standard realism (fragmentalism proper) is "a conceptual gesture", then the relativist reading of P1 is the only coherent one, and then SV O1 concedes it: "whether the relativist reading still deserves the name resolution". On the relativist reading, "all standpoints are on a par", and the view is Horn B of the translation argument. Deng's own conclusion (passage = succession of times) is the B-theory.
- *Passage is not delivered (R3, O12).* Deng's argument shows that no account of passage can match the intuitive dynamic picture. R3's "growth of fragments" is succession of nested sets, which is the B-theoretic "succession" she says is what passage can be. The view therefore does not provide the dynamism that would favour it over the B-theory.
- *Minimal A-theory is a cheaper rival.* A minimal A-theorist accepts tensed truths and a notion of "present" as a primitive with no robust passage, which captures every row of the SV table without fragmentation. The view has to show that the theory's extra machinery (non-standard realism, standpoint pluralism) is justified over this.

**Already answered?** O1 and O12. Neither answers whether a relativist P1 *deserves* vindication language. O12's honesty (passage not visible from the whole) is the right concession, but it is then equivalent to Deng's succession reading.

**Severity.** **Serious** (relativist reading collapse), and especially for the stronger P1 if Deng is right about non-standard realism. For the fragmentalist reading, the question is whether it is workable at all; the incoherence charges in the literature (Hofweber and Lange; Lipman's defence) are separate.

**Best reply.** Make the choice explicit: either (i) fragmentalism proper, with an account of coherence relations across fragments (O10 supplies inclusion and shared causal geometry), or (ii) relativism, in which case withdraw the claim that the view *resolves* the presentist-eternalist divide and call it a classification of local becoming relations. Cost: if (ii), the O2 charge succeeds in substance.

**Prior anticipation.** None for causal-past content. Deng's treatment of Fine is the nearest critical reception of the non-standard realism the view depends on.

**Sources.** Deng, "Fine's McTaggart, temporal passage, and the A versus B debate", *Ratio* 26(1) (2013), 19-34 (abstract read via web search: https://philarchive.org/rec/DENFMT-3); Deng, "Our experience of passage", *Phil. Studies* (2013) and "On explaining why time seems to pass", *Southern J. Phil.* (2013), from memory.

## 9. Skow-style and Dainton-style "passage is nothing over and above"

**Steelman.** Two relevant lines. (a) Skow: in *Objective Becoming* (OUP 2015) he develops an account on which objective becoming is a matter of what is true at times in a tensed or "time-indexed" sense; his earlier paper "Relativity and the Moving Spotlight", *J. Phil.* 106 (Dec. 2009) 666-678 (bibliographic details confirmed by web search; content only from memory) argues that the moving-spotlight theory is compatible with special relativity, by letting the spotlight illuminate spacetime points or regions rather than a frame-relative hyperplane. (b) Dainton (*Time and Space*, 2001/2010; *Stream of Consciousness*, 2000) allows that phenomenal passage is real and compatible with the B-theory: passage is the structure of experience itself, an "experiential" passage ("nothing over and above" the sequence of experiences, or a feature of consciousness not of the physical world). Deflationists of this stripe say that once the succession of events and the right relations among them are given, no further fact of passage is needed.

**Strongest attacks.**
- *"Nothing over and above".* Direct form of O2: R3's growth is nothing over and above the nested inclusion F(b) in F(b'), i.e. the B-relations. The view's own reply to O12 ("passage is a standpoint-internal fact") already says nothing is added at the level of the whole.
- *Skow's spacetime-point spotlight anticipates the structure.* A spotlight on a single event with a relation to what is "behind" it is event-indexed becoming within an eternalist block. If Skow (2009) places the spotlight on a point and takes the "past" to be what is causally behind it (I do not remember this; unverified), then the moving spotlight in relativity is an eternalist rival with the same event-indexed structure. The view must say what fragmentalism adds beyond a spotlight. Even if the details differ, the attack stands in schematic form: any eternalist view with a distinguished event-index plus a causal-past domain already has the content R1-R3; the fragmentalist's distinct claim is that there is no single distinguished index. That is a version of the (non-)privilege thesis; it also shows the view is the "democratised spotlight".
- *Dainton's experiential passage.* If passage can be located in the structure of experience (overlapping specious presents), the demand that physics supply passage is unnecessary, and the view's second motivation (becoming is real) is met on the experience side while ontology stays B-theoretic.

**Already answered?** O12 and O2, partly. The specific comparison with Skow's spotlight-on-points is not addressed anywhere in SV or PRIOR_WORK.

**Severity.** **Serious**, as a priority/novelty and framing risk, **manageable** for substantive philosophy. A spotlight view with event-indexed spotlights (Skow-type) plus the Stein-GR theorem would be the same mathematics under an eternalist heading, which sharpens O2.

**Best reply.** Add Skow 2009 and Skow 2015 to the reading list, and distinguish: spotlight views privilege one index at a time (monadic absolute present), fragmentalism does not; fragmentalism's distinct commitment is non-privilege plus constitutive standpoint-relative reality. Cost: it then sits between a "democratic" spotlight and perspectival B-theory, and the vindication-of-presentism rhetoric weakens.

**Prior anticipation.** Skow 2009 on moving spotlight and relativity (verify content). Dainton's compatibilism: no overlap with causal pasts.

**Sources.** Skow, "Relativity and the Moving Spotlight", *J. Phil.* 106(12) (2009), 666-678 (https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/53743, abstract level via search results); Skow, *Objective Becoming* (OUP 2015); Dainton, *Stream of Consciousness* (Routledge 2000), *Time and Space* (Acumen 2001; 2nd ed. 2010). Content from memory.

## Top threats from this group

1. **The translation argument (O2 in full).** Every R-result and P-principle translates into tenseless statements about the causal order and index-relative domain restriction, the theorem is explicitly ontology-neutral, and the bridge premise (becoming = "real from b" in a stronger-than-domain sense) has no test. SV O2's "both standpoints exist" reply does not block the dilemma (Horn A: eternalism plus restricted quantifiers; Horn B: semantic indexing with a primitive "constitutive" label). Reinforced by Deng's rejection of non-standard realism, Price's perspectival B-theory and Ismael's physical-perspective account, all of which can accept R1-R3 as a theorem while denying the metaphysical gloss. Severity: serious to fatal for the claim "resolves the divide"; manageable for "classifies local becoming relations".
2. **Orientation and direction are imported (Callender, Price).** The theorem is time-reversal symmetric; the past lobe enters as input (chronological past in CB/P2). So "becoming is real and directed" (R3) and the asymmetry between time and space (O11a) rest on the choice of orientation, which the deflationists ground in thermodynamics/agency. Severity: serious; mostly a wording and honesty problem, but it removes the one structural asymmetry offered in reply to Fine.
3. **No account of persisting objects, and every event as a standpoint (Sider, Hawley, Ismael).** SV has no theory of what is real-from-b when an object extends beyond J^-(b), and gives no non-agent-based motivation for all events being standpoints. Severity: serious but repairable.

**Single repair that would do the most good.** State an explicit *grounding/fundamentality* thesis as the content of the bridge premise: tense facts about b are grounded in b's standpoint and its causal past alone, not in the global tenseless order; P3 is the corresponding principle of locality of ground. Then concede openly that the view is truth-conditionally and empirically equivalent to eternalism, and that it differs on what is fundamental. This answers the translation argument by changing the claim from "a third option about what exists" to "a thesis about fundamentality", at the cost of the rhetoric of resolving the presentist-eternalist divide. Pair it with rewording R3 to make the orientation an explicit input.

## Prior anticipations found

- **Mellor** (*Real Time*, 1981; *Real Time II*, 1998): token-reflexive truth conditions for tense (tensed truth relative to a token event) and a causal theory of time order. Close to the translation T; not a causal-past-as-reality thesis. From memory.
- **Skow 2009** (*J. Phil.* 106): moving spotlight compatible with relativity; possible event-indexed structure. Content unverified; must be read before claiming novelty of event-indexed becoming within an eternalist frame.
- **Grunbaum** (1960s): mind-dependent becoming, an event "becomes" relative to awareness. Possibly relevant to causal-past formulations; no confirmed link. Lead only.
- **Dieks 2006** ("Becoming, relativity and locality"), **Arthur 2006**, **Savitt 2000**: local or point-like present/becoming in Minkowski spacetime. From memory; already in the Stein/Clifton-Hogarth neighbourhood.
- **Ismael** (2007, 2011): agent-centred perspectives as regions with an information-flow asymmetry; structurally close to "world of an observer = what it has access to". Lead only, not a derivation.
- **Deng 2013** (*Ratio* 26): dismisses Fine's non-standard realism as a conceptual gesture. Not an anticipation, but a direct hostile reception of the view's metaphysical frame.
- No work in this group, as far as recalled or searched, derives "an event's reality is its causal past" from a locality theorem. That part of the novelty claim is not threatened by this group.
