A point that recurs across the whole group. The A-theories are not a single target. Their common ground is that a *global* tensed fact (what is present, what exists) is objective and not perspectival. Local fragmentalism denies exactly that (P1, R2, R6). So against this group the key question is not whether the theorem is true, but whether a dynamic ontologist must accept the bridge from "B_M(x,b)" to "x is real from b" (the P2 identification). The earlier RED_TEAM.md (A, B) already notes that F(b) ⊇ I⁻(b) makes the fragment a growing block, not presentism; I do not repeat that except where this group sharpens it.

## 1. Standard presentism (Prior, Crisp, Markosian, Zimmerman)

**Steelman.** Only present objects exist, and "exists" is the unrestricted quantifier. The past and future were/will be, but are not. Tensed truths are fundamental, so there is a fact of the matter about what is the case now simpliciter. This is not because presentists love a metaphysical flourish: the view takes ordinary experience of passage at face value, avoids a block that must be supplemented by a primitive flow, and takes seriously that the past (e.g. Caesar's crossing) is not among the things there are. Presentists also generally accept that special relativity is a challenge but hold that the challenge is to *physics' representation* and not to the ontology (Craig, Zimmerman, Monton, Markosian each say something like this, from memory). A typical presentist accepts that a causal-past or light-cone view of the present is available (Hinchliff's "cone" and "point" presentism, see below) but regards it as a retreat.

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *Misdescription of the view as a vindication of presentism* [ontology]. F(b) contains I⁻(b), i.e. the whole causal past of b. A presentist denies that the past exists at all. So "presentists are right about how reality is from every standpoint" (STRONGEST_VIEW §1) and table row 1 are false by the view's own R1. What holds from every standpoint is a growing block with an apex. This is also the content of RED_TEAM.md section A. From a standard presentist's viewpoint this is the worst rhetorical error: the view has appropriated the name of the opposing party while denying its central thesis. Prior's first requirement is that "it was the case that p" does not involve a present entity corresponding to a past event.

(b) *Unrestricted quantification is the whole dispute* [ontology]. The presentist's thesis is stated with the unrestricted existential quantifier. P1's relativisation (v(b) = F(b), "exists from the perspective of b") either (i) is a restriction of quantifiers, which presentists, eternalists and growing-blockers all grant is harmless and uninteresting (we can restrict to the causal past of b whenever we like), or (ii) denies that there is an unrestricted quantifier, which is Fine's fragmentalism. Reading (ii) is a bridging premise from philosophy, not something the theorem supplies (the paper's own "Limits" paragraph concedes it does not decide between presentism, the growing block and eternalism). The presentist will say: the theorem is about a binary relation B on a manifold; a manifold of events with all of its points *is* the eternalist's block. Quantifying over "events b" in "for each event b there is a fragment F(b)" is already to quantify over all the events, past and future of one another. That is the standard "relativity presupposes the manifold" charge against any presentist-friendly gloss (cf. Putnam 1967, Sider 2001 ch. 2, from memory). The view cannot state its thesis without a quantifier ranging over all events. If that quantifier is the fundamental one, this is eternalism with indexed truth; if it is not, the paper's main object (B_M ⊆ M × M) is not what is fundamental.

(c) *Locality (P3) and the off-shell premise beg the question* [ontology; clock time]. The presentist says the A-facts are about *this* world, with its actual physics and the actual present, not about a category GH that includes every Lorentzian metric. (LC) requires that the rule be the same on every globally hyperbolic metric, including ones that are not solutions of Einstein's equations and ones with energy-condition-violating matter (O4, paper Remark 2 and Section 7). Why should an ontology of *time* in the actual world be constrained by what it says about metrics that are physically impossible? O4 answers via Gödel's own modal premise, but Gödel's argument is one that presentists reject too (they typically also reject the move from "our world's matter distribution" to "time's nature"). The theorem's power comes entirely from the gluing argument, which requires auxiliary spacetimes that, according to Section 7, are not available under the dominant energy condition. This is the theorem's biggest bridge-gap for presentists.

(d) *The cone/point presentism alternative shows the "forced" fragments are a choice* [ontology; causal order]. Hinchliff's "cone presentism" and "point presentism" (from memory: Hinchliff 1996 "The puzzle of change", Phil. Perspectives 10; Hinchliff 2000 "A defense of presentism in a relativistic setting", Phil. Sci. 67, S575–S586; check both) take the present as the point or the past light cone. A presentist can say: if you grant locality, you get my options. The question is why the whole solid cone J⁻(b) is real from b, instead of the surface (cone presentism) or the apex (point presentism). The answer must be P2's chronological inclusion and transitivity (CB, T). But CB is derived by Clifton and Hogarth from "worldline becoming", which a presentist rejects: for the presentist, what is real for me is not the *whole* past of my worldline.

(e) *Experience of passage* [time-consciousness; passage]. Objective passage is global: it happens to the world, not to each event. Local fragmentalism offers "fragment growth along a worldline" (R3). The presentist answers that growth of a set of facts about causal pasts along a worldline is not passage; it is a monotone map from events to sets, a mathematical fact available to every eternalist (see 6 for the same attack in the moving-spotlight register).

**Already answered?** Partly. (a): not answered in STRONGEST_VIEW; RED_TEAM.md records it as a needed fix. §1 and table row 1 should say "presentism's negative claim: the future is not real from any standpoint". (b): O2 is the closest, and it concedes empirical equivalence; it states a "thesis about which standpoints exist". That reply holds against a presentist only if the presentist is willing to say that "exists from b" is the same as "exists" for the case of a single standpoint, which they will refuse. The reply to O2 treats the "whole" (P7) as another standpoint; to a presentist, a standpoint from which the future exists is just the eternalist's mistake, and the view's endorsement of it is a vice. (c): O3 and O4 address it, but O3's "independent motivation" (locally covariant QFT, Viglione) is motivation from physics practice, not from the philosophy of time; the presentist accepts QFT and rejects that its covariance is metaphysical (Craig, Zimmerman, from memory). O4 is rated OPEN by the view itself. (d): not addressed.

**Severity.** *Serious* (a is a framing fix; b-c together are serious because they target the bridge). Not fatal: the earlier concessions (O2) suffice to show the view is a distinct thesis, and the view never claims to refute presentism, only to show the dispute rests on a shared present.

**Best reply.** Drop the presentist label for the thesis: say the view is a *point-relative growing block* and that it keeps presentism's negative claim for each standpoint. For (b): argue that the presentist cannot state their own thesis relative to a *frame-invariant* quantifier in relativity, because the unrestricted quantifier has no invariant extension (that is just Stein's dilemma; the paper's trilemma). For (c): promote the Gödel-style answer, and present the view as conditional on off-shell locality, as the paper already does. Cost: conceding that the view is not a version of presentism and is incomplete as an answer to a presentist who rejects locality (R8 horn 1/2).

**Prior anticipation.** Hinchliff's cone presentism / point presentism (see above) and Stein 1968 / Putnam 1967 already hold that the present is point-like or cone-shaped. Godfrey-Smith 1979 ("Special relativity and the present", Phil. Studies 36) is, from memory, also a point-like-present proposal; verify. The solid-cone (rather than surface) and the locality theorem are distinct. Wüthrich (2010, in repo as WuthrichChristian2010PetkovColl_NoPresQG.pdf; not read by me) also discusses light-cone variants; PRIOR_WORK already notes it.

**Sources.** Prior, *Past, Present and Future* (OUP 1967) [from memory]; Prior, "Thank Goodness That's Over", *Philosophy* 34 (1959) [from memory]; Crisp, "Presentism", in Loux & Zimmerman eds., *Oxford Handbook of Metaphysics* (2003) [from memory]; Markosian, "A defense of presentism", *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 1 (2004) [from memory]; Zimmerman, "The privileged present: defending an 'A-theory' of time", in Sider, Hawthorne & Zimmerman eds., *Contemporary Debates in Metaphysics* (2008) [from memory]; Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (OUP 2001) [from memory]; Putnam, "Time and physical geometry", *J. Phil.* 64 (1967) [from memory]; Hinchliff as above [from memory]. All discovery/from-memory.

## 2. Ersatz presentism and Bourne's "A Future for Presentism"

**Steelman.** The ersatz presentist (Adams-style and Crisp-style; from memory) says only the present concrete world exists, but there are abstract entities (propositions, world-stories, "ersatz times", or maximal tensed properties) that exist now and represent how things were and will be. These provide the truthmakers (or grounds) for past- and future-tensed truths without concrete past objects. Bourne (2006, *A Future for Presentism*, OUP; from memory) defends an unusual presentism: the future is genuinely real in the sense that there are truths about the future (the "open future" is not required, and he argues against treating presentism as committed to an open future; check chapter locators). The upshot of these views for relativity is that *the metaphysical frame is not the spacetime manifold*: the manifold is an ersatz representation and the concrete reality is whatever is present. So the standard disputes about the status of the 4D manifold are a category error from the ersatzer's point of view.

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *The fragments are ersatz objects* [ontology]. The ersatzer can absorb the whole of local fragmentalism as a theory of the *representational structure* the abstract entities have. F(b) = J⁻(b) is then a structural feature of the ersatz world-story (the causal-order structure of the representing model), and the theorem is a constraint on the *model*, not on concrete reality. "Real from b" is a relation between a representation (the model of M) and a representing location b. The bridge from B_M to "x is real" is lost; the ersatzer's reality is a single concrete present. This makes the view a species of the "representation transformation, not ontology" worry from the AGENTS.md research standards.

(b) *What is the present on the ersatzer's picture, given no global time?* [ontology; clock time]. The ersatzer needs a concrete present, which is the very thing R2 says does not exist (co-presence is trivial). The ersatzer says: relativity shows the ersatz world-story contains a Minkowskian causal structure, but the concrete world could still have a foliation that the ersatz story is silent about (a hidden preferred frame, see also 8). The theorem says nothing against that because it concerns relations on the *category* GH. A foliation is extra structure, not within the category (paper "Limits" and Section 8 of the paper's philosophy part, R8).

(c) *Grounding objection reversed* [ontology]. The standard grounding objection targets presentism (what grounds the truth that Caesar crossed the Rubicon?). Ersatzers answer with abstracta. The view in question grounds past-tensed facts in concrete past events being real from b. The ersatzer will say this is not a rival *explanation*; it is a relabelling, because "real from b" is not metaphysically fundamental without a story about what real-from-b-ness is, and the view admits P1 may be only a relativist reading (so no further ontological thesis).

(d) *Bourne's own relativity move* [ontology]. I recall that Bourne offers a "presentist-friendly" treatment of relativity and defends the possibility of a single present with a preferred relation of simultaneity; I cannot give a locator (check). If so, the Bourne-style presentist accepts (8)-style structure and the "add structure" horn of R8, and rejects the claim that this "inherits Wüthrich's and Viglione's objections" as a refutation, since for the ersatzer the structure is not physics but metaphysics.

**Already answered?** Not directly. O2 (eternalism with tensed vocabulary) is the nearest and treats presentism as a thesis about which standpoints exist; the ersatzer's point is different: there are *two* levels (concrete and ersatz), and the theorem lives at the representation level. R8 mentions Monton's move but treats the "added structure" horn as THESIS, not PROVED.

**Severity.** *Manageable.* The attack takes away the metaphysical reading of the theorem, but it does so for any metaphysical use of it, not specially for local fragmentalism; and the view concedes that Stein-GR "concerns relations of becoming, not ontology".

**Best reply.** Say that the view's P1–P2 bridge is a *proposal for how ontology should answer to relativistic physics*, and that an ersatzer who accepts a model-independent concrete present owes an account of why that present never shows up in the physics of any region (P3w: supervenience on D(Σ)). The cost: concede that for an ersatzer the view yields only a conditional ("if locality governs becoming, then...").

**Prior anticipation.** None that I know of beyond the general point that abstract world-stories can encode causal-past structure. Check whether Bourne's treatment of relativity (2006, check chapter on relativity) endorses cone-like or foliated structure.

**Sources.** Bourne, *A Future for Presentism* (OUP 2006) [from memory, locators unchecked]; Adams, "Time and thisness", *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 11 (1986) [from memory]; Crisp, "Presentism and the grounding objection", *Noûs* 41 (2007) [from memory]. From-memory only.

## 3. Thisness presentism (Ingram)

**Steelman.** Ingram (*Thisness Presentism*, Routledge 2019; from memory) argues that presentism should be formulated with *thisnesses* (haecceities) of non-present individuals as presently existing abstract properties: Socrates's thisness exists now, uninstantiated, and can ground past-tensed singular truths about him without Socrates existing. The view aims to solve the grounding objection and the problem of singular propositions about non-existents (and, I recall, to say something about how presentism is consistent with relativity, but I cannot recall the details; check).

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *Same as ersatzer (a)–(b)*: the fragments are fragments of structure, and the real present is whatever concrete thing instantiates the present-tensed facts. Thisnesses of past events exist for the thisness presentist at the one present; fragments, being event-indexed, require thisnesses of events to be tied to the standpoint b, which means that distinct standpoints have non-overlapping groups of presently-exemplified thisnesses. Under local fragmentalism the thisness of an event x in J⁻(b) is a constituent of F(b), but also x's own fragment F(x) ⊆ F(b) (R3). There are two present events for the thisness presentist: x and b, each with a present set of facts, and the thisness presentist's *unique present* has no home. The attack is: individuation of standpoints by events is a thisness-style device, and the view needs Ingram's apparatus to say what makes b *this* standpoint while allowing no global haecceity of the present.

(b) *Individuation of the apex* [ontology]. The theorem proves J⁻(b) = J⁻(b') implies b = b' in GH spacetimes (the paper's identification in O11 note). This is the claim that fragments individuate events. The thisness presentist retorts that individuation by qualitative (causal-past) structure is ruled out by symmetric spacetimes: in a highly symmetric spacetime, a symmetry maps b to b' and J⁻(b) to J⁻(b') with all the qualitative structure preserved. The view's fragments are the *same up to isometry*; only the thisness distinguishes them. So the view needs primitive event identity, which is a thisness-type thesis anyway; this is a small point, not an attack on the theorem. It is more a point that P1's "for each event b" presupposes event identity independent of the fragment structure.

(c) *Cross-fragment reference* [ontology; time-consciousness]. A statement made from b of the form "x is in my past" refers to x by thisness or by description. If the view has facts from different fragments that "need not be coherent", then how can a speaker at b refer to x at all if x's own fragment says things inconsistent with F(b)? The thisness presentist grounds reference in the (present) thisness; the fragmentalist needs inclusion F(x) ⊆ F(b), which R3 supplies. So (c) is answered by R3 for the timelike/causal cases. For spacelike-separated pairs, there is no cross reference at all, and the view must hold that I cannot even refer to a spacelike-separated event as real. That is an *epistemic and semantic* cost that thisness presentists can exploit: we do refer to distant events (the Sun now).

**Already answered?** (c) partly: O10 gives coordination by inclusion, but this does not address reference to spacelike events. The "Sun now" worry is not addressed anywhere in STRONGEST_VIEW (O7 is about the specious present; O3 and R8 mention locality costs). It concerns the *tense semantics* of "now" in relativistic settings, which Fine and Lipman discuss under "chauvinism" (PRIOR_WORK). Lipman 2026 not read.

**Severity.** *Manageable* for (a) and (b); *serious* for the semantic cost in (c) if sharpened (see Top threats #3).

**Best reply.** Use Fine's own relativisation: "the Sun now" is a coordinate-dependent description that picks out a frame-relative event; from b, a thought about "the Sun now" is a thought about an event which is not real from b (only the retarded Sun is). The cost: accepting that ordinary reference to distant things "now" is either to events in J⁻(b) (retarded) or to events not in F(b) (and so is defective or a representational convenience). This is a significant, but arguably correct, relativistic revision.

**Prior anticipation.** None.

**Sources.** Ingram, *Thisness Presentism: An Essay on Time, Truth, and Ontology* (Routledge 2019) [from memory; publisher/date to be checked]. From-memory, discovery only.

## 4. Growing block (Broad, Tooley, Forrest; Correia & Rosenkranz)

**Steelman.** The past and present exist; the future does not, and reality grows by the addition of new present layers. Broad (*Scientific Thought*, 1923) introduces it; Tooley (*Time, Tense and Causation*, OUP 1997) gives it a causal and truthmaker defence; Forrest (e.g. 2004 Analysis, "The real but dead past", check) defends the view against the "how do you know it is now" epistemic objection; Correia and Rosenkranz (*Nothing to Come*, Springer 2018, check; the brief's "As Time Goes By" title I could not confirm and do not recall with confidence, possibly an earlier paper) develop the "no-nonsense" growing block with a relative-to-time instant of "now". The growing block takes asymmetry of past and future (fixed past, open future) as primary metaphysical data and takes causal asymmetry as its basis (Tooley: causation as what makes the future not exist yet). The growing block has been regarded as the A-theory most friendly to local approaches because the "edge" of reality is the "leading edge of becoming", which relativists generalise.

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *The view is a growing block with a relativity-appropriate edge, so it is not "resolving" but a species of the growing block* [ontology]. Table row 2 of §4 ("Becoming is real and directed (growing block)") already concedes this. The earlier red team (A) pins the same point. The growing-block theorist will say: you have found the right relativistic version of my view, and the earlier work on relativistic growing blocks (Stein's own becoming relation is a growing-block-ish relation; Sider 2001 ch. 2 critiques "growing block and relativity"; check) shows the same. You then cannot claim a resolution of the eternalist-presentist dispute: you have just reinforced one A-theory. The "both sides are right" rhetoric (§1, §4 diagnosis) is inconsistent with the actual content, which keeps *only* the A-theory's side of the table of reality-from-b and relegates eternalist truths to the "totality" (P7) or to invariants (P5).

(b) *Epistemic objection (am I at the edge?)* [time-consciousness; passage]. The classic worry (Braddon-Mitchell 2004, "How do we know it is now now?", *Analysis* 64, from memory) says that if the past is as real as the present, then past people's thoughts are as real as mine, and they think "it's now" too; how do I know I'm at the edge? Local fragmentalism *dissolves* this because each b is at the apex of its own fragment (R2), and every thinker at an apex is correct that their apex is the edge. But the dissolution comes with a cost: the "edge" is *relative*, so the view has to answer the *real* form of the problem: the relativist about "now" (Braddon-Mitchell-type) is just the eternalist, since "now" is an indexical. This is O2 again in the epistemic register. Correia & Rosenkranz propose a "relative to a time" solution; the relativity-version is analogous. The view's advantage is real but small.

(c) *Passage in the growing block is the addition of new layers* [passage]. The growing blockers' passage is global addition of an *absolutely new* layer, which grounds A-theory claims about the unreality of the future. R3 gives only a partial order on fragments (F(b) ⊆ F(b') when b ≤ b'), but there is no *event of adding* a fragment; fragments of events are all "there" in the totality (R6′), where nothing grows. O12 concedes this: "passage is not the kind of fact that obtains from the whole". The growing blocker will say this is exactly the eternalism-in-disguise reading (O2): the "totality" is a static union and the growth is only inclusion order. In Tooley's case, becoming is tied to causation: the view's P6 is close to Tooley's. But for Tooley the "growth" is an objective, non-perspectival fact.

(d) *Locality reading of the edge* [causal order; ontology]. Tooley's causal theory (as I recall) has the future-unreal because of causal relations it will enter; in relativity the corresponding statement is that an event's reality is fixed by causal relations (P6). The Stein-GR theorem shows (given LC) that if the edge is local then it can only be the light cone. But a growing blocker can reject LC and use a *preferred* foliation (a "growing block with a cosmological-time edge", Proposition 5/ R8 horn 1). Several growing-block authors, I believe (check), have explicitly proposed global, preferred-frame edges. The theorem does not refute them.

(e) *Spacelike causation from the quantum side* [causal order]. Clifton and Hogarth show (via O6) that if spacelike causation exists, fragments become everything. The growing blocker's response to quantum nonlocality is to adopt a foliation; the view is thus "hostage" (O6). This attack is not specific to the growing block but gains force there since the growing block adds *real collapse events* naturally.

**Already answered?** (a) The table and §4 acknowledge the growing block as vindicated for becoming. O2 treats "eternalism with tensed vocabulary" only. The specific objection "your view is a species of growing block and cannot claim to *resolve* the dispute" is not answered. The correct reply is to restate the claim: the view locates the correct *relativistic* A-theory (a point-relative growing block) and shows that the remaining dispute is over whether the unrestricted quantifier exists. (c) is O12 and is not fully answered; O12's own wording admits the cost. (d) is R8.

**Severity.** (a) *serious as a framing issue*, (c) *serious*, because it is the version of O12 coming from the people who actually hold a passage view. (b), (d), (e) manageable.

**Best reply.** Concede (a) in the text: present the view as a *relativistic growing block with a fragmentalist or relativist gloss*, with presentism-for-each-standpoint as a gloss on the apex. For (c), supply a substantive local notion of passage: R3 as a *local, ordered, objective* growth along each worldline, and argue the global addition-of-layers picture is not coherent in GR (which is the trilemma). Cost: conceding that there is no passage "of the whole".

**Prior anticipation.** Significant. The idea that the edge of becoming for an event is its causal past, in a growing-block spirit, is Stein 1968, Clifton & Hogarth 1995, and (from memory) in the philosophy-of-time literature on "relativistic growing block" (e.g. Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* ch. 2, and later defenders, check). Rovelli's local becoming is another (PRIOR_WORK). Tooley's causal construal of the growing block (1997, *Time, Tense and Causation*, from memory) anticipates P6 in spirit: reality grows by causal accretion. Note on the "closest" case: any "relativistic growing block" in the literature with point-relative edges should be checked before a novelty claim is made.

**Sources.** Broad, *Scientific Thought* (Routledge 1923) [from memory]; Tooley, *Time, Tense, and Causation* (OUP 1997) [from memory]; Forrest, "The real but dead past: a reply to Braddon-Mitchell", *Analysis* 64 (2004) [from memory; check]; Braddon-Mitchell, "How do we know it is now now?", *Analysis* 64 (2004) [from memory]; Correia & Rosenkranz, *Nothing to Come: A Defence of the Growing Block Theory of Time* (Springer 2018) [from memory; the brief's title "As Time Goes By" unverified]; Sider as above. All from-memory.

## 5. Shrinking tree / branching (McCall)

**Steelman.** McCall (*A Model of the Universe*, OUP 1994; from memory) models the universe as a tree of branches: the past is a single trunk (fixed), the future a branching set of possibilities, and as time passes branches are "pruned" (the shrinking tree), so the future shrinks to one actual branch. McCall ties the asymmetry of time to an objective physical probabilistic asymmetry (the parallel-lines model of quantum measurement, from memory), and he uses the idea of a *branching* structure to relate to relativity (the tree's branching is along time and can be given in a relativistic way; from memory, McCall wrote on branching and relativity, e.g. with Lowe "The definition of branching" and others; check). The shrinking tree is a tool for the open future (indeterminism with real objective chances), different from the growing block, because the future is real (as possibilities) and shrinks.

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *The view has no account of the open future* [open future]. R3 says fragments grow; but the theorem is about the *causal past*. Nothing in J⁻(b) tells us whether the future from b is open. The view is silent on determinism, so it does not capture McCall's data. If McCall's motivation for the objective flow is the open future (branching), the view gives a growth of fragments but cannot say that anything about b's future is *settled-not-yet*. The view's "not real from b" for the future is consistent with a deterministic block. STRONGEST_VIEW never separates the open-future thesis from the passage thesis; the brief says to keep these apart. Stein's own motivation was explicitly not indeterminism, so the view inherits that.

(b) *Branching in GR is not locally covariant* [causal order; open future]. A branching-spacetime model (Belnap's branching space-times, 1992, from memory) is *local* in a different sense: it ties indeterminism to a partial order with histories and "choice points". The theorem's category GH has no branches; B_M is a relation on a single manifold M. A McCall-ist says the real question is the relation between the branches (the possible futures), and the theorem says nothing about it. More sharply: in a branching ontology, the causal past of b is the same on all branches that pass through b; but the structure to which locality applies includes the branching, and in Belnap's branching space-times the "causal past" of an outcome is a more refined notion (the "causal cone" of a point-event plus branch). The view's identification of "real from b" with a *region of a single spacetime* fails to describe real from b in a branching model, where the "past" of b is a set of branch segments.

(c) *The shrinking tree is the dual of local fragmentalism's fragments* [ontology]. In the tree, the *future* of b as a branching set of possibilities is real and shrinks; in the view, the past is real and grows. A defender says the roles are symmetric: you could define b's possibility fragment as J⁺(b) with its set of possible branches, and the view would need an argument for why *only the past* is real. The tense-logical asymmetry here is not derived from physics (GR equations are time-symmetric); it is imported by (CB) via the time orientation chosen. This is the arrow problem: the view takes its time orientation as given (the paper's "oriented and time-oriented" Lorentzian manifolds) and gets the asymmetry of becoming from the asymmetry of "chronological becoming", which is an A-theoretic premise. [arrow] Stein's choice of the past is motivated by causal influence P6 ("can causally influence b"), not by the thermodynamic arrow; but P6 itself presupposes direction of influence (past to future), i.e. already the A-theorist's asymmetry. This is a general attack on the "direction" of becoming, sharpest from McCall's side.

(d) *Quantum branching* [open future; ontology]. Dieks's perspectivalism (O6) is no-collapse (Everettian). McCall's tree is a collapse-style picture. If collapse is real and nonlocal (as many McCall-type views hold), then O6 says fragments grow toward everything. So the view's only path out of collapse is exactly the route McCall rejects.

**Already answered?** (a) No. The view's O6 touches quantum; there is no treatment of the open future. (b) No. (c) Partly: R3 plus P6 gives the direction; the earlier red team RED_TEAM.md (not read in full by me) might discuss; STRONGEST_VIEW does not discuss time-orientation as an input. (d) O6 states the dependence honestly.

**Severity.** (a) *manageable*: the view can say it is neutral on determinism, but must say so (a missing separation). (b) *serious as a gap* if the view is to be offered as the account of dynamic ontology generally: theorem scope is single-history spacetimes. (c) *serious* in the arrow register: the direction of becoming is imported. (d) restates O6.

## 6. Moving spotlight (Skow, Cameron, Deasy)

**Steelman.** All times (events) exist, as in eternalism, but one is objectively present, and the "spotlight" of presentness moves. Skow (*Objective Becoming*, OUP 2015; from memory) argues passage is a matter of what is true relative to a time, with temporal operators and a primitive "it is present" that is metaphysically fundamental (a "time-indexed" account where what is the case moves, and the view has the resources to avoid the Mellor/McTaggart-style paradoxes); he also discusses the compatibility with relativity (a spacetime version; I recall he considers a point-like or cone-like spotlight but not firmly; check). Cameron (*The Moving Spotlight: An Essay on Time and Ontology*, OUP 2015; from memory) argues for a view with a "temporal" ontology of properties of being present, with the distinction between A-facts and B-facts. Deasy ("The moving spotlight theory", *Philosophical Studies* 172 (2015), from memory) defends a version combining the spotlight with a relation of presentness, arguing that the spotlight is dispensable... I do not recall details; check.

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *The view is the spotlight theory with the spotlight indexed to each event, and the spotlight theorist has resources it lacks* [passage]. A spotlight theorist can adopt a *multi-spotlight* or *point-relative* spotlight: in relativistic settings (cf. Skow's remark on relativity, check), the presentness is attached to a worldline or point; the only objective fact is that each event is present from its own standpoint; this is just P1 with the unrestricted block (the spotlight theory keeps all events existing). The spotlight theorist says: you have my view, except that you refuse the unrestricted block; but the totality F(M) = M under P7 is the block, so you have my ontology, and your "passage" is relative-to-b presentness, i.e. my spotlight indexed to b. This is O2/O12 again, specifically in the form "the view is a moving-spotlight theory in which each event gets a spotlight, and spotlight theories already allow `p is present` to be standpoint-relative".

(b) *Spotlight theory has an absolute passage fact, and the view's local growth is not an absolute passage* [passage]. The spotlight theory says there is one fact about which time is present *now*. Locally, "it is present from b" is true of b at b. In a relativistic setting no event is distinguished; so either the view is a many-spotlight version (which loses the unique absolute present and is in conflict with the standard spotlight motivations: Skow's and Cameron's views take the single, absolute, changing fact of presentness as data) or it has no passage.

(c) *Objective becoming is not captured by growth of J⁻* [passage; ontology]. Skow's "objective becoming" concerns the *change in what is the case* (as I recall: that how things are changes, not merely that facts accrue). R3 is a mere inclusion order on sets of events; a spotlight theorist says this is "the B-theory of becoming" (cf. Mellor, Oaklander). There is no sense in which F(b) changes for b: F(b) is a fixed set. What "grows" is the family of fragments along b's worldline, a B-series structure. This is the same as the standard B-theorist's observation that "accumulation of events" is analyzable tenselessly. Nothing in the view is *dynamic* beyond an ordering of fixed sets. The view's answer (O12) is that passage is a standpoint-internal fact, but with no standpoint-internal change this answer needs an explanation of what "from b, the future is not real, and later from b' it is" amounts to, other than fragment inclusion. In Fine's version this is explained by the *incoherence* of facts across fragments, i.e. a genuine difference in reality-from-standpoint. In the relativist reading (P1 weak), it collapses to the tenseless order. So the weak reading of P1 dissolves passage.

(d) *Cameron's "temporal" ontology distinction* [ontology]. Cameron's view (from memory) says that the temporal facts are about how things are, with past and future objects existing but not "present"; it is a *B-ontology with an A-property of presentness*. The view could say it adds structure-relative existence. A spotlighter will say: more parsimonious to keep an unrestricted existence and a single moving presentness.

**Already answered?** O2, O12 and O13 address these in part. O12 admits that the cost remains. O13 notes Lipman's atemporal standpoint, which the view obtains as a consequence of P7, but that makes the view the same as the spotlighter's picture (static whole + moving local presentness). The unresolved question is whether a many-standpoint spotlight with an "uncentered" static totality is a *new* view, or just the spotlight theory made relativistic.

**Severity.** (a) *serious*, close to the deepest objection O2 (this is the sharpest form of "eternalism plus tensed gloss": the spotlight theory is the cleanest case where tense is gloss on a block). (b)/(c) *serious*: they bear on whether the view captures objective passage at all under the weak (relativist) reading of P1.

**Best reply.** Embrace the comparison: local fragmentalism *is* the relativistic generalisation of the moving spotlight in which the spotlight is replaced by the event-indexed apex, and the point of the theorem is to show which indexed structure survives relativity. Then the novelty is the theorem and the claim that no *single* spotlight can be local. To retain passage, adopt the strong (Fine) reading of P1, where facts from different fragments are genuinely incoherent, and state that on the weak reading the view reduces to a spotlight theory with a relativity-compatible spotlight family. Cost: giving up the ambition to be neutral between P1's two readings (the paper's theorems are neutral, the philosophical claim is not).

**Prior anticipation.** Moderate. The moving spotlight with relativity: I recall Skow (2015 ch. on relativity, check) and Cameron (2015) both discuss whether the spotlight can be adapted to relativity (e.g., a spotlight moving along a worldline, a "worldline spotlight", or a hyperplane spotlight), and a worldline- or point-indexed spotlight would anticipate the "event-indexed presentness" part of the view. I cannot give locators; this is the first place to check before any novelty claim about P1/P2 as a point-indexed A-theory.

**Sources.** Skow, *Objective Becoming* (OUP 2015) [from memory]; Cameron, *The Moving Spotlight: An Essay on Time and Ontology* (OUP 2015) [from memory]; Deasy, "The moving spotlight theory", *Philosophical Studies* 172 (2015) [from memory; details unverified]. From-memory.

## 7. Prior's tense logic and the tensed-facts tradition

**Steelman.** Prior's tense logic (*Past, Present and Future*, 1967; *Papers on Time and Tense*, 1968) treats tense operators (P, F, and later "it is now the case that") as primitive and irreducible to talk of dated events. Tensed facts are fundamental (the "tensed theory"), and truth is evaluated at a time, with the present time privileged (Priorean "ontology of the present" is the philosophical gloss). The key logical features: time as a linear (or branching) order of instants; the "nowness" operator; the claim that tense logic is the logic of change. The tradition continues in Kamp's and Kamp-style "now" operators, and in work on "Priorean tense logic and relativity" (the question of whether tense logic can be given a Minkowskian semantics with the causal order as accessibility relation; this is a well-known line: Goldblatt, "Diodorean modality in Minkowski spacetime", *Studia Logica* 39 (1980), from memory, shows that the modal logic of the causal-future relation is S4.2... and Shehtman's work; check).

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *Tense logic on the causal order is already a standard framework; the view's fragments are the modal "box past" semantics* [causal order; ontology]. The tense-logical content of "p was true at x" with R = ≤ is the Kripke semantics for P over the causal order (Goldblatt 1980: the logic of Minkowski spacetime with ≤ as accessibility is S4.2, from memory). The view's R(b, x) iff x ∈ F(b) = J⁻(b) is exactly this accessibility relation. So 'F(b) = J⁻(b)' says: Minkowski tense-logic with Goldblatt's accessibility. The tense logician will say the novelty is a derivation of the accessibility relation from locality (the theorem), but the standpoint-logic *interpretation* is just Kripke semantics with an ontological gloss. It is a "representation transformation" (AGENTS.md): Lipman's hybrid-logic reading of R as "is a standpoint from" is notation for Kripke semantics with nominals. It provides no *independent* check that the semantic accessibility is an ontology.

(b) *Priorean tense logic requires a unique present at the point of evaluation* [passage; ontology]. In Prior's system, the "now" operator picks out the actual present; the view's evaluation at b is Kripke evaluation at a world b, with no distinguished world: that's relativism about truth (all b equal), which Prior would reject because it makes tensed truth *as* relative as spatial truth. This is Fine's O11 objection restated from the tense-logical side. The O11(a) concession ("here is as objective as now") is exactly what Prior's tradition rejects: Prior's famous asymmetry between "Thank goodness that's over" and spatial analogues. The view concedes that "here" is as objective as "now" and replaces the asymmetry with fragment inclusion. A tense logician can say: inclusion is a property of the accessibility relation and is just the *difference between a linear and a non-linear* relation. It is no more evidence of objective passage than the transitivity of "to the left of".

(c) *Past-directedness vs future-directedness in the logic* [arrow; open future]. Tense logic is symmetric under P and F in the minimal system; asymmetry (open future) is added by extra axioms (branching). The view's F(b) = J⁻(b) is the *backward* accessibility relation only. The view then gives the logic of P with ≤ and no F-modality of the same standing; "it will be" has no fragment content. Tense logicians accept this only if they add an extra modality for the future (and the Peircean/Ockhamist semantics for F in a branching model). So the view's logic is half of a tense logic, and the other half is silent. That repeats 5(a).

(d) *Truth and truthmaker* [ontology]. The tensed-facts tradition (e.g., Bourne, Crisp, also Tooley) holds tensed facts exist *absolutely* and are the truthmakers for tensed sentences. P1 makes tensed facts standpoint-relative and invariants (P5) the "absolute facts"; these are tenseless, so the view *reverses* the tensed tradition: absolute facts are the B-facts, relative facts are A-facts. By the tradition's own lights, this is the B-theory with an A-relativisation (West-coast style "new tenseless"?). This is the same as O2; the specific diagnostic is P5.

**Already answered?** (a) No. The paper itself (Section on prior work) is silent on the Goldblatt line, and STRONGEST_VIEW does not mention Goldblatt-type tense logics of Minkowski spacetime. R1 is a theorem about which relation is permitted, but the *modal logic* of ≤ and the relation to standpoint logic (Lipman) is the unaddressed item. (b) O11 concedes. (d) P5 and O2.

**Severity.** (a) *manageable* but a novelty/priority risk (a missing prior-work line, and the claim in §2b that the theorem "puts the physics in the metaphysicians' own formalism" overstates: the formalism is Kripke semantics, already used with the causal order); (b) *serious*; it is the Fine/Prior asymmetry, and the view's reply is a concession. (c) manageable. (d) serious as part of O2.

**Best reply.** For (a), cite Goldblatt and the tense-logic-of-Minkowski-spacetime literature and recast §2b as: the theorem selects which Kripke frame (accessibility relation) is locally covariant. For (b), strengthen O11's structural asymmetry: show that the asymmetry between F(b) ⊆ F(b') (time) and no inclusion (space) is *logical* (it validates a transitivity-and-directedness axiom for time and none for space), and say that this logical asymmetry is what the tense-logic tradition meant by the asymmetry of tense. Cost: conceding that Prior's stronger asymmetry (a privileged now) is not recovered.

**Prior anticipation.** Significant at the formal level: tense logics with the Minkowski causal order as accessibility relation (Goldblatt 1980; Shehtman and others; from memory) already assign to an event b the truth of "P p" iff p holds at some x ≤ b. Using that as a semantics is not new; deriving it from locality is. Add to PRIOR_WORK. Not read.

**Sources.** Prior, *Past, Present and Future* (OUP 1967) and *Papers on Time and Tense* (OUP 1968) [from memory]; Goldblatt, "Diodorean modality in Minkowski spacetime", *Studia Logica* 39 (1980) 219–236 [from memory; page range unverified]. From-memory.

## 8. Craig's neo-Lorentzian presentism and Zimmerman's preferred-foliation presentism ("add structure")

**Steelman.** Craig (*Time and the Metaphysics of Relativity*, Kluwer 2001; *The Tensed Theory of Time*, 2000; from memory) argues that the special theory is, as a theory of empirical content, equivalent to a neo-Lorentzian theory with an undetectable absolute simultaneity; that Einstein's operationalist reading of the theory is a verificationist add-on; and that the best metaphysics (including divine time-reckoning) supports a cosmic time grounded in God's frame or in the cosmic expansion. Zimmerman ("Presentism and the space-time manifold", in Callender ed., *Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time*, OUP 2011; from memory) argues presentism can be combined with a preferred foliation, that the case for GR's lack of a preferred foliation is not decisive (e.g., because of cosmological time in FLRW and the possibility of undetectable structure), and that the A-theory's motivations outweigh the cost of unobservable structure. Both are the "add structure" horn of R8, taken on the chin. Their motivation is the standard "empirical equivalence" point that the project's own rules take seriously (AGENTS.md: "Synthesis must preserve incompatible assumptions and empirical equivalence").

**Strongest attacks.**

(a) *Empirical equivalence undercuts the case for locality (P3) as a metaphysical requirement* [ontology; clock time]. The view's strongest premise is P3w; the paper's cosmological-time rule (Proposition 5) shows that a natural non-local rule satisfies everything else. The neo-Lorentzian says: yes, Proposition 5 shows my option is mathematically coherent and empirically equivalent; locality is motivated by physics practice (LCQFT, Viglione), and I deny that physics practice is metaphysical evidence about which structure is real. P3's "independent motivation" (O3) is exactly what a Craig/Zimmerman presentist denies is *independent* of an anti-presentist metaphysics. The earlier RED_TEAM.md section D (law-locality vs fact-locality) is the same point; a preferred-foliation theorist accepts law-locality (dynamics is local relative to the foliation) and denies fact-locality (supervenience of becoming on D(Σ)).

(b) *P3w is rejected without rejecting physicalism about becoming* [ontology; causal order]. STRONGEST_VIEW §7 item 11 asks exactly this. Answer from this school: yes. A foliation-theorist's becoming supervenes on the physical state of the whole slice (the foliation is physical structure: a dynamical field, e.g. a preferred-frame vector field as in Hořava-Lifshitz or Einstein-aether, or the CMB rest frame), but not on D(Σ) of a *thin compact slab* with the *induced geometry only*: it supervenes on geometry plus the foliation field, which is data on Σ too. So with the foliation field included in the data, the local rule *is* local: becoming is local relative to the richer structure, as the paper itself states (Section "What this shows", first paragraph, "in effect Monton's move"). The response of the view (R8) is "inherits Wüthrich's and Viglione's objections", and [THESIS]. So the "supervenience on the physics of any region that determines the two events" argument against a shared present reduces to: add a field to the physics. The view then has no knockdown argument; the theorem is a conditional on the *category* (which structure is counted as physical).

(c) *Neo-Lorentzian theories have empirical content in principle (quantum nonlocality, §6 of STRONGEST_VIEW, and "thing 1" in §8: a privileged foliation)* [ontology]. §8 item 1 admits a dynamical preferred frame would count against the view. Craig-type views say the foliation is *undetectable* in principle (the ether is hidden), which means the view's falsifier is unavailable and the dispute is metaphysical. The view's sole advantage is then parsimony and relativistic fit (no added structure). Parsimony is not decisive in a dispute about whether the A-theory is true: the neo-Lorentzian says it buys the A-theory (a real now, a real passage) at the price of the extra structure, and a good bargain.

(d) *The 'empirical equivalence' of the two options is exactly the O2 problem* [ontology]. If the view and a neo-Lorentzian presentism are empirically equivalent, then the case for the view over the neo-Lorentzian rests on non-empirical virtues (locality, parsimony, no brute foliation). The paper says nothing to show these favour fragmentalism over the neo-Lorentzian. In particular the neo-Lorentzian's foliation can be *conventional-free* and *explanatory* (it explains why the quantum correlations are what they are, a point where Bohmians make a similar claim: the preferred foliation does explanatory work in nonlocal quantum mechanics; from memory).

(e) *Presentism in GR with cosmological time* [clock time]. In big-bang FLRW and many realistic cosmologies, there is a canonical global time (cosmological time, CMB frame). Zimmerman and others (from memory) argue the actual universe has a natural foliation, so the "no global time in GR" premise (O11b) is not true of the actual world. O11(b) says generic spacetimes have no global inertial frames; true, but irrelevant to a theory of our universe, which is nearly FLRW. The paper's Proposition 5 uses cosmological time as the *non-local* rule; O11(b) says it "requires a foliation or reference field, which is added global structure or, without it, non-locality". But in globally hyperbolic spacetimes with finite cosmological time, such a foliation is canonical (determined by the metric), so in the actual universe the foliation is *definable from the metric*. So the "added structure" horn can be dissolved: nothing is added in FLRW. The nonlocality is the only price, and it is not a physics-practice cost in cosmology (where nonlocal definitions like cosmological time are in daily use). This is a strong, specific attack on R8 and O11(b).

**Already answered?** (a) O3 gives physics-based motivation; this does not address a school that questions that motivation, but it is honest that rejection is coherent. (b) R8, partly. (c) §8 item 1 states the falsifier but does not argue parsimony. (d) Not answered. (e) Paper Proposition 5 shows cosmological time fails (LC) but the view's answer to "why is failing locality bad here?" is only the O3/R8 appeal to Viglione and Wüthrich. Neither is a demonstration. The strongest available reply is that cosmological time is not stable under local deformation (it depends on the whole past, including data arbitrarily far away or at the big bang): the foliation of a region depends on the global history, so the present is not determined by anything locally observable. This is a respectable but contestable physical claim.

**Severity.** *Serious*, and arguably the most dangerous attack from this group on the *bridge* (not on the theorem). It does not make the theorem false; it denies that the theorem's premise (P3) is the right one, using exactly the structure the view calls an extra cost. It becomes *fatal to R9 and to the "diagnosis"* if read as a uniqueness claim: R9 says the view is "what is left once those five commitments are made", but commitment (iii) (locality) is what the opponent denies, and R8's cost accounting ("inherits Wüthrich's objections") is not an argument but a bookkeeping entry.

**Best reply.** Re-scope the claims from "resolves the divide" and "uniqueness" to a conditional with a stated price: *given locality, no shared present; the choice to keep locality is as justified as the choice to retain Lorentz invariance of the fundamental ontology*. Strengthen the case that foliation-based becoming is not local in the relevant sense: in FLRW the foliation is metric-definable but depends on the entire past including conditions at the singularity; in generic globally hyperbolic spacetimes there is no canonical foliation (Proposition 5 only gives a rule where τ is finite), and for a sheaf of local theories the foliation must be a physical field whose dynamics must be specified (and whose presence could be probed; the view's falsifier §8.1). Cost: the view abandons the claim of a refutation, and R8 becomes a trade-off statement honest about empirical equivalence.

**Prior anticipation.** The "locality vs preferred foliation" split is the standard picture in the philosophy literature (Craig, Zimmerman, Monton, Wüthrich, Dieks; PRIOR_WORK). No anticipation of the theorem or of causal-past fragments. But note Craig and Zimmerman each already say that without extra structure relativity provides only a "peculiarly" local or point-like present: I recall Zimmerman 2011 considering point-like and cone-like candidates and rejecting them as too thin (check); this would anticipate the *diagnosis* (that the relativistic non-foliated alternative is a point present), though not the theorem.

**Sources.** Craig, *Time and the Metaphysics of Relativity* (Kluwer 2001) [from memory]; Craig, *The Tensed Theory of Time: A Critical Examination* (Kluwer 2000) [from memory]; Zimmerman, "Presentism and the space-time manifold", in Callender ed., *The Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time* (OUP 2011) [from memory]; Monton, "Prolegomena to any future physics-based metaphysics", in Zimmerman ed., *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion* (2006?) [from memory; unverified], where the reference-field/foliation move is, I think, advocated; check. From-memory.

## Top threats from this group

1. **The foliation-presentist rejection of P3/P3w, via the metric-definable cosmological foliation (8b, 8e).** It targets the bridge from mathematics to metaphysics: the theorem is a conditional on locality, and in the actual, nearly-FLRW, universe a canonical foliation is definable from the metric, so "added structure" is not needed and "non-locality" is the only cost. R8 and R9 and the "diagnosis" then say less than they appear to. This is the biggest threat because the view stakes uniqueness on the premise the opponent is entitled to reject, and has no non-question-begging argument for it beyond physics practice (O3) and Viglione's informal case. Empirical equivalence is conceded in O2.

2. **The view is a relativised moving spotlight/growing block, and passage on the weak reading of P1 dissolves (4a, 4c, 6a-c, 7b).** Several schools converge: fragment growth is an inclusion order, which every B-theorist accepts; P5 makes the absolute facts tenseless; the "totality" (P7) is a static block. Unless P1's strong (Fine) reading is adopted, nothing in the view is dynamic that the B-theorist lacks. This is O2/O12 sharpened, and the reply to O12 (passage is not a fact from the whole) concedes the point to anyone who wants passage to be global.

3. **Misdescription of the position as vindicating presentism, and the unaddressed semantic cost (1a, 3c).** F(b) ⊇ I⁻(b) makes the fragment a growing block; and the view's consequence that spacelike-separated events are not real from b makes ordinary talk of "the Sun now" or of distant simultaneity defective. The first is an easy fix; the second has no reply in STRONGEST_VIEW.

**Single repair that does the most good.** Restate the headline claim as a conditional theorem about *local* dynamic ontologies, and demote "resolution" and "uniqueness" (R9, §1, §4 diagnosis) to: "given locality, and given the strong fragmentalist reading of P1 so that passage is not just inclusion order, the fragments are causal pasts; the alternatives are a foliation (with the costs and the empirical equivalence stated) or the block". This answers threats 1 and 2 together by moving the burden to the stated premises, and drops the presentism label (threat 3).
