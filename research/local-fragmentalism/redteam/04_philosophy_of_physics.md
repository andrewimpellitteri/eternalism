## 1. Belnap–Müller–Placek: branching space-times (BST)

**Steelman.** BST (Belnap 1992; Belnap, Müller and Placek, *Branching Space-Times*, OUP 2022) models indeterminism on a partial order of point events with "no backward branching". Histories are maximal directed, historically connected subsets. A proposition is *settled at* a point event e iff it holds in every history containing e. The core principle is the prior choice principle: any two histories that differ have a *choice point* in the past of the divergence, where "past" means the causal past. So settledness is relative to point events, not to hyperplanes, and it is exactly what relativity needs: no foliation. The causal past of e is settled; what is unsettled at e is what depends on choices not yet "prior" to e. Spacelike-separated outcomes are also unsettled at e unless fixed by e's past. BST also gives the best-developed treatment of causation and of the "causae causantes" (basic transitions), and of why correlations (EPR) need not be explained by causal influence.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *DET / ONTOLOGY conflated.* The view's "not real from b" is an ontological status; BST's "unsettled at e" is a *modal* status. In BST all of the branching structure, including every branch, is on a par ontologically (a modal "block of possibilities"). A BST-er says local fragmentalism takes an epistemic/modal notion (what is determined by e's past) and relabels it as reality. In a deterministic GR spacetime (globally hyperbolic, with a unique history), BST gives one history and every proposition is settled at every point; the view's R1 would still say the future is not real from b. So the content of "not real" cannot be settledness, and the view owes its own account (O2 again).
(b) *The justification of P3w is deterministic and time-symmetric.* The text says D(Σ) "is exactly the region whose physics is fixed by data on Σ". That is Cauchy determinism, and it is symmetric in time: data on Σ fix both D^+ and D^-. It therefore cannot motivate the asymmetry J^- versus J^+; that comes only from the time-orientation built into the category (CB) (see §8). In an indeterministic (branching) world D(Σ) is not fixed by Σ at all, so the supervenience premise changes meaning. P3w's rationale ("a physicalist about becoming can hardly reject this") is therefore tied to determinism at the very place where BST says becoming and indeterminism are entangled.
(c) *Spacelike events.* BST's settledness at e does not require spacelike events to be real-for-e, but it also does not make them unreal; they are "not yet settled", with a definite status in the branching tree. The view's R2 (no co-presence) has no BST analogue; BST is silent on whether e's spacelike events *exist*.

**Already answered?** Partly. O2 states the dialectic and concedes empirical equivalence, but nothing in STRONGEST_VIEW addresses BST's modal reading, or the deterministic limit. O6 mentions indeterminism only through collapse. The reply to (b) is not present anywhere.

**Severity.** (a) manageable, if the view says explicitly that "real from b" is not "settled at b" and that R1 holds in deterministic worlds, so the claim is not modal; but then the content question O2 stands unaided. (b) manageable but should be repaired: restate P3w's rationale without determinism (e.g. as covariance only).

**Best reply.** Say plainly that fragments are a *temporal-ontology* claim, independent of determinism, and add a one-paragraph BST comparison: BST settledness is the modal shadow of a causal-past ontology, and the two coincide in extension where BST's past is J^-(e). Restate the rationale for P3w as naturality (isometric-embedding invariance), not as Cauchy determination. Cost: losing the "a physicalist can hardly reject P3w" rhetoric.

**Prior anticipation.** Substantial *structural* overlap: BST's "past of e is settled, determined by the causal-order downset, no hyperplane, no foliation" is the same order-theoretic move (Belnap 1992; Müller, various; from memory). But BST claims only a modal/indeterminist thesis and, as far as I know, no tense-ontological one, and no uniqueness theorem. Note also that BST in the "Our World" sense assumes a Minkowskian-like causal structure (a partial order, with the "historical connection" axiom); the "no backward branching" is a stronger postulate than anything in the Stein-GR paper. Flag for novelty: the structural idea "the settled/real for e is its causal downset" is not new, even if the tense-ontological identification is.

**Sources.** Belnap, "Branching space-time", *Synthese* 92 (1992), 385-434 (verify pages). Belnap, Müller, Placek, *Branching Space-Times: Theory and Applications*, OUP 2022 (the brief gives this; verify title). Müller, "Branching space-times, general relativity, the Hausdorff property, and modal consistency" (2013-14; verify). Read depth: from-memory.

## 2. Causal-set becoming: Dowker, Rideout–Sorkin, Wüthrich–Callender

**Steelman.** In causal set theory (CST) spacetime is a locally finite partial order; in the classical sequential growth (CSG) dynamics of Rideout and Sorkin (2000) elements are born one by one, each new element choosing its ancestors by a random process obeying *discrete general covariance* (label-independence) and *Bell causality* (the birth probability of a new element depends only on its past, i.e. the "ancestors" in the stem). Dowker proposes that the passage of time is the birth of spacetime atoms, an *objective, physical, stochastic, non-spatial* becoming: not a motion along a pre-existing time but the process by which the order is generated. Dowker stresses that this "becoming" has no external time (no clock for the process), is not relative to any slicing, and that "the past" of an element is what it has been born with, while its "now" is simply the process of its birth. Wüthrich and Callender (2017) ask what happens to this once labelling is gauge: they argue that only the unlabelled poset is physically meaningful, so the CSG "passage" is gauge, and the best that can be salvaged is a relative, Stein-type becoming (an element's past is what has become for it).

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *Becoming on this view is an event of the whole, not relative to a standpoint.* In CSG the process is a single stochastic growth of the entire poset; the pre-existing "stem" is the shared state of the growing whole. A shared, extended, objective present-like object (the set of already-born elements, "the growing poset so far") exists in the formalism. The view's R2 ("no shared present") is then contradicted by the closest-to-physics becoming theory. The reply "that stage is gauge" is Wüthrich–Callender's, and costs: if the stage is gauge, so is the temporal structure of the birth-order and the "becoming" of Dowker is not an additional physical fact, *which supports the view*; if it is not gauge (the so-called "covariant-labelling" refinements; Sorkin-style "stem" observables), the shared present returns.
(b) *Dynamics, not just kinematics.* The Stein-GR theorem is a kinematic classification. CSG supplies a stochastic *dynamics* in which Bell causality does the work P3 is supposed to do and does so as a dynamical law with probabilities, not as a supervenience premise. Why should the view not simply be CSG-with-fragments? And what does the theorem add? A CST-er says: Proposition 19 (R5) is trivial in the relevant sense, since becoming-as-ancestry is built into the definition of the structure.
(c) *R5 uses non-manifoldlike posets* (STRONGEST_VIEW R5, "using non-manifoldlike posets"). CST's physical posets are (approximately) manifoldlike; the exactness of the discrete theorem rests on posets that CST regards as unphysical (e.g. the Kleitman-Rothschild type dominating at large cardinality). The discrete case is "exact" only where it is not physical. This is the same off-shell issue as for GR (§12).
(d) *Quantum.* In CST dynamics the order itself is quantum (sum over causal sets); a "causal past" is not defined in a superposition of orders. The classical CSG is a toy model, so R5 is a result about the toy.

**Already answered?** R5 and the abstract of the paper mention the Wüthrich-Callender failure and the "same hypothesis" success. But the view does not address (a) the *shared growing stage*, nor (b) the dynamical Bell-causality parallel, and says nothing about gauge status of the stage.

**Severity.** (a) serious: it is the one place where a physics-based becoming theory contradicts R2/R6, unless the stage is gauge. (b), (c) manageable. (d) manageable.

**Best reply.** Adopt Wüthrich and Callender's (and, I think, Dowker's own) point that the CSG stage is label-dependent: the covariant content of the growth is exactly the poset with stem structure ("an element together with its ancestors"), which *is* a causal-past fragment. Then CSG becoming becomes the best physical example of local fragmentalism, not a counterexample. Cost: the view should cite it as its closest dynamical analogue and concede that Dowker's ontology says the same thing about the individual element.

**Prior anticipation.** **Strong.** Dowker's talk of an element being "born" with its past, and Sorkin's emphasis that in CSG the physical content is the stem (the past of an element, up to relabelling), is a physics-side anticipation of "an event's reality is its causal past" (Dowker 2014; Rideout and Sorkin 2000; from memory). Wüthrich and Callender note the Stein-type reading explicitly (from memory; verify). This should be recorded in PRIOR_WORK.md, and it affects the novelty of R5 and of the "event standpoint with causal-past fragment" idea in the discrete setting. The Stein-GR paper does not establish anything for general relativity from this.

**Sources.** Dowker, "The birth of spacetime atoms as the passage of time", *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1326 (2014), 18-25 (verify). Dowker, "Being and becoming on the road to quantum gravity, or, the birth of a causal set" (in a conference or volume on being and becoming; verify venue and year). Rideout and Sorkin, "Classical sequential growth dynamics for causal sets", *Phys. Rev. D* 61 (2000), 024002 (verify). Wüthrich and Callender, "What becomes of a causal set?", *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 68 (2017), 907-925 (verify). Read depth: from-memory; the paper's own abstract (main.tex) was read.

## 3. Earman, "Reassessing the prospects for a growing block model of the universe" (2008)

**Steelman.** Earman (*International Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 22(2), 2008, 135-164; verify pages) takes the growing block model seriously as a candidate for "the way the world is" and asks whether it can be made compatible with relativistic physics. A web search this session confirmed only the venue and that later literature (Deng; Pooley, Kent growing-block blog) reads it as a survey of problems the growing block meets from contemporary physics; Pooley's summary, as reported by the search tool, says the approach is that a relativistic growing block only needs the physics and the spacetime structure of the growing block to obey relativistic laws. Beyond that I cannot say from memory what Earman does with Stein and Clifton–Hogarth.

**Does it anticipate the GR extension of Stein?** I cannot confirm. My memory is that Earman discusses the Stein/Clifton–Hogarth results in the Minkowski setting and the difficulties in generic general-relativistic spacetimes (absence of global time functions in non-stably-causal spacetimes, causal pathologies such as Gödel's, quantum gravity) and does not prove a result of the kind in main.tex. If that is right, he anticipates the *problem* that Clifton and Hogarth also flagged (their p. 385 remark quoted in main.tex), not the locality-gluing solution. This is a lead only. **Recommendation: obtain and read the paper before the author claims priority over the GR extension.** It is the one item on my list where a hidden anticipation is plausible and where I am unable to rule it out.

**Strongest attacks (if the paper says what I recall).**
(a) *Growing block is not the right target.* Earman's growing block is a global growth; the view says it is not a growing block (no shared stage) but a family of growing downsets. Earman-style rejoinder: if there is no shared "leading edge", then "block" is misnamed and one gets no answer to Merricks-style epistemic objections *for each fragment*: the "am I at the edge?" problem recurs at every standpoint (cf. the Merricks objection reported in the arXiv paper surfaced by the search). Here the view's "from b the future is not real" answers: each b is at its own edge by construction (P2, R3, R2), so no skeptical problem arises from the standpoint-internal side. Fine, but then the objection returns as the "senses of reality" problem (O2).
(b) *Non-globally-hyperbolic and CTC cases* are in play physically (Earman 1995 book on spacetime pathologies; from memory). The view restricts to GH and says non-GH is isolated or unaddressed. A growing-block theorist says a theory of becoming that is silent on AdS, spacetimes with big-bang or big-crunch singularities that are not Cauchy-complete, and any non-GH extension of our universe is incomplete as a physics-compatible proposal.

**Already answered?** O4/R7 address the CTC part. Nothing addresses AdS or non-GH-but-causal spacetimes; main.tex's "Limits" paragraph says they are untreated.

**Severity.** Priority risk: unknown until read. Attacks (a)-(b): manageable.

**Best reply.** Read the paper. If Earman does have a "past light-cone growing block", cite it as predecessor of R1 for GR-in-spirit and keep the theorem as the new part. Cost: adjust novelty claims.

**Prior anticipation.** Possible; unresolved. Flag to the author as the highest-value check in this group.

**Sources.** Earman, "Reassessing the prospects for a growing block model of the universe", *International Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 22 (2008), 135-164 (verify). Read depth: discovery (bibliographic confirmation by web search only; contents from-memory and unverified).

## 4. Maudlin: passage as fundamental

**Steelman.** In *The Metaphysics Within Physics* (OUP 2007, ch. 4 "On the passing of time"), Maudlin argues that the passage of time is an intrinsic, primitive feature of the temporal structure of the world; the direction of time is not to be reductively analysed (neither by entropy nor by causation), the arrow does not need "explaining" by something non-temporal, and the "moving spotlight" and "flow" images are bad metaphors for a perfectly good fact: events are *ordered* with a direction, and laws are to be understood as producing later states from earlier ones. Passage does not require a global present; in relativity it is passage along worldlines, which have an intrinsic direction (time orientation; I recall he treats the structure of Minkowski spacetime as including the causal/temporal orientation, not reducible to the metric). He holds the "fundamental laws of temporal evolution" view: laws are generative, which explains the direction.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *PASSAGE: relativization is idle.* On Maudlin's view the passage along a worldline is just the intrinsic directedness of the time-orientation, an objective, standpoint-independent fact. Local fragmentalism's "fragment of b grows as b moves up the worldline" adds a layer of "reality from b" that does no explanatory work; everything the theorem delivers is the *tenseless* order J^-. This is O2/O12 stated from the Maudlin side: if passage is the directedness of time orientation, the view's R3 (fragments grow) is just a restatement of the partial order, and "becoming is real" is a label on a tenseless fact. The Stein-GR theorem says that the only local-covariant becoming candidate is the causal order, which is *exactly what a Maudlinian says passage already is*. So the view treats as discovered what Maudlin takes as primitive.
(b) *ARROW/PASSAGE direction is an input, not an output.* The category GH has orientation and time-orientation as part of its objects and morphisms. R3 ("becoming is real and directed") therefore uses direction supplied by hand. Maudlin is happy with this (the direction is primitive) but then the view should not present directedness as one of what it "vindicates" (table in §4). See §8.
(c) *Fundamentality.* Maudlin's passage is fundamental and not relative to anything. A standpoint-relative passage is an "A-theory in drag": what is relative is "reality from b", not the passage. Maudlin would call this a verbal relabelling of the B-theory.
(d) *The Bell connection.* Maudlin also holds (in *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity*, 3rd edn 2011) that Bell nonlocality is an unavoidable feature of the world and that the most natural relativistic-compatible way of making sense of it may require additional spacetime structure (see §5). If so, he would regard P3 as simply false of our world.

**Already answered?** O2, O12 and O8 address (a)/(c) in the abstract. The reply in O12 (passage is a standpoint-internal fact like "here") does not meet Maudlin's version, which says passage is *not* a fact-from-a-standpoint at all but the objective directedness of time; the "here" analogy begs the question since Maudlin denies tense parity with indexicals. (b): not addressed.

**Severity.** (a)/(c): serious. They are the O2 objection in its best form and the view's reply depends on a distinction (standpoint-internal versus invariant) that the Maudlinian does not recognise. (b): manageable. (d): see §5.

**Best reply.** Concede that the view and Maudlin share the *structure* (a primitive time orientation plus causal order) and differ on whether "reality" is indexed. Argue that the indexing is the content of the dispute with the presentist: Maudlin's primitive passage, in a relativistic setting, either has local content only (then it is the view) or global (then it needs a foliation). Cost: the "new ontology" claim is weaker; the view becomes a structural thesis about the *absence* of global passage.

**Prior anticipation.** Maudlin's position that passage in relativity is along worldlines and does not require a global present is close to the view's R3 *without* indexing and without the J^- theorem (from memory; verify what ch. 4 says about relativity). Moderate.

**Sources.** Maudlin, *The Metaphysics Within Physics*, OUP 2007, ch. 4 (brief gives this; read depth: from-memory). Maudlin, *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity*, 3rd edn, Wiley-Blackwell 2011 (from-memory). Maudlin, *Philosophy of Physics: Space and Time*, Princeton 2012 (from-memory).

## 5. Quantum objections: collapse/flash, Bohmian foliation, Valentini, Bell vs P3

**Steelman.** (i) Bell's theorem: any theory reproducing quantum correlations violates Bell's local causality. (ii) Maudlin (2011): this nonlocality is a physical, causal-structure-relevant feature, and the simplest theories that exhibit it (Bohmian mechanics, GRW with a nonlocal collapse) are formulated with a preferred foliation. Dürr, Goldstein, Norsen, Struyve and Zanghì ("Can Bohmian mechanics be made relativistic?", *Proc. R. Soc. A* 470 (2014); verify) propose a foliation determined *dynamically* by the wave function, so there is no fixed background structure but there is still a preferred slicing. (iii) Valentini argues the nonlocality of the hidden-variable level requires absolute simultaneity, which is hidden at "quantum equilibrium"; Lorentz invariance is an emergent, equilibrium symmetry, and the real structure is neo-Lorentzian (Valentini, "Hidden variables and the large-scale structure of space-time", in Craig and Smith (eds), *Einstein, Relativity and Absolute Simultaneity*, Routledge 2008; verify). (iv) Counterpoint: Myrvold (2002, *SHPMP* 33; "On peaceful coexistence"; verify) and Tumulka (2006, "A relativistic version of the GRW model", *J. Stat. Phys.* 125; the rGRWf flash theory; verify) show that collapse can be Lorentz-covariant *without* a preferred foliation; the price is the nonlocal law. So relativity plus nonlocality does not by itself force a foliation.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *P6 plus Bell gives universal becoming.* P6 says: whatever can causally influence b is real from b. In any theory reproducing quantum correlations, the outcome at b is not screened off from spacelike-separated outcomes by the data in J^-(b) (Bell, 1976/1990; Norsen 2011; from memory). If that dependence counts as causal influence (Maudlin's reading; Clifton–Hogarth's own maxim in the presence of spacelike causation), then by P6 and (T) the whole spacetime is real from b: eternalism. STRONGEST_VIEW O6 concedes this conditional. But the view lists the "hostage to quantum foundations" status as "OPEN" and offers Dieks. Honest status: the view's central claim is hostage to a live dispute about whether Bell-correlation is *causation*. If it is, the view is dead (for this world); if it is not, the view needs the claim that nomological dependence without causation does not generate reality-from-b, and that is not argued.
(b) *The two uses of "locality" are different.* P3 is a *supervenience/naturality* locality for a becoming relation: B(x, b) depends only on a convex region containing x and b. Bell locality is about probabilistic screening-off by J^-. The view says LCQFT locality motivates P3. But LCQFT's locality (Einstein causality: commuting algebras at spacelike separation, plus naturality) is perfectly compatible with Bell-violating *states* of the field; QFT states violate Bell inequalities at spacelike separation (Summers–Werner, 1987; from memory). So the physics the view appeals to for motivation *contains* the nonlocal correlations, and says nothing about whether the correlated outcomes are "real from" each other's standpoint. This is a gap: the physical motivation for P3 (algebraic locality) is silent on exactly the quantum feature that threatens P6.
(c) *Flash ontology.* In Tumulka's rGRWf the matter ontology is a discrete set of spacetime point events (flashes); the law is Lorentz-covariant, there is no foliation, and the joint distribution of flashes violates Bell locality. Here fragments look exactly right *as an ontology of points*, but the law is a joint distribution over spacelike-separated flashes: the flash at b has a probability that depends on flashes outside J^-(b). So rGRWf is a physical theory whose ontology is the view's kind (events) and whose dynamics is not local in P3's sense of what is real from b. The view must say whether spacelike flashes that co-determine b's probability are "real from b".
(d) *Bohm/Valentini and the empirical-equivalence charge.* If a preferred foliation exists, P3 is false of the actual world even though no experiment shows it. The view's §8(1) lists this as a refutation condition, but the relevant physics (quantum equilibrium hides the foliation) makes the refutation unobservable *in principle* in equilibrium. The view then cannot be refuted but also cannot exclude that the actual world has a shared present: both are empirically equivalent, and the choice is by theoretical virtue (simplicity, Lorentz-invariance of the fundamental law). Valentini's argument turns the view's R8 ("a shared present costs locality or structure") into "the structure is a price worth paying to get determinism and an objective measurement-free ontology".
(e) *Gluing across entanglement.* Even setting causation aside, the Stein-GR gluing is a statement about relations on the *manifold*. A state-dependent becoming (reality from b depends on which entangled state is prepared) is excluded by the rule being "one rule for every spacetime" independent of matter content. Bell correlations are state-dependent. The view, to be compatible, needs state-independence of which events are real from b: fine, but this forces the view to treat Bell correlation as *no evidence about reality-from-b*, which is the same as saying that quantum nonlocality is irrelevant to ontology, a substantive claim.

**Already answered?** O6 and §8(2): acknowledged, with Dieks as a route (a quantum-perspectival, no-collapse reading). But (a) in the strong form (P6 ⇒ universal becoming under Bell) is conceded, not answered, and (b)-(e) are not in the document. Dieks does not help against (c) in the collapse case, and Dieks also does not help if one reads P6 causally for Everettian branching.

**Severity.** (a): **serious** (possibly fatal for *this* world; it depends on unresolved foundations). The document handles it candidly, but it is the single most empirically exposed premise. (b)-(c): serious. (d): manageable (it is a standoff, which the view accepts under O3). (e): manageable.

**Best reply.** Replace P6's "causally influence" by an explicit restriction to *signalling* (light-signal) influence, which is what Clifton–Hogarth's argument for J^- uses, and argue that nonlocal dependence in Bell experiments is not signalling and so does not trigger P6. Cost: P6 is weaker, and the Clifton–Hogarth spacelike-causation argument for universal becoming is then not answered, only declined: they said spacelike causation forces universal becoming; the view would say Bell dependence is not causation. That is a real concession: the view becomes a package "P6 for signalling influence + a no-causation reading of Bell correlations" (Dieks/Everett/relational readings). Also note that in the presence of a physically real nonlocal collapse the theory predicting that is rGRWf: the view should state it as a refutation condition tied to that theory (and Bohm), not just to "a preferred foliation".

**Does P3/P3w beg the question against preferred-foliation views? (physicist's answer, combined with the question list.)** Not in the strict logical sense, but it fixes the category. The theorem is a statement about functors on GH (spacetimes without foliation). A foliation theorist can reply that the fundamental objects are triples (M, g, 𝓕) with morphisms preserving 𝓕; becoming is then locally covariant *in that category*, the theorem does not apply (main.tex says this, "the category... has changed") and one recovers the shared present (the leaves) with a rule that is local relative to the extra structure. Hence P3 is not an independent argument against foliations; it is a *conditional* ("if the fundamental kinematics is Loc"). What the view can say, as a physicist, is that (1) every precision test supports Lorentz invariance of the effective laws and generic Lorentz-violating foliation structure in QFT suffers fine-tuning (Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia, Vucetich, *PRL* 93 (2004) 191301; from memory) which counts against *fundamental* preferred foliations that are not hidden by equilibrium; (2) the foliation is dynamically idle in GR itself unless one adds Hořava-type or aether-type dynamics. These are theoretical-virtue arguments, not derivations. The claim "locality is independently motivated" (O3) is therefore true only as motivation within the LC-category; the neutral statement is: P3 is the *conservative* premise, and R8 describes the price of rejecting it. I would call that adequate for a conditional thesis, not for the "resolves the divide" language (§4).

**Prior anticipation.** Clifton–Hogarth's 1995 argument itself anticipates the Bell-conditional (spacelike causation forces universal becoming). The Bell-vs-relativistic-becoming dialectic is also treated by Maudlin (2011) and Myrvold (2002). Tumulka's rGRWf is the nearest physical ontology of spacetime points.

**Sources.** Bell, *Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics* (1987; 2nd edn 2004) and "La nouvelle cuisine" (1990) (from memory). Maudlin 2011 (above). Tumulka 2006 and Myrvold 2002 as above (verify). Dürr et al. 2014 as above (verify). Valentini 2008 as above (verify). Summers and Werner, "Bell's inequalities and quantum field theory", *J. Math. Phys.* 28 (1987) (verify). Dieks 2025 (read in full by the author, per PRIOR_WORK). Read depth: from-memory.

## 6. Problem of time, timelessness, emergent spacetime

**Steelman.** In canonical quantum gravity the Hamiltonian constraint (Wheeler–DeWitt equation) leaves the state of the universe annihilated by the Hamiltonian: no time evolution at the fundamental level (the "frozen formalism"; Kuchař 1992, Isham 1993 surveys). Barbour (*The End of Time*, 1999) argues that time does not exist; reality is the set of "Nows" (configurations of the universe in configuration space, "Platonia"), and the appearance of passage is explained by time capsules (configurations containing records). Huggett and Wüthrich (2013, "Emergent spacetime and empirical (in)coherence", *SHPMP* 44; and *Out of Nowhere*, OUP 2025; verify) argue that in several QG approaches spacetime is not fundamental, and that temporal notions (including local causal order) must emerge. Wüthrich (2010, "No presentism in quantum gravity", a paper in the repo) concludes presentist structure is lost.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view's inputs are not fundamental.* The theorem is about GH Lorentzian manifolds with causal structure. If spacetime is emergent, then the standpoints (events), the fragments (causal pasts), the "invariant facts" (P5, causal geometry) and "becoming" are all emergent: the view is a thesis about an effective regime, not about reality. A strict reading of the "reality" language (R1, "Reality is fragmented by standpoints") claims too much.
(b) *Timelessness gives eternalism, or something else, at the fundamental level.* In WDW or Barbour-type frameworks, a "Now" is a configuration of the *whole*, i.e., exactly the global "present" the view denies; Barbour's picture is a shared-instant ontology whose "past" is an internal record structure, not a causal past. So the quantum-gravity alternative to relativistic becoming is not the view; it is an even more radical eternalism with *global* configurations.
(c) *Indefinite causal order.* Quantum-gravity-motivated frameworks (Hardy's causaloid, Oreshkov–Costa–Brukner process matrices, 2012; superposed causal orders; from memory) make J^-(b) ill-defined. The theorem has no analogue; fragments are undefined for events in a superposition of causal orders. This is a failure mode the view does not list among §8's "neck" items.
(d) *Hole argument and event individuation.* In GR points of M are not physically individuated except through field values; "event b" as a standpoint is gauge-dependent unless defined by dynamical coincidence. Manageable (the theorem is covariant, with morphisms defined as isometries, which is the hole-argument-safe formulation), but the metaphysical reading "each event has its own reality" needs a statement of how the events are individuated; and Earman–Norton substantivalism debates apply.

(b) misfire as an attack on the view and a threat to *all* relativistic becoming theories. (c) manageable but should be added to §8 as a "neck". (d) manageable. ## 7. Gödel and Yourgrau

**Steelman.** Gödel (1949, in Schilpp, *Albert Einstein: Philosopher-Scientist*) argues: time in relativity has no objective lapse, because an objective lapse would require a system of "nows" (a layered reality that comes into existence successively) and that requires an absolute time; and even if our universe has a cosmic time, the rotating Gödel solutions (with closed timelike curves) are equally *possible* solutions of the field equations and in them no absolute time exists. Since whether time passes should not depend on "the particular way in which matter and its motion are arranged in the world" (the phrase quoted in O4), there is no lapse in ours either. Yourgrau (*A World Without Time*, 2005) presents this as Gödel's "proof" of the ideality of time and the starting point of his larger view. The modal premise is: the metaphysical nature of time is the same across all nomologically possible worlds.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The view relies on the very modal premise it needs to restrict.* O4 justifies the off-shell uniformity premise by saying it is a "local cousin" of Gödel's modal premise. But R7 says Gödel universes are *isolated*: the rule may differ between totally vicious spacetimes and causal ones. That is precisely to *deny* Gödel's premise that the same account of time applies across possible worlds: in vicious spacetimes becoming is universal and fragmentation collapses; in causal ones it is J^-. Having it both ways: uniform across all GH spacetimes (to get the theorem), non-uniform with respect to Gödel-type spacetimes (to avoid the argument). A defender of Gödel's argument says the restriction is ad hoc: the nonlocal modal uniformity that justifies the GH-wide rule also covers the vicious ones.
(b) *Whether time passes depends on global causal structure.* In a universe containing a vicious region elsewhere (non-GH), a Gödelian says the metaphysical status of time here would depend on matter there. The view accepts this (by R7, and §8(4)) but then the local content cannot be "metaphysically basic": that fragments are J^- depends on the absence of CTCs, a global matter-arrangement fact (Gödel's premise exactly).
(c) *The "local cousin" is not evidently weaker.* Gödel's premise is about what time is across worlds; the off-shell premise says becoming is defined for geometries that are not worlds at all (no field equation). That is *stronger* in one respect: it includes impossible spacetimes.
(d) *Yourgrau's point inverts:* if in Gödel universes everything becomes for everything, the view says in them time is block-like; so the existence of such a world means "time passes" is world-contingent, which for Gödel/Yourgrau is the reductio of any tensed ontology.

**Already answered?** O4 and R7 address (a)-(b) formally: Proposition 20 shows no morphism connects the two classes. They do not answer the *philosophical* premise: the response "the view needs only the local version" is a weaker commitment, which is an argument that the view is not refuted by Gödel, not that Gödel's premise is false. (c) is only partly addressed by the "open pocket problem" statement.

**Severity.** (a): manageable. The view could simply say: Gödel's modal premise is rejected and only the local rule is used. But it should not claim it as support. (b): manageable (accepted as a cost). (c)-(d): manageable.

**Best reply.** Rewrite O4 to say: the off-shell premise is not Gödel's premise and the view does not appeal to it; Gödel's argument targets layered-"now" ontologies, which the view lacks (it has no system of nows to be undone), and Gödel universes are accepted as a case where the local rule gives the block. Note also that causal (non-vicious) rotating solutions exist (from memory, e.g. Bianchi-type models; verify) and the Gödel premise would equally lead there, but the J^- rule is still well-defined in them. Cost: lose the "local cousin of Gödel" motivation.

**Prior anticipation.** Gödel himself: his "layers of now" is the target, and the idea that lapse requires a family of nows is the very idea the view rejects. Earman (*Bangs, Crunches, Whimpers and Shrieks*, OUP 1995, on Gödel) and Savitt ("The replacement of time", *Australasian J. Phil.* 1994, from memory) argued that Gödel's argument depends on a global-time conception of lapse. None anticipates J^-.

**Sources.** Gödel, "A remark about the relationship between relativity theory and idealistic philosophy" (1949), in Schilpp (ed.), *Albert Einstein: Philosopher-Scientist*. Yourgrau, *A World Without Time*, Basic Books 2005. Earman 1995 and Savitt 1994 (from memory; verify). Read depth: from-memory (O4's quoted phrase is from the target document, not checked against Gödel).

## 8. Thermodynamic arrow and the Past Hypothesis (Albert, Loewer)

**Steelman.** Albert (*Time and Chance*, 2000) and Loewer (e.g. "Two accounts of laws and time", *Phil. Studies* 2012; "Counterfactuals and the second law" / the "Mentaculus", from memory) hold that the temporal asymmetries of our world (records, causation-by-intervention, the second law, the sense of open future) are all underwritten by the Past Hypothesis (a very low entropy macrostate at the "beginning") plus a statistical postulate, in a world whose fundamental dynamics is time-symmetric. The direction of time is the entropy gradient; there is no further fundamental arrow or passage. The Humean-Mentaculus picture is a block with a low-entropy boundary condition.

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *ARROW: the view needs a direction and does not derive it.* The category GH has time-oriented objects; all results are stated for a given choice of orientation. The time-reversed theory with J^+ in place of J^- is exactly isomorphic. So R3 ("becoming is real and directed") is not derived from the physics but from the choice of which lobe of the cone is "past". On the Albert-Loewer view the choice is fixed by the thermodynamic arrow, a *global*, boundary-condition-dependent fact (Past Hypothesis), and so the direction on which the view's "growth" depends is fixed non-locally, contrary to the view's own P3 spirit. A local region of spacetime does not carry an intrinsic orientation if the arrow is statistical-global.
(b) *The Humean/Mentaculus block already gets the content.* If direction is the entropy gradient, then "the past is real from b, the future is not" is, for Albert–Loewer, an epistemic/record asymmetry plus a statistical asymmetry in intervention. The tense-ontological gloss adds nothing physical and the view faces O2 with the arrow supplying an anti-realist deflation.
(c) *Locality is not the source of asymmetry.* P3w's rationale (D(Σ) fixed by data on Σ) is time-symmetric (see §1(b)); the only time-asymmetry in the whole derivation is (CB). The theorem tells us that *if* the chronological past has become then nothing beyond J^- has; it doesn't say why the past rather than the future.
(d) *Where the arrow is actually relevant:* if there are regions of a universe with opposite entropy gradients (e.g. a Boltzmann-style or Carroll–Chen cosmology; from memory), the orientation reverses, but Past Hypothesis-type accounts make the two orientations equally good *locally*, so the global orientation of the category GH would not be a local fact: local covariance with *time-orientation-preserving* morphisms presupposes a globally coherent orientation. That is a hidden global premise, not merely a convention.

**Already answered?** Not directly. §8 of STRONGEST_VIEW does not list the arrow. R3's "directed" and the table line "Becoming is real and directed (growing block)" claim more than the proofs deliver. main.tex follows Clifton–Hogarth by taking (CB) as given.

**Severity.** (a), (c): manageable but must be fixed in wording: "directed" is an *input*. (b): serious in so far as it is O2 with a deflationary alternative. (d): manageable, exotic.

**Best reply.** State explicitly that direction is a posited primitive (as Maudlin has it) or that it is borrowed from any account of the arrow (Albert's included), and that the view's content is *which sets* are real from b given a direction, not *why* there is a direction. Remove "directed" from the list of vindications or footnote it. This respects the AGENTS.md instruction not to conflate the thermodynamic arrow, causal order and passage. Cost: smaller claim; no loss of results.

**Prior anticipation.** Reichenbach/Grünbaum-type "causal theory of time" accounts hold that time order is derived from causal order and direction from entropy; the view inherits their structure. No anticipation of J^- fragments.

**Sources.** Albert, *Time and Chance*, Harvard UP 2000. Loewer, "Two accounts of laws and time", *Philosophical Studies* 160 (2012) (verify). Read depth: from-memory.

## 9. Malament/Zeeman causal reconstruction and conventionality of simultaneity

**Steelman.** Zeeman (1964, "Causality implies the Lorentz group", *J. Math. Phys.* 5) and Hawking–King–McCarthy (1976) and Malament (1977, "The class of continuous timelike curves determines the topology of spacetime", *J. Math. Phys.* 18) show that the causal order determines much of the spacetime structure (the conformal structure; with volume, the metric). Malament's other 1977 paper ("Causal theories of time and the conventionality of simultaneity", *Noûs* 11) shows that, given an inertial worldline O, the only non-trivial equivalence relation definable from the causal structure and invariant under the automorphisms fixing O is standard (Einstein) simultaneity, a result usually read as against Reichenbach's conventionalism. Sarkar and Stachel (1999, *Philosophy of Science* 66) argue it does not establish non-conventionality because its definability and non-triviality premises are not neutral (from memory; verify the argument).

**Strongest attacks.**
(a) *The Stein-GR theorem is a Malament-type theorem and inherits its limits.* Malament's lesson is "from causal structure alone, nothing but Einstein simultaneity (relative to O) is definable". The conventionalist's reply is that there is more to the world than what is definable from that structure. The Stein-GR theorem has the same shape: from *local, covariant* structure, nothing but J^-/I^- is definable. A preferred-foliation theorist answers exactly as the conventionalist does: add structure. This is the §5 point; but the Malament comparison sharpens it: the theorem is a *no-go for definability from restricted resources*, not an argument that the resources are all there is.
(b) *Worldline-relative presents.* Malament's relation is an equivalence relating *spacelike* events, defined relative to a worldline. In Minkowski spacetime it is local in the sense of lying inside the diamond spanned by the worldline segment and the event (the construction uses a signal sent and received). A worldline-based present (Einstein simultaneity slice of O at proper time t) is, so far as I can see, a natural, causally-defined candidate for "the present for (O, t)" that is *not* a single event. The Stein-GR paper handles worldline-relative becoming by its corollary on worldline-dependent becoming (Corollary on worldline in main.tex, which I did not check in detail). The attack is that the standpoint "O at t" gives a perfectly local present; the view's R2 "from b, b alone is present" is then an artefact of taking events as standpoints and requiring transitivity across standpoints (O5: transitivity). Whether the Einstein slice can coexist with (T) and (CB) is exactly Stein's dilemma, so the view must say why the *standpoint* should be an event rather than a worldline-at-a-time.
(c) *Conventionality at the cone.* Reichenbach's ε ∈ (0,1) excludes the endpoints ε = 0, 1 (which would place the distant event on the observer's own light cone). The view's choice between J^- and I^- (R4) is the choice about the endpoint, so the view's fragment boundary coincides with the degenerate "simultaneity" convention. This is worth stating: the boundary of F(b) is what conventionalists treat as the limiting convention, and this is where the theorem leaves freedom (R4). Fine, but it says the cone is the one place where conventionality, not physics, might be thought to decide.
(d) *Conformal invariance — CLOCK.* The causal order determines only the conformal class. Fragment structure is blind to proper time, duration, rates. The view's "passage" has no rate. This is correct as a thesis about becoming but must not be sold as an account of *time passing* in the sense of duration; it is silent on CLOCK.
(e) *Support.* On the other side, the reconstruction theorems *support* P5: the invariant content really is the causal geometry. But they also make O2 sharper, since all structure the fragments carry is already definable from the causal order: fragments are definable from J^-.

**Already answered?** O10 and P5 treat the invariant causal geometry; no mention of Malament or conventionality in STRONGEST_VIEW. (a) is partially covered by "R8: shared present costs locality or structure". (b) is not addressed with respect to Einstein simultaneity; (c), (d) not at all.

**Severity.** (a): manageable (the view already concedes that adding structure escapes the theorem). (b): serious as a framing question (why events and not worldline-instants) but answered formally by the paper's worldline corollary (verify that it covers Einstein slices). (c): manageable. (d): manageable (a clarification).

**Best reply.** Add a short paragraph: the theorem is a no-go for *definable local* presents; it is the becoming analogue of Malament's theorem and has the same status: it excludes presents definable from causal structure and local data, not presents given by added structure. Cost: concede openly that the argument against shared presents is conditional on the category.

**Prior anticipation.** Stein (1968, 1991) and Clifton–Hogarth (1995) are the direct ancestors; Malament's definability theorem is the other half of that tradition (a shared-present-relative-to-an-observer theorem), already in the literature. Putnam (1967) and Rietdijk (1966) are the earlier arguments; none gives GR extension.

**Sources.** Zeeman 1964; Malament 1977a (*J. Math. Phys.* 18) and 1977b (*Noûs* 11); Sarkar and Stachel 1999, *Philosophy of Science* 66; Hawking, King and McCarthy 1976, *J. Math. Phys.* 17 (all from memory; verify). Read depth: from-memory.

## 10. QFT: the timelike tube theorem as an argument for I^- over J^-

**Steelman.** Borchers (1961, *Il Nuovo Cimento* 19; verify) proved that in a Wightman QFT in Minkowski space, the algebra (field operators) of an arbitrarily thin open neighbourhood of a timelike curve segment generates the algebra of its *timelike envelope*: the region swept out by deforming the tube through timelike curves. For a segment from a to b the envelope contains the open causal diamond I^+(a) ∩ I^-(b). Strohmaier and Witten ("The timelike tube theorem in curved spacetime", arXiv:2303.16380, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 405 (2024); confirmed by web search this session as to venue and volume) extend the theorem to real-analytic spacetimes under analyticity assumptions on the states (companion paper "Analytic states in quantum field theory on curved spacetimes", arXiv:2302.02709; per the search result). Witten's "Algebras, regions, and observers" (2023) uses it to argue that an observer's worldline algebra is that of a diamond.

**Does it select I^- over J^-?**
Arguments for:
(i) The envelope is defined with *timelike* curves and open sets, so what a worldline "determines" algebraically is an open region; for a worldline ending at b, taking the union over earlier points a gives I^-(b), not J^-(b). The null cone itself carries no algebra of its own in this construction.
(ii) Null sets are measure-zero; algebras are assigned to open regions; the closed J^-(b) would need an outer-regularity convention. So a QFT-motivated "has become for b" would be naturally open.

Arguments against (more weight, in my view):
(iii) *Bridge.* The theorem is about algebraic determination (analytic continuation of operators from a tube), not about reality or becoming. To use it one needs a new bridge premise: "x is real from b iff operators at x are algebraically determined by the algebra of a neighbourhood of a worldline through b". This is a different, *epistemic/algebraic* notion with no argued link to P2.
(iv) *It is time-symmetric.* The envelope of a tube is symmetric in time: the diamond contains the points just as determined by the tube's earlier and later parts. It cannot give a J^- vs J^+ asymmetry and cannot give "future not real from b"; for a tube ending at b the envelope lies in I^-(b) only because the tube ends at b. The theorem is thus no source of the temporal asymmetry.
(v) *Analyticity.* The theorem needs real-analytic spacetimes and states; its content is "unique continuation", a strong rigidity not available for generic smooth spacetimes, and is in tension with P3 as a statement about *any* convex region: if the algebra of a tube determines far more than the tube, QFT locality in the sense of independence of subregions is subtler than the view's use of LCQFT suggests. (Haag–Kastler independence of spacelike regions remains; but timelike tubes generate large regions, which is a form of *timelike* nonlocality of algebras.)
(vi) *Against I^-: the singular support of massless propagators sits on the cone.* In 4D Minkowski, the massless commutator function is supported exactly on the light cone, and in curved spacetime the singular support of the Hadamard parametrix lies on the cone. So the physical *propagation* of influence for massless fields is along null rays: this supports P6 (light signals influence) and hence J^- over I^-. The tube theorem favours open timelike regions; the propagator favours the closed cone. Net: QFT does not decide, and each side supports one of the view's two "independent" selectors (P6 versus openness).
(vii) R4's claim "closed gives J^-, open gives I^-" is a topological regularity property of the relation B, not of the algebra net. Matching algebraic openness (a property of the net) to B being open is a category mistake absent a bridge.

**Already answered?** Not mentioned. R4's two selectors (P6 causal, P6′ topological) are the relevant internal ones; the tube theorem could count as a third, favouring I^-, which would actually *disagree* with them. The view's "two independent principles agree" (R4) would then face a third that disagrees.

**Severity.** As an argument for I^-: manageable-to-misfire (bridge missing, time-symmetric, needs analyticity, countered by propagator support). As a threat to R4's "principled J^-": manageable. As a source of a worry on P3: manageable.

**Best reply.** Record it: the tube theorem shows that QFT regional algebras are generated through timelike deformations, so a QFT-based rule would be open (I^-); P6 reads influence off the singular support of propagators (closed, J^-). Both are physical, so "the choice of J^- is principled" should be softened to "J^- is selected by P6 or P6′, I^- by a QFT-algebraic reading, and the theorem classifies the freedom". Cost: R4's "no remaining parameter" becomes "one parameter fixed by P6 or P6′".

**Prior anticipation.** None found for becoming. (Clifton–Hogarth debated causal vs chronological becoming on non-QFT grounds.)

**Sources.** Borchers 1961 (from memory; verify). Strohmaier and Witten, arXiv:2303.16380 and *Commun. Math. Phys.* 405 (2024) (venue confirmed by web search, abstract level, not full text). Strohmaier and Witten, arXiv:2302.02709 (as reported by search). Witten, "Algebras, regions, and observers" (2023; from memory; verify). Read depth: discovery (search snippets) plus from-memory.

## 11. Assessment: does local covariance (P3/P3w) beg the question against preferred-foliation views?

Short answer as a physicist: **not circular, but conditional and not independent of the choice of fundamental kinematics.**
1. The theorem is a statement about functors on GH. A foliated-spacetime category makes the theorem inapplicable (main.tex says so). So P3 is not neutral between "fundamental structure is Loc" and "fundamental structure is Loc plus foliation". It is neutral in *form* (anyone can state locality relative to their own structure) but the *theorem* needs the Loc version.
2. The independent motivation (O3) is real in two ways: effective Lorentz invariance is empirically extremely well tested, and fundamental, non-hidden foliations are expected to leak into low-energy physics (the fine-tuning argument of Collins et al. 2004, from memory). But a foliation hidden by quantum equilibrium (Valentini) or a foliation fixed dynamically by ψ (Dürr et al.) is designed to evade that. So the motivation is a theoretical-virtue argument, not a derivation.
3. P3w's rationale (D(Σ) as the region whose physics is fixed by data) is Cauchy-deterministic and time-symmetric; it is *dynamical*, whereas its use in the theorem is *kinematic* (off-shell). See §12.
4. The truth-in-advertising point: "locality, a shared extended present, and non-trivial becoming cannot all hold" is a correct trilemma; what is not shown is that locality is the horn to keep. The §4 diagnosis ("only the shared present was taken away by relativity") is persuasive only if one has already counted Bell nonlocality as non-ontological (§5).

Severity: **serious** as a limit on what is claimed ("resolves the divide"), **manageable** as an objection to the theorem. Best reply: describe the thesis as conditional ("if fundamental kinematics is locally covariant"), and present the foliation view as the main rival, with the Bell/rGRWf/Bohm discussion as the empirical interface.

## 12. Assessment: is the off-shell premise (Remark "off-shell", O4) acceptable?

As a physicist: **acceptable for a kinematic thesis, unsatisfactory for the physicalist rhetoric attached to it, and the weakest technical premise in the package.**
1. *Why it is natural in LCQFT.* Brunetti–Fredenhagen–Verch's category Loc includes all GH metrics because QFT on curved background is a theory of test fields on arbitrary backgrounds: the metric is a non-dynamical parameter, so no field equation constrains it. That justification does not transfer to GR, where the metric is the dynamical variable and satisfies field equations with matter.
2. *The proof uses unphysical auxiliaries.* The conclusion for a physical spacetime M comes from morphisms into auxiliary spacetimes M' that violate energy conditions. If becoming is defined only on solutions (on-shell), the equivalence in (LC) gives no constraint through M'. Section "Energy conditions" of main.tex says this explicitly (the transfer reduces to an open gluing problem). So R1, R9 and the "fully determined" claim of R4 are proved for the off-shell theory; for the on-shell theory they are open. STRONGEST_VIEW lists this as OPEN but keeps R1/R9 headlined as PROVED.
3. *Tension within the view.* P3w is defended as "facts about what has happened supervene on the physics of any region that determines the two events". "Physics" of a region means a solution of the dynamical equations; for off-shell metrics there is no physics to supervene on. So the justification offered for P3w is on-shell and dynamical, while the proof requires the off-shell kinematic version. This is a real inconsistency of motivation, not just a gap.
4. *Matter content.* The rule is a function of geometry only (not matter fields), a further modelling choice. It matches Gödel's "independent of matter arrangement" but is not itself obvious; a tense-realist could let becoming depend on matter (e.g. on the presence of a clock-like field or of a preferred reference field).
5. *The Gödel analogy (O4).* See §7: the analogy cuts the other way (R7 denies Gödel's uniformity), and I would remove it.
6. *Possible escape.* The standard physicist's move is: the becoming rule is a property of the *kinematical* arena (conformal-causal structure), the same way the light cone is; the field equations never enter. That is coherent: it defends the off-shell premise as part of "what becoming is" (structure-first), not as a consequence of physics. The cost is that then the claim is not "physics fixes fragments" but "relativistic kinematics fixes fragments, if becoming is purely kinematic".

Severity: **serious** for the PROVED labels on R1/R9/R4 as applied to *our* physics; **manageable** if re-labelled "PROVED for the kinematic theory; on-shell OPEN" in the headline text (not only in §6's table).

## Top threats from this group

1. **Bell nonlocality against P6 (§5(a)-(b)).** If dependence of b's outcome on spacelike-separated events counts as causal influence, P6 and transitivity give universal becoming, i.e. eternalism for this world. The physical motivation for P3 (LCQFT, Einstein causality) is silent on Bell correlations, and rGRWf shows an event ontology of exactly the view's kind with a Bell-nonlocal law and no foliation. The view hands this to an open foundations question and offers Dieks as a possibility.
2. **The off-shell premise (§12) combined with the conditional status of locality (§11).** R1/R9/R4 are proved only for the kinematic, off-shell theory, while P3w's rationale is dynamical and time-symmetric; and the theorem does not engage foliation views, which change the category. The headline "resolves the divide" outruns "a conditional classification theorem".
3. **Idle indexing: the O2/Maudlin/Albert pincer (§4, §8).** Maudlin says passage is the intrinsic directedness already in the time-orientation, so relativizing to standpoints adds nothing; Albert–Loewer say the direction itself is a global thermodynamic matter. Either way, the proved content is tenseless causal order with an input orientation, and "becoming is real and directed" is not derived. (Honorable mention: the unread Earman 2008 paper might contain a precursor; and Dowker/Sorkin's "stem" in causal-set growth is a close physical anticipation.)

**Single repair that would do the most good.** State the thesis explicitly as a *conditional classification* in three clauses: (i) locally covariant kinematics (no added foliation, off-shell for the kinematic theory); (ii) a given primitive time orientation; (iii) a restriction of P6 to signalling influence together with a stated reading of Bell correlations as non-causal (or a stated refutation condition tied to Bohm/rGRWf). Then list the physical neck items accordingly, and re-label R1/R9/R4 "PROVED, off-shell" in the headline text. This costs the "resolution" rhetoric and the Gödel and "physicalist cannot reject P3w" gloss, and buys defensibility against every attack in this group.
