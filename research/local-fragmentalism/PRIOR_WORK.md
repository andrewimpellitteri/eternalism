## Verdict (updated after Fine 2006, Lipman, Dieks 2025)

- Fragmentalism about tense: Fine's (frame-based). Not new.
- Point-relative, causal-past becoming: Stein. Not new in Minkowski.
- Event-indexed standpoints with nonstandard realism: Fine 2006 (p. 411–412).
  Not new. Single-event fragments: noted by Lipman (n. 1), after Stein; Fine
  2005 argues against them.
- Apparently new in the sources checked (narrower than first claimed): the
  *content* of an event's fragment, fixed as its causal past (up to the null
  boundary) by a locality theorem across spacetimes (the GR Stein theorem);
  the resulting answer to Fine's space/time-indexical objection to
  single-event fragments; the trilemma; the totality having no standpoint; the
  reply to Hofweber–Lange.
- Fine 2005 read: event standpoints considered and rejected on space/time
  asymmetry grounds; frame fragments preferred. The view's distinctive reply is
  general-relativistic (no global frames), see STRONGEST_VIEW O11.
- Confirmed (Stapp 1977, full text): Whitehead, as Stapp reads him, already
  makes an event's actual world its backward light cone and suggests relative
  existence. Read since: Čapek 1975 (no derivation), Earman 2008 (explicitly says the
  Clifton–Hogarth results no longer apply in GR and does not attempt
  definability), Belnap 1992 and Goldblatt 1980 (no anticipation). Still
  unread: Whitehead's own text, Čapek 1961, Lipman 2026, Iaquinto–Torrengo
  ch. 4. Until these
  are read, the claim "apparently new" covers only the derivation of the
  fragment's content by a locality theorem, not the content itself.
- Still unread: Lipman 2026 chs. 4 ("A Fragmentalist Theory of Time") and 6
  ("Spacetime and the Lessons of Relativistic Physics"); Iaquinto & Torrengo
  2022 ch. 4 "Relativity" (request-a-copy via the UniUPO repository).
