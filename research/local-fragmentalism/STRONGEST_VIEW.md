# Local fragmentalism: the strongest version

25 October 2026 (revised after the multi-school red team, `redteam/SYNTHESIS.md`).
Position statement. Not reviewed by a philosopher. Claims are tagged:

- **[PROVED]** a theorem in `research/stein-gr/main.tex` (four proof reviews
  and a final read, all repairs applied; still needs an expert);
- **[PROVED here]** a short argument given in this document;
- **[CONDITIONAL]** follows from proved results plus a stated premise;
- **[THESIS]** a philosophical claim argued, not proved;
- **[OPEN]** not established.

**What changed after the red team.** No group found an error in the theorem;
every group found the bridge to the metaphysics overstated. This version
therefore (i) states the view as a thesis about grounding and concedes
truth-conditional and empirical equivalence with eternalism; (ii) replaces the
old invariance principle P5, which was incoherent as stated; (iii) restricts
causal sufficiency to signalling and names Bell-nonlocal dynamics as a
falsifier; (iv) demotes extended standpoints (P7) to an optional add-on; (v)
drops "resolves the divide", "uniqueness" and "directed becoming"; (vi) adds a
scope statement, a settledness result (R10), and replies to Rakić, to
persistence, and to "why every event?".

**Priority status** (see PRIOR_WORK.md). The standpoint frame, including event
standpoints, is Fine's (2006, pp. 411–412). Rakić (1997, read in full) already
has an event-relative, transitive "realized with respect to" relation; she
argues it is not definable from Minkowski causal geometry and adds it as a
primitive. Whitehead reportedly makes an occasion's "actual world" its
backward light cone (via Stapp 1977; secondary, unverified). Branching
space-times and causal-set "becoming" (Dowker, Sorkin) have related
structures. Čapek (1975, read) already holds that an event's present is
itself alone and that its absolute future need not be postulated, on
epistemic grounds. Earman (2008, read §§5–7) builds worldline becoming from
causal pasts and states that "once the symmetries of Minkowski spacetime are
lost, the Clifton-Hogarth results on the unique definability of the Becoming
relation no longer apply" (p. 151); he does not attempt a GR result. Still
unread: Whitehead's own text, Lipman 2026 chs. 4 and 6, Iaquinto & Torrengo
2022 ch. 4. So the content "an event's reality is its
causal past" may well not be new. What no source checked so far contains is
the *derivation* of that content from a locality principle across curved
spacetimes (§3), and the replies below that depend on it.

## 1. Thesis

**Core thesis.** What has happened is relative to a standpoint, and the
standpoints are events. If what has happened by b is (i) transitive, (ii)
inclusive of b's chronological past, (iii) a local matter, the same rule in
every spacetime, and (iv) not everything, then what has happened by b is b's
causal past, undecided at most at the light cone. With causal sufficiency it
is exactly b's causal past. Hence no two events share a present.
[CONDITIONAL on (i)–(iv) and the off-shell premise]

**The metaphysical claim is about grounding.** The view and eternalism agree
on every tenseless truth and every observation. They disagree about what is
fundamental. For the eternalist, the causal order of a single block is basic,
and "what has happened by b" is derived by restricting a quantifier to b's
past. For local fragmentalism, reality-from-a-standpoint is basic, and the
tenseless causal geometry is the agreement among standpoints (P5). [THESIS]
Whether such fundamentality disputes are substantive is itself contested;
see O2. The theorem matters on either answer: it fixes what the fragments
contain, which the eternalist must also accept as the content of "what has
happened by b".

**Scope.** The view is about temporal ontology and becoming as a structural
matter. "Standpoint", "present" and "past" are positions in causal structure.
It takes no stand on lived time and the specious present, on the
thermodynamic arrow, on duration, or on whether the future is open (though R10
says something precise about settledness). It takes the time orientation as
given rather than explaining it.

## 2. The view: principles

**P1. Standpoints.** For each event b there is a fragment F(b): what is real,
what has happened, as things stand from b. [THESIS] There are two readings.
- *Relativist core (official):* facts obtain relative to standpoints, all
  standpoints are on a par, and facts from different standpoints are jointly
  consistent. This is Fine's external relativism with events as standpoints.
- *Fragmentalist strengthening (optional):* facts from different fragments
  need not cohere (Fine's non-standard realism).
Every result below holds on the relativist core. The strengthening adds
nothing the theorem needs and invites Hofweber–Lange-type objections (O10), so
the official view does not adopt it.

**P2. Becoming is reality-from-a-standpoint.** "x has become for b" means
"x is real in F(b)". This relation is
- transitive: what is real for something real for b is real for b;
- chronologically inclusive: what lies in b's chronological past is real for
  b (Clifton and Hogarth derive this from worldline becoming and worldline
  transitivity).
[THESIS]

Why transitivity is not an extra assumption: read "x is real from b" as "x
has happened by b". If y has happened by b, and x had happened by y, then x
has happened by b; transitivity is part of what "having happened by" means.
Clifton and Hogarth's worldline transitivity says the same thing for
observers: two observers who meet agree on what has happened (pp. 362–363).

**P3. Locality.** Whether x is real from b's standpoint is settled by any
causally convex region containing x and b, by the same rule in every
spacetime. This is the "same theory in all spacetimes" principle of locally
covariant QFT and is akin to Viglione's Causal Connection Condition.
[THESIS, independently motivated]

What the theorem actually uses is weaker (checked against every appeal to
(LC) in the Stein paper): **(P3w)** one rule for every spacetime; invariance
under isometries; and, whenever a thin compact acausal slab Σ contains x and b
in its interior, the verdict on (x, b) depends only on int D(Σ) with its
geometry. D(Σ) is the region whose physics is fixed by data on Σ, so P3w says
that facts about what has happened supervene on the physics of any region
that determines the two events. Two caveats from the red team. First, P3w is
used *kinematically* (one rule for all geometries), while its motivation is
dynamical (D(Σ) is what Σ determines); the motivation supports the premise
but does not entail the off-shell form of it (O4). Second, P3w is exactly what
a defender of a canonical cosmic time denies (O3).

**P4. Non-triviality.** Not everything is real from every standpoint. If it
were, there would be no becoming, only the block. [THESIS]

**P5. Persistence and agreement** (replaces the old "invariance" principle,
which said the absolute facts are those holding from every standpoint; since
no event is real from every standpoint, that yielded no facts about events).
- *Persistence:* if b ≤ b', every fact that holds from b holds from b'. What
  has happened stays happened.
- *Agreement:* two standpoints agree on every tenseless fact about events real
  from both.
The tenseless causal geometry is then not a further fact "from nowhere": it
is the agreement of the standpoints, and each part of it holds from every
standpoint that contains its subject matter. [THESIS] (On the relativist core
of P1 agreement is automatic; on the fragmentalist strengthening it is a
substantive constraint.)

**P6. Causal sufficiency (signalling form).** Whatever can send a signal to b,
that is, whatever lies on a causal curve to b, is real from b. This is the
signalling form of Clifton and Hogarth's maxim: "if an event ... can causally
influence b, that event must have become for b. Otherwise, how could an
unsettled, unreal, event for b possibly contribute to bringing about an event
that is settled for b, namely, b itself?" (1995, p. 364). It gives
J^-(b) ⊆ F(b). [THESIS] The restriction to signalling matters: if
non-signalling Bell dependence counted as "contributing to bringing about b",
the maxim plus transitivity would make everything real from b (O6). The
restriction is therefore a substantive bet that the fundamental dynamics
contains no spacelike contribution to bringing events about, and it is
falsifiable (§8). (Clifton and Hogarth also record a competing intuition, that
null lines might be ontologically on a par, and suggest it may be the one to
give up, ibid. pp. 364–365. Local fragmentalism gives it up.)

**P6′. Limit closure (an independent route).** If events x_n converge to x and
each x_n is real from b, then x is real from b: each fragment is closed. This
pointwise closedness suffices: F(b) then contains the closure of I^-(b),
which is J^-(b) in globally hyperbolic spacetimes, so the argument of
Proposition 12 (Regularity) goes through. [THESIS]

**P7. Extended standpoints (optional add-on, not part of the official view).**
A region R may count as a standpoint, with F(R) = ⋃_{b∈R} F(b). The red team
showed two costs: region standpoints such as hypersurfaces readmit something
like a Fine-style shared present, and the standpoint of the whole makes the
eternalist block the theory's home (O2, O13). Results that need P7 are marked.

## 2b. The formal frame

Lipman's standpoint logic (*J. Phil.* 2023, read in full) works with models
⟨D, W, R, v⟩: W ⊆ D are the standpoints, "w1Rw2 represents the claim that,
from the standpoint of w1, w2 is a standpoint", R is reflexive, v(w) ⊆ D
"represents entities that exist from the perspective of w", and "if w1Rw2 then
w2 ∈ v(w1)". Local fragmentalism is the following class of such models, one
for every globally hyperbolic spacetime M:

- D = W = the events of M;
- v(b) = F(b), the events real from b;
- R(b, x) iff x ∈ v(b).

Lipman's constraint and reflexivity hold by construction (b ∈ F(b)). P2 adds
transitivity, so R is a preorder, and with antisymmetry in causal spacetimes
(R2) a partial order: the frame is an S4 frame (with F(b) = J^-(b), R is the
converse of the causal order). Goldblatt (1980, *Studia Logica*, abstract and
main theorem read) proved that the modal logic of the reflexive Minkowski
causal order is S4.2; by time reversal the same holds for "from every
standpoint real from here". So the logic of the frame is a known object; the
view adds which frame physics selects. P3 says the assignment M ↦ (W, R, v) is
locally covariant. The Stein-GR theorem is then a **classification of locally
covariant standpoint frames**: every non-universal such family has
I^-(b) ⊆ v(b) ⊆ J^-(b) in every spacetime, and with P6, v(b) = J^-(b).
[PROVED, given the identification; the identification is THESIS]

**Truth from a standpoint.** Write @b φ for "from b, φ". Past-tensed and
tenseless claims about events in F(b) are evaluated in F(b). A claim about an
event outside F(b) has no truth value from b, unless it is settled from b in
the sense of R10. Future-tensed claims from b are thus either settled (R10) or
without truth value from b; this is a Thomason-style gap, not a falsity. A
full object language with a completion semantics is still owed. [OPEN]

## 3. What follows

**R1. Fragments are causal pasts.** In every globally hyperbolic spacetime,
I^-(b) ⊆ F(b) ⊆ J^-(b). [PROVED: Theorems 7 and 10 (main theorem;
classification), given P2–P4 as the axioms (CB), (T), (LC) and
non-universality, plus the off-shell premise.]

**R2. There is no shared present.** Co-presence (each real for the other) is
trivial: from b, b alone is present. [PROVED: Corollary 8 of the Stein paper.]
This holds for co-presence understood as mutual reality, the sense in which
Putnam's and Stein's arguments are posed; it says nothing about other notions
of a present, such as a set of events none of which is real from another. It
echoes Stein's remark that the converse of Putnam's premise yields "a
peculiarly extreme (but pluralistic!) form of solipsism" (1968, p. 18), now in
every curved spacetime. It is not solipsism about reality: the whole causal
past is real from b.

**R3. Fragments grow along the time orientation.** If b ≤ b', then
F(b) ⊆ F(b'). [PROVED from R1.] The direction comes from the time orientation,
which (CB) uses; the theorem does not derive it. What the theorem adds is that,
given an orientation, the real region is the past lobe alone, not a
time-symmetric region.

**R4. The light cone is the only freedom.** Which lightlike-separated events
are real for b is not fixed; a rule may let curvature along the light ray
decide. Closed fragments give J^-(b), open give I^-(b). [PROVED: Theorems
10–11 and Proposition 12.] With P6, F(b) = J^-(b) exactly. P6′ gives the same
result by a topological route. [PROVED given P6 or P6′] Quantum field theory
does not obviously settle the choice: the timelike tube theorem suggests open
regions, massless propagation the closed cone (red team 04; unverified).

**R5. The discrete case is exact.** In causal set theory, fragments are
exactly causal pasts, with no freedom. [PROVED: Proposition 19.] Causal-set
theorists (Dowker, Sorkin) already treat becoming as the growth of causal
pasts; the result is a covariance theorem for their picture, not a new
picture.

**R6. No event standpoint sees a present for the universe.** Co-presence is
defined among events and is trivial (R2), so there is no event standpoint
from which the universe has a present. This turns the "shape problem" of the
Brading paper (`paper/main.tex`) from an objection into a prediction: nothing
fixes the universe's present, because there is none. [PROVED given R2]
*With P7:* the totality is a standpoint with F(M) = M; the view from the whole
is the block (R6′). [PROVED given P7]

**R7. In Gödel universes fragmentation collapses.** In totally vicious
spacetimes every fragment is everything, and the view coincides with
eternalism there. No morphism connects such universes with causal ones, in
either direction, so this does not reach ours. [PROVED: Proposition 20.]

**R8. A shared present costs locality.** Any view with a shared, extended
present (in the sense of R2) must violate P3, either by a rule that is
definable from the metric but non-local, such as cosmological time or a CMC
foliation (Proposition 5), or by adding global structure (Monton's move).
[PROVED for the definable rules; the second horn is THESIS.] The red team's
correction: in near-FLRW cosmologies the canonical foliation is definable from
the metric, so the cost there is non-locality, not *added* structure. That is
a real price only for someone who accepts P3.

**R9. Classification relative to the commitments.** Among views on which
(i) reality is relative to event standpoints, (ii) becoming is transitive and
includes the chronological past, (iii) the rule is local in the sense of P3w,
(iv) becoming is non-trivial, and (v) whatever can signal to b is real from b,
exactly one remains: F(b) = J^-(b). [PROVED given (i)–(v) and the off-shell
premise] This is a classification, not an argument for (i)–(v).

**R10. Settled is not the same as real.** Call x *settled from b* if every
past-inextendible causal curve from x meets F(b): then, under deterministic
local laws, data real from b fix what happens at x.
- In Minkowski spacetime, with F(b) = J^-(b), no event outside J^-(b) is
  settled from b. [PROVED here] Proof: put b at the origin and let
  x = (t, X) ∉ J^-(b). Take the past-directed null ray from x moving away from
  b, s ↦ (t − s, X + s n) with n = X/|X| (any unit n if X = 0). A point of it
  lies in J^-(b) iff |X| + s ≤ s − t, i.e. iff |X| ≤ −t, i.e. iff
  x ∈ J^-(b). So the ray never meets J^-(b).
- Settledness can extend beyond the fragment in other spacetimes. In the
  Einstein static universe R × S³ (unit radius), the past light cone of b
  reaches the antipode after time π, so J^-(b) contains every slice
  {t = c} with c ≤ t(b) − π. These are Cauchy surfaces, so every event is
  settled from b. [PROVED here]
- The general criterion is OPEN; containing a Cauchy surface is sufficient.
So "is the future open from b?" has a precise structural answer separate from
"is the future real from b?": the second is always no; the first depends on
the spacetime and the laws. This answers Rakić's sea-fight case (O14) and the
open-future objections (O17).

## 4. What the view explains, and what it concedes

| Intuition | What the view says | Concession |
|---|---|---|
| The present is special (presentism) | From each standpoint, it is the apex of what is real (R1, R2) | Only for presence as the apex of a fragment; not for lived presence |
| Becoming is real (growing block) | Fragments grow along worldlines (R3) | Direction is given by time orientation, not explained |
| No moment is privileged (eternalism) | No standpoint is privileged (P1) | — |
| Reality is the block (eternalism) | Every tenseless truth is accepted; the geometry is the standpoints' agreement (P5) | Truth-conditional and empirical equivalence with eternalism |
| No global now (relativity) | No shared present at all (R2), no foliation used | Conditional on locality; foliation views pay non-locality, not incoherence (R8) |
| The future is open | The future is never real from b; whether it is settled is a separate, precise question (R10) | Openness is not guaranteed |

**The trilemma.** If a shared present is mutual reality among distinct events,
then locality, a shared present and non-trivial becoming cannot all hold
[PROVED as R1/R2, given the off-shell premise]. Presentists who keep the shared
present must give up locality; eternalists give up becoming; local
fragmentalism gives up only the shared present. That is a classification of
the options, not a proof that the third is right.

## 5. Replies to objections

**O1. "Fragmentalism is incoherent or too costly."** The official view adopts
only the relativist core of P1, which avoids non-standard realism. Whether a
relativism deserves the name "fragmentalism" is terminological; the label is
kept because of Fine's framework.

**O2. "This is eternalism with tensed vocabulary"** (raised independently by
five red-team groups). The strongest form is the *translation argument*: every
claim of the view translates into tenseless causal-order facts plus a
quantifier restricted to J^-(b). If "real" means real simpliciter, the view is
eternalism with restricted quantifiers; if it means only "real from b", it is
an indexed semantics, a perspectival B-theory (Deng, Price, Ismael accept
R1–R3 and decline the gloss).

Reply. The translation is granted: the view concedes truth-conditional and
empirical equivalence. What remains is a claim about which side of the
translation is fundamental (§1). That is a substantive claim only if disputes
about fundamentality are substantive (Sider says yes; the deflationists, no).
So the view stands or falls with the A/B debate itself as a debate about
fundamental structure. If the deflationist is right, the dynamic side of the
debate has no content left to vindicate, and what survives of this project is
the theorem, which the eternalist must accept as the content of "what has
happened by b". If the deflationist is wrong, the view is a statable rival to
eternalism whose fragments are fixed by physics rather than chosen. Either
way the old phrase "resolves the divide" overclaimed; this version withdraws
it. [THESIS; the most important open question for the view]

**O3. "Locality begs the question against presentism."** Partly granted.
Locality is independently motivated (locally covariant QFT; Viglione; without
it nothing is constrained, Proposition 4), but a defender of a canonical
cosmic time (Craig, Zimmerman) rejects exactly P3w, and in near-FLRW
spacetimes the cosmic time is definable from the metric (R8). The theorem's
contribution is to make the price exact: such a view must hold that whether x
has happened by b depends on spacetime structure outside every region that
determines x and b.

**O4. "The off-shell premise."** The rule is required for every globally
hyperbolic geometry, as Stein's and Clifton–Hogarth's are defined from
geometry alone. This is a local cousin of Gödel's own modal premise, that
whether time lapses should not depend on "the particular way in which matter
and its motion are arranged in the world". Restricting to solutions with
reasonable matter is the open pocket problem; energy conditions make it
genuinely harder (`research/stein-gr/ENERGY_CONDITIONS.md`). One further
obstruction: domains of dependence of disjoint patches of one Cauchy surface
contain no chronologically related pair, so a flat pocket below x cannot be
the domain of dependence of a patch on the Cauchy surface that carries the
curved slab; it must be fed from other times. [OPEN]

**O5. "Transitivity and chronological inclusion are contestable"** (Sklar;
Clifton–Hogarth's own discussion). Drop either and anything goes. The view
adopts them as what objective becoming means (P2).

**O6. "Quantum nonlocality"** (red teams 04, 07). If the fundamental dynamics
makes spacelike events contribute to bringing b about (collapse dynamics,
Tumulka's relativistic flash theory rGRWf, Bohmian mechanics with a
foliation), then Clifton and Hogarth's maxim forces universal becoming, and
the view collapses into eternalism. P6 is restricted to signalling precisely
so that this is a clear bet, not a hidden premise. The view fits no-collapse,
perspectival readings (Dieks, *Found. Phys.* 2025: perspectives "remain"
independent "even when they make causal contact"), and Stapp's reported
retreat from a Whiteheadian causal-past picture because of Bell shows the bet
is not idle. [OPEN; listed as a falsifier in §8]

**O7. "Experience presents an extended now"; "the present is lived, not a
point"** (Husserl, Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, Ricoeur, Bergson). Out of scope
by §1: the view's present is a position in causal structure, not lived
presence. The specious present is a duration along a worldline, and the
worldline form of the theorem (Corollary 9) is available to anyone who takes
worldlines rather than point events as the basic standpoints. What the view
cannot claim is that it has said anything about lived time.

**O8. "Denying the universe a present is a cost."** Only for views, like
Brading's, that need one. Here it is a consequence (R6).

**O9. "This has been said before."** Partly granted (priority status above).
Fine has the frame; Rakić has event-relative realization; causal-set
theorists have growth of causal pasts; Whitehead may have the content. The
view's claim to novelty is the locality derivation across curved spacetimes
and its consequences (R1, R4, R8, R10, O11b). Earman 2008 explicitly leaves that
derivation undone (p. 151). [Pending Whitehead's own text, Lipman 2026,
Iaquinto–Torrengo ch. 4]

**O10. "Coordination between fragments is brute"** (Hofweber & Lange against
Fine, *Noûs* 2016). On the official relativist core, the fragments are
coordinated by inclusion (F(b) ⊆ F(b') for b ≤ b') and by agreement (P5). The
red team's dilemma: either the geometry is prior, so fragments are
appearances, or the coordination is brute. The view takes neither horn as
stated: by §1, standpoint-relative reality is basic and the geometry *is* the
agreement, so the coordination is not a further fact. Whether that is
explanatory or merely a relabelling is O2 again. [THESIS]

**O11. "Locational standpoints erase the difference between time and space."**
Fine's own objection (Fine 2005, §10; self-archived manuscript pp. 70–71,
book ≈ pp. 304–305): "One of the primary motivations for the presentist view
is that it enables one to distinguish between space and time. Temporal
indexicality is metaphysically significant, while spatial indexicality is
not; there is an objective 'now', even though there is no objective 'here'.
However, once we adopt the locational view, this asymmetry between space and
time disappears." Fine concludes that the "frame-theoretic form of nonstandard
realism constitutes the only reasonable way of reconciling presentism with
SR" (ms. p. 72, book p. 306).

(a) Concession: the fragment of b is centred on b, so "here" is as objective
as "now", and Fine's asymmetry is not restored. What the view gives instead is
a structural asymmetry: future displacement enlarges a fragment, spatial
displacement yields fragments neither of which contains the other. Price will
say this is his here/now analogy; it is, with the difference that the cone
structure, not a convention, fixes which displacements enlarge.
(b) The general-relativistic reply, the view's main contribution relative to
Fine. His argument for frame fragments rests on special relativity having
global inertial frames ("SR embodies enough of an asymmetry between space and
time ...; one can simply take ... temporal indexicality to be relativity to a
frame-time pair"). A generic curved spacetime has none. Frame-time
standpoints then need a foliation, which is either non-local or added
structure (R8). In general relativity Fine's preferred option is no longer
free, and the locational option is the only local one left. [THESIS]
(c) Fine's remark on event standpoints in n. 36 (ms. p. 79, ≈ book pp.
310–311) reports Sprigge's view that "every event must be present from its own
point of view, and as it really is", and continues: "the notion of each event
having its own 'perspective' on reality is decidedly odd; and I assume that
there must be some more plausible way in which one might accommodate the
universal presentness of events within our overall conception of what is
real." Local fragmentalism is a candidate for that "more plausible way": it
says what an event's perspective contains, and why. [THESIS]

Page numbers: the self-archived manuscript is reflowed, so there is no
constant offset to the book; see PAGE_MAP.md. Spot-check the print edition
before quoting. Note: Fine (n. 31) identifies the world-line/location framework
as "essentially the framework of Clifton & Hogarth [95]", and (n. 32)
distinguishes standpoint-equivalence from Putnam's "y is real for x". Under
local fragmentalism no two distinct events are equivalent standpoints
(J^-(x) = J^-(y) forces x = y in globally hyperbolic spacetimes).

**O12. "Fragmentalist passage is anaemic"** (Andreoletti's review of Iaquinto
& Torrengo 2022, *Organon F* 32 (2025), §3: "from a global perspective,
reality (the totality of fragments) features no passage at all"; Maudlin;
Bergson). Passage, on this view, is the growth of fragments along worldlines
(R3), a standpoint-internal fact, and its direction is the time orientation.
Conceded: there is no passage from the whole, and the view does not explain
the direction. Anyone who requires passage to be standpoint-independent, or to
explain time's direction, will find it anaemic, and the view accepts that
cost. The official view does not posit a standpoint of the whole (P7 is
optional), so it does not claim a static "view from the whole" either.
[THESIS]

**O13. "Why not add an atemporal standpoint, as Lipman does?"** P7 would
supply one, derived rather than posited (F(M) = M). The official view declines
it, for the reasons in P7: it readmits shared region-presents and makes the
block the theory's home. Without it, the eternalist's truths are recovered as
the agreement of standpoints (P5). [THESIS]

**O14. Rakić's sea-fight** (*BJPS* 48 (1997), §5, read in full). An admiral's
decision e2 lies in the causal past of an observer at e1; the sea-fight e3 it
settles lies in e1's elsewhere. Rakić concludes that what is realized with
respect to e1 should include e3, so realization is not definable from causal
geometry and must be added as a primitive. Reply: this runs together settled
and real (R10). Even if e3 were settled from e1, settledness does not make e3
real from e1. And in relativistic physics e3 is generally not settled from
e1: e3 depends on events in J^-(e3) \ J^-(e1), which is non-empty. The case
stipulates that the sea-fight "depended solely" on the decision, which local
physics does not allow; in Minkowski spacetime nothing outside J^-(e1) is
settled from e1 (R10). Rakić's primitive is the added-structure horn of R8.
[THESIS, using R10]

**O15. Persistence, and "the Sun now"** (Sider, Hawley; red team 01). Objects
are worldtubes; from b, an object exists as its part within J^-(b). The Sun
real from b is the Sun of about eight minutes ago and earlier. "The Sun now"
picks out a part outside J^-(b), so from b it is fixed only by a simultaneity
convention, not by a fact about b's fragment. Conceded: ordinary talk of
distant presents is conventional on this view. That is relativity's verdict,
not an extra cost of the view.

**O16. "Why every event?"** (Ismael). Nothing in physics marks the events
that host observers. A standpoint is a location of possible becoming, not of
consciousness. Anyone who prefers observers' worldlines as the only
standpoints gets the same fragments from the worldline form of the theorem
(Corollary 9). But that alternative has a cost the event version avoids.
Earman (2008, §6) notes that with worldline standpoints the union of what has
become along a worldline can miss part of spacetime (accelerated observers;
event horizons, as in de Sitter), and that observationally indistinguishable
spacetimes (Malament 1977) then leave the global geometry unfixed by the
standpoints. With every event a standpoint, the fragments jointly cover M
(every b lies in F(b)), so the agreement of standpoints (P5) fixes the whole
geometry.

**O17. "Not real is not open"** (Hartshorne, Čapek, Prigogine, Deleuze; the
open-future logicians). Granted. R10 separates the two questions: the future
is never real from b; whether it is settled from b depends on the spacetime
and the laws. In Minkowski spacetime nothing outside the fragment is settled;
in a spatially closed static universe, everything is, under deterministic
laws. So the view neither secures nor excludes an open future; it says what
openness would consist in.

## 8. Where the view sticks its neck out

These would count against it:

1. **Bell-nonlocal dynamics at the level of events.** If the fundamental
   dynamics makes spacelike events contribute to bringing events about
   (collapse, flash ontologies such as rGRWf, foliation-dependent Bohmian
   mechanics), the signalling restriction of P6 is false of the world and the
   view collapses into eternalism (O6).
2. **A physically privileged foliation.** If physics has a preferred global
   time, P3's motivation weakens and a shared present returns.
3. **Locally definable spacelike co-reality.** Any well-motivated local
   relation of co-presence between spacelike-separated events would refute a
   premise, since R2 says there is none.
4. **Closed timelike curves in our universe.** In totally vicious regions
   fragments are everything (R7).
