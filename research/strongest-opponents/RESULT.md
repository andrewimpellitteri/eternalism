# The strongest opponents: Saunders and Pooley

Sole-worker analysis, 2026-10-21. Input to the reframed article
(`paper/REFRAME.md`). This section reconstructs the strongest published
challenges rather than the weak inferences the draft used to rebut.

## Saunders: presentism needs an invariant equivalence relation

Saunders's *Tense and Indeterminateness* (PhilSci-Archive 2241, 2000) states a
structural requirement for presentism: the present must be captured by an
invariant, definable relation of co-presence that is an equivalence relation
(symmetric and transitive). His strongest formulation, at p. 3:

> Clifton and Hogarth (1995), thinking that they were proving Stein right, went
> on to prove that no transitive worldline-dependent relation could be defined,
> given an arbitrary collection of inertial lines. They have strengthened
> Putnam's point.

And, on Stein:

> Stein's proof that Con is the only relation which is transitive, defined in
> terms of the structure of Minkowski space (Stein 1991). Therefore there is no
> equivalence relation, proving Putnam's point.

Two cautions. First, Saunders's paraphrase of Clifton–Hogarth ("no transitive
worldline-dependent relation") is broader than their theorem, which leaves
past-chronological, past-causal and universal relations. The narrower and
stronger point is about a **nontrivial equivalence relation of co-presence**:
the surviving relations are not symmetric equivalence relations of a present
(except the universal one, which is trivial). Second, the challenge is
structural, not a claim that every local or system-relative present fails.

**Strongest form.** A presentist ontology needs a physically definable,
invariant, non-trivial relation of co-presence that supports the claim that
"these events are all present together." Relativity plus the definability
results leaves no such relation. A system-relative present is not an
equivalence relation: it is a region assigned to a system, not a symmetric
co-presence relation among all events. Therefore Brading's local present either
(a) gives up the equivalence-relation requirement and with it the standard
presentist claim, or (b) needs an additional structure that defines co-presence.
The coverage objection is the set-theoretic shadow of (a): local presents can
cover the history without giving a global equivalence class.

**What the reframed paper can say.** It should not pretend to refute Saunders.
It can accept the structural point and use it: if presentism requires a global
equivalence relation, Brading's system-relative present is not presentism in
that sense; if it does not, the view needs an account of co-presence, and the
coverage problem shows that local boundedness is not enough. Either way, the
existential restriction is not derived from the physics.

## Pooley: taking both passage and relativity seriously

Pooley's *Relativity, the Open Future, and the Passage of Time* (2013)
distinguishes two easy routes to reconciling passage with relativity:

> (1) provide a deflationary analysis of passage compatible with the block
> universe or (2) argue that a privileged global present is compatible with
> relativity. (1) does not take passage seriously. (2) does not take relativity
> seriously.

His project is the harder route: a "properly relativistic" A-theoretic view that
"eschews a global Now" and links passage to the open future, using a
non-standard interpretation of branching spacetimes (abstract and introduction,
pp. 1-4). The challenge is not "no invariant simultaneity, therefore no
passage." It is: **bare compatibility is cheap; a view that takes passage
seriously must explain how the open future is settled without a privileged
global present, and must do more than redescribe the block.**

**Strongest form.** Brading's local present is a candidate for the hard route:
it eschews a global Now and ties the present to dynamical unity. But the
coverage objection shows that it can be read as a division of the block into
system-relative presents, with the rest of the world "as real as we are"
(Brading 2013, p. 1110). That is route (1): a deflationary/relational account,
not robust passage. If the view adds an actuality or privilege premise to get a
real restriction, it moves toward route (2) or a hidden privileged structure.
The paper's limited conclusion is exactly this dilemma.

**What the reframed paper can say.** It can concede that it does not vindicate
robust passage. Its contribution is to show that Brading's local present does
not by itself supply the existential restriction that robust passage would
need. That is a negative result about one bridge, not a refutation of Pooley's
project. It also explains why the "easy compatibility" charge is not merely
rhetorical: the coverage objection gives it a precise form.

## Verdict for the article

The strongest opponents do not refute the reframed paper's negative thesis;
they sharpen it. Saunders shows that a global equivalence relation of
co-presence is unavailable; Pooley shows that a local present must either be
deflationary or add a privileged structure. Brading's view, on the coverage
reading, is a sophisticated version of the first horn unless an actuality
premise is added. The paper should state this as a concession, not as a victory:
it does not establish eternalism; it establishes that the local-present route
does not by itself restore the presentist existential restriction.

  cited preprints.

  and the paper's limited reply.
