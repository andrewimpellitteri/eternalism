# Independent priority audit: doubly-principal complex percolation

Checked 2026-10-19. This is a bounded defeat-search, not a global priority
certificate. I screened forward-citing records from two citation indexes,
searched primary arXiv/author records and recent work, and read the closest
sources at the sections stated below.

## Target and distinction between event algebras

The claim under audit fixes the complex transitive-percolation cylinder values
\(A_p(C)=p^{L(C)}(1-p)^{\binom{|C|}{2}-R(C)}\) and asks whether they extend as a
countably additive finite complex measure to the **labelled sigma-algebra
generated by doubly-principal cylinders**, \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\). Here a
finite causet is doubly principal when it has a unique maximum and its
restriction after deleting the final label also has a unique maximum. The
claimed classification is extension iff \(p\in[0,1]\).

This must be kept separate from three nearby statements:

| Domain / statement | What the screened literature establishes |
|---|---|
| Full labelled cylinder sigma-algebra \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}\), with all finite-cylinder values prescribed | Dowker–Johnston–Surya (2010), Sec. 4, prove the complex-percolation vector measure has unbounded variation except for real nonnegative amplitudes. Surya–Zalel (2020), Sec. 2.3 and Sec. 3.3, restate the full-algebra nonextension result and build a broader criterion. |
| Principal-cylinder sigma-algebra \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_b\) | Dowker–Zalel (2023, arXiv v2 last revised 2023-07-17; the HTML page also displays a 2026 build date), Sec. III.4, gives a numerical asymptotic conjecture for nonextension outside \([0,1]\), not a proof. |
| Doubly-principal labelled sigma-algebra \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\) | Same paper, Sec. V, explicitly says its extension test is inconclusive and develops a sharper criterion. I found no later proof of this prescribed-value classification in the screened sources. |
| Covariant intersection \(\mathcal R_p=\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\cap\mathcal R\) | Dowker–Zalel, Theorem 14, identifies the covariant algebra, and Theorem 15 gives an almost-sure observables result for cyclic dynamics. Neither theorem classifies extension of the prescribed complex amplitudes on the labelled generator algebra. An extension on the smaller intersection would not imply one on \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\). |

The full-cylinder result is not an immediate corollary for the doubly-principal
algebra. Restriction of a measure on the larger domain would imply one on the
smaller domain; failure on the larger domain supplies no contradiction for the
smaller one. Dowker–Zalel explicitly encounter this gap in Sec. V: the
doubly-reduced tree contains valency-one nodes, so the earlier minimum-\(\zeta\)
criterion cannot decide the issue. Their revised bound still leaves the
behavior of its valency-one contribution unresolved. This is the strongest
direct prior-work lead and also the closest independent novelty defeater to
check against any claimed proof.

## Independent check of the direct weighted-sum obstruction

I checked the coordinator's simpler estimate against the amplitude and
 generator definitions. For \(p\notin[0,1]\), put \(q=1-p\),
\(a=|p|>0\), \(b=|q|>0\), \(t=a+b>1\), and \(r=a/t, s=b/t\).
Every doubly-principal causet of size \(k+2\) is uniquely
\(C=G\oplus\{u\}\oplus\{v\}\), where \(G\) is an arbitrary naturally
labelled \(k\)-order. The first cap adds one cover from each maximal element
of \(G\); the second adds the cover \(u\prec v\). Thus, writing \(M(G)\) for
the number of maximal elements, \(I(G)=\binom{k}{2}-R(G)\) for the number of
incomparable pairs, and \(L(G)\) for the number of covers,
\[
 \sum_{|C|=k+2,\,C\;dp}|A_p(C)|
 =a\sum_G a^{L(G)+M(G)}b^{I(G)}
 \ge a t^k\sum_G r^{L(G)+M(G)}s^{I(G)}.
\]
Here \(L(G)+M(G)\ge k\), since every nonmaximal element has an outgoing
cover, and \(t>1\), so the termwise comparison is valid.

The final sum is exactly the real-parameter \(r\) transitive-percolation
probability that the last label lies above every earlier label: \(G\) has
probability \(r^{L(G)}s^{I(G)}\), and conditional on \(G\), its new last
element lies above all of \(G\) iff it has links to all \(M(G)\) maximal
elements, of probability \(r^{M(G)}\). Exposing vertices in reverse order
gives
\[
 \sum_G r^{L(G)+M(G)}s^{I(G)}
 =\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1-s^j)
 \ge c_s:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-s^j)>0.
\]
The product is positive because \(0<s<1\) and \(\sum_j s^j<\infty\).
Consequently each doubly-principal level has absolute amplitude sum at least
\(a t^k c_s\), which diverges as \(k\to\infty\). Each level is a finite
disjoint family of generator cylinders. A finite countably additive complex
measure has finite total variation, so these level sums would have to be
uniformly bounded. This contradiction proves nonextension for
\(p\notin[0,1]\). For \(p\in[0,1]\), ordinary real transitive-percolation
probability on full histories restricts to the target algebra.

This estimate directly evaluates the contribution left open by Dowker–Zalel:
their Sec. V already gives a finite-level variation criterion for the
doubly-reduced tree, and explicitly singles out
the unresolved valency-one contribution as a sum over doubly-principal
causets. The estimate above computes a divergent lower bound for that
contribution. I found no prior source that states this lower bound or the
resulting all-\(p\) classification. The paper's criterion alone does not
contain the estimate, so this audit treats the result as a short possible
corollary/strengthening of an existing criterion, not as an already-published
classification. The novelty claim is therefore best framed as this weighted-sum estimate and
its application to their criterion, rather than the general measure-extension
criterion itself. It remains evidence for plausibility of
novelty, not proof of priority.

## Limits and strongest possible defeater

The strongest direct defeater remains a prior proof, code, dissertation result,
or unpublished manuscript deriving the same \(p\in[0,1]\) classification for
the labelled doubly-principal generator sigma-algebra, perhaps under another
name. I found none in the works above. A proof only for all finite cylinders,
the principal algebra, a different complex decoherence functional, or the
covariant intersection is not automatically the same claim. The search did
not exhaust non-indexed theses, books, non-English repositories, unpublished
notes, or every general theorem about complex measures. Global publication
priority remains unresolved.
