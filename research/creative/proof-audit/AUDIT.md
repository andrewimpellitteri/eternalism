# Fresh proof audit: doubly-principal CTP cylinder algebra

**Verdict:** I found no counterexample to the stated iff result, provided
“complex measure” has its standard meaning: a countably additive
`C`-valued measure on the generated sigma-algebra. Such a measure has finite
total variation. The positive half and the nonclassical obstruction both
survive an independent derivation. There is a shorter obstruction than the
initial repeated-block proof: successive levels obtained by capping all finite
orders twice have absolute-amplitude sums growing without bound.

This verdict concerns the labeled sigma-algebra generated by doubly-principal
cylinders, which Dowker–Zalel denote `\tilde{R}_p`. It does not classify
measures defined only on the covariant intersection `R_p = \tilde{R}_p ∩ R`.

## Primary-source domain and amplitude

I read the target sections of Dowker–Zalel, arXiv:2212.01149v2, directly:

- §III.3, HTML lines 260–270: reduced-poscau nodes for principal causets are
  singleton labeled causets, while other extensions are grouped; the node
  sets correspond to the principal cylinders and their Boolean differences.
- §III.4, lines 273–292: the labeled-history amplitude is the CTP cylinder
  value `A(C)=p^L q^(choose(n,2)-R)`, `q=1-p`, with `L` the number of links
  and `R` the number of strict relations. Their full-cylinder extension
  theorem is stated for `p∈[0,1]`.
- §V, lines 351–386: `R_p` is the covariant event algebra restricted to
  histories with infinitely many posts (up to its null complement), and
  Theorem 14 states `R_p = \tilde{R}_p ∩ R`. The paper defines
  `\tilde{R}_p` as generated by cylinders of principal causets whose
  restriction deleting the last label is itself principal; these are exactly
  the doubly-principal generators in the target claim. The text says the
  doubly-reduced-tree extension criterion is inconclusive, then gives its
  `S_n` criterion and numerical evidence through levels 2–4.

The distinction between `\tilde{R}_p` and `R_p` is essential. The theorem in
`RESULT.md` correctly states the larger labeled algebra. Its corollary to the
larger labeled reduced-poscau algebra follows by restriction. Neither result
settles extension on the covariant intersection alone.

The paper counts labeled poscau nodes. In the argument below, each distinct
relation on a fixed natural-label interval is therefore a separate cylinder;
no division by automorphisms or quotient by order-isomorphism is appropriate.
The last label of a principal causet must be its unique maximum, because
relations respect natural-label order. Thus the finite combinatorial
definition used in `RESULT.md` matches §V's generator definition.

## Fresh derivation of nonextension

I first reconstructed the initial principal-only Harris proof. A
coordinator follow-up then suggested summing the entire doubly-principal level
by capping every order, not just the principal subfamily. I independently
checked that simplification below; the simpler route is therefore recorded
as a coordinator-prompted audit finding, not as an independent discovery.

Put `a=|p|`, `b=|q|`. If `p∉[0,1]`, the strict triangle inequality gives
`t=a+b>1`, with `a,b>0`. Set `r=a/t`, `s=b/t`, so `r,s∈(0,1)` and `r+s=1`.

For every naturally labeled order `G` of size `k`, let `M(G)` be its number
of maximal elements and define

```
u(G) = L(G) + M(G)
v(G) = choose(k,2) - R(G)
Z_k(x,y) = sum_G x^u(G) y^v(G).
```

The sum is over all naturally labeled `k`-orders, not only principal ones.
Every nonmaximal element has an outgoing cover, so `L(G) ≥ k-M(G)` and
`u(G) ≥ k`; also `v(G) ≥ 0`. Term by term,

```
Z_k(a,b) = sum_G t^(u+v) r^u s^v ≥ t^k Z_k(r,s).
```

To identify `Z_k(r,s)`, put independent Bernoulli-`r` edges on all forward
pairs of `k+1` naturally ordered vertices `0,...,k`, then take transitive
closure. The induced order `C` has probability
`r^L s^(choose(k+1,2)-R)`: each cover edge is required, each incomparable
pair must have no edge, and redundant comparable edges are unrestricted and
sum out. The event that vertex `k` is above every earlier vertex partitions
bijectively by the induced order `G` on `0,...,k-1`. Appending `k` adds one
cover from each of the `M(G)` maximal elements, adds `k` relations, and
creates no new incomparable pair. Its probability is therefore exactly
`Z_k(r,s)`. Backward sequential exposure gives

```
Z_k(r,s) = F_k(s) = ∏_{j=1}^k(1-s^j) ≥ c_s,
c_s = ∏_{j=1}^∞(1-s^j) > 0.
```

At each backward step, conditioned on the `j` later vertices already being
below `k`, the next vertex is below `k` iff at least one of its `j`
independent outgoing edges to them is present. The product is positive because
`0<s<1` and `Σ_j s^j<∞`. Hence `Z_k(a,b) ≥ t^k c_s`.

For every labeled `k`-order `G`, form `C_G=G⊕{u}⊕{v}`, placing `u,v` at
the last two labels. These are exactly the doubly-principal orders of size
`k+2`: deleting final label `v` leaves a principal order whose unique
maximum must be `u`, so every earlier element lies below `u` and `u<v`.
Conversely these two ordinal caps always give a doubly-principal order.
Distinct labeled `G`s give distinct prefixes of the same size; therefore
their cylinders are pairwise disjoint members of `\tilde{R}_p`, and this
family exhausts that level's doubly-principal cylinders.

The first cap `u` adds `M(G)` covers and the second cap `v` adds one more.
Together they add `2k+1` relations, exactly the number of added pairs, so
the `q` exponent remains `v(G)`. Thus the sum over this entire level is

```
V_(k+2) = Σ_G |A_p(C_G)|
         = a Σ_G t^(u(G)+v(G)) r^u(G) s^v(G)
         ≥ a t^k F_k(s)
         ≥ a t^k c_s → ∞.
```

The factor `t^(u+v)` varies with `G`; only the inequality replaces it by
`t^k`. No equality with `a t^k F_k(s)` is asserted.

For a finite complex measure `μ` extending these values,
`|μ|(Ω) ≥ Σ_G |μ(cyl(C_G))|` for every finite disjoint family at a fixed
level. The displayed level sums are unbounded as `k` grows, contradicting
finite total variation. This direct exact-level argument needs neither
Harris association nor repeated ordinal blocks.

For `p∈[0,1]`, independent-edge transitive percolation defines a probability
measure on the full labeled-history sigma-algebra and gives exactly these
cylinder amplitudes (with the usual endpoint conventions for `0^0`). Its
restriction to `\tilde{R}_p` is the required extension. This includes `p=0`
and `p=1`.

## Harris and endpoint checks

The initial principal-only route uses the event that 0 reaches all
vertices and all vertices reach `k`. Its Harris step is valid: both events
are increasing in the finite independent edge bits, and for increasing
functions `f,g`, conditioning on one bit gives covariance term
`r(1-r)(f_1-f_0)(g_1-g_0)≥0`; induction proves positive association. The
route's `c_s²` lower bound is correct, without assuming independence between
the two events, but it is weaker and unnecessary because the exact full-level
sum above yields `c_s` directly.

The strict case excludes `p=0,1` before division by `a` or use of
`r,s∈(0,1)`. For any other real `p`, the triangle inequality is strict:
`|p|+|1-p|>1`, including real `p<0` and `p>1`. The positive case handles
the full closed interval separately. The endpoint labels and sizes are
consistent: `G` has `k` elements; `C_G` has `k+2`; deleting only its final
top leaves a principal `(k+1)`-element causet.

## Full-cylinder extension/lifting check

The nearby results do not make this theorem an immediate corollary of
full-cylinder nonextension:

- Dowker–Johnston–Surya, arXiv:1007.2725v1, §2 (especially the total-variation
  discussion around HTML lines 126–154) states that countable additivity of a
  complex measure implies bounded total variation, and that restriction to a
  subalgebra preserves bounded variation. Its later full-history examples
  concern their specified full event algebra.
- Surya–Zalel, arXiv:2003.11311v1, §2.3 and Appendix B (HTML lines 183–198,
  517–555), state Theorem 2.1 for the algebra generated by finite set
  operations on **all** labeled cylinder sets, with extension to that
  algebra's generated sigma-algebra iff bounded variation holds. Their
  scalar theorem is not a theorem that an extension on an arbitrary smaller
  sigma-algebra must extend while matching the values of every missing
  cylinder.

A measure-lifting theorem, when its hypotheses allow one, can at most lift a
given measure on `\tilde{R}_p` while preserving its values on
`\tilde{R}_p`. It does not assign the prescribed CTP amplitudes to the full
cylinder events outside that sub-sigma-algebra. Requiring those extra values
is exactly the separate full-cylinder extension problem. Thus the known
full-algebra obstruction cannot be pulled back by a generic lifting result.
The direct disjoint-cylinder variation argument above avoids that issue.

## Exact small-instance certificate

`verify_small.py` enumerates all forward-edge masks and distinct induced
orders for `k=0,...,4`, with exact `Fraction`s. For `(r,s)=(2/5,3/5)` and
the symmetric normalized point `(1/2,1/2)`, it checks the all-order sum
`Z_k(r,s)=F_k(s)`, the bijection from all `k`-orders to all doubly-principal
orders of size `k+2`, and the exact finite level sums at `p=(1+i)/2` in
`Q(√2)`. It also checks the older principal-only Harris inequality for
`n=2,...,5` and the finite post-future precursor sum through three births.
All checks pass. They are finite consistency checks; the all-`k` proof is the
analytic argument above.

## Supplemental review: conditional covariant prescription

I also checked the separate conditional proposal in
`research/creative/covariant-boundary/BOUNDARY.md` at the coordinator's
request. Its distinction is correct: `D_H=E_H∩P_∞` is covariant and is
contained in `P_∞`, so it is in `R_p`; a labelled principal cylinder need
not be in that covariant algebra. The value rule (P) is an additional
compatibility assumption, not implied by the labelled-cylinder theorem.

At fixed size `n=k+2`, the two-cap construction enumerates every labeled
doubly-principal causet exactly once. Grouping these labels by unlabeled
shape `H` gives
`Σ_[H] N(H)|A_p(H)| = V_(k+2)`. Since the `D_H`s of a fixed post rank are
disjoint, prescription (P) forces variation at least
`|c(q)|V_(k+2) ≥ |c(q)|a t^k c_s`, which diverges for
`p∉[0,1]`, `|q|<1`. Thus the conditional proposition follows directly from
the stronger full-level bound. It still says nothing about a covariant
measure without (P).

The finite-prefix product in (P) is consistent with CTP's precursor rule:
after `j-1` future births have been required above the candidate post, the
next birth is above it iff its ancestor subset intersects the post and those
`j-1` descendants. Summing all precursor subsets gives `1-q^j`; choices among
older past elements sum to one. The script enumerates these subsets exactly
for `p=2/5`, `q=3/5`, prefixes of size 1–3, and up to three future births.
The holomorphic identity-theorem corollary is valid under its stated added
hypotheses, including holomorphy of each event value and agreement with the
classical measure on a real interval. Such holomorphy is not automatic.

A stronger proposed axiom prescribes
`ν(D_H)=κ N(H)A_p(H)` with one `κ` for **every principal** unlabeled `H`,
and also requires `ν(P_∞)=1`. Under that stronger scope, the conditional
classification can be extended to all `p`: if `κ≠0`, the exact level bound
rules out every `p∉[0,1]` without a restriction on `|q|`. If `κ=0`, the
events `D_H` for all principal `H` cover `P_∞` and form a laminar family:
at equal ranks distinct shapes are disjoint; across ranks, the later post's
finite inclusive past determines whether the earlier post of a specified
rank and shape occurred. Every finite intersection is therefore either
empty or another family member. If `κ=0`, inclusion-exclusion gives value
zero to every finite union. Enumerating the countable family and using
continuity from below for a countably additive complex measure gives
`ν(P_∞)=0`, a contradiction. At `p=0, κ≠0`, every principal `H` of size
at least 2 has `A_0(H)=0`; each history in `D_singleton` has a later post and
therefore lies in a countable union of compatible rank-at-least-2 events.
The same finite-union/continuity argument forces `ν(D_singleton)=0`,
contradicting its prescribed value `κ`. Classical transitive percolation
supplies the required measure for `0<p≤1`, with `κ=c(1-p)`; the almost-sure
infinite-post result is already stated in Dowker–Zalel §III.1.

This `κ=0` exclusion fails if the prescription covers only doubly-principal
`H`. For example, the infinite ordinal sum of blocks
`A₂⊕{p_j}` (`A₂` a two-element antichain) has infinitely many posts `p_j`,
but none has doubly-principal inclusive past: immediately below each post,
the two antichain elements remain distinct maxima. Its Dirac measure on
`R_p` has `ν(P_∞)=1` and annihilates every doubly-principal `D_H`, satisfying
the restricted κ=0 rule for any `p`. Thus “all principal `H`” is a necessary
part of the stronger conditional classification.

## Limits and residual assumptions

1. The contradiction uses the standard theorem that a countably additive
   complex measure on a sigma-algebra has finite total variation. The phrase
   “finite-valued” in the draft is best replaced or clarified as “finite
   complex measure” to prevent a reader from treating finite scalar values
   alone as a variation hypothesis. The cited extension literature supports
   the standard convention.
2. The proof assumes the cylinder formula in Eq. (26) and standard
   transitive-percolation interpretation, including that `L` counts cover
   relations and `R` counts all strict comparable pairs.
3. The infinite-product lower bound is a standard mathematical fact; the
   result depends on `0<s<1`, already guaranteed by `p∉[0,1]`. Harris
   association is valid for the older proof but is not required by the
   two-cap level-sum proof.
4. I did not evaluate global novelty or publication priority. The paper itself
   poses the doubly-principal extension question and gives inconclusive
   criteria/numerics; that is close prior art. No formal proof assistant or
   independent external mathematical referee was used.
5. Nothing here supports or excludes eternalism, presentism, objective
   passage, determinism, or a general quantum-gravity claim. The conclusion
   concerns one labeled event algebra and a scalar complex measure.
