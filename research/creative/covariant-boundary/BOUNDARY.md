# Covariant boundary for complex percolation

Status: bounded conditional obstruction, derived 2026-10-19. It does not show
that the labelled-cylinder theorem transfers to the covariant intersection
without an added prescription for covariant event values.

## Algebra and target

Write \(q=1-p\). In the notation of Dowker–Zalel (arXiv:2212.01149v2,
Sec. V, Eqs. (29)–(30), Theorems 14–15),
\[
\mathcal R_p=\{E\in\mathcal R:\mathcal P_\infty^c\subseteq E
  \text{ or }\mathcal P_\infty^c\subseteq E^c\}
 =\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\cap\mathcal R,
\]
where \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\) is generated by doubly-principal
labelled cylinders and \(\mathcal P_\infty\) is the event of infinitely many
posts. A labelled cylinder is generally not in \(\mathcal R\), so a measure
on \(\mathcal R_p\) has no obligation to equal its labelled amplitude.
The measurable covariant post events below are the target on which a proposed
covariant-only value rule can be stated.

For an unlabeled finite order \(H\), let \(\mathrm{Lab}(H)\) be its distinct
naturally labelled representatives on the first \(|H|\) labels, and let
\(N(H)=|\mathrm{Lab}(H)|\). This is the number of representatives, already
quotiented by automorphisms; it is not the number of vertex permutations.
If \(H\) is principal, let \(E_H\) be the covariant event that a post
has exactly \(|H|-1\) ancestors and inclusive past isomorphic to \(H\), and
put \(D_H=E_H\cap\mathcal P_\infty\). The post of that rank, if it exists, is
unique: two posts are comparable, and the later one's past strictly contains
the earlier one. Thus, for fixed \(|H|=n\), the events \(D_H\) for distinct
unlabeled \(H\) are pairwise disjoint. Each \(D_H\) is covariant and lies
inside \(\mathcal P_\infty\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal R_p\) directly by
Eq. (29); Theorem 14 then places it in \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\). The
doubly-principal-stem characterization in Theorem 15 is consistent with this
identification.

## Covariant post events and their labelled cutoff sums

For \(M\ge n=|H|\), sum the complex percolation amplitudes over the finite
labelled prefixes in which the first \(n\) elements form one of
\(\mathrm{Lab}(H)\), and the top of \(H\) precedes each of the next \(M-n\)
elements. These are finite disjoint unions of labelled cylinders. Their
amplitude sum is
\[
 V_{H,M}(p)=N(H)A_p(H)\prod_{j=1}^{M-n}(1-q^j).
\]
At the first birth after the candidate post, the new element is above it just
when the precursor choice includes that post (weight \(1-q\)). After \(j-1\)
future elements have been required above it, the next birth is above it just
when the precursor subset intersects those \(j\) elements (weight
\(1-q^j\)); choices among the earlier past elements sum to one. This proves
the displayed product by sequential exposure. It is an algebraic cylinder-sum
identity and makes no assertion of continuity of a nonexistent measure.

For \(|q|<1\),
\[
 c(q):=\prod_{j=1}^\infty(1-q^j)
\]
converges and is nonzero. First impose this broader **stationary post
factorization premise** on a covariant-only complex measure \(\nu\): there is
one fixed finite scalar \(\kappa\in\mathbb C\) such that
\[
 \nu(D_H)=\kappa N(H)A_p(H)
 \quad\text{for every principal }H. \tag{S}
\]
This says the future continuation factor following any finite post past is
independent of its size and shape. It is an additional renewal compatibility
axiom, not a consequence of the labelled cylinder values. For \(|q|<1\),
the more specific cutoff-limit prescription
\[
 \nu(D_H)=\lim_{M\to\infty}V_{H,M}(p)=c(q)N(H)A_p(H)
 \quad\text{for every principal }H \tag{P}
\]
is the special case \(\kappa=c(q)\). This is a specified limit of finite
labelled-cylinder sums, not continuity of a nonexistent measure. It is not
implied by agreement on doubly-principal cylinders: those generators need
not be covariant, and \(D_H\) removes finite-post histories.

**Theorem (stationary post-factorization boundary).** Let \(\nu\) be a finite
countably additive complex measure on \(\mathcal R_p\) with
\(\nu(\mathcal P_\infty)=1\). Suppose it satisfies (S) for one finite
\(\kappa\), for every principal finite order \(H\). Then \(p\in(0,1]\).
Conversely, for every \(p\in(0,1]\), classical transitive percolation
restricted to \(\mathcal R_p\) satisfies these premises with
\(\kappa=c(1-p)\).

**Proof.** The post events form a laminar family across ranks. If
\(D_H\cap D_J\ne\emptyset\) and \(|H|<|J|\), a history in the intersection
has an earlier post with past \(H\) and a later post with past \(J\). The
earlier post is also a post inside \(J\), so every history in \(D_J\) has
that earlier post and \(D_J\subseteq D_H\). Otherwise the events are
disjoint. Every history in \(\mathcal P_\infty\) has a first post, whose
finite inclusive past is principal; thus the countable family \(D_H\) over
principal orders covers \(\mathcal P_\infty\).

If \(\kappa=0\), each \(D_H\) has zero measure. For any finite rank bound,
there are finitely many such orders. Every nonempty intersection of a finite
subfamily of this laminar family is one of the zero-valued events; finite
inclusion-exclusion therefore gives zero for the union. These finite unions
increase to \(\mathcal P_\infty\), so countable additivity gives
\(\nu(\mathcal P_\infty)=0\), contradicting normalization. Hence
\(\kappa\ne0\).

At \(p=0\), every principal \(H\) of size at least two has \(A_0(H)=0\).
Those events alone cover \(\mathcal P_\infty\), because an infinite-post
history has at least two posts. The same finite-union and
continuity-from-below argument contradicts normalization. So \(p\ne0\).

Now suppose \(p\notin[0,1]\). Put \(a=|p|\), \(b=|q|\), and \(t=a+b\).
Strict triangle inequality gives \(t>1\), with \(a>0\). Let \(V_n\) be
the sum of \(|A_p(C)|\) over all naturally labelled doubly-principal orders
\(C\) of size \(n\). We derive a direct lower bound on \(V_n\).

Set \(k=n-2\). Every doubly-principal \(C\) has a unique decomposition
\[
 C=G\oplus\{u\}\oplus\{v\},
\]
where \(G\) is an arbitrary naturally labelled \(k\)-order and \(u,v\)
are successive universal maxima. This is a bijection on labelled orders.
Write \(M(G)\) for the number of maximal elements of \(G\), and
\(v(G)=\binom{k}{2}-R(G)\) for its incomparable-pair count. Capping by
\(u,v\) adds \(M(G)+1\) covers and creates no incomparable pairs, so
\[
 V_n=a\sum_G a^{L(G)+M(G)}b^{v(G)}.
\]
Let \(r=a/t\), \(s=b/t\), so \(r+s=1\) and \(0<s<1\). Since
\(L(G)+M(G)\ge k\), termwise comparison gives
\[
 V_n\ge a t^k\sum_G r^{L(G)+M(G)}s^{v(G)}.
\]
The sum on the right is the real transitive-percolation probability that
the last of \(k+1\) elements is above every previous element: a causet on
the first \(k\) labels is \(G\), and the final transition contributes
\(r^{M(G)}\). By reversing the independent-edge order, this equals the
probability that the first element is below all later elements. Sequential
exposure gives that probability as
\[
 F_k(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1-s^j),
\]
because after the first element reaches the preceding \(j\) elements, the
next element is above it exactly when at least one of those \(j\) vertices
has an edge to it. Thus
\[
 V_n\ge a t^{n-2}F_{n-2}(s)\ge a t^{n-2}c_s,
 \qquad c_s:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-s^j)>0.
\]

Now group all doubly-principal labelled orders of size \(n\) by their
unlabeled order \([C]\). Every natural labeling of a doubly-principal order
is counted exactly once, so (S) gives the exact equality
\[
\Gamma_n:=\sum_{[C]\,\mathrm{doubly\ principal}}|\nu(D_C)|
   =|\kappa|\sum_{[C]}N(C)|A_p(C)|=|\kappa|V_n.
\]
For fixed \(n\), the \(D_C\) in this sum are pairwise disjoint. Hence a
finite countably additive complex measure would have total variation at
least \(\Gamma_n\) for every \(n\). But \(|\kappa|V_n\ge
|\kappa|a t^{n-2}c_s\to\infty\), since \(t>1\) and \(\kappa\ne0\). This
contradicts finite total variation. Finally, for \(0<p\le1\), ordinary
transitive percolation has infinitely many posts almost surely. The
probability of \(D_H\) is \(c(1-p)N(H)A_p(H)\): the finite past has the
cylinder probability, and the future post condition contributes the
independent factor \(c(1-p)\). This proves the converse. \(\square\)

The theorem covers every nonclassical \(p\), without a condition on \(|q|\),
under the all-principal stationary factorization premise (S). This premise is
stronger than imposing a rule only on doubly-principal events. For example,
let one infinite causet be the ordinal sum of repeated blocks
\(A_2\oplus\{x_i\}\), where \(A_2\) is a two-element antichain. Each
\(x_i\) is a post, so its Dirac measure has \(\nu(\mathcal P_\infty)=1\).
Before each post, however, there are two incomparable maximal elements, so
no inclusive post past is doubly principal. All doubly-principal \(D_H\) are
empty at this causet; this Dirac measure satisfies the doubly-principal-only
version with \(\kappa=0\) for any \(p\). Thus that narrower premise cannot
support the \(\kappa=0\) argument.

The cutoff-limit prescription (P) is the more specific special case
\(\kappa=c(q)\) when \(|q|<1\), including \(p=(1+i)/2\). The theorem makes
no claim about a measure on \(\mathcal R_p\) without the explicit premise
(S).

## Analytic variant

Under a different explicit assumption, (S) follows. Suppose
\(U\subset\{|1-p|<1\}\) is connected and open and intersects \((0,1)\).
Suppose there is a finite complex measure \(\nu_p\) on \(\mathcal R_p\) for
every \(p\in U\), normalized by \(\nu_p(\mathcal P_\infty)=1\). Suppose for
every principal \(H\), the function \(p\mapsto\nu_p(D_H)\) is holomorphic,
and for real \(p\in U\cap(0,1)\), \(\nu_p\) agrees on these events with
classical transitive-percolation probability. For these real parameters the
value is \(c(1-p)N(H)A_p(H)\), so the identity theorem forces (S) throughout
\(U\), with \(\kappa(p)=c(1-p)\). The theorem then rules out such a family at
any nonreal \(p\in U\). This is only a conditional corollary: holomorphy of
these event values is additional structure and is not established by the
cited papers. General obstructions to holomorphic families of measures are
already known; no general theorem about such families is claimed here.

## Why the bare labelled result does not transfer

There are two different questions:

1. Does a complex measure on \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\) exist that matches
   every doubly-principal labelled-cylinder amplitude? The working theorem in
   RESULT.md answers this negatively off \([0,1]\).
2. Does a measure exist on \(\mathcal R_p\), with what values on its
   covariant events? This is not specified by that labelled theorem.

Restriction would transfer a contradiction only if a measure on the smaller
algebra were known to extend to the larger algebra with the labelled values.
There is no such automatic extension. Conversely, simply taking a disjoint
partition of the doubly-reduced labelled tree does not yield a covariant
partition: its nodes encode label-dependent prefixes. Dowker–Zalel's
Sec. V, Eqs. (30)–(31), explicitly leave the extension question on
\(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_p\) inconclusive, and point to a possible future
post-probability calculation for the valency-one correction. Neither that
criterion nor the larger-algebra theorem assigns (P).

An immediate falsifier of the stationary-factorization theorem would be a
finite countably additive normalized \(\nu\) on \(\mathcal R_p\) satisfying
(S) for every principal \(H\) at a parameter \(p\notin(0,1]\). The
doubly-principal-only Dirac example above shows why imposing (S) only on those
shapes is insufficient. A different event-value prescription is a different
covariant-only extension problem.

## Primary comparisons and read depth

- Dowker & Zalel, *Observables for cyclic causal set cosmologies*,
  arXiv:2212.01149v2, Sec. V, Eqs. (29)–(31), Theorems 14–15. **Targeted
  full-text read** of the definitions of \(\mathcal R_p\), its doubly-principal
  generators, the covariant-intersection theorem, and the inconclusive complex
  extension calculation. They prove the algebra identity and discuss the
  labelled reduced-tree criterion; they do not prescribe values for a
  covariant-only complex measure.
- Dowker, Johnston & Surya, *On extending the Quantum Measure*,
  arXiv:1007.2725v1, Sec. 4 (especially Eqs. (45)–(48) and the discussion of
  covariant observables near the end of that section). **Targeted full-text
  read.** Their bounded-variation obstruction is on the labelled cylinder
  algebra; their discussion says the post event needs a separate, cumbersome
  conditional-convergence prescription. This supports the distinction
  between the cylinder theorem and an extra event-value rule, not proposition
  (P) itself.
- Surya & Zalel, *A Criterion for Covariance in Complex Sequential Growth
  Models*, arXiv:2003.11311v1, Secs. 1–3. **Targeted full-text read** of the
  labelled/covariant algebra setup, bounded-variation criterion, and complex
  percolation application. It treats extension to the full labelled sigma
  algebra as the condition for all covariant events to be measurable; it does
  not derive the post values in (P).
- Bombelli, Seggev & Watson, *A Computation of the Expected Number of Posts in
  a Finite Random Graph Order*, arXiv:0809.2258v2, Sec. 2, Theorem 2.1,
  Eqs. (1)–(2). **Targeted full-text read.** For real \(0<p<1\), they derive
  \(\kappa(q)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^j)\) as the future-side post factor and give
  \(\Pr(k\text{ is a post})=\lambda_{k-1}(q)\kappa(q)\) in Theorem 2.1
  and its proof. This makes the
  classical renewal factor prior work; the present premise extends a
  shape-independent factor to complex covariant event values and does not
  claim that classical calculation as new.

No paper claim above proves the stationary-factorization theorem. The two-cap
lower bound is an independent derivation; the stationary factorization is an
explicit additional assumption, motivated on the real interval by the known
classical renewal calculation.
