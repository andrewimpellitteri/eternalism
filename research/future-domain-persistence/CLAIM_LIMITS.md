# Claims the project may and may not make after this audit

28 October 2026. Companion to RESULT.md; no new source claims.

| Proposed claim | Status from this audit |
|---|---|
| A causal-past domain contains extended timelike material histories. | Yes, at the level of the explicit event-support construction. |
| A limb distant from the observer satisfies C&R's examined criterion of current persistence in that domain. | No: its required location reduces to the observer point, which the limb avoids. |
| Observing a limb guarantees a later material event inside the same standpoint's existence domain. | No: null emission has no timelike successor still in the past cone. |
| Advancing the observer can add later locations on the same stipulated material line. | Yes: retarded cutoff advances from −r to h−r. Identity of a material object remains a bridge premise. |
| The edge of the causal-past domain is literally just a point. | Only if edge means causal maximality; its topological null boundary is extended. |
| The extended boundary answers the classical recapture objection. | No: it supplies neither a spacelike simultaneous present nor the required recapture bridges. |
| Field determination from a causal-past restriction establishes that this restriction exhausts reality. | No: determination and existence require separate premises. |
| The example favors pointy or bow-tie existence empirically. | No observation model or discriminating prediction was derived. |

For future manuscript use: describe J⁻(b) as a stipulated event domain with
known persistence costs. Do not claim that retaining old locations already
answers the authors' requirement of continuing location, and do not call a
weaker history-interval criterion their own persistence criterion.
