## Mathematical and source-correspondence review

I independently traced the proof in `research/creative/RESULT.md` against the finite objects and code rather than relying on agent agreement:

1. For a naturally labelled finite order, unique maximum implies every earlier element lies below the last label. Applying that fact again after deleting the last label gives the unique decomposition `G ⊕ {u} ⊕ {v}`. Conversely, the two universal caps ensure double principality. This preserves natural labels; it is not an unlabelled quotient.
2. The first cap adds one cover from each maximal element of `G`; the second adds the cover `u < v`. No new incomparable pairs are introduced. Thus the modulus sum over the full labelled level has the displayed form, with `w = L + M ≥ k` and `h = binom(k,2) − R ≥ 0`.
3. With `a = |p|`, `b = |1−p|`, `t = a + b`, `r = a/t`, and `s = b/t`, the strict triangle inequality gives `t > 1` exactly when `p` is outside `[0,1]`. Termwise, `t^(w+h) ≥ t^k`. The remaining normalized sum is the probability of the last vertex lying above all previous vertices in forward-edge percolation. Summing redundant comparable edges out gives order probability `r^L s^(binom(n,2)−R)`. Reverse-order exposure gives `F_k(s) = product_{j=1}^k (1−s^j) ≥ c_s > 0`, so the level sums diverge.
4. At fixed size, distinct naturally labelled cylinder endpoints are disjoint members of the target sigma-algebra. Their absolute values are bounded by total variation. This contradicts finite total variation. The `p = 0, 1` cases are handled by the positive percolation extension and do not pass through the strict-case division by `a, b`.

This matches the exact target domain recorded in the theorem and the primary-source audit: the labelled sigma-algebra generated by doubly-principal cylinders. The result does not transfer to the smaller covariant intersection by restriction. Conversely, the corollary to the larger principal-cylinder algebra is valid by restricting any proposed larger-algebra extension to this subalgebra. The primary-source review records that Dowker–Zalel Sec. V identifies this doubly-reduced case as inconclusive; the present derivation computes a divergent lower bound for the previously unresolved level contribution. ## Conditional extension and probe disposition

- **Common-factor covariant theorem — survive narrowly.** `COVARIANT_RESULT.md` makes the rule `ν(D_H) = κ N(H) A_p(H)` for every principal `H` an added model assumption, rather than deriving it from covariance. Grouping the labelled doubly-principal level by unlabelled shape then gives the same variation obstruction. Do not state an unconditional classification for arbitrary covariant measures.
- **Doubly-principal-only factor rule — kill as a classifier.** The explicit ordinal-sum history `A₂ + {z₁} + A₂ + {z₂} + …` has infinitely many posts, while none of its post pasts is doubly principal. Its Dirac measure satisfies the weakened rule with `κ = 0` for any `p`, but fails the all-principal rule. Both the structural argument and executed checks support this distinction.
- **Rank-only factors — unresolved.** The bound `|κ_n| V_n ≤ ||ν||` is necessary and excludes a constant factor outside `[0,1]`. It does not construct or rule out exponentially decaying factors. The boundary script says this explicitly.
- **Infinite-dimensional vector measure — survives as a concrete norm-prescribed extension on a locus.** For `|p|² + |1−p|² = 1`, let `r = |p|²`, take real CTP probability `P_r`, and set `μ(E) = 1_E` in `L²(P_r)`. Then `μ` is countably additive in Hilbert norm, and each cylinder satisfies `||μ(C)||² = P_r(C) = |A_p(C)|²`. At `p = (1+i)/2`, this is a nonreal example. The construction preserves cylinder norms, not the complex phases, so it does not challenge the scalar theorem. Its semivariation is one while its total variation is infinite by the scalar level-sum divergence.
- **Finite-dimensional norm-prescribed vector measure — killed outside the real segment.** Every countably additive finite-dimensional vector measure has finite total variation: bound its norm by a finite sum of coordinate absolute values, each coordinate being a finite scalar measure. The divergent labelled level norm sums therefore rule out an `R²` extension for `p` outside `[0,1]`, including nonreal points on the norm-matching circle. The previous finite-level `R²` polygon witness does not extend to a countably additive infinite measure.
- **Remaining vector scope — narrower and unresolved.** The `L²(P_r)` construction answers the norm-prescribed existence question on `|p|² + |1−p|² = 1`; a norm-prescribed infinite-dimensional extension away from that locus remains unestablished. A fixed-direction, phase-preserving vector lift remains excluded whenever the scalar theorem excludes `p`.

## Subsequent vector-measure correction

The implementation test suite and initial report above were completed before the concrete vector finding in the later `research/adversarial-review/proof/` and `source-priority/` reviews. I inspected their argument and `research/adversarial-review/source-priority/PRIMARY_EVIDENCE.md` after that finding; I did not rerun the suite because the correction is an analytic construction, not a source-code change in my lane. The identity is direct: on `|p|² + |1−p|² = 1`, the real CTP cylinder probability at `r = |p|²` is `r^L(1−r)^(binom(n,2)−R) = |A_p(C)|²`; indicator functions give a countably additive `L²`-valued measure. Scalarization by `g∈L²` has variation at most `||g||₂`, whereas the vector variation is at least the sum of cylinder norms on each disjoint level, which diverges off `[0,1]`. Coordinate scalar measures imply the contrasting finite-dimensional no-go. This supersedes my initial broad “vector-valued variants remain open” wording. The explicit example is not phase-preserving and does not affect the scalar or conditional covariant conclusions.
