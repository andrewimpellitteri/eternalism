# Candidate classification for norm-prescribed Banach-valued measures

Coordinator derivation, 2026-10-21. **Provisional: independent proof and priority
review still required.** This classifies existence in some Banach space for a
norm-only variant. It does not classify Hilbert-space extensions, recover complex
phases or supply a physical decoherence functional. Do not treat it as an
accepted novelty claim or quietly substitute it for the selected scalar theorem.

## Exact candidate statement

Let `a=|p|`, `b=|1-p|`, `A_p(C)=p^L(1-p)^h`. On the labelled sigma-algebra
S_DP generated by doubly-principal cylinders, consider norm-countably additive
measures μ with values in some real or complex Banach space X, satisfying

`||μ(Ω)||=1`, `||μ(cyl C)||=a^{L(C)}b^{h(C)}` for every DP C.

Such a measure exists **if and only if**

`p=1`, or `|p|<1 and |1-p|≤1`.

This domain includes p=0. At nonclassical parameters it admits a measure of
semivariation one and infinite total variation. If instead every labelled
cylinder is norm-prescribed, the candidate existence domain is

`p in {0,1}`, or `|p|<1 and |1-p|<1`.

Thus the boundary `|1-p|=1, 0<|p|<1` distinguishes the two norm prescriptions.
Both statements allow the Banach space to depend on p. They assert no equality
with the original complex scalar amplitudes as vectors.

## 1. Necessary conditions for the DP prescription

A norm-countably additive Banach-valued measure on a sigma-algebra has bounded
range. One elementary justification is uniform boundedness on X*: for each
x* the scalar countably additive measure x*μ is finite-valued and has finite
variation, hence `sup_E |x*(μ(E))|<∞`. Apply Banach–Steinhaus to the continuous
evaluation maps `T_E:X*→K`, whose norms are `||μ(E)||` by Hahn–Banach.
The concrete obstructions below can also avoid this general theorem: the chain
cylinders are nested and hence their vectors converge by continuity from above;
the antichain-plus-two-caps cylinders are pairwise disjoint and hence their
vectors must tend to zero.

- If a>1, n-chain DP cylinders have norms `a^{n-1}` tending to infinity.
- If b>1, p≠0. Take a k-element antichain with two universal caps. Its amplitude
  modulus is `a^{k+1}b^{k(k-1)/2}`, tending to infinity.
- If a=1 and p≠1, b>0. For n≥1 let C_n be the ordinal sum
  `chain_n ⊕ A_2 ⊕ {u} ⊕ {v}`. It is DP, has size n+4, L=n+4 and h=1. The
  cylinders are pairwise disjoint: the first non-chain prefix occurs at a
  different birth. Their norms are all b. Countable additivity requires terms
  of every disjoint series μ(cyl C_n) to tend to zero, a contradiction.

The remaining possibilities are a<1,b≤1 and the exceptional p=1. The endpoints
p=0,1 have the usual scalar probability extensions.

## 2. A c0 construction on the remaining DP domain

Assume 0<a<1, 0<b≤1. By the triangle inequality a+b≥1. Choose a real r such that

`0<r<1`, `r≤a`, `1-r≤b`.

Such r exists: choose within `[1-b,a]`; when b=1 choose, for example, r=a/2.
Let P_r be the ordinary labelled CTP probability on the full history
sigma-algebra, restricted to S_DP when necessary. Every finite cylinder has
strictly positive P_r probability.

Index coordinates by Ω and all naturally labelled DP cylinders C. Put
`w_Ω=1`, `P_Ω=P_r`, `w_C=a^{L(C)}b^{h(C)}`, and
`P_C(E)=P_r(E∩C)/P_r(C)`. Define

`μ(E)=(w_C P_C(E))_C` in `X=c0` with the supremum norm.

There are finitely many coordinates at each rank. A principal n-order has at
least n−1 covers: each nonmaximum element has an upper cover, and these edges
have distinct lower endpoints. Therefore `w_C≤a^{n-1}`. The coordinate weights
tend to zero, so μ(E) belongs to c0, uniformly in E.

For disjoint E_j, the vector remainder is μ of the tail union. Given ε, discard
all but finitely many coordinates whose weights are at least ε. On the finite
set, each scalar probability P_C of the tail tends to zero; on the remaining
coordinates it is uniformly at most ε. Hence μ is countably additive in norm.
It is defined directly on the full sigma-algebra, so restriction needs no
additional extension theorem. Its total mass has norm one.

Fix a DP cylinder D. Any other cylinder C is disjoint from D or nested with it.
If C⊆D then its coordinate on D is w_C≤w_D, since L and h never decrease on
extension and a,b≤1. If D⊆C, then

`w_C P_C(D)=a^{L(C)}b^{h(C)} r^{L(D)-L(C)}(1-r)^{h(D)-h(C)}≤w_D`.

The Ω coordinate has the same bound. At coordinate C=D the value is precisely
w_D, so `||μ(D)||=w_D`. This proves the required norm prescription.

For a finite disjoint family and coefficients |α_j|≤1, every coordinate of
`sum_j α_j μ(E_j)` has modulus at most its weight, hence norm at most one.
Taking E=Ω shows semivariation exactly one. At nonclassical p the audited scalar
level-modulus lower bound shows infinite total variation, since the prescribed
DP norms have the same divergent sums.

This construction does not generally prescribe the norms of non-DP cylinders:
those cylinders were not included as coordinates. That distinction is essential
on the b=1 boundary, not a technical omission.

## 3. Prescribing every cylinder

Necessity of a<1 apart from p=1 remains as above. If b>1, even antichain cylinders
have unbounded prescribed norms. If b=1 and p≠0, let F_n specify an antichain on
the first n births and a new element at birth n with precisely one parent among
those births. Its first relation appears at birth n, so these cylinders are
pairwise disjoint as n varies. They have L=1 and norms a>0, contradicting
countable additivity. The endpoint p=0 remains allowed.

For strict a<1,b<1, the already-known indicator construction suffices: a+b≥1 and
`a^α+b^α` is strictly decreasing in finite α>0, from a+b at α=1 to zero at
infinity. Thus there is a unique finite α≥1 with `a^α+b^α=1` (α=1 for classical
interior p). Let r=a^α and use μ(E)=1_E in L^α(P_r). Its norm on every cylinder is
`P_r(C)^{1/α}=a^L b^h`, and countable additivity follows by the tail probability.

Alternatively, the same c0 construction works directly after indexing all
cylinders. Put ρ=max(a,b)<1. Every newly born vertex after the first contributes
at least one cover if it has any ancestors, and otherwise contributes at least
one incomparable pair. Old covers and incomparable pairs persist. Hence
L(C)+h(C)≥n−1 for every n-order and `w_C≤ρ^{n-1}`. The weights again vanish,
and the nesting/conditional-probability argument from Section 2 is unchanged.
Thus the classification does not depend on selecting an Lalpha exponent.

## Checks and priority limitations

`check_c0_construction.py` uses independent forward-edge masks through rank five,
exact rational probabilities and exact weights. At p=(9+12i)/25 (a=3/5,b=4/5)
and p=(50+120i)/169 (a=10/13,b=1), all 12 tested coordinates, including Ω,
attain the prescribed norm; conditional probability, finite additivity and
partition bounds pass. It also checks finite instances of the two disjoint
boundary families. The infinite proof is the argument above, not these checks.

Bounded-range and indicator-measure ingredients are standard vector-measure
facts. The bounded-range proof is supplied explicitly; the earlier source review
records Diestel–Uhl's indicator example. A small public search on 2026-10-21 for
c0-valued vector measures with prescribed cylinder norms and conditional
probability coordinates did not locate this exact specialization. Several broad
queries returned unrelated work; one primary journal page was inaccessible.
That limited search establishes no priority. The weighted-coordinate
construction may be a standard capacity/measure representation in another
terminology. Audit that possibility and the exact scope before promotion.

No inference about quantum dynamics, covariance as gauge, observer access or
temporal ontology follows from this deliberately flexible norm-only problem.
