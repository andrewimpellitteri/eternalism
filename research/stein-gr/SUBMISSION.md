## arXiv abstract (plain text, under the 1,920-character limit)

Stein proved that in Minkowski spacetime a reflexive, transitive relation of becoming that is invariant under the time-orientation-preserving symmetries, makes some past event definite for an event and leaves some events unsettled must be past causal connectibility. Clifton and Hogarth showed that, assuming only that the chronological past has become, the options are the causal past, the chronological past and the universal relation; they extended the result to worldline-dependent becoming and remarked that there is "no obvious way" to extend it beyond Minkowski spacetime. We give such an extension and locate exactly what it depends on. In a single curved spacetime, symmetry-invariance is too weak: a spacetime can have no symmetries. The right replacement is local covariance in the sense of locally covariant quantum field theory: one rule of becoming for all globally hyperbolic spacetimes, compatible with isometric embeddings of causally convex regions. Under that hypothesis we prove, by a gluing argument, that either everything has become for everything in every spacetime, or in every spacetime becoming lies between the chronological and the causal past; the worldline-dependent form follows. The residual freedom is exactly the status of lightlike-separated pairs, and we classify it: it can depend on curvature along the connecting light ray, so uniqueness fails even for relations determined by conformal structure alone; topological regularity removes it. In causal set theory the same hypothesis yields an exact Stein theorem, although the analogue fails under invariance within a single causal set. Locality also isolates Gödel-type universes: no morphism connects them to globally hyperbolic spacetimes. Finally we show why the result does not transfer routinely to spacetimes restricted by energy conditions, and isolate the open problem on which such a transfer depends.
