## arXiv abstract (plain text, under the 1,920-character limit)

Stein proved that in Minkowski spacetime a transitive, symmetry-invariant relation of becoming that makes some past event definite must be the causal past or degenerate; Clifton and Hogarth refined this to the causal past, the chronological past or the universal relation, extended it to worldline-dependent becoming, and noted "no obvious way" to go beyond Minkowski spacetime. We give such an extension and locate what it depends on. In a single curved spacetime symmetry-invariance is too weak, since generic spacetimes have no symmetries. The right replacement is local covariance, as in locally covariant quantum field theory: one rule of becoming for all globally hyperbolic spacetimes, compatible with isometric embeddings of causally convex regions. Under it we prove, by a gluing argument, that either everything has become for everything in every spacetime, or in every spacetime becoming lies between the chronological and the causal past; the worldline-dependent form follows, and no two distinct events share a present. The residual freedom is the status of lightlike-separated pairs. We classify it: it can depend on Weyl curvature along the light ray, so uniqueness fails even for relations fixed by causal structure, and topological regularity removes it. In causal set theory the same hypothesis gives an exact Stein theorem, although the analogue fails under invariance within a single causal set. Locality also isolates Gödel-type universes. Finally we show why the result does not transfer routinely to spacetimes restricted by energy conditions, and state the open problem on which such a transfer depends.
