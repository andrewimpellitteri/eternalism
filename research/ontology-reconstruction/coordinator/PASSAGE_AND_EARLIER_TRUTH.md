# Does eventual resolution establish truth now?

30 October 2026. Coordinator philosophical argument and restricted
counterinterpretation. The logical observation is elementary and belongs
to an established future-contingents debate; no priority claim. It is
not a full semantics of a named theory or a refutation of bivalence.

## 1. The prior challenge and its strongest answer

Wawer challenges the identification of a unique future grounding source
and compares it with counterfactual grounding.
[Primary paper, pp.418–420](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/server/api/core/bitstreams/b1907807-fb27-446f-a0a0-23f2e370b2ce/content).
Correia–Rosenkranz answer that passage connects present and later reality,
whereas actuality need not transition to a counterfactual world. They
separately treat conditional later-grounding explanations as compatible
with disagreement over present bivalence; their reply to Torre denies
that explanation must persuade a sceptic about its explanandum.
[Replies to Critics, §§6–7, pp.474–477](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370321332_Replies_to_Critics).

These are stronger positions than the generic objection that later
grounds secretly exist now. The test below grants distributed grounding
as a live proposal and asks what the passage premise itself establishes.

## 2. Separate four claims

Fix a future-directed utterance q at b about the value of a specified
observable at duration T. Distinguish:

1. **Outcome existence:** in each admitted complete realization the target
   occurs and has a definite value.
2. **Settlement:** every realization admitted by the chosen base gives
   q the same outcome value.
3. **Truth now:** q already has an absolute truth value at b, independent
   of a later assessment context.
4. **Grounding:** the appropriate obtaining event/fact explains that
   truth, perhaps under a later temporal operator.

The first does not entail the second: different complete realizations
can have different definite values. The second does not follow merely
from saying that one realization is actual. The third is not the same
claim as either. A proposed grounding explanation addresses the fourth
conditional on there being a truth to explain. None of this requires a
present cause or a mechanism selecting a complete history.

## 3. A restricted counterinterpretation to the passage inference

Use two possible complete realizations w₀,w₁ of a small system, with a
shared earlier stage b and a later target value i∈{0,1} in realization
w_i. Let q say that this value is 1. In both realizations use the same
growth rule: the target event is absent from the current inventory at b
and included at its later occurrence. Every realization continues to
that occurrence. The labels are comparison devices, not a currently
concrete ensemble or a physical branching spacetime.

Two semantic policies fit this same occurrence/growth pattern:

**L:** evaluated in a designated realization w_i, q is true at b iff i=1,
and false iff i=0. If true, its truth at b can be assigned a later ground
at the obtaining value-1 occurrence. This is the restricted later-grounded
bivalent policy, without requiring that occurrence to exist at b.

**S:** at b evaluate q over {w₀,w₁}; q is neither supertrue nor superfalse.
At the later stage the realized value is settled. A later assessment
restricted to w_i may evaluate the earlier utterance by that realization,
but this does not change the fact that its earlier assessment at b was
unsettled. This is a restricted supervaluation/assessment construction,
not the full account of MacFarlane or anyone else.

Both policies have eventual occurrence, a definite eventual value and
the same change of inventories. They disagree about truth now. Thus
that occurrence/growth pattern alone does not choose L over S.

Explicitly, the toy evaluation parameters are temporal stage b, complete-
realization index w, and S's alternatives Ω_b={w₀,w₁}. L uses
True_L(b,w_i,q) iff i=1. S uses True_S(b,q) iff q holds in every member
of Ω_b and False_S(b,q) iff it fails in every member. At a later assessment
c_i, its alternatives may shrink to {w_i}. The world index in L represents
actuality for that toy evaluation; it is not a present event, a causal
selection mechanism, or a proof of which realization is actual. A later
ground, if assigned, is evaluated at the obtaining event with the
explanandum time b held fixed. Its conditional form remains:

    if q is true at b, then later there is a witness whose obtaining
    explains q's truth at b.

The later operator scopes over the witness and explanation. It does not
export the witness into b's current domain. The clause presupposes its
explanandum when applied; it does not derive its truth.

The example does not show that either entire theory is coherent, that
both have the same grounding theory, or that their explanations are
equivalent. Nor does it prove S preferable. It refutes only the inference
from eventual resolution and this growth pattern to current bivalence.
If “resolution” is instead defined to include confirmation of an already
true proposition, it imports the very semantic premise being tested.

## 4. The extra principle needed

L adds an outcome-to-earlier-truth policy:

    In realization w, if the T-outcome makes q hold, q is true at b;
    if it makes q fail, q is false at b.

This can be motivated by the content of future tense, truth's role in
assertion, or a general semantic argument. The counterinterpretation
does not show those motivations fail. It shows that the policy is an
additional commitment; it has not been derived from inventory growth
or guaranteed later occurrence alone.

Likewise S adds a policy connecting current truth to invariance over
admitted continuations. Its choice of continuations needs justification,
and equating settlement with truth is not a neutral physical theorem.
The comparison is symmetric: neither policy receives an automatic
entitlement from the physical model.

This preserves the legitimate explanatory role of a ground. Explaining
why a true sentence is true need not explain why its obtaining event
occurs instead of a different event. Nor must the explanation force an
opponent to accept that the sentence is true. Our objection is to using
success at that explanatory task as a sufficient argument for the truth
policy or ontology itself, not to the explanation's conditional form.

## 5. No unique-history conclusion from bivalence of a small language

For the finite readout vector r=(r₁,…,r_N), bivalent answers to the
propositions r_j>0 specify a sign cell. Many different readout vectors
and physical histories occupy that cell. They do not select one history.

If the language instead contains all rational thresholds r_j>a, a
jointly realizable bivalent signature determines the vector: distinct
real values are separated by a rational threshold. Even this does not
determine the full spacetime. A history conclusion additionally needs
a family of propositions separating the proposed history classes and
a joint-realizability assumption. Mere bivalence does not supply either.

Consequently “there is a determinate answer to this future question”,
“there is a full actual truth signature”, “one mathematical realization
represents what occurs”, and “all future events currently exist” must
not be treated as interchangeable. The earlier selection audit already
distinguishes representative choice from actuality. This note makes the
language and joint-realizability dependence explicit.

## 6. What the smooth spacetime result adds

Subject to the interpolation theorem's review, the physical class can
provide many definite finite outcome vectors while holding the entire
pointed causal past and incoming mass fixed. Completeness ensures that
the sample targets exist at every prescribed finite proper time in
each realization. Thus neither a missing target nor a nonsmooth center
is needed for the contrast in §3.

This is an improvement to the physical example, not a derivation of
metaphysical branching. The models have different exterior Cauchy data.
Moreover the full bow-tie matching contract fixes their future under
the deterministic smooth-MGHD hypotheses. The choice of an observer's
past as the settlement base cannot be silently identified with a
rival's entire concrete realm.

It also does not establish the existence-openness mechanisms in which
later reality may fail to occur. Those depend on a different modal
class, examined in MAXIMALITY_AND_EXISTENTIAL_OPENNESS.md.

## 7. Philosophical judgment and next test

The later-grounding reply succeeds against the generic claim that a
present truth always needs a presently existing concrete future ground.
It does not by itself make growth a better explanation of current
bivalence, since the distinguishing outcome-to-earlier-truth policy can
be retained or rejected while holding the small growth pattern fixed.
The same criticism applies to claiming that a tenseless inventory alone
establishes the right truth policy.

The remaining positive question is which proposed explanation of tense,
assertion, dependence or actualization independently supports its policy
and inventory. A successful answer should identify the explanatory step
that a rival cannot reproduce, rather than insist that the rival lacks
the target by definition. That is an argumentative burden, not a demand
for a new physical prediction from every metaphysical difference.

Defeat conditions: a justified additional principle may rule out one
policy; a fuller quantified grounding theory may invalidate the small
construction; or the intended explanandum may differ from the one
specified here. Those would narrow this comparison. They would not
restore the inference from the bare eventual-resolution premise alone.
The unavailable Correia–Merlo full text remains a source gate for claims
about its particular logic; nothing here reconstructs that paper.
