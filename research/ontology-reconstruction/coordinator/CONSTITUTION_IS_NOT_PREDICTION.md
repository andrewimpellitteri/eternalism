# What would make a physical region constitutive of the present?

30 October 2026. Coordinator philosophical integration with the Brading
article in paper/. This is an argument about inference, not a fresh
interpretation of Brading's full texts or a manuscript revision. Source
depth for her claims remains controlled by paper/SOURCE_AUDIT.md and
the cited primary-review lane. No priority or ontology verdict.

## 1. Three steps that need three arguments

A proposed physical route to temporal ontology can proceed through:

    R determines a system's evolution under the admitted laws/models;
    R constitutes or sustains the system's unity;
    R is the extent of the system's concrete present.

The first is a determination claim, the second a constitutive claim,
and the third an interpretation of concrete existence/presentness.
Treating the first as sufficient for all three would make a physical
uniqueness theorem do work it does not state.

This does not establish that constitution or presentness are arbitrary.
It identifies the additional achievement an account must demonstrate.
A law-constitutive theory that starts with a genuine criterion of unity
may already be trying to supply the second step; showing that many
regions are predictively sufficient does not refute such a theory.

## 2. The new geometric contrast makes the first gap concrete

In the smooth scattering class an early full Cauchy surface, the open
bow-tie realm containing it, and the complete development all fix the
future readings under the appropriate field-preserving comparison.
These are very different extents. The observer's causal past can fail
to fix those same readings. The distinction is exact, rather than a
contrast between weak and strong observational accuracy.

It follows that the property “sufficient to determine the readings”
does not uniquely identify a present region. Increasing the collection
of target readings to the entire deterministic development still does
not make one Cauchy surface uniquely sufficient: each complete Cauchy
data set fixes the same development, given its law and maximal model
category. The earlier dense-data argument supplies another reason not
to equate exact information with a privileged physical extent.

No ontology follows from the following two moves either. Taking all
events in a sufficient region as real does not show that every event
outside it is unreal. Taking the smallest region in some admissible
family as real makes the choice of that family and its necessity
criterion part of the philosophical argument. Smoothness of the metric
does not settle either choice.

## 3. Why a constitutive account can answer this objection

A defender may say that an initial-data surface encodes a process but
does not physically sustain it. A living system, bound state or interacting
whole has a dynamics with a characteristic extent; constitution concerns
that activity rather than the information from which a mathematician
can reconstruct it. This is a substantive answer, not a misuse of a
uniqueness theorem.

Our response should therefore be to ask for the constitutive criterion,
not to insist that the surface must be the real present. The criterion
should identify the relevant unity independently of the desired inventory,
explain which interactions or constraints make its parts one system,
and show why their physical extent has the proposed ontological role.
There may be several adequate grounds; uniqueness is required only if
the theory claims a uniquely determined present for that system.

At the universe scale, an initial-data description can be complete without
being a concrete universe-stage. Conversely, a concrete constitutive
region need not be identified just by its predictive completeness.
That is why the whole-history versus stage issue in the existing article
cannot be settled by our scalar-control or Cauchy-rigidity theorem alone.

## 4. A correction to an attractive anti-block shortcut

Suppose a proper region R fixes all physical outcomes. An advocate might
infer that the future beyond R need not be concrete: nothing beyond R
is needed for determination. This mistakes explanatory sufficiency for
ontological dispensability. The outcome occurrences can still be part
of what the law describes as actually happening, even if their description
is derivable from R. Derivability is not a general criterion for eliminating
its subject matter.

The symmetric realist shortcut is no better. The complete solution
contains the outcome occurrences in its mathematical event domain, but
that does not by itself establish that all of them exist from a given
standpoint. A growing account can represent what will occur using that
domain without endorsing the realist's current-inventory interpretation.
The two shortcuts rely on opposite versions of the same missing bridge.

The question is which interpretation gives a better account of the
system's constitution and temporal character, not which representation
can contain or derive its trajectory.

## 5. How maximality enters the constitution problem

The uniqueness of a complete future uses a model category. A proper smooth
restriction can obey the same local equations and induce the same Cauchy
data while ending before a target. MAXIMALITY_AND_EXISTENTIAL_OPENNESS.md
distinguishes that possibility from the maximal-development comparison.

Accordingly, a statement that R is dynamically sufficient must say whether
it determines values where both models exist, existence through a specified
duration, or the maximal development. These claims differ. A constitution
argument that relies on one of them cannot quietly borrow the others.

This gives the universe-stage question a more precise structure:

* Which physical extent or data specify the unity?
* Which admissible continuations or endings does the theory permit?
* Which interpretation connects that unity and possibility space to
  concrete existence from a standpoint?

A time parameter or a foliation may help with the first question but does
not answer all three. A theorem excluding a preferred time under chosen
symmetries likewise does not exclude every proper constitutive region.
Those alternatives require their own arguments.

## 6. A fair burden on the existing article

The current article explicitly labels its universe-unity premise U and
its stage-family premise S. Those are philosophical bridges. They should
remain visible rather than being relabelled as consequences of hyperbolic
evolution. If a defender denies S and offers a proper constitutive region
without a global sequence of universe-stages, the article must evaluate
that positive account. Failure to select a stage does not, by itself,
prove that every interaction in the full history is the only possible
constitutive ground.

Likewise Brading's account should not be assimilated to the simple rule
“whatever determines the future exists.” Our objection concerns that
possible reconstruction, and the requirement that the constitutive-to-
ontological step be defended. The original source claims and readings
remain those audited for the article; the new spacetime results do not
upgrade their source depth or establish its priority.

## 7. A positive target for an ontology argument

The next useful positive argument would establish that a specified unity
has a constitutive temporal extent for reasons independent of the desired
answer to the existence debate. It would then defend why this extent
exhausts, or does not exhaust, what is concrete for that system. The
strongest rival should receive the same physical dynamics and all the
same successful predictions.

A successful comparison could favour a restricted present if it explains
a genuine unity or temporal phenomenon that the unrestricted account
cannot recover without extra commitments. It could favour event realism
if the proposed restriction adds no such achievement while sacrificing
an independently warranted concrete-relations explanation. Neither
outcome follows simply from counting primitives or choosing a name.

My present judgment is that determination-based arguments have reached
their limit as ontology selectors. Constitution and the interpretation
of actual occurrence now carry the burden. This is a reason to change
the philosophical target, rather than to abandon the mathematical work
or to claim that all ontologies are equally good.

## 8. Concrete next tests

First choose one physical unity and one agreed explanatory target. State
the constitutive criterion before selecting its present region. Test
two predictively sufficient extents that differ constitutionally, rather
than merely two descriptions of the same data. Check whether the claimed
constitution needs the full temporal history, a proper process interval,
or an independently motivated stage. Repeat the explanatory comparison
with a strong rival inventory, preserving its tense and grounding rules.

If no independent criterion is available, record that as the exact missing
premise. A further future-control theorem will not supply it merely by
being more technically difficult. If a criterion succeeds, a focused
prior-work audit and independent philosophical review must precede a
new ontology or novelty claim. The current manuscripts are left unchanged.
