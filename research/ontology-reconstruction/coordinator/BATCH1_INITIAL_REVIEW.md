## 1. Equivalence lane: useful separation, several repairs required

Read RESULT §§0–6 and ran its standard-library poset enumeration. All its
finite checks pass: the base `a<b`, with isolated c, is rigid and has three
linear extensions; `c is born before b` varies across those extensions.
This is a sound bounded example of information lost under forgetting.

However:

1. **The displayed causal-past definition is wrong.** Section 4 says
   `J⁻(b) = {x : x≪b or x=b}`. That is the other candidate,
   `I⁻(b)∪{b}`, and omits the nonvertex null cone. On Minkowski space a
   correct chronological-language definition is

       J⁻(b) = {x : for all y, (b≪y implies x≪y)}.

   Causal-past x passes by causal/chronological composition. If x is not
   in the causal past of b, a sufficiently small vertical future
   displacement y of b still fails x≪y. Thus the formula separates the two
   advertised candidates. Repair this before using the definability claim.

2. **The poset demonstrates absence of uniform recovery, not absolute
   inexpressibility in its fixed finite base.** The elements are individually
   definable: a is the unique element with a strict successor, b the unique
   element with a strict predecessor, and c the unique isolated element.
   Therefore each particular linear order can be defined by a finite
   disjunction of these element descriptions. What fails is a *single*
   base-language definition recovering the chosen growth order across all
   three expansions. The worker's varying-truth argument establishes this
   latter claim, not its broader language that the tenseless base cannot
   state any such claim. Retain the information-loss example with that scope.

3. **The claimed 1+1 failure is unsupported and can be repaired directly.**
   Although the full chronological-automorphism group is larger in 1+1,
   it contains translations, orthochronous boosts, positive dilations and
   spatial reflection. The origin stabilizer is still transitive on the
   entire nonzero past null cone: reflection exchanges its two rays and
   boosts/dilations adjust magnitude. Since chronological inclusion and
   the imposed bound `E_b⊆J⁻(b)` leave only the null boundary undecided,
   the same two-case argument works there. A larger group cannot create
   additional invariant subsets of an already transitive subgroup orbit.
   The dimension≥3 bounded statement survives, but its stated contrasting
   1+1 census does not. No full Zeeman classification is needed for this
   orbit argument.

4. **Causal boundedness is stronger than nonuniversality.** Explicitly
   assuming `E_b⊆J⁻(b)` already excludes eternalism, a bow tie containing
   spacelike points, and other candidates. The resulting binary cannot
   select among all temporal ontologies. The worker mostly acknowledges
   the limited family; retain that warning in any synthesis.

5. **The general logical map needs its own source audit.** The result
   switches between fixed-model invariance, theory-wide invariance, and
   per-model definability; these must not be identified without their
   different quantifiers. Coordinator fetch of Barrett's PhilPapers PDF

## 2. Bridge lane: distinguish an unentailed premise from a circular one

The useful result is an explicit inventory of where additional existence,
constitution or explanatory-role premises enter. The surviving symmetric
anchoring test is a proposal, not a result selecting an ontology.

Section 1.3 goes too far in saying physical supervenience cannot be stated
neutrally and is therefore circular. One can stipulate a physical model as
a representational reduct, add a distinct existence predicate, and ask
whether all admissible expansions of each reduct agree on that predicate.
That is a substantive uniqueness premise. It does not become formally
circular merely because the mathematical reduct represents future events.
Whether that reduct already has an ontological reading is an additional
interpretative question.

Nor do the two alternatives E_all and E_past refute supervenience outright:
each defines a different supervenience function within its own model class.
They show that covariance and the other shared structural requirements do
not determine *which* function or interpretation is correct. A class that
admits both expansions violates uniqueness, while a rival that asserts
supervenience may reject precisely that class. Repair the verdict to
“the physics considered here does not entail or uniquely specify this
bridge”; reserve “circular” for an argument that actually assumes its desired
existence conclusion.

Likewise the clipped-spacetime example establishes a failure of inference
from matched past information to equal future lifetime. It does not by
itself show that completeness has no observational or predictive content
under every protocol or comparison class.

Section 7.3's proposed anti-redescription criteria are not yet established:

  about explanatory priority or metaphysical grounding must therefore
  identify the extra structure that the relevant translation fails to
  preserve; “translations do not map derivations” is not a general reason.
* The asserted “iff” collapse conditions are proposed criteria without a
  demonstrated necessity/sufficiency proof. Do not promote them to a result.
* The Bell-nonlocality exposure argument inherits an unresolved premise
  from STRONGEST_VIEW rather than independently establishing it. A
  signalling clause and a nonsignalling dependence clause are distinct.
  Showing that an ontology loses its motivation or requires revision is
  also weaker than proving it collapses into eternalism. This is an open
  modal-interpretation argument, not a discriminator already established
  by known quantum theory.
