# Can reality stop growing? Maximality and existential openness

30 October 2026. Coordinator philosophical argument for adversarial review.
This uses the new smoothness result and older clipping/rigidity arguments;
it does not assume the whole interpolation theorem has passed review.
No novelty or preferred ontology is established. The central proposal is
a constraint on an argument for an ontology, not a new definition of time.

**Controlling qualification:** the mathematical exclusion of regular cuts
holds within the stipulated maximal-development class. It does not refute
primitive cessation or establish that an available continuation must become
concrete. The argument below defends a methodological premise for a physical
comparison; it does not claim that C&R's intended modal space has been closed.
“Complete history” below means a candidate whole-world history rather than
a stage. It does not mean geodesic completeness or current concrete
existence of every event. Those are separate claims.

## 1. The question the technical improvement brings into view

The strongest next question is: **which complete histories count as
physically possible, and why must a regular history continue?**

The smoothness work puts the chosen scattering limits in a complete,
inextendible MGHD category. That prevents an artificial missing future
from accounting for their behaviour. But selecting maximal developments
is a substantive model-class choice. A local differential equation holds
on a proper open restriction of any solution too. It cannot by itself
distinguish a complete world from a regular world cut off early.

This matters for an existential-openness argument. If the proposed
openness is that no later reality need occur at all, one must explain
whether such endings are admitted by the physical theory, supplied by
an additional metaphysical possibility, or merely obtained by deleting
the future from a mathematical model. These are different claims.

## 2. A precise restriction example

Let (M,g,φ) be any smooth representative of the compact-profile class,
with global center-normalized time T. Fix T₀<T_* and restrict to

    M_<={p∈M:T(p)<T_*}.

The restriction is an open smooth Einstein–scalar spacetime. It has the
same complete Cauchy data on {T=T₀}. Its constant-T slices remain Cauchy:
the speed bound is finite on every compact ambient T interval, so there
is no spatial escape or interior endpoint at finite T. An inextendible
curve in M_< has past T→−∞ and future T→T_*, and crosses T₀ exactly once.
Equivalently, use that the restriction is a past set and the standard
time-slice argument applies. The central unit geodesic reaches
its missing boundary in the finite proper duration T_*−T₀.

The field equations have not failed at any point of M_<. Yet this is a
proper subdevelopment of M, so it is not maximal. A scalar reading at a
later proper time is defined in M and undefined in M_<. The disagreement
concerns future extent, not a different scalar value at a shared point.

For T_* after the launch b, the full pointed causal past of b is
unchanged. This is ordinary restriction, not a novel construction, and
not a black-hole or singularity mechanism. The older sharper example in
[COMMON_EXTENSION_REPAIR.md](../../lawful-completions/coordinator/COMMON_EXTENSION_REPAIR.md)
even preserves its stipulated whole potential realm in a nonmaximal
vacuum class. The present example isolates the role of a smooth final
cut in an otherwise regular physical history.

M_< has no last event on the excluded boundary. It therefore does not
model a theory requiring a final included moment. It shows that the local
equations and common Cauchy data alone do not establish the chosen
maximality/extent condition. A last-moment model needs its own boundary
semantics and field equations; it cannot be obtained by changing the
description of this open cut.

## 3. Three principles with different strengths

**Local lawfulness:** every included point satisfies the field equations.
Both M and M_< satisfy this. It is too weak to fix future extent.

**Continuation is possible:** a regular end with no specified boundary
obstruction admits a mathematical extension satisfying the same laws.
The inclusion M_<→M witnesses this. Possibility of extension does not
entail that the extension is actual or compulsory.

**Maximal-world admissibility:** complete histories representing physically
possible worlds must be maximal developments, unless an additional
physical end/boundary rule is stated. This excludes M_< as a complete
world of the unchanged theory. It does not exclude it as an initial
development or a description of a stage.

Only the third principle closes the artificial-cut option. It must not
be presented as a consequence of the first. MGHD theory proves the
existence/uniqueness of maximal developments; identifying that maximal
category with the admissible complete histories is an interpretation
of the physical theory. Conversely, retaining arbitrary restrictions
as complete worlds changes what is held fixed in a determination claim.

## 4. A case for maximal-world admissibility, and its limit

There is a defensible methodological argument for the third principle.
When the stipulated theory gives a unique continuation and supplies no
boundary mechanism, choosing a shorter domain imposes an additional
contingent restriction on the complete-history class. This need not be
a new concrete object or a physical cause. The local equations themselves
do admit the restriction as a nonmaximal solution; they do not select it
as a competing maximal prediction. Admitting every stop while advertising the original theory
as complete leaves its existence claims radically weaker than its
usual development interpretation. A proposal to do so should say what
licenses the stop and whether that is a law, primitive possibility or
limitation of the theory.

This is not a deductive refutation of brute endings. A philosopher may
hold that regular reality can simply cease, with no further law or
cause. Nor must every contingency have a cause or every model difference
be an extra object. The narrower point is about explanatory attribution:
solving the local equations does not decide which domain restrictions
represent complete possible worlds. An argument that growth explains an open future by admitting
these endings owes this extra modal commitment explicitly.

The argument also does not establish that every physical model is
complete in proper time. An MGHD can be singular/incomplete. Our selected
class has an additional completeness theorem; other Einstein–matter
classes do not. Maximality and completeness must remain distinct.

## 5. Why accepting maximality does not concede eternalism

A complete mathematical history and a currently existing concrete
inventory play different roles. A growing-block account can use M to
represent everything that does, did or will happen, while assigning a
proper event inventory at each standpoint. A tenseless realist can
interpret the same actual occurrences as concrete without that
standpoint restriction. The representation alone decides neither.

In particular, a proponent of growth may accept that the world must
continue under the physical theory and that its continuation is fixed
by complete Cauchy data, while denying that later events exist from
the current standpoint. This gives growth with a physically determined
future. Its consistency at the level of the physical and point-domain
policies is sufficient to refute the inference from growth alone to
physical indeterminism; it is not a model of every axiom of a named
metaphysical theory.

The converse distinction is just as important. A truncated manifold used
to describe what exists *so far* is not automatically a complete world
in which nothing will happen later. Conflating a growing stage with an
ended history makes the alleged existential openness arise from the
representation rather than from an argued modal claim.

## 6. Three forms of openness that the new construction separates

| Question | Comparison required | Status in the selected class |
|---|---|---|
| Can finite future values differ with the observer's full causal past fixed? | Same pointed causal past and incoming mass across admitted completions | Conditional open-range result; integrated control review remains open |
| Can they differ with the whole open bow-tie metric and scalar fixed? | Onto realm matching with marked extension/germ | Rigidity in the smooth MGHD category, under the explicit matching contract |
| Can the target occurrence fail to occur because reality stops before it? | A class admitting different future extents | Excluded by completeness in this selected class; arbitrary regular cuts lie outside its maximal-world category |

The second row has an especially important philosophical consequence.
The whole bow-tie realm contains an entire early Cauchy slice, including
exterior radiation. It can determine later readings even though its
point-domain policy excludes future events. Thus determination does
not by itself force a future-inclusive inventory. The first row, in
turn, does not force a future-exclusive inventory. Both inferences fail
at the same missing bridge.

The sample event at each finite central proper time exists in every

does not assert a material detector or that the event exists *now* in
any candidate ontology. Variation of its scalar value is different from
variation of the event's occurrence or temporal extent.

## 7. The source-based rival is stronger than a prediction argument

Correia–Rosenkranz's reply to Torre distinguishes location-based causal
indeterminism from existence-based indeterminism. Their proposed
asymmetry permits later growth to fail while retaining what already
exists. They also expressly refrain from treating that possibility as
an independent argument establishing GBT. Our target is therefore the
modal class needed by such a possibility, not a claim that their text
infers GBT from observer ignorance.
[Replies to Critics, §7, pp.480–482](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370321332_Replies_to_Critics).

Their chapter-7 determinism formulas use a nomological necessity operator,
as recorded in the original chapter audit. One should not automatically
reclassify the entire proposal as a purely metaphysical possibility exempt
from physical comparison. The missing correspondence is between that
operator, their existence-based world-states, and a specified physical
category. An explicitly broader metaphysical claim may range beyond this
Einstein–scalar class. Our completeness result cannot disprove it. But
if an argument invokes this physical theory as evidence, it must state
where and why its possibility space is enlarged. A possibility permitted
by an ontology is not yet a distinctive phenomenon explained by it.

## 8. Provisional philosophical conclusion

The strongest defensible claim is that **the scope of possibility is a
separate commitment from the extent of present existence**. An ontology
does not earn an explanatory advantage merely by assigning a different
boundary to the current inventory, or by admitting regular ended worlds
that its physical comparison class excluded.

This is a positive constraint on a temporal ontology: specify its law-
relative possibilities, distinguish stages from complete histories,
and explain whether its existence distinction changes what can happen
or changes how the same occurrences are interpreted. A view may do
either, but it owes different arguments for each. Determination cannot
serve as a neutral definition of concrete existence.

I provisionally favour maximal-world admissibility for comparisons
claiming to hold a governing Einstein–scalar theory fixed, unless an
explicit boundary principle is part of that theory. This is a defended
methodological choice, open to the brute-ending reply, and compatible
with more than one temporal ontology. It narrows the live philosophical
dispute instead of pretending that the physical theorem has settled it.

## 9. Defeat conditions and next work

This argument weakens if a source supplies an independently motivated
physical end rule compatible with the stipulated theory, if a proposed
openness claim never intended a nomological range, or if the complete
inventory matching contract was stronger than the rival's actual claim.
The mathematical contrast fails if the uniform cone, ambient Cauchy
transport or field-preserving MGHD hypotheses fail; those have a separate
bounded geometric audit. No argument here equates all metaphysical
possibility with general-relativistic models.

The next positive ontology test should hold the physical model and modal
class fixed, then identify a target for which the disputed existence
distinction performs an indispensable explanatory step. That is the
existing EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST.md applied to a concrete case, with the
maximality and truth-policy commitments now included in the accounting.
