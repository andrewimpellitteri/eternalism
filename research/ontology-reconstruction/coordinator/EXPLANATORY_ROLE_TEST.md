29 October 2026. Coordinator version 2. Elementary restricted construction and philosophical proposal; no novelty claim, full-theory consistency result, or accepted ontology preference. This strengthens and narrows ROLE_OF_EXISTENCE.md. It retracts any presumption that calling a view 'event realism' already earns it a default advantage.

## 1. Correction to the earlier argument

The earlier argument was too quick to describe growth or relative existence as an additional distinction. Theories may trade concrete inventory against primitive tense, or derive an existence relation from already accepted causal structure. They need not share an event-realist ontology as a common core plus an optional growth predicate. Conversely, treating every actual-history representation as concrete is itself a realist commitment.

Therefore fewer written predicates, fewer primitive terms, or more uniform event quantification does not by itself rank the rivals. A numerical measure of theoretical cost has not been supplied, and should not be invented. Event realism remains a candidate, not an entitled default.

The improved target is an identified explanatory dependence: does explaining a specified phenomenon require the disputed ontological commitment? A negative answer for one explanandum does not show the commitment has no role anywhere.

## 2. A deliberately small comparison

Use a finite line of evaluation labels T={0,1,2}, ordered in the usual way, and event labels e_0,e_1,e_2. The labels are mathematical comparison devices. Their inclusion in the metalanguage is not an assertion that their concrete referents exist at every evaluation label.

At each event let a modeled detector have a value v_i in {0,1}. Define a restricted language Q_i: 'the detector at label i reads 1'. Its target is fixed across evaluation labels; future/past vocabulary may be supplied by comparing i with the evaluation label b. Stipulate an outcome-matching truth policy:

    True_at_b(Q_i) iff v_i=1.

Compare three event-domain policies:

    E_all(b) = {e_0,e_1,e_2}
    E_grow(b) = {e_i : i<=b}
    E_present(b) = {e_b}.

These are minimal domain policies, not reconstructions of complete named theories. In particular no inference about relativistic presentism, persistence, branching, or the logic of arbitrary propositions is intended. At b=0, e_2 is admitted only by E_all; at b=2, e_0 is admitted by E_all and E_grow but not E_present. The disagreement is in event existence, not the valuations.

For each i let f_i name the simple field-value occurrence associated with e_i. The same existence policy applies to its concrete witness. At label i, f_i exists under all three policies. For a true Q_i evaluated at b, provide the restricted explanatory clause:

    At evaluation i: the truth-at-b of Q_i is explained by f_i obtaining.

For false Q_i, apply the analogous clause to not-Q_i, using the value-0 occurrence. The truth-at-b parameter remains fixed when the explanatory evaluation moves to i. This is an assigned grounding clause, not a physical derivation of actualization or a proof that the assigned dependence is metaphysically correct.

### Domain-insensitivity observation

For every b,i, the witness required by the displayed clause belongs to E(i) under all three policies. Thus the schema

    At some evaluation c, an existing occurrence explains Q_i's truth-at-b

is satisfied for each true atom by choosing c=i, regardless of which policy is chosen. The same holds for false atoms via their negations. Neither the outcome-matching truth policy nor this weak distributed-grounding schema fixes the off-diagonal answers e_i in E(b) when i!=b.

The proof uses only self-inclusion at the occurrence's own label. No currently-existing future witness is inferred. In contrast, requiring the witness to exist at b immediately distinguishes the policies; that is an extra contemporaneous-grounding premise, not a consequence of the displayed schema.

This is a restricted independence example. The designated valuation is shared metamodel information, not a selection mechanism available in a present. The explanatory clause does not settle the reference, quantification, constitution of facts, negative generalizations, or full free logic of any rival. Non-atomic grounding principles, existence-dependent propositions, a stronger factivity clause, or constraints linking grounding to current domains might distinguish the policies. Those additions require separate examination.

## 3. What the example earns philosophically

It defeats this narrow inference: 'My theory accommodates outcome-matching truth and later/earlier grounds; therefore its temporal inventory is preferable.' All three displayed inventories accommodate the restricted package.

It also defeats the reverse shortcut that the package must secretly include eternalism. The witness is existentially available at the explanatory evaluation; its availability from the original evaluation is not required.

Neither result proves that the three complete ontologies are equivalent or that none can explain truth better. Relevance, intimacy, dependence direction, reference and the admissibility of the explanatory operator are possible differences. The improvement is to locate exactly where a superior account would have to differ instead of claiming superiority from mere consistency with the package.

This comparison is our construction, in the neighborhood of already known grounding disputes; no priority is claimed. The project's original chapter audit supplies the source-faithful warning about 'sometimes' versus current grounds. It does not certify this construction as the authors' formal semantics.

## 4. Replace generic simplicity with an explanatory-role test

For a particular pair of complete theories A and B:

1. Specify the explanandum in terms both parties can recognize. If they disagree about the explanandum itself, record that disagreement instead of silently choosing one description.
2. Display A's explanatory steps. Mark every use of its distinctive existence, tense or grounding claims, including uses hidden in reference or quantifier clauses.
3. Seek a B-account that preserves the agreed target without those claims. A truth-conditional translation alone is not enough: it must preserve the aspect of dependence being offered as the explanation.
4. If replacement succeeds, A has not established an advantage for THAT explanandum from the distinctive commitment. If it fails, identify the exact obstruction; do not merely say the rival cannot explain passage because it lacks passage.
5. Repeat in the other direction. Only then consider wider explanatory integration, independently justified primitives and competing costs.

This is a test of a claimed explanatory advantage, not a general theory of explanation and not a decisive parsimony rule. Both theories might succeed without one dominating. A genuine disagreement about which facts are fundamental may remain even when all derived truths agree.

## 5. Apply it to four proposed advantages

| Proposed achievement | What would show a distinctive role? | What does not yet show it? |
|---|---|---|
| Future truth | A relevant truth/ground explanation that a rival cannot reproduce without changing an independently motivated commitment | Giving the displayed outcome-matching valuation and existential-at-some-time witness |
| Open future | A precisely stated modal contrast that depends on the ontology under fixed laws and possibility rules | Mere lack of access, absence of present grounds, or absence of current concrete future events |
| Passage/experience | An independently characterized experiential or metaphysical target and a substantive explanatory link to growth | Calling the target 'the experience of literal becoming' and then declaring becoming indispensable |
| Persistence and cross-temporal relations | A coherent account of the same object's changes and relations with a demonstrable rival obstruction | Merely listing worldtube parts, or merely asserting that an enduring object changes |

The modal row must retain C&R's actual proposal: their chapter-7 existence-openness options concern possible empty later times or failure of later times to exist, not simply indeterminism or unsettled detector values. Whether those possibilities are legitimate under a chosen physical category is a real question. Replacing them by our own settlement definition would evade the rival.

## 6. The positive case that survives

A conditional case for event realism can now be stronger because it is less sweeping:

- It directly treats actual occurrences and their relations as concrete, giving a clear candidate account of cross-temporal physical processes.
- It is a good choice IF this account provides the required explanations and rival restrictions introduce independent commitments without an independently supported explanatory benefit.
- It loses that advantage if the unrestricted concrete interpretation is itself less justified, if it borrows primitive becoming in an explanation, or if a rival supplies a better integrated account.

The directness claim is a characterization of the proposed interpretation. Calling it better is an abductive judgment requiring comparison; it is not established by uniqueness of field evolution. The present note does not show that all target phenomena are adequately explained by event realism.

The philosophical question has therefore become more precise: which theory gives the best independently motivated explanation of what actually happens, its temporal character, and its truth, with its interpretation of concrete existence included in the accounting? We should prefer a demonstrated explanatory achievement over a blanket default.

## 7. Most useful source and next adversarial step

Priority full text: Fabrice Correia and Giovanni Merlo, 'Cross-temporal grounding', Analytic Philosophy 65(3) (2024), 333-352, DOI 10.1111/phib.12287 (first online 22 February 2023). Publisher abstract available; full arguments and extended logic remain unread. Author publication page confirms the item but provides no exposed download link in the retrieved page. The classroom handout found in search is not a substitute for the paper.

The exact passages needed are the arguments from intimacy and past explanation, their account of the legitimacy of cross-temporal explanation, and the constraints in the extended logic. Test whether their explanatory demands or logic invalidate the small comparison, and whether any argument extends to future-directed grounds. Past/future symmetry must not be presumed.

Until that source arrives, use the original chapter/reply audit for attributed rival claims and treat this construction as a deliberately weak conditional example. A failed extension of the example to their full system would be useful progress, not something to conceal.
