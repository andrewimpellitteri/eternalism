# Which actual facts survive a change of law?

31 October 2026. Elaboration of the one recovery in [RESULT.md](RESULT.md),
not a second ontology proposal. The question is Tooley's own challenge in
§4.1, printed pp79–84, and §4.5.3, pp107–111. No novelty is claimed.

## The source case

Tooley compares adding a law R-to-P to a world with alternating P/Q chains.
One alternative retains the earlier R and changes an infinite downstream
chain; another removes one R and retains the later chain. Ordinary event-count
similarity favors the latter. He argues the former is the correct alternative,
and asks why earlier history deserves that priority. A growing account appeals
to a stage where R is actual but the conflicting later events are not.

This is a serious explanatory challenge. A rival cannot answer it merely by
agreeing on the actual trace or by asserting that either world is logically
possible. It needs a reason to prefer the relevant comparison. Conversely,
retaining an infinite dependent chain is not automatically preferable just
because it retains more things that actually exist: that would import a
particular metric of similarity, not derive one from existence.

## A concrete rival explanation

In the finite example, hold the exogenous values x,z fixed and compare:

    old law setting: Y=Z,
    new law setting: Y=Z OR X.

At x=1,z=0, the old Y=0 and new Y=1. The root states are retained, while
the effect changes. At x=0, Y does not change. These are alternative global
law settings; the example asserts no moment when a law literally switches on.

The rival's reason is **directed dependence**: the modified mechanism receives
X as an input and determines Y, whereas it does not determine X. Keeping the
input mechanism fixed and replacing the output mechanism makes the relevant
contrast intelligible. This is a modal claim with content beyond the actual
trace. A non-Humean interpretation can take that dependence as real; it need
not reconstruct it from correlations or causal forks. The probability version
also holds a root distribution fixed, which is an additional family assumption.

This proposal is conditional on the mechanism boundary and permitted variation
being warranted. A factorization chosen just to get the desired answer would
not explain it. The ordinary probability calculation verifies consequences of
the proposed modular comparison; it does not make that comparison true.

## The actuality explanation needs a boundary too

“Retain what is actual” cannot be an unrestricted instruction across alternative
laws. Consider a different example with an earlier state Z=1, a later state
X=1, and an additional law requiring Z to cause X=0. If the new law and Z are
retained, X=1 cannot also be retained. X was actual in the original history;
its actuality there does not decide whether that history is the comparison
appropriate to the new law. A growing alternative can retain Z and develop
X=0 instead, without ever erasing its own past.

This is a compatibility control, not a counterexample to Tooley's specific
X-to-Y postulates. It changes a mechanism upstream of X, whereas the earlier
example changes one downstream. That difference is exactly the point: a
cross-law comparison must identify causal roles and compatible antecedent
conditions. Bare actuality in the original world is not the whole rule.

The native follow-up correctly treats this as a shared compatibility boundary:
Tooley's account can also preserve the earlier Z and allow the later X to
differ. The control alone shows no defect in his full rule and no advantage
for the rival's explanation.

Tooley can reply that actuality at the relevant stage provides a genuine
ground for preserving those antecedents, and directed governance determines
which stage is relevant. The rival can reply that governance already provides
the needed direction and boundary, with all events equally real. Neither
reply is a complete metaphysics, and their explanatory grounds need comparison.

## A more demanding criterion of explanatory gain

A successful growth argument should identify an independently defended feature
of actuality that explains the privileged law-variation rule, rather than
describe that rule by adding a changing inventory. A successful tenseless
response should independently defend the real directional dependence and its
modular boundary, rather than label a preferred factorization “causal”.

The comparison should ask:

- Which actual law-change counterfactuals does the proposed ground determine?
- Which facts or distributions may be held fixed, and which variations are
  inconsistent with the other laws and retained background?
- What explains the direction if the statistical history has no helpful forks
  or entropy gradient, as in Tooley's simple-world objection?
- Does the account explain why the rule is appropriate, or just entail an
  answer after the desired rule is assumed?
- Does its appeal to causation, temporal order and actuality provide mutually
  informative constraints, or rely on the disputed bridge twice? Mutual
  dependence is not automatically a vicious circle; the analysis and grounding
  roles must be specified before that criticism is justified.

These are comparative explanatory questions, not demands that every ontology
produce a different experimental prediction. The finite test supports a
restricted conclusion: causal/modal asymmetry and changing inventory must be
distinguished, and their proposed explanatory relation has not been settled
by the displayed probability identities. It neither establishes explanatory
parity nor gives a default victory to the account with fewer primitives.
