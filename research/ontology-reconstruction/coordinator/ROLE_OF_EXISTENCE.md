# What work does existence do? A provisional argument for event realism

> **Version-2 correction:** [EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST.md](EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST.md) replaces the suggestion that event realism has earned a default advantage with a symmetric test of particular explanatory claims. The conditional argument below remains a candidate, not a justified overall ranking.

29 October 2026. Coordinator philosophical argument for adversarial review. It is neither a physical theorem nor an accepted ontology verdict. No novelty claim. Source depths are recorded in sources.jsonl; the central comparative argument is ours.

## 1. The positive hypothesis

Take the actual events described by an adequate physical theory to belong to concrete reality, wherever they occur in the actual history. Interpret an observer's present, access, records and anticipation through relations within that reality. This is a provisional form of tenseless event realism. It does not say that all mathematically allowed histories are actual, that evolution is deterministic, or that temporal order and change are illusions. It also does not settle whether persistence uses temporal parts or enduring objects.

This hypothesis deserves investigation because it gives one concrete arena for occurrences, causal relations and cross-temporal explanations without separately assigning events an additional changing entitlement to exist. The proposal is abductive: it must justify treating the theory's event representation realistically. A completed mathematical solution, by itself, cannot perform that justification.

The principal challenge is therefore explanatory, not pictorial: what does a fundamental changing/relative existence distinction explain that the event structure and its relations do not?

## 2. The proposed selection principle

> When two accounts explain the same specified phenomena, prefer the one whose fundamental commitments do not include an additional distinction unless that distinction has an independently defended explanatory role.

This is a defeasible methodological principle. It concerns unearned primitive commitments, not a numerical count of events, nor a rule that unobservable things are unreal. Laws, probabilities and grounding can have explanatory roles without supplying new detector readings.

Three reasons support the principle. First, an account should explain its target rather than redescribe it with a new primitive. Saying that passage is explained by becoming requires an account of becoming's content beyond the original passage claim. Second, an unconstrained primitive permits incompatible accounts with equally good empirical fits; an independent explanatory role supplies a reason to choose. Third, the principle treats realism and restriction symmetrically: interpreting represented events as concrete needs justification too. It is no victory to omit a 'now' predicate but retain a gratuitous realism thesis.

Its limitation is explicit: some facts may be fundamental without further explanation. The principle supplies a presumption in a comparison, not a proof that all unexplained primitives are illegitimate. A rival can defeat it by showing that our preferred explanation already presupposes the distinction we propose to omit.

## 3. The argument, premise by premise

1. The target phenomena include physical change, causal and temporal relations, records, anticipation, persistence, future truth, and the experienced present.
2. A successful event-realist account explains these without a further fundamental distinction between actually occurring events that exist and actually occurring events that do not exist from the selected standpoint.
3. A rival retains the relevant event/relational explanations, or supplies adequate alternatives, and introduces a fundamental existence/growth distinction.
4. If that distinction supplies no independent explanatory benefit and incurs an additional primitive commitment, the selection principle favors event realism in this comparison.
5. Therefore event realism is provisionally preferable to SUCH a rival.

Premises 2 and 3 are substantial open burdens. They are not consequences of relativity, screening, causal-set growth, or scalar uniqueness. In particular, premise 2 has not been established for experience or persistence. The argument is deliberately conditional on these burdens, rather than calling its conclusion a discovery of physics.

The conclusion is stronger than 'all accounts fit': it identifies a default hypothesis and a precise kind of successful objection. It is weaker than a universal refutation of growing blocks or local grounding.

## 4. Why determination does not settle the question

Suppose complete admissible data and laws uniquely determine tomorrow's detector reading. That tells us which reading follows. It does not, without further premises, tell us whether tomorrow's event is concrete from today's standpoint, whether the truth is grounded today or later, or whether the future is objectively coming into existence.

Conversely, two models with identical observer pasts can have different future readings because exterior data differ. That is not an actualization mechanism, an indeterminism proof, or a reason that the future fails to exist. Our technical results map determining bases. They do not independently explain the ontological status of the determined events.

This blocks a tempting argument on both sides: neither certainty nor uncertainty supplies a criterion of concrete existence.

## 5. The strongest growing-block reply

The rival says that existence/growth does explain something: the difference between what has happened and what has yet to happen. A tenseless order describes earlier and later events but, the rival argues, cannot account for their becoming actual. It can also allow future truths whose grounds arrive later without admitting future concrete events now.

That is a serious reply. Our prior chapter/reply audit already blocks the demand that all grounds be currently available. Correia and Merlo's later paper also expressly develops cross-temporal grounding for past-directed facts; the publisher abstract has been checked, but its full arguments remain unread here. Its past-directed results must not be transferred to future-directed grounding by an unsupported symmetry inference. [Primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/phib.12287).

Our counterquestion is narrower: which part of the target is inadequately explained without growth? If it is causal production, identify an actual deficit in the event-realist account of production. If it is future contingency, distinguish openness from existence. If it is experience, identify the feature of experience and a connection to existence/growth that does not merely assert that experience detects becoming.

If the answer is that actualization is irreducible, that is a possible metaphysical stopping point. It is no longer an explanation derived from the physics. Nor is it refuted merely for being irreducible. The comparison becomes whether this primitive is independently credible enough to justify its cost.

## 6. Why later grounding is not 'secret eternalism'

A future event may be invoked under a tensed explanatory operator without being asserted to exist from the present standpoint. Rejecting that possibility requires an existence-entailing account of the relevant operator. One cannot obtain it merely by writing the explanation as an ordinary two-place relation in our preferred metalanguage.

Wawer's primary abstract directly identifies temporally local grounding as the disputed assumption and treats semantics and tensed ontology as substantially separable. That blocks the generic inference from true future claims to an already existing future. Only the abstract and introductory material available in the primary search result were consulted in this pass; no whole-paper verification is claimed. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.2478/disp-2021-0023).

This changes the comparative argument. Later-grounded future truths need not be additional primitive present information encoding the outcome. They may be derivative in the rival's theory. We should not count them as gratuitous duplicates merely because our model of their truth consults a complete history.

But successfully explaining future truth does not yet show that growth does explanatory work: the account must explain what the changing existence distinction contributes beyond its cross-temporal truth/ground clauses. That is the remaining question, not an inconsistency we have established.

## 7. The strongest local-grounding reply

A local view can identify the reality from b with a causally structured fragment, without adding a preferred global slice. Its existence relation may be definable from physical structure. That rebuts an argument that every non-eternalist ontology adds an extra geometrical object.

The issue then becomes explanatory priority. If causal structure explains which events are real from b, adding the label 'real from' might be an interpretation of existing structure. If instead the relative realities fundamentally ground the global structure, the view owes a directional grounding account. Reconstructing a global model from compatible fragments does not by itself establish that grounding direction.

Event realism must likewise explain why a global concrete interpretation is preferable to the local interpretation. It cannot simply assume that the reconstructed whole is fundamental. A demonstrated local explanation of object persistence, presentness or temporal asymmetry unavailable on the event-realist interpretation would weaken or defeat our provisional preference.

## 8. The strongest experience objection

The event realist has described all experiences as occurrences, but why does experience have this present, renewed character? Providing a temporal ordering of reports does not automatically explain their phenomenal character. Claiming that the whole history exists does not answer that question either.

This objection is a live burden on premise 2. The rival must also show how changing existence produces or explains the relevant character, rather than simply locating the same question in a growing realm. Neither side can win by redescribing the experience using its favored ontology. A serious future investigation must separate psychological models, phenomenological claims and proposed metaphysical explanations; no empirical consciousness claim is established here.

## 9. Why semantic success cannot decide the ontology by itself

MacFarlane's preprint supplies a concrete assessment-sensitive semantics and explicitly distinguishes truth from settledness. Its retrospective example keeps the earlier unsettledness while changing the assessment context. This is an example of a precise semantic alternative, not a derivation of a concrete inventory. The selected primary passages do not establish that his semantics is compulsory. [Author preprint](https://www.johnmacfarlane.net/garden.pdf), 28 January 2007, sections 5–7, especially displayed clauses (20)–(24).

The comparison must therefore evaluate truth policy and ontology separately before asking how they fit. Bivalence does not establish eternalism; gaps do not establish becoming; assessment sensitivity does not establish local fragmentalism.

## 10. Provisional verdict and conditions for revision

My present proposal is to treat tenseless event realism as the baseline to beat on explanatory grounds, while keeping its representational-to-concrete bridge visible. That is an abductive working preference under stated burdens, not a project-wide accepted verdict. It should not guide mathematical task acceptance or source interpretation.

The claim is: replacing changing/relative existence with event occurrence, temporal relations and perspective is attractive IF these resources can explain the targets at least as well. The hardest remaining challenge is whether that replacement loses something essential about actualization, persistence or experience.

Defeat conditions are concrete:

- show a target the baseline cannot explain without borrowing fundamental becoming or relative existence;
- produce a charitable rival with a better independent grounding/persistence account;
- show that the physical event domain is representational bookkeeping whose concrete interpretation is less justified than the rival's ontology;
- show that our anti-idle-primitive principle cannot discriminate these accounts symmetrically;
- or demonstrate an empirical/dynamical role for a privileged temporal structure.

The next philosophical task is therefore to argue about explanatory replacement, not merely to accumulate more future-determination theorems. The four queued agents remain independent; they should challenge this baseline rather than be instructed to defend it.
