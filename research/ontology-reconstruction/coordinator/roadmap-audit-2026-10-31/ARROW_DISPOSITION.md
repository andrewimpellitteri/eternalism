## Retained result and repairs

For an isometry theta of M reversing time orientation, the mirror formula is
valid when pullback is a bijection of the **entire admissible field class** L:

    theta(F^-_L(b)) = F^+_L(theta(b)).

To check it without linearity, take any pair of fields in L agreeing on the
target future base; their pullbacks agree on the original past base. Equality
at x transports to equality at theta(x). Apply the inverse pullback for the
reverse inclusion. No subtraction of solutions is required. The corresponding
Det identity follows by transporting inextendible causal curves.

This establishes a correspondence, not equality of the two fixing sets at b.
A reversing symmetry fixing b yields a transported equality at that base,
theta(F^-_L(b))=F^+_L(b); it still does not identify the two untransformed sets.
The closed-dust example in the manuscript uses different reflected epochs
and is consistent with this qualification. Reversing a theory's orientation
must transport its solution subclass and boundary conditions as well as its
equations. It need not send a selected incoming-scattering family into itself.

Retain the review's replacement proposition and scope paragraph. The broad
claim that equally based fixing must be equally symmetric in one fixed model
is not established by that proposition. The paper's own partial source-depth
labels stay in force.

The Past-Hypothesis expectation is explicitly conjectural in the source.
Reject its offered general rationale, not a physical theorem: coarseness of a
boundary restriction alone gives no bound on exact fixing. The native toy
correctly shows exact-value selection, but explicitly does **not** demonstrate
a change of its own fixing set. The following elementary check fills only that
logical gap; it is not a cosmological example or a priority claim.

## Coarse restriction can enlarge a fixing set without selecting one history

Let M be 1+1 Minkowski spacetime, b=(0,0), u=t-x, and let F,G be nonzero smooth
bumps supported respectively in (1,2) and (3,4), normalized so that

    integral (F')^2 = integral (G')^2 = 1.

Consider the specified globally admissible subclass of wave-equation solutions

    L = {phi_(a,c)(t,x)=a F(t-x)+c G(t-x): a in {0,1}, 0<=c<=1}.

These are smooth solutions of phi_tt-phi_xx=0, with finite conserved energy

    E(phi) = (1/2) integral [(phi_t)^2+(phi_x)^2] dx = a^2+c^2.

Disjoint bump supports remove the cross term. Every member vanishes on I^-(b),
since t<-|x| implies u<0 there. Thus all members agree on the entire base.
At any event with F(u) nonzero, the members with (a,c)=(0,0) and (1,0) differ,
so that event is not in F_L(I^-(b)).

Now restrict the energy on the early Cauchy slice t=-3 to the coarse bin
E<1/2. Conservation gives

    L' = {c G(t-x): 0<=c<1/sqrt(2)}.

Every member of L' is zero at the F-support events. Those events therefore
belong to F_L'(I^-(b)). At G-support events the remaining continuum still
varies. Exact fixing has strictly increased without fixing the entire field
or selecting a single history. More generally L' subset L always implies
F_L'(S) contains F_L(S), because it removes comparison pairs.

The discrete restriction on a was deliberately built into this toy class;
it has no asserted thermodynamic motivation. This is a counterexample to a
general inference from “coarse” to “little exact information”, not to any
particular physical Past-Hypothesis conjecture. A physical claim requires an
actual phase space, boundary set and admissible class. Probabilities additionally
require a measure and a conditioning rule. None is supplied by the fixing
definition alone.
