# Red-team report: actuality, maximality, and openness

30 October 2026. Review of `coordinator/MAXIMALITY_AND_EXISTENTIAL_OPENNESS.md` and `coordinator/PASSAGE_AND_EARLIER_TRUTH.md`. This is an analytic audit, not an ontology vote or a review of the technical Einstein–scalar proofs.

**Files changed:** `AGENT_REPORT.md`, `RESULT.md`, and `sources.jsonl` in this owned directory. **Checks:** read the required project files and brief; read the two target notes; directly read selected Wawer passages on pp. 418–420 and C&R §§6–7, pp. 471–482. No computational checks were needed or run.

## Strongest concern

The maximality note's central bridge is defensible as a methodological preference, but it is not supplied by the field equations or by the MGHD theorem: “complete histories representing physically possible worlds must be maximal developments” (its §3). The note recognizes this in §4, yet its rhetoric sometimes slides from “an artificial cut is not a prediction of these equations” to “an ending adds structure” and then toward excluding that ending from the physical possibility class. Those claims need to stay conditional. The note's own open cut M_< is another nonmaximal smooth globally hyperbolic development of the same initial data, satisfying the same local equations. It is excluded only when the comparison category is stipulated to consist of maximal developments; uniqueness of the maximal development does not erase the nonmaximal development. A brute metaphysical possibility that reality ceases, without a physical boundary event or a law-governed continuation, is a further interpretation, not refuted by the maximal-development theorem. Calling the stop an “additional contingent structure” already adopts a completeness/explanatory principle that the rival may reject.

The source-based rival requires a more careful mapping. In chapter 7, C&R explicitly define both location-based and existence-based world-state comparisons, use a box marked as nomological in (DET_loc)/(DET_ex), and distinguish determinate truth-value from being nomologically predetermined. For existence-based indeterminism they say GBT may allow reality as it is at a time to end then even if it actually grows; they later decline to insist this vindicates the asymmetry intuition without an argument for GBT. Thus it would be too quick either to classify their whole proposal as only metaphysical and outside physical comparison, or to identify their nomological operator with the selected Einstein–scalar MGHD category. Whether their relevant ω_t^ex states and nomological alternatives map to maximal or nonmaximal developments with fixed Cauchy data remains open. The coordinator's construction supplies one same-data nonmaximal development; the maximal-category result constrains only comparisons that stipulate maximal developments. A broader metaphysical cessation remains a possible response, but it is not an interpretation imposed on C&R's source. (C&R, *Nothing to Come*, ch. 7, pp. 113–116, as recorded in `research/future-truth-rival-chapters/CLAUSES.md`; and “Replies to Critics,” §7, pp. 480–482; full-text passages read.)

**Repair:** state the result as a conditional comparison: within a fixed nomological class stipulated to contain only maximal developments, regular proper restrictions do not establish a second admitted *maximal* development. At the same time, M_< is a genuine nonmaximal smooth development of the same data, so it does establish that local lawfulness and the data alone do not impose maximality. For C&R, leave the translation from their nomological operator and existence-worldstate to the selected MGHD category unresolved. If one separately proposes primitive metaphysical failure of existence to continue, the mathematical result leaves that broader proposal open; do not assume that this is the source's only or intended reading. Do not count “the equations have a unique maximal continuation” as a reason that this continuation must exist simpliciter unless an independent bridge principle is supplied.

## Passage and earlier truth

The L/S construction is fit for its stated, deliberately limited purpose: eventual occurrence and a definite value, together with a growing inventory policy, do not by themselves entail present bivalence. It is not a full semantics of GBT, supervaluationism, or C&R. Its strongest hidden modal premise is the choice of comparison base. L evaluates at a designated realization; S evaluates over the two-member set of realizations. That difference is precisely where the truth-policy disagreement enters. The note says the labels are comparison devices, but should explicitly say that “designated realization” is an actual-world index in the toy interpretation, not a present event, a causal selection process, or a mechanism that picks a history. The example needs no present-selection mechanism. It also does not derive which realization is actual, explain why, or show that S's two continuations are the right admissible set.

There is a related stage/world distinction. L's q-at-b truth is indexed to a complete realization containing a later value; this does not mean the later event is in the current inventory or is a present ground. S's unsettled-at-b verdict is relative to the specified alternatives, while a later assessment can be indexed to a realized continuation. To keep the counterinterpretation clean, give the evaluation clauses and identify separately (i) the temporal stage, (ii) the complete realization/world index, (iii) the modal alternatives used by S, and (iv) the later ground, if L's true q receives one. Then the counterinterpretation establishes semantic underdetermination by the shared occurrence pattern without pretending both policies form one theory or have identical grounding.

The source treatment is substantially fair. On printed p. 417 (PDF p. 7), Wawer says a growing-block theorist can use abstract possible states as continuations and endorse supervaluationism. The passage suggests abstract states; it does not itself provide a full semantics (reviewer assessment). The note should retain its description of S as restricted and should not report Wawer as having supplied a complete GBT semantics. In the exact challenge passage, Wawer asks whether future tense identifies a unique grounding circumstance, by analogy with counterfactual cases involving multiple relevant possibilities (Jacek Wawer, “Tensed Metaphysics and Non-Local Grounding of Truth,” *Disputatio* 13(63) (2021), pp. 418–420, DOI 10.2478/disp-2021-0023; full text read, especially pp. 418–419). This is a challenge to the grounding-source/selection principle, not an argument that a present mechanism must select a future.

C&R's reply must be represented conditionally. In §6 they say their schema is premised on the assumption that the future-contingent sentence is presently true, and that a grounding explanation's dependence on there being something to explain is unsurprising; their claim is that the grounding requirement alone does not settle the bivalence dispute (pp. 475–477). In §7 they distinguish giving a grounding explanation from persuading someone who denies its explanandum, and describe the later-grounding clause as a ground that will obtain under the later temporal operator (pp. 477–479). The coordinator's note captures this well. The review finds no basis for demanding a present selector or for treating C&R's conditional grounding schema as an independent proof of present truth. Keep the note's criticism aimed at the narrower inference from successful conditional explanation to entitlement to the truth policy or ontology.

**Repairs:** preserve C&R's “if presently true, then later-grounded” structure verbatim in formalization; keep “actual realization” an index, not a selection mechanism; and explicitly label the two-world L/S setup a counterinterpretation of one inference only. Wawer's concern about uniqueness is not answered just by stipulating one actual world for L: the stipulation implements the bivalent policy under test. That is acceptable for a countermodel, provided it is not presented as a derivation or a grounding defense.

## Maximality, completeness, and mathematical scope

The note correctly separates local lawfulness, mathematical extendibility, and maximal-world admissibility. It also correctly says that maximality is not proper-time completeness, that an MGHD can be incomplete, and that accepting maximal histories does not itself entail eternalism: a growing-block interpretation can represent a complete history while maintaining a standpoint-relative inventory. These distinctions should remain explicit. “Complete history” is ambiguous between a whole represented solution, a maximal development, and a world whose events all exist simpliciter; none implies the others without an additional premise.

The open restriction M_< is useful as a model-theoretic control: it preserves local equations and the specified initial data while omitting later points. It is another nonmaximal smooth globally hyperbolic development, and thus directly shows why local lawfulness plus those data do not enforce maximality. It has no final included event, however, so it is not itself a model of an ontology on which reality has a last moment and then stops. The note states this caveat; repeat it whenever M_< is used against existential openness. Nor does extension possibility establish compulsory continuation. The extension map answers a mathematical question; whether its target is part of the concrete world is the disputed bridge.

**Repairs:** call M_< a nonmaximal development of the same data; distinguish it from both a maximal development and a model with an explicit terminal-time semantics. Present maximal-world admissibility as a premise motivated by physical-model practice and explanatory economy, with brute-ending metaphysics as one live defeater. For C&R, keep their explicit nomological operator in view and leave its translation to this development category open. Avoid describing maximality as the unchanged equations' own modal verdict.

## Conditional physical inputs and conclusion

The notes appropriately distinguish scalar-value variation, occurrence of a target, and existential openness. Preserve the stated conditions: finite-control/readout variation remains conditional on the pending control argument; smoothness, cone bounds, Cauchy transport, full-realm matching, and completeness are distinct geometric inputs with their own scopes. A finite-control result would concern a bounded family of readouts under its hypotheses; it would not establish a general theorem about future truth, physical branching, or ontology. The maximal-category result constrains only the selected physical category, while M_< remains an available nonmaximal development. Do not combine these results into an unconditional “physics proves the future exists” conclusion.

The passage note's surviving conclusion is appropriately narrow: eventual resolution alone does not select present bivalence, and conditional later grounding need not persuade a bivalence skeptic. The maximality note's surviving conclusion is likewise conditional: distinguish the existence of same-data nonmaximal developments from uniqueness within the stipulated maximal-development class, then separately establish how C&R's nomological existence-worldstates map to that class. Neither result decides an ontology. The next positive argument must independently defend the bridge principle and compare it to the strongest rival.
