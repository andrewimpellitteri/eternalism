## Mechanical check

With the stated potential and \(m=1\), the component equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_1'&=x_2-x_1,\\
 p_2'&=x_1-x_2+\kappa(x_3-x_2),\\
 p_3'&=x_4-x_3+\kappa(x_2-x_3),\\
 p_4'&=x_3-x_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus both displayed subsystem momentum derivatives have the stated signs,
and their sum vanishes, as translation invariance requires. Under the draft’s
state-space definition—equations for every variable in \(S\) must depend
only on \(S\)’s variables for every admitted full initial state—a positive
crossing spring makes autonomy fail: vary its outside endpoint while fixing
all variables in \(S\). If no positive spring crosses the cut, every force
term is internal, so autonomy holds. The iff claim and the component
classification follow. This conclusion depends on the stipulated material
coordinates and the universal-initial-state definition; it is not a general
criterion for physical unity.

For the Euclidean operator norm, \(K_0\) is the direct sum of two pair
Laplacians and has norm 2. The edge Laplacian \(L_{23}\) also has norm 2,
so \(\|A_0\|=2\) (indeed \(\max(1,\|K_0\|)\)) and \(\|B\|=2\).
Consequently \(\|A_\kappa\|\le 2+2\kappa\le4\). Variation of constants
gives, for either sign of \(t\),

\[
 \|z_\kappa(t)-z_0(t)\|
 \le \kappa\int_0^{|t|} e^{4(|t|-s)}\,2\,e^{2s}\,ds\,\|z(0)\|
 \le 2\kappa |t|e^{4|t|}\|z(0)\|.
\]

The draft uses the still looser valid bound \(e^{4|t|}\) for both
propagators; either yields its stated estimate. For negative time, use the
backward variation-of-constants integral and the same exponential norm
bounds. The estimate is two-sided, uniform on each fixed compact time
interval, and does not give uniform control as the interval grows without
bound. The tolerance condition is sufficient only, and depends on \(T\),
\(R\), and the chosen norm. For dimensional clarity, say the variables and
Euclidean norm are taken after a fixed nondimensionalization; setting mass
and spring constants to one alone should not be used to suggest that raw
position and momentum have identical physical units.

## Modes and interval refinement

The orthogonal diagonalization of the real symmetric stiffness matrix, with
the same orthogonal map on positions and momenta, is canonical and separates
the equations into normal modes. The path graph is connected for \(\kappa>0\),
so it has one zero-frequency translation mode and three positive modes; at
\(\kappa=0\), each dimer can translate, giving two zero modes. This is a
coordinate decomposition, not evidence that the material bodies are
physically noninteracting or that the change of coordinates is a gauge
symmetry. The draft appropriately qualifies its conclusion by holding the
material decomposition fixed and asking what physical grounds support that
choice.

The interval lemma is valid as stated for open intervals containing the
marked time: if one qualifies and qualification is inherited by every
smaller such interval, there can be no inclusion-minimal qualifying interval.
It needs both the existence of at least one qualifying interval and the
heredity premise. It establishes neither singleton qualification nor a
zero-duration physical object. A completed activity can defeat heredity;
then its duration may select an activity-extent, but a further argument is
needed to identify that duration with the present extent of the continuing
system. The autonomy and conservation criteria are time-local and hereditary
on subintervals, so they do not select a smallest positive extent or uniquely
select the whole history.

## Philosophical stress test and repairs

The strongest reply is already substantially represented: approximate
independence can support realism about subsystems, and Brading’s proposal
allows interacting parts to be unities. The oscillator calculation therefore
cannot refute Brading by showing that a nonzero coupling joins the exact
autonomy graph. The coordinator’s source ledger reports a selected primary
reading of Brading (2015), §§4–5, printed pp. 16–18, and Brading (2013), §4,
printed pp. 1104–1105. This review did not reread those papers; attribution
here inherits that ledger’s stated depth and limits. In particular, do not
attribute the graph criterion, oscillator model, or interval lemma to Brading.

Exact exclusion and approximate unity must remain separate. The calculation
shows what follows if exact material autonomy is adopted as the criterion:
at every positive coupling, no proper nonempty material subset is
autonomous. It does not show that approximate systems are unreal, that
outside events are unreal, or that a fundamental present must have a sharp
boundary. A proposal making an exact exhaustive exclusion claim bears the
burden of defending that boundary; a scale-relative or approximate account
can survive, provided it explains how its existential language works.
Likewise, a plurality of constitutive extents is a live answer unless the
proposal claims uniqueness. The note’s strongest surviving conclusion is
methodological: the physical criterion and its temporal/existential
interpretation need separate warrants. It neither establishes an ontology
nor rules out a source-faithful, nonhereditary sustaining-process account.

Finally, the finite overlap example is a valid witness against the bare
inference from agreement on shared members to transitivity of whole-domain
inventories. The assignments can be restrictions of one consistent
valuation; \(b\) belongs to \(U_a\) and \(c\) to \(U_b\), while \(c\)
does not belong to \(U_a\). There is no disagreement on an overlap and no
contradiction. Inventory transfer is an additional principle. The example
does not model relativity or Brading’s view, and rejecting transitivity still
leaves a substantive explanatory question about relations among local
presents.
