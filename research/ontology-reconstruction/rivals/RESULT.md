## 0. What was built, in one paragraph

Four charitable, source-anchored model contracts — (E) whole-history
eternalism, (G) preferred-slicing growing block, (B) Correia–Rosenkranz
bow-tie spatiotemporaryism, (F) local becoming / local fragmentalism —
each specified on the ten-field contract from
`research/ontology-reconstruction/ROADMAP.md` (inventory; standpoint
status; absolute vs standpoint existence; persistence; truths and
grounds including later grounding; passage; added structure and its
physical role; determinism/openness; empirical commitments; strongest
objection and permissible translations). A comparison matrix aligns
them. One pair — E vs F — is then developed as the requested case that
**agrees on physics but disagrees ontologically**, with a precise
account of what blocks collapse into vocabulary, and with the strongest
escape routes from our existing objections for each contract.

## 2. Contract E: whole-history eternalism

Anchors: Putnam 1967 (primary, full text retrieved this run); Broad's
1921 eternalist phase via SEP (secondary, abstract); Sider/Mellor via
knowledge-base records (abstract/discovery — see gates). This is the
physics-facing member of the eternalist family, not a claim that all
eternalists argue from relativity.

**E1 Inventory.** All events, things and field configurations of the
one spacetime, tenselessly. [Putnam, p. 246]: "all future things are
real ('things' here includes 'events'), and likewise that all past
things are real, even though they do not exist now."

**E2 Standpoint status.** Standpoints (observers, worldline points)
are *locations*, not conditions of reality. Principle III forbids
letting any observer's position fix what is real: "If it is the case
that all and only the things that stand in a certain relation R to
me-now are real, and you-now are also real, then it is also the case
that all and only the things that stand in the relation R to you-now
are real." [Putnam, p. 241].

**E3 Absolute vs standpoint existence.** Existence is absolute and
tenseless; locatedness is a relation. [Putnam, p. 246]: the tenseless
notion of existence "amounts to 'will exist, or has existed, or exists
right now'", and "the notion of being 'real' turns out to be
coextensive with the tenseless notion of existence." There is no
operator E(b, x) over and above "x exists" + "x bears location
relations to b".

**E4 Persistence.** Not fixed by the physics anchor. Standard options
in the family: four-dimensionalism/perdurantism (the worldtube is
concrete as a whole) — Sider record is abstract-depth only [gate:
Four-Dimensionalism full text not fetched]; or stage theory; or
adverbialism. The contract requires only that all temporal stages of
concrete things exist.

**E5 Truths and grounds.** Bivalent tenseless truth-at-a-time. No
later-grounding pressure: every putative ground tenselessly exists, so
a truth about t is grounded by whatever is located at (or before) t,
which exists simpliciter. Putnam's separate, stronger step —
"contingent statements about future events already have a truth value"
[p. 246] — is recorded as *his* claim; the foundations lane's audit
marks the future-contingent discussion as distinct from the
reality-transfer argument and not conflated with eternalism
(`knowledge/foundations/sources.jsonl` `found-putnam`).

**E6 Passage.** Not fundamental. Passage talk is indexical variation
over the tenseless earlier–later order. [Putnam, p. 247]: "I do not
believe that there are any longer any philosophical problems about
Time; there is only the physical problem of determining the exact
physical geometry of the four-dimensional continuum that we inhabit."

**E7 Added structure.** None beyond the relativistic geometry itself.
A privileged foliation or absolute simultaneity is explicitly
disallowed on the anchor's own terms: describing reality transfer by
"the coordinate system of me-now" would commit the real things "to the
view that the real things bear to me-now a relation which is not
Lorentz-invariant and, hence, that they define an absolute
simultaneity" [Putnam, p. 246].

**E8 Determinism/openness.** Determinism is *not* implied by E; E is a
thesis about what exists, not about law-determined outcomes. Putnam's
own text separates the reality argument from the determinateness
argument; the project ledger keeps them separate
(`research/CURRENT_SYNTHESIS.md`: "lawful determination does not imply
present existence").

**E9 Empirical commitments.** None beyond the shared physics. E adds
no structure, so it adds no observation.

**E10 Strongest objection and translations.** The classic route from
relativity (reality transfer through overlapping nows plus transitive
closure) is an invalid inference to *existence*: Stein 1991's analysis
and the project's check "eternalism is not inferred from the domain of
a spacetime model" (`research/ontology-reconstruction/ROADMAP.md`,
required check 1) both block it; Principle III is a bridge premise, not
physics (`found-putnam` open question). Strongest translation pressure:
see §7 — the restricted-quantifier translation of F into E.

**Charity note [ours].** E must not be equated with "the model M
contains all events, therefore they exist" (that is the invalid
inference). The charitable E *argues* that no operator beyond
tenseless existence is needed, and bears the burden of showing why
rival operators are ill-defined rather than merely false.

## 3. Contract G: preferred-slicing growing block

Anchors: Broad 1923 (primary, exact passage verified this run against
the Internet Archive OCR, printed pp. 66–68); Bell's Lorentzian
preferred frame (primary, printed p. 77 of the 1987 reprint); Ellis
2006 EBU (abstract verified; body unread [gate]); Earman 2008
(abstract this run; §§5–7 read full-text by the local-fragmentalism
lane from a repo-root PDF **not present in this worktree** [gate]);
project records (`phil-bohm-foliation`, `phil-latest`, STRONGEST_VIEW
R8 note).

**G1 Inventory.** The events of the past and the present slice exist;
future events do not exist (yet). [Broad, p. 66]: "such a theory as
this accepts the reality of the present and the past, but holds that
the future is simply nothing at all."

**G2 Standpoint status.** The objective present is a *global leaf*
Σ_τ of a preferred slicing — not event-relative. Observer standpoints
live inside leaves; no standpoint fragment structure.

**G3 Absolute vs standpoint existence.** Existence is absolute but
extent-indexed: the existing totality is the accumulated history up to
the current leaf. [Broad, p. 67]: "The sum total of existence is
always increasing … A moment t is later than a moment t' if the sum
total of existence at t includes the sum total of existence at t'
together with something more." The past stays existent: "Nothing has
happened to the present by becoming past except that fresh slices of
existence have been added to the total history of the world. The past
is thus as real as the present." [Broad, pp. 66–67].

**G4 Persistence.** Objects are growing life-histories. [Broad,
p. 66]: "A Specious Present of mine is just the last thin slice that
has joined up to my life-history"; slices are "tacked on" as becoming
proceeds. Ordinary objects persist as the accumulated part of their
history; future parts do not exist.

**G5 Truths and grounds.** On the classical pairing, present grounding
plus GBT yields truth-value gaps for future contingents — as
Correia–Rosenkranz summarize the target: "Present grounding plus GBT
(no future things) gives: neither Fφ nor F¬φ is true" [C&R, p. 102,
quoted in the project chapter record]. The bivalent later-grounds
variant is available (Contract L in
`research/future-truth-rivals/coordinator/PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md`) and the
project has shown the two truth policies can be combined with different
existence bases; G in its Broad form takes the gap option, without
prejudging the bivalent hybrid.

**G6 Passage.** Fundamental. [Broad, p. 67]: "a complete analysis of
the qualitative changes of things is found to involve the coming into
existence of events. … Let us call the third kind of change Becoming."
Becoming is the third kind of change on which the other two depend.

**G7 Added structure and its physical role.** A preferred slicing /
cosmic time. Three documented realizations, with different costs:

- *Lorentzian real rest frame.* [Bell, p. 77]: "Lorentz, on the other
  hand, preferred the view that there is indeed a state of real rest,
  defined by the 'aether', even though the laws of physics conspire to
  prevent us identifying it experimentally. The facts of physics do
  not oblige us to accept one philosophy rather than the other."
- *Metric-defined cosmic time.* In near-FLRW cosmologies the canonical
  foliation is definable from the metric, so the cost is *non-locality*,
  not added structure — the project's red-team correction, retained
  from `research/local-fragmentalism/STRONGEST_VIEW.md` R8 [ours/lane].
- *Dynamical foliation.* Bohmian-type guidance structures define a
  foliation through dynamics; the knowledge-base lead
  (`phil-bohm-foliation`, Hypersurface Bohm–Dirac models) is
  abstract-depth [gate].

**Honest scoping [ours].** Ellis's evolving block universe is the
nearest physics-motivated growth view, but it is explicitly *not* a
preferred-surface view: "this time evolution is not related to any
preferred surfaces in spacetime; rather it is associated with the
evolution of proper time along families of world lines" [Ellis 2006,
abstract, v5; body unread]. G must not cite Ellis as a defender of the
slicing variant; he is recorded as a neighbouring variant whose
difference from G is exactly the pointy/slicing issue.

**G8 Determinism/openness.** Open future by non-existence of future
events; fully compatible with deterministic laws (a "deterministic
growing-block theorist" is an acknowledged stance in
`research/CURRENT_SYNTHESIS.md`). The project's causal-set work keeps
mathematical growth separate from objective passage: a nonextension
theorem does not refute becoming, and a completed representation is
not an ontological completed history [project].

**G9 Empirical commitments.** None distinctive. Bell's point cuts both
ways: the preferred frame is hidden by the laws' conspiracy, so no
current observation selects G; only Lorentz-violating physics
(knowledge-base lead `phil-sme`, abstract) could in principle expose a
physical foliation.

**G10 Strongest objection and escapes.** Earman 2008: in the
Newtonian setting Broad-type models are built "in a cheating fashion
by starting with a Newtonian block model, carving chips off the block"
[Earman 2008 abstract, retrieved this run; §§5–7 read full-text by the
local-fragmentalism lane, primary PDF absent here], and the paper finds
no serious non-cheating construction, ending with causal-set growth as
the promising mechanism. Add the edge epistemic problem (how do you
know you are at the latest edge — `phil-latest` lead) and the
fragmentalism red-team point that a global leaf is non-local in generic
GR. Escape routes: §8, item G.

## 4. Contract B: Correia–Rosenkranz bow-tie spatiotemporaryism

Anchors: *Nothing To Come* chapters 7 and 9, read full-text by the
chapter lane from user-supplied PDFs with printed-page locators
(`research/future-truth-rival-chapters/CLAUSES.md`; locally re-verified
this run — the file is in this worktree); *Replies to Critics* §8,
pp. 482–484 (lane full-text record);
`research/future-truth-rival-chapters/RESULT.md` derivations.

**B1 Inventory.** Spacetime points are "the basic points of
evaluation"; the only objective structure used is "the fourfold
division" of each point into itself, causal past, causal future,
elsewhere [C&R, pp. 135–137]. Concrete inventory is point-based; no
events-over-and-above-points are required for the chapter-9 framework.

**B2 Standpoint status.** Every spacetime point is a standpoint; @x
operators evaluate at x; (A22R) ∀x(Sx → @xφ) → φ, (A23R) Sx → @xE!x
[C&R, p. 145].

**B3 Absolute vs standpoint existence.** Existence is *point-relative
and primitive*, never absolute: (P2R) Sx → @x▴¬E!x and (P1R) E!x →
▾E!x [C&R, pp. 148–149], with the bow-tie elsewhere clause (BO) Sx →
@x◂E!x [p. 149]. Derived extent at b [project derivation, RESULT.md
§4]:

    E_b^pointy = J⁻(b)                       (= Stein 1968's domain, as C&R note, p. 149)
    E_b^bow    = M \ (J⁺(b) \ {b})

Existence-at is explicitly *intransitive* — the "intransitivity of
being" is their §9.4 title and accepted conclusion (pp. 155–156).

**B4 Persistence.** The bow-tie motivation: on pointy views, "the
perceived limbs located in my causal past cannot still exist *and
remain located* here-now"; bow-tie views avoid this persistence-of-
limbs objection [C&R, pp. 151–152, after Savitt]. Objects are located
at points; what exists from b includes the whole elsewhere region, so
nearby bodies persist across the standpoint.

**B5 Truths and grounds, including later grounding.** Chapter 9
contains no truth or grounding clause (recorded gap, CLAUSES.md);
chapter 7 supplies (GR): "For all ψ, Sometimes, ∃X((Now, ψ is true)
because X exist)", with 'Now' rigidly restoring the utterance time
[C&R, p. 110], which "allows for the possibility that a statement
about the future be true now whose present truth is sometimes in the
future explained by what exists then, while there exists now nothing
that would explain its present truth" [p. 110]. Bivalence for future
contingents is defended (pp. 111–112); the *Replies* §8 distinction:
a present derivative fact *that* Q is true is compatible with the
future explanans not currently existing [Replies, pp. 482–484, per
`research/future-truth-rivals/coordinator/POINT_OF_DISAGREEMENT.md`].

**B6 Passage.** Relativistic becoming per standpoint: what has become
at b is E_b. The edge of becoming is not a point — the recapture
argument against pointy views: "no suggestion in classical GBT either
that the edge of becoming is not really an edge but a point" [C&R,
p. 160]. Bow-tie is "the best version of relativistic GBT" [p. 150],
and "has never been defended in print" as of their writing [p. 150].

**B7 Added structure.** None beyond causal geometry: no metric, no
foliation, no field dynamics in chapter 9's main text [CLAUSES.md,
"What chapter 9 does not contain"]. B's entire ontology is read off
the fourfold division. Its distinctive *commitment* is not structure
but logic: intransitive existence-at.

**B8 Determinism/openness.** Openness is (IND_ex) of grade (II′) or
(III′): "it is nomologically possible, given everything existing now,
that later times exist unpopulated or never exist at all",
"perfectly compatible with the bivalence of all future-tensed
statements" [C&R, pp. 115–116]. Determinism per se is not the issue;
grade (I) variation (same points, different properties) is a different
availability. Permanentists cannot say (III′) [p. 116]. A fixed-M
solution class represents only grade (I); II′/III′ need variable-domain
models [project limitation, RESULT.md §5].

**B9 Empirical commitments.** None.

**B10 Strongest objections and translations.** (i) Intransitivity of
being is the accepted cost; a transitive friend of E or F will treat
@bE!y as explicable and false. (ii) This project found their own second
argument against (BO+) model-dependent: the converse of (AR2) fails in
closed dust [project derivation, RESULT.md §7], though their conclusion
stands on their first argument. (iii) From the project side: the
bow-tie base actually *improves* determination — for the massive scalar
on closed dust, F_m^bow-tie(b) = M for every standpoint, against
F_m^pointy(b) = J⁻(b) for 0<τ_b<π [project derivation, RESULT.md §5] —
so our past-data obstruction does not touch B; it presses only on the
pointy view (Stein's). Translation pressure: B vs E differ exactly on
@bE!y for y ∈ J⁺(b)\{b} (B: no; E: the question does not arise since E
has absolute existence), i.e. on whether existence-at is primitive —
see §7.

## 5. Contract F: local becoming / local fragmentalism

Anchors: Stein 1968's definition as quoted by C&R [p. 149]: "those
events, and only those, have already become … which occur at points in
the topological closure of the past of a" (Stein primary unread this
run [gate]; Stein 1991 full-text in `knowledge/foundations`, printed
theorem p. 149 with the omitted condition (iv) recorded); Clifton–Hogarth
maxim (p. 364, full-text read by the local-fragmentalism lane, primary
paywalled this run [gate]); Fine 2005 standpoint framework (lane

manuscript `research/stein-gr/main.tex` (in this worktree; theorem
statements read this run); `research/local-fragmentalism/STRONGEST_VIEW.md`
(our reconstruction, red-teamed).

**F1 Inventory.** Events of M; fragments F(b) = what is real from b.
With the causal-sufficiency premise P6, F(b) = J⁻(b) [ours, on Stein's
content + C&H's maxim]. Physics content [PROVED (project),
`stein-gr/main.tex` Thm. "No becoming outside the causal past"]:
either the relation is universal everywhere, or I⁻(b) ⊆ F(b) ⊆ J⁻(b)
for every globally hyperbolic M; residual freedom lies at the light
cone (Thm. "Classification of the residual freedom"; Weyl-tensor
non-uniqueness Thm.).

**F2 Standpoint status.** Every event is a standpoint (Fine's event
standpoints, adapted [gate: Fine PDF absent; lane full-text record];
STRONGEST_VIEW P1). Optional worldline restriction available
(Corollary 9).

**F3 Absolute vs standpoint existence.** Reality-from-b is basic;
absolute reality is *derived* as the agreement of standpoints
(persistence + agreement, P5-replacement) [ours]. There is no
standpoint-independent concrete inventory; the tenseless geometry is
"the agreement of the standpoints" [STRONGEST_VIEW §1].

**F4 Persistence.** Objects are worldtubes; "from b, an object exists
as its part within J⁻(b)". "The Sun now" picks out a part outside
J⁻(b), fixed only by convention from b [STRONGEST_VIEW O15].

**F5 Truths and grounds.** Tenseless/past claims evaluated in F(b).
Future-tensed claims from b are settled-by-R10 or valueless from b —
a Thomason-style gap, not a falsity; full object language still owed
[OPEN, STRONGEST_VIEW §2b]. No later grounding is invoked: truth is
settlement-based, contrast B. Distinct project result [ours]: settled
≠ real — "under deterministic local laws, data real from b fix what
happens at x" (R10), nothing outside J⁻(b) is settled in Minkowski,
while in the Einstein static universe everything is settled from any
b (proofs in STRONGEST_VIEW).

**F6 Passage.** Growth of fragments along worldlines (R3, from R1);
direction imported from the time orientation, not explained [conceded,
O12].

**F7 Added structure.** None beyond causal structure *given* locality
(P3/P3w); rivals with a shared present pay either non-locality (metric-
definable foliation, near-FLRW) or added global structure (R8). The
red-team correction stands: locality is the contested premise, not a
theorem.

**F8 Determinism/openness.** Determinism is orthogonal to reality
(R10 separation). The future is never real from b; whether it is
settled depends on spacetime and laws; the view neither secures nor
excludes an open future (O17).

**F9 Empirical commitments.** Truth-conditional and empirical
equivalence with eternalism is *conceded* [STRONGEST_VIEW §6]; the
one falsifier listed is event-level Bell-nonlocal dynamics, which
would collapse F into E (O6). No discriminator without an
independently defended exclusivity premise [project].

**F10 Strongest objections and escapes.** The translation argument
(O2, raised independently by five red-team groups) and the Bell
falsifier are the strong ones; off-shell scope (O4) and P5 replacement
(red-team C) are the formal ones. Escapes: §8, item F.

## 7. The developed pair: E vs F — same physics, different ontology

### 7.1 The physics agreement, precisely

Both sides accept every theorem of the shared physical core:

1. The stein-gr classification [PROVED (project)]: any non-universal
   becoming relation that is local in the stated sense satisfies
   I⁻(b) ⊆ F(b) ⊆ J⁻(b) in every globally hyperbolic spacetime; with
   causal sufficiency (P6) or limit closure (P6′), F(b) = J⁻(b)
   exactly; the residual freedom is confined to the light cone and is
   non-unique (Weyl-tensor rule).
2. No observation discriminates the two: "truth-conditional and
   empirical equivalence with eternalism — CONCEDED"
   [STRONGEST_VIEW §6]; the project ledger records that no branch
   establishes a separating prediction
   (`research/CURRENT_SYNTHESIS.md`).
3. Determinism, settlement behaviour (R10), records, horizons — all
   shared. The eternalist reads (1) as a theorem about *which
   standpoint-relative descriptions the geometry supports*; the
   fragmentalist reads it as a theorem fixing *the fragments*. Same
   mathematics, same observations.

### 7.2 The ontological disagreement, precisely

- E: concrete inventory = the whole history; standpoint-relative talk
  is quantifier restriction. There is no further fact of the form
  "x is real from b" over and above x's tenseless existence plus
  x ∈ J⁻(b) [Putnam p. 246; B3].
- F: standpoint-relative reality is basic; the tenseless geometry is
  the agreement of standpoints [STRONGEST_VIEW P5-replacement]. The
  restriction reading is explicitly declined: what is at issue is
  "which side of the translation is fundamental" [STRONGEST_VIEW O2
  reply].

The disagreement is therefore not about any tenseless truth, any
record, or any law, but about the *direction of derivation*: E derives
standpoint-relative claims from an unordered whole; F derives the
whole's geometry from standpoint agreement.

### 7.3 What prevents collapse into vocabulary [ours]

The charge to answer: with an equivalence conceded, is "real from b"
just a defined abbreviation in E's language — E plus notation? Three
criteria, then the precise collapse conditions.

**(1) The derivational-tree criterion.** Two positions are vocabulary
variants only if each side can adopt the other's derivational tree as
its own preferred explanation. A translation T maps theorems to
theorems; it does not map *derivations*. E's tree takes the whole
block as primitive and gets standpoint-relative claims by restriction;
F's tree takes standpoint-relative reality as primitive and gets the
block's geometry as agreement (persistence + agreement). Under T, one
of the two trees is deleted, not translated. Since the dispute is
about which claims are primitive — a claim about the *basis*, not the
theorem set — T cannot settle it. This sharpens the lane's own reply
(O2: "a claim about which side of the translation is fundamental")
into a criterion that does not presuppose a doctrine of fundamentality:
it says only that translation is the wrong instrument for
primitive-basis disputes.

**(2) The falsifier-exposure criterion.** Vocabulary variants agree on
which physically possible scenarios refute them. They do not agree
here. F carries a distinctive exposure: if the fundamental dynamics is
event-level Bell-nonlocal (collapse, rGRWf, foliation-Bohmian), then
P6 fails and F collapses *into E* — the view's own admission
[STRONGEST_VIEW O6, §8 item 1]. E has no corresponding exposure: no
piece of physics removes E's inventory, because E posits nothing
beyond it. Exposure profiles that differ under a common physics are a
content difference, not a notational one: paraphrases are exposed to
the same worlds. The asymmetry is visible only modally — through what
each position would lose in which physically possible world — which is
exactly where a truth-conditional translation stops.

**(3) Collapse conditions, stated precisely.** The E/F disagreement
collapses into vocabulary iff all three of the following are granted:

  (a) *Fundamentality deflationism*: primitive-basis disputes with no
  truth-conditional residue are verbal by fiat.
  (b) *P6 as mere physics*: the signalling restriction is reclassified
  as an ordinary bet about the actual dynamics, so that its failure
  costs F only a false dynamical belief, not its ontology.
  (c) *Defined existence*: E(b, x) is stipulated as an abbreviation —
  "x exists tenselessly and x ∈ J⁻(b)" — and the fragmentalist has no
  independent reason to reject the definition.

Each of (a)–(c) is a substantive position, not a theorem. (a) is the
contested deflationist premise the lane already isolated as its
"most important open question". (b) misdescribes F's own use of P6:
there it is constitutive of what reality-from-b contains; if it were
mere dynamics, the fragments would not be what the view says they are.
(c) is exactly the move the project's required check 1 forbids reading
as neutral: a definition of E(b, x) over the block presupposes that
the block is the concrete inventory — which is the point in dispute
(`research/ontology-reconstruction/ROADMAP.md`, check 1).

**Relation to the coordinator's explanatory-role test.** The
coordinator's version-2 test
(`research/ontology-reconstruction/coordinator/EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST.md`)
defeats two shortcuts — consistency-with-a-package ≠ advantage, and the
package ≠ secret eternalism — and replaces generic parsimony with an
identified explanatory dependence. My criteria are compatible with
that outcome and independent of it: criterion (2) needs no theory of
explanation, only modal exposure; criterion (1) needs no cost metric.
The two can diverge, and the divergence is a real research question:
a commitment might have *no* distinctive explanatory role (so the
coordinator test awards it nothing) yet still fail to be vocabulary
(criterion 2), if some physically possible world would force its
abandonment. I flag that divergence for the bridge-premises lane
rather than resolving it here.

**Independent-argument note (required by brief).** The above is this
lane's own argument. It does not follow the coordinator's preference,
and it does not reinstate a parsimony default: nothing here ranks E
above F or F above E; the deliverable is the precise location and
non-collapsibility conditions of the disagreement.

### 7.4 Secondary illustration: E vs B on the deterministic dust model

On closed dust with the massive scalar, the bow-tie existing realm
determines every field value from every standpoint
(F_m^bow-tie(b) = M [project derivation, RESULT.md §5]), so B and E
agree on all physics of this model; they differ on whether future
points exist *from b* ((P2R) says no; E asks no such question). Within
the fixed-M model class the pair's difference is expressible only
through the primitive E! operator and reference to non-existent points
— and it *would* collapse into vocabulary exactly if free-logic
reference to non-existents were granted to B *and* the II′/III′ modal
openness were waived (C&R pp. 115–116), since grades II′/III′ are not
representable over one fixed manifold [RESULT.md §5]. Stated
precisely: the C&R/eternalist disagreement on this model survives only
through the modal existence-openness clause; that clause, not the
point-domain arithmetic, is what a translation would have to break.
Appendices 2–3 (Kripke semantics; translation manual) are unread
[gate], so the formal half of this claim stays conditional.

## 8. Strongest escape routes from our existing objections

The project's live objections used here: the three tests in
`research/future-truth-rivals/coordinator/PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md`; the
supervenience principle isolated in
`.../coordinator/POINT_OF_DISAGREEMENT.md`; the domain-insensitivity
observation and contemporaneous-grounding premise in
`research/ontology-reconstruction/coordinator/EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST.md`;
red-team points A–E in `research/local-fragmentalism/redteam/SYNTHESIS.md`;
ROADMAP required checks 1–8.

**E (eternalism).**
- Objections: reality-transfer route invalid (Stein 1991; ROADMAP #1);
  Principle III is a bridge premise, not physics (`found-putnam` open
  question).
- Strongest escapes: (1) *Diagnose the rival primitive*: argue E(b, x)
  is ill-defined rather than false — "realness ≠ present-existence" is
  Putnam's own opening diagnosis, and the bow-tie's intransitivity is
  the price of keeping the primitive; (2) *Rebuild the route as
  abduction*: E as the interpretation with no added existence
  structure — but note this is currently under-supported: the
  coordinator test shows uniform quantifiers alone rank no rivals, and
  we have supplied no cost metric (correctly, per EXPLANATORY_ROLE_TEST
  §1); (3) *Defend Principle III independently* as an observer-neutrality
  constraint — with the honest cost that Stein's 1991 result shows such
  neutrality does *not* force E, since standpoint-relative becoming
  satisfies the same automorphism invariance.

**G (preferred-slicing GBT).**
- Objections: Earman's "carving chips off the block" (no non-cheating
  construction); edge epistemics (`phil-latest`); non-local leaf in
  generic GR; mathematical growth ≠ passage by definition (ROADMAP #8).
- Strongest escapes: (1) *Scope to near-FLRW*: the canonical foliation
  is metric-definable there (R8 correction), so the charge of added
  structure lapses and only non-locality remains — and locality is
  itself a contested premise (this view denies P3w, so no begging);
  (2) *Bell–Lorentz move*: unverifiable-in-principle is not unreal
  [Bell p. 77]; the "conspiracy" is itself a lawful fact a realist can
  interpret; (3) *Bivalent hybrid*: adopt later grounds (Contract L)
  so C&R's own gap objection (pp. 101–105) does not bite the
  deterministic version; (4) *Discrete realization*: causal-set growth,
  the mechanism Earman himself ends on (abstract; §§5–7 lane record).

**B (bow-tie spatiotemporaryism).**
- Objections: intransitivity of being as a cost; II′/III′
  unrepresentable over fixed M [ours]; our closed-dust frame fact
  against the converse of (AR2) [RESULT.md §7]; location clauses are
  not existence-entailing, so fusions do not deliver point locations
  (`lawful-completions/coordinator/FUSION_MODEL.md`).
- Strongest escapes: (1) *Persistence motivation*: the limbs argument
  is a positive case pointy views cannot answer [C&R pp. 151–152];
  (2) *Determination gain*: the existing realm suffices for all field
  truth in the deterministic model — a demonstrated explanatory
  achievement in the coordinator's sense (the existing realm, plus
  laws, fixes every value), which the pointy view lacks for τ_b<π;
  (3) *Own the intransitivity*: treat non-transitive existence as a
  studied logical feature, not a bug — the project's own fusion and
  persistence classification supplies tools for exactly that;
  (4) *Later grounds*: (GR) keeps bivalence without present future
  grounds [C&R p. 110; Replies pp. 482–484], answering the
  contemporaneous-grounding pressure by declining the extra premise,
  as the coordinator test requires.

**F (local becoming/fragmentalism).**
- Objections: O2 translation argument (five red-team groups); Bell
  falsifier (O6); off-shell premise (O4); P5 replacement (red-team C).
- Strongest escapes: (1) *Fundamentality defense* — the view stands or
  falls with A/B fundamentality disputes being substantive; my criteria
  §7.3(1)–(3) give it a sharper statement and a sharper refutation
  condition than the lane's current reply; (2) *Relativist core*
  (Fine's external relativism) to avoid non-standard-realism costs;
  (3) *Signalling bet as falsifiable content*: the Bell exposure is
  re-framed by criterion (2) as genuine modal content — a paraphrase
  would share its exposure profile, E does not; (4) *Worldline
  restriction* (Corollary 9) where observer standpoints are preferred,
  at the recorded horizon cost (O16).
