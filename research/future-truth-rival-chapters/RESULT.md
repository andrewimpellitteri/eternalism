## 1. Faithful minimal rival

**Ontology [C&R].** Prerelativistic GBT: past and present things exist,
future things do not, and time is linear, not branching (p.112).
Relativistically, existence is relative to spacetime points: (P1R)
E!x → ▾E!x and (P2R) Sx → @x▴¬E!x. The preferred version adds bow-tie (BO):
at each point, the whole elsewhere region exists (pp.148–150).

**Truth [C&R].** Bivalent classical semantics on linear time, including for
future contingents (pp.111–112). Being determinate in truth-value is
distinct from being predetermined (p.112).

**Grounding [C&R].** (GR) For all ψ, Sometimes, ∃X((Now, ψ is true) because
X exist), where 'Now' rigidly returns evaluation to the utterance time
(p.110). The mechanism (E1)–(E3) is restricted to unproblematic simple
present-tense φ (p.109). Grounds are pluralities of whatever the ontology
admits: "things, facts, tropes, events, etc." The future event is *not*
said to exist now. (GR) "allows … while there exists now nothing that would
explain its present truth" (p.110).

**Fact/event domains.** Chapter 7 separates (i) the truth of '↑q' now
(explanandum), (ii) the later existents X that explain it, and (iii)
Cambridge-like or Lucretian properties of present things. Case (iii) is set
aside on the assumption "that Lucretianism is of no avail," not refuted
(p.101). The *present derivative fact* vocabulary used in the coordinator
lane comes from the later *Replies to Critics* §8, not from chapter 7.
Chapter 7 itself only asserts present truth without a present explainer.
[ours] The two are consistent: a truth-fact is an explanandum, not a ground.

**Openness [C&R].** Openness is (IND_ex) of grade (II′) or (III′): it is
nomologically possible, given everything existing now, that later times
exist unpopulated or never exist at all. This is unavailable to
permanentists and compatible with bivalence (pp.115–116). The asymmetry
between past and future lies here, in existence, not in grounding.

**Negations and generalizations [C&R].** ¬Fnφ is reduced to Fn¬φ if time
continues, or else grounded by a brute last-moment fact. That fallback
presupposes "a last moment of time … not an entirely innocent assumption"
(p.111). Grounding generalizations and negative existentials is left open as
"a problem for everyone" (p.111, n.5).

**Scope of 'sometimes' and 'because' [C&R].** 'Sometimes' takes widest scope
over the existential and the 'because' claim. Inside, 'Now' pins the
explanandum to the utterance time. 'Because' is asymmetric and transitive
(pp.106, 109). Chapter 9 gives no relativistic counterpart of (GR).

## 3. Contract S is not the supervaluationism C&R refute

C&R's semantic objection (p.105) targets supervaluation over histories that
are maximal linear sets of *existing* times. On GBT the present is the last
existing time, so there is one history and ¬Fφ & ¬F¬φ comes out supertrue.

[derived] Contract S supervaluates over C_b(s*), complete solutions on the
whole fixed manifold M. Its "histories" contain representations of points
that do not exist at b on the proposed E_b. C&R's objection therefore does not
apply to Contract S as stated. But S escapes it only by quantifying over
representations of non-existent future points. That is the ersatz route,
whose quantifier problems Loss diagnoses in Briggs–Forbes (already recorded
in `research/future-truth-grounding/tense-domain/RESULT.md`). The main text
of chapter 7 does not discuss ersatz supervaluation; its reference list was
not available. So: S is not refuted by chapter 7, and S's reference debt is
confirmed to be essential rather than incidental.

Chapter 7 also notes supervaluation "assumes that time is forward-branching"
(p.104). Contract S keeps one fixed M and varies the *solution*, not the time
order. This is a structural difference to state whenever S is compared with
Thomason/MacFarlane or with C&R's target.

## 4. Our event domain E_b = J⁻(b) is pointy relativistic GBT (Stein)

[derived] Use only (A23R), (A33R), (A34R), (AR6), (P1R), (P2R), and (PO) or
(BO). Evaluate at b, where b exists by (A23R). Instantiating ⚫∀z(…) at b uses
the existence-relative quantifier convention of chapters 1–3 (not read).

- y prec b: (A23R) gives @yE!y; with (P1R), @y▾E!y; (A34R) then gives @bE!y.
  **Exists at b.**
- b prec y: (P2R) gives @y▴¬E!y; (A33R) then gives @b¬E!y. **Does not exist.**
- y sep b: (PO) gives @b¬E!y via (AR6) and the symmetry of sep. (BO) gives
  @bE!y. **Pointy: does not exist. Bow-tie: exists.**

Read prec as the closed-dust causal relation minus identity:
(τ,Y)≺(σ,Z) iff (τ,Y)≠(σ,Z), τ≤σ and dₛ₃(Y,Z)≤σ−τ, as in
`research/future-truth-rivals/gr-compatibility/RESULT.md`. This relation is
irreflexive and transitive, as (A30R) and (A31R) require. The points existing
at b are then

    pointy:   {b} ∪ {y : y≺b} = J⁻(b)
    bow-tie:  M \ (J⁺(b) \ {b})

Consequences:

1. The shared event-domain proposal in PAIRED_CONTRACTS.md is C&R's *pointy
   relativistic GBT*, which they identify with Stein 1968 (p.149). It is a
   named prior position, not a new proposal. This matches the project's
   existing PRIOR_WORK entries on Stein.
2. C&R argue *against* exactly this view. The persistence-of-limbs objection
   (pp.151–152) says that on pointy views, perceived extended objects in my
   causal past cannot still exist and remain located. The recapture argument
   says pointy views turn "the edge of becoming" into a point (p.160). Any
   project text using E_b=J⁻(b) owes a reply to both. The project has not yet
   given one.
3. We have not checked that the dust frame validates every listed axiom.
   The computation above needs only the named axioms. One frame fact is
   useful anyway (§7).

## 5. Under C&R's preferred bow-tie domain, the dust obstruction disappears

This is the main new consequence of reading chapter 9.

[derived] At b=(τ_b,Y_b), every point with τ<τ_b lies outside J⁺(b), since
J⁺(b) requires τ≥τ_b. So the open slab U_b={τ<τ_b} is contained in the
bow-tie domain at b. U_b contains the level set {τ=c} for any c<τ_b, which is
a smooth spacelike Cauchy surface of closed dust, together with an open
neighbourhood of it. Two smooth solutions of the linear massive scalar
equation that agree on U_b have a difference with zero Cauchy data on {τ=c}.
By uniqueness for the linear Cauchy problem on this globally hyperbolic
spacetime, they agree on all of M. This is the same global well-posedness the
horizons draft relies on: `research/horizons/main.tex` Corollary cor:cauchy
and the app. D citation of Wald 1984 at line ~339, which were not re-audited
here.

So, for **every** standpoint b and every mass m>0:

    F_m^bow-tie(b) = M,   compared with   F_m^pointy(b) = J⁻(b) for 0<τ_b<π

The pointy case is the reviewed positive-mass classification in
`research/future-truth-grounding/coordinator/RESULT.md` §3.

Conditional on the bridge premise that field values at existing points are
natural (non-Lucretian) features of what exists:

- Under bow-tie relativistic GBT, every one-point predicate Q_(x,q) is
  extensionally fixed by the intrinsic field content of what exists at b,
  together with the laws. Bivalent truth needs no later grounds in this model.
- The bow-tie compatibility class is the singleton {s*}. Contract S therefore
  returns the same bivalent verdicts as Contract L, and **the L/S disagreement
  in our dust example vanishes.**
- The supervenience principle isolated in POINT_OF_DISAGREEMENT.md is
  *satisfied* by bow-tie GBT for this deterministic field. Our model puts no
  pressure on C&R's preferred relativistic view.

[ours] So the past-data obstruction is an obstruction for **pointy**
domains (Stein; our E_b), not for growing-block views as such. The choice
between pointy and bow-tie is the hinge. Bow-tie buys determination by
admitting existents at b that are causally inaccessible from b and not
causally upstream of it. That is C&R's "intransitivity of being" (§9.4), and
it is exactly what a causal-past ontology refuses.

Two further remarks, both [ours]:

- With deterministic hyperbolic dynamics, bow-tie GBT leaves this field
  without future contingents in C&R's p.100 sense. This assumes the
  relativistic reading of "present or past of now" as the bow-tie region plus
  the causal past, which is our transcription, not theirs. Openness would then
  have to come from indeterministic laws or from grades (II′)/(III′).
- A fixed-M solution space represents only grade (I) variation (same points,
  different field values). It cannot represent (II′) or (III′), where points
  or times fail to exist. Our model class is structurally unable to express
  the openness C&R say is distinctive of GBT.

## 6. Closed dust: bounded time with no last moment

[derived] In closed dust, τ ranges over the open interval (0,2π). Every
point (τ,Y) has (τ+ε,Y) in its causal future for small ε>0, so no point is a
last moment. Along the comoving worldline, the remaining proper time
A(2π−τ_b+sin τ_b) is finite, using a=A(1−cos τ) and t=A(τ−sin τ). Time does
not go on for every n units, yet there is no last moment. This is exactly the
case C&R flag as violating their presupposition (p.111).

[ours] This does not refute (GR). In the deterministic model, the crunch is
predetermined by the present state and the laws, so ¬Fn⊤ for n beyond the
remaining time is not a future contingent. It can be settled by what exists
now, if laws may ground. The unaddressed case is a *contingent* end of time
with no last moment. Chapter 7's case analysis for ¬Fnφ and ¬Fφ does not
cover it, and a standard cosmology shows the shape is physically
unexceptional. Whether this shape is a live possibility is a further
premise.

## 7. Small frame observation on C&R's argument against (BO+)

C&R's second argument against (BO+) uses the converse of (AR2), ⚪φ → ◃◃φ.
They say it "presumably" holds in many well-behaved GR spacetimes (p.155).

[derived] On the closed-dust frame it is invalid. Take b=(2π−ε,Y) and
z=(ε,−Y) with ε<π/2, so z≺b. A point y=(σ,W) spacelike to both would need
2π−ε−σ < d(W,Y) and σ−ε < π−d(W,Y). Adding the two gives 2π−2ε<π, which
fails; the boundary cases σ≥2π−ε and σ≤ε fail directly. So no point is
spacelike to both. A valuation true only at z makes ⚪φ true at b and ◃◃φ
false there.

Their first argument, from causally related pairs inside the elsewhere
region, still applies in closed dust. Their conclusion against (BO+) is
therefore unaffected. Only one of its two supports is model-dependent.

## 8. Temporal asymmetry of grounding

[C&R] (GR) is direction-neutral. Past truths "were, in the past, grounded"
(p.112), and GBT's existing past gives it no grounding advantage without
tropes or events (pp.107–108). The distinctive asymmetry is (IND_ex)
(II′)/(III′) openness.

[ours] For the open `future-ground-temporal-asymmetry` task: C&R do not
claim a principled grounding asymmetry, and their view does not need one.
That task should not presuppose that GBT relies on past-directed grounds
being privileged. It should test whether a grounding asymmetry follows from
the *existence* asymmetry: does the existence of past grounds at the time of
evaluation matter, given that C&R's 'sometimes' clause ignores it?

## 9. The distance analogy under relativity

[C&R] Later grounding is likened to grounding "at a distance" of a truth
here (p.108).

[ours] Prerelativistically, distant simultaneous grounds exist now, so the
analogy runs from an accepted case to a contested one. Under pointy
relativistic GBT, distant (spacelike) happenings do not exist at here-now
either, so both sides of the analogy are contested. Under bow-tie GBT, the
analogy survives intact. The analogy's force thus depends on the same
pointy/bow-tie choice as §5.

## What does not follow

- Nothing here shows bow-tie GBT is true, coherent in its full semantics
  (Appendix 2 unread), or preferable to the pointy view. Nor does it show
  that C&R's persistence objection defeats Stein-type views.
- §5 needs the bridge premise that field values at existing points are
  natural features of what exists, plus standard Cauchy uniqueness. It holds
  for one deterministic linear field on one fixed background, not for
  indeterministic dynamics or variable spacetimes.
- The (GR_R) transcription, the relativistic reading of "future contingent",
  and the Contract L correspondence are ours.
- The dust frame has not been checked against every chapter-9 axiom. §7 is a
  single frame fact, not a model-theoretic audit.
- No novelty claim. §§5–7 are elementary consequences of standard causal
  structure. Their value is in locating where the project's argument applies.
