## Scope

Philosophical claims attached to the Stein-GR and horizons papers and `research/local-fragmentalism/STRONGEST_VIEW.md`. This does not require a pure mathematical paper to adopt or vindicate a temporal ontology.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] State one exact contribution with explicit physical premises, mathematical definitions and philosophical bridge premises. Keep the calculations useful to an eternalist who rejects the interpretation.
- [ ] Separate existence, passage/tense, lawful alternatives, semantic settledness, ignorance and practical prediction. State the comparison class and specify the observable that may differ.
- [ ] Say “past data together with these laws and admissible solutions”; do not silently count global laws or restrictions as events already real in the past fragment.
- [ ] Give the strongest determinist growing-block reply (“unreality was never meant to imply indeterminism”) and the strongest eternalist reply. Identify exactly which inference the result challenges and what it concedes.
- [ ] Treat transitivity and cross-spacetime locality as substantive premises to defend. Compare the strongest preferred-clock/restricted-category rival, not a caricature.
- [ ] Restrict persistence appropriately: tenseless past-event facts are not centered tensed claims. Distinguish metric causal reachability, available signalling and Bell dependence.
- [ ] If local fragmentalism is defended, specify its explanatory or grounding gain beyond an eternalist restriction to causal pasts; acknowledge empirical/truth-conditional equivalence where applicable. If no gain is established, retain it as an interpretation or remove the stronger claim.
- [ ] Distinguish consistent event-relative realism from fragmentalism requiring incompatible fragments; the name is not an argument.
- [ ] Supply concise proposed paper prose, source locators and an independent critical verdict. Do not close from philosophical agreement among agents.
