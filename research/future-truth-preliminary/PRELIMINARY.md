# Future truth without future existence: preliminary investigation

27 October 2026. Research sidecar; manuscripts unchanged. This is an argument
map and a proposed research contract, not a new metaphysical theorem or a
completed literature review. The point of entry is: **Can a statement about
tomorrow be true today if tomorrow's event does not yet exist? What makes it
true?**

## Preliminary judgment

This is a worthwhile next challenge, but the generic question already has
substantial answers. The defensible opportunity is narrower: connect explicit
relativistic field models to a carefully stated account of future truth and
its grounds, and identify the extra premise that would make local becoming
more than an interpretation of those models.

The main pressure point is not whether we can write down a semantics. We can.
It is whether the alternatives in that semantics represent ignorance,
compatibility with a local past, physically open possibilities, or different
ways reality can grow. The same set of mathematical alternatives does not
settle that question.

## What the literature already occupies

Briggs and Forbes's [The Real Truth about the Unreal Future](https://academic.oup.com/book/36237/chapter-abstract/315923246) addresses our
entry question directly through ersatz timelines and several logics. The
publisher dates it 2012, pp. 257–304; another institutional record uses 2013.
Only the publisher abstract was checked here; the blocked PhilArchive fetch
was not a full-text reading. Their later open-access [The future, and what
might have been](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-017-1026-y) (online 2018, volume 2019), §1.1, explicitly proposes past
and present entities together with laws as truthmakers for future truths;
the introduction describes the primitive-law framework. Those passages were consulted,
not independently vindicated. These are strong growing-block rivals, not
positions defeated by the mere existence of future truths.

Roberto Loss's [Open future, supervaluationism and the growing-block theory:
a stage-theoretical account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8581605/) (2021, corrected publication 2022) supplies a
particularly relevant warning. The HTML sections on supervaluationism,
stages, and “quantifier troubles” distinguish possible stages of growth from
times within a block. Loss argues that fixing the quantifier to today's
domain while shifting only the time parameter can fail to express genuine
growth. This is an author's diagnosis, not our completed adjudication of
Briggs–Forbes. A causal-domain version must therefore say which parameter
changes under tense. Merely placing a future operator in front of a frozen
domain restriction is inadequate.

Martin Glazier's [Symmetries and ground](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-024-02133-7) (2024), §§2.1, 3.3, the opening of §4 and note 25,
distinguishes grounding claims from truth-condition claims. His symmetry
principle concerns full, unpruned explanations. Note 25 explicitly allows
symmetry to be restored when all fragments are included. Thus a symmetric
family of standpoints cannot be rejected merely because one selected
standpoint is asymmetric. The paper's incompatible-fact fragmentalism is
also different from our consistent standpoint-relative core. No conclusion
about our grounding direction follows without specifying the full
explanandum and the relevant symmetry.

Two current leads further narrow originality. Kelikli's [Records, chance,
and determination](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-026-05620-y) (2026) explicitly separates history-relative truth,
settlement, probability and records. Its abstract and introduction were read;
its technical theorems were not audited. De Florio and Frigerio's [Normal
Future Assertions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-026-01090-3) (2026), abstract and §§1–2, distinguishes competing
future-tense semantics and argues that assertion practice need not select
one. Its later argument remains unchecked here. Neither “separate truth
from settlement” nor “people reasonably predict tomorrow” is our discovery
or a decisive metaphysical argument.

Additional checks still needed: Thomason 1970 itself; the complete
Briggs–Forbes chapter; Correia–Rosenkranz's bivalent response; MacFarlane's
assessment-sensitive alternative; the relevant Belnap literature; and
truthmaking objections including Filomeno. Source depths and URLs are in
`sources.jsonl`. No comprehensive novelty judgment is available.

## Five questions hidden inside the entry question

| Question | Required input | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| What exists from b? | An ontological domain E(b), with an interpretation | What happens in all lawful alternatives |
| Is P true in a specified continuation? | A continuation s and satisfaction relation | That P is fixed from b |
| Is P fixed from b? | A nonempty class of admissible continuations | Why those continuations exhaust genuine possibility |
| Can someone know or responsibly assert P? | Records, access and epistemic/assertion norms | That P is metaphysically grounded in those records |
| What makes P true? | A defended grounding or truthmaking account | Nothing follows merely from calculating entailment |

“Tomorrow” also needs a clock and reference convention. For a first model,
choose a specified observer worldline and a proper-time offset, with the
stipulation that this target exists in every admitted continuation. That is
extra observer structure, not a cosmic present. If future existence itself
varies between continuations, this stipulation must be removed and a
free-logic or counterpart/reference clause supplied. A name for a future
event in a mathematical model is not already an ontological concession to
eternalism, but it must not be silently treated as a currently existing
truthmaker either.

## A neutral baseline attached to our field examples

Fix a background spacetime M, a classical solution class L and an actual
solution s*. Put P_b=I^−(b) and define

    C_b(s*) = {s in L : s|P_b = s*|P_b}.

This class is nonempty because it contains s*. Let a proposition Q be a set
of solutions, or equivalently a Boolean-valued property of solutions.

    Q is true in s*                 iff s* belongs to Q.
    Q is settled true from b       iff C_b(s*) is a subset of Q.
    Q is settled false from b      iff C_b(s*) is disjoint from Q.
    Q is unsettled from b          iff the class meets Q and its complement.

These are definitions of relative compatibility and truth in a model. They
do not identify settledness with truth simpliciter. Identifying truth now
with settled truth produces a supervaluation-style account; evaluating in a
designated actual continuation produces another account. Neither is an
automatic consequence of the PDE.

At fixed b, agreement on P_b is an equivalence relation on L. Universal
quantification over its equivalence classes has the familiar S5 modal
structure. This is a structural observation, not a claim that ideal
past-agreement is human knowledge. Importantly, this construction requires
no branching dynamics: different complete initial data for deterministic
equations can lie in the same local-past class.

If b causally precedes c, P_b is a subset of P_c, so
C_c(s*) is a subset of C_b(s*). Settledness of a fixed proposition persists
along that actual progression. An indexical sentence such as “it is raining
here now” changes its content when the center changes; it is not covered by
this persistence observation. Nor does it establish persistence of ontology.

### Elementary example

Take two continuations with identical past data, one with tomorrow's rain
and one without. “It rains tomorrow” is true in the first continuation,
false in the second, and unsettled from their shared past. Its classical
excluded-middle disjunction is true in each and hence supertrue. This
preserves excluded middle without making either disjunct supertrue; it is
not strong Kleene evaluation of the disjunction of two gaps.

If the admissible class is empty, universal quantification instead makes
both Q and its negation vacuously universal. Our actual-solution baseline
prevents that failure. A later theory admitting arbitrary partial stages
must explicitly impose extendibility or handle inconsistency.

## What the field results can add

Our horizons definition F_L(b) is uniform over every pair of solutions
agreeing on P_b, not initially relative to one particular actual solution.
This quantifier difference matters for nonlinear or restricted classes.

For the nonempty class of all smooth real solutions of a fixed linear
equation, it can be resolved. Let

    V_b = {v : v solves the homogeneous equation and v|P_b = 0}.

Then C_b(s*)=s*+V_b. At a target x, evaluation v -> v(x) is a linear map
to R. Its image is either {0} or all of R. Consequently:

* If x is fixed, every actual-past class fixes its value there.
* If x is not fixed, every actual-past class allows every real value there.
  A witness v with v(x) nonzero can be rescaled to reach any target value.

Thus, conditional on the reviewed positive-mass classification in
`../horizons/mass-classification/RESULT.md`, before maximum expansion every
x outside J^−(b) permits every real scalar value across past-compatible
solutions. Every Boolean predicate of that single value whose truth set is
a nonempty proper subset of R is therefore unsettled in this precise
compatibility sense. After the threshold, all point-value predicates are
settled because all point values are fixed. This is an elementary semantic
corollary of linearity and the field result, not an independently novel
classification or a proof of ontic openness.

The scope restrictions are essential:

1. A general nonfixed quantity need not make each proposition about it
   unsettled. A class allowing values 1 and 2 leaves the exact value
   nonfixed while settling positivity. The stronger conclusion above uses
   unrestricted affine scaling, not merely the definition of nonfixing.
2. A finite norm bound destroys unrestricted scaling. The existing O(m²)
   bound can keep a threshold proposition settled when the reference value
   stays farther from the threshold than the permitted ambiguity. One must
   specify the norm, reference class and margin before claiming this in a
   concrete case. Requiring each individual smooth solution to have finite
   energy on a compact Cauchy slice is not such a common bound: multiplying
   finite energy by any finite scaling factor still gives finite energy.
3. Separate point values can vary while joint constraints remain fixed.
   Even the toy family (q,q), q in R, fixes equality between two readings
   although each reading ranges over R. Actual PDE correlations need their
   own calculation; this toy family is not one.
4. The solution class fixes M. Every completion already contains the same
   target event mathematically. These calculations cannot test creation or
   nonexistence of future spacetime points.
5. Exact agreement on an entire open past is far stronger than finite
   records. No detector or assertion result follows from this semantics.

The mathematically interesting transition is therefore a transition in
which predicates remain invariant over a specified compatibility class.
Calling it the birth of truth would require a further semantic bridge;
calling it the birth of reality would require yet another ontological one.

## The central conditional obstruction

Here is a small argument worth pursuing, with every philosophical premise
exposed. It is a familiar supervenience tension in a localized form, not a
claimed new no-go theorem.

Assume there are two admitted models s and t with the same past at b but
opposite outcomes for a fixed future proposition Q. Consider:

**A. Local ground completeness.** Everything relevant to the truth of Q
from b is exhausted by grounds G_b and fixed laws. Matching those grounds
and laws entails matching truth values from b.

**B. Past-ground identification.** Same classical past at b entails the
same G_b. There are no additional primitive future-directed facts,
dispositions, selections or laws differing between s and t.

**C. Outcome-matching bivalence.** Q has a bivalent truth value from b that
matches its outcome in the designated complete model.

A and B make Q's truth value equal in s and t; C makes it different. These
three commitments cannot coexist for that pair. The derivation is immediate.
The hard philosophical work lies in defending or rejecting the premises.

In particular B is NOT a consequence of “only the causal past exists.”
Objects may have primitive powers or tensed properties not encoded in the
classical field restriction. Conversely, equality of current evidence is
not equality of all grounds. A claimed physical counterexample must show
that the selected models agree on the proposed complete grounds, not just
on convenient records.

Nor is A harmless. Grounding is generally finer-grained than entailment,
and a truthmaker necessitation/supervenience demand must be defended in the
relevant modal and temporal sense. Filomeno's abstract-level objection to
imposing truthmaker maximalism is a mandatory adversary for the next phase.
This argument assumes the displayed supervenience principle directly; it
does not derive it from factivity or from the word “ground.”

## Strong replies that must survive our comparison

| Position/version | Coherent response | Remaining obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Eternalism with designated history | Actual future occurrence can supply a truth condition; other past-compatible solutions do not refute actual truth | Explain the proposed truthmaking relation rather than identify it with prediction; no commitment to determinism |
| Deterministic growing block with primitive governing laws | Future existence can be absent while laws and adequate existing state fix outcomes | Show the existing state really is adequate; a causal past need not contain Cauchy data |
| Gappy growing block | Reject C for unsettled propositions and use a defended supervaluation semantics | Explain quantifiers, passage, assertion and retrospective reports; acknowledge existing stage-theoretical work |
| Bivalent growing block | Reject A or B, allow appropriately tensed/nonlocal grounds or a different temporal truthmaking principle | Specify the extra structure and its cost; future truth need not entail present future existence |
| Consistent standpoint realism | Keep centered domains and a coherent global description, but make standpoint facts fundamental | Give an argument for that grounding direction beyond a domain restriction or a successful translation |

No row is rejected by the horizons calculation alone. The alternatives are
not an exhaustive taxonomy of temporal ontology.

## The sharper next research contract

The promising question is:

**Can a covariant, event-relative theory identify complete existing grounds
for future truths, preserve the intended bivalent or gappy logic, and explain
cross-standpoint agreement without covertly supplying the whole future as
primitive structure?**

This needs three bounded follow-ups, rather than immediate manuscript prose:

1. **Domain and tense contract.** Construct a minimal varying-domain
   language on causal standpoints. Specify target reference, existence,
   time/standpoint shifts and reports of earlier utterances. Reproduce Loss's
   frozen-domain objection before attempting a relativistic repair. Compare
   branching-spacetime frameworks; do not import a linear-past tree silently.
2. **Grounds contract.** For one strong growing-block account and one
   eternalist rival, list complete candidate grounds, their temporal status,
   modality and laws. Ask whether the same-past differing-outcome models
   really agree on those grounds. An explicit refusal of truthmaker
   maximalism is an answer to examine, not a disqualification.
3. **Discriminating result.** Seek either a defensible obstruction under
   independently motivated premises, or a pair of complete interpretations
   sharing truth conditions but differing in explanation. In the latter
   case, identify what explanatory difference could count as a reason for
   preference. Do not announce ontological equivalence from a translation.

Stop or narrow the project if it only reproduces supervaluationism, changes
the meaning of “true” by definition, or gives every rival the same model
with a different label. A worthwhile negative outcome is an exact account
of what the physics leaves undecided and which philosophical bridge bears
the burden. The present prelim establishes that research target; it does
not close it.
