# What would distinguish earlier from later grounds?

28 October 2026. Bounded conditional audit. The essential Correia–Merlo
article remains unread beyond its abstract; no reconstruction of its two
arguments or extended grounding logic is claimed.

## Source boundary

Correia and Merlo, *Cross-temporal grounding*, Analytic Philosophy 65(3)
(2024), 333–352, DOI 10.1111/phib.12287, proposes that present past-directed
facts can have past grounds. Its abstract names arguments from intimacy and
past explanation, plus an extension of grounding logic. None was accessible

Primary text actually inspected: C&R chapter 7, selected pp.106–112 from

pp.482–484, via the public ResearchGate full text. The chapter's sometimes
operator places the existential grounding witness outside the utterance-time
operator's scope. The reply distinguishes a present truth-fact from its
later explanatory fact. Neither text licenses moving that later witness
into the present domain.

## Separate three questions

1. **Explanatory direction:** if A grounds B, B does not ground A.
2. **Temporal direction:** the occurrence described by A is no later than
   the truth or fact described by B.
3. **Present availability:** grounds of a truth at t exist at t, even when
   located earlier.

These are different claims. The third can be compatible with past things
on GBT while failing for merely future things. It nevertheless adds a
restriction to the book's temporally distributed grounding requirement.
Location and existence must not be interchanged in that inference.

## An exact candidate restriction and its cost

Our candidate, not an attributed author principle:

**Availability.** A truth at standpoint t must have sufficient explanatory
resources existing at t, and its truth supervenes on the intrinsic
constitution of those resources together with fixed laws.

The supervenience clause matters: bare existence of grounds supplies no
complete account of their properties or explanatory adequacy. Existing
Nero alone does not explain his former madness (chapter 7 pp.107–108).

Assume a class of possibilities Ω, a map R_t recording *all* the stipulated
intrinsic resources and laws at t, and an outcome predicate Q:Ω→{0,1}.
Assume outcome-matching bivalence, and that Q differs on one fibre of R_t.
Availability is then incompatible with these assumptions: supervenience
requires a function k with Q=k∘R_t, whereas two equal R_t values with unequal
Q values contradict that equation. This elementary factorization argument
is already in the selection lane; it is not a new theorem.

For a past-directed predicate constant on every such fibre, this obstruction
does not arise. Constancy proves factorization as a set function; it does
not prove that an explanatory ground exists. The asymmetry is therefore
conditional on the chosen resource base and its different fixing power for
the two predicates. A causal-past field map supplies a restricted example;
it does not establish a complete metaphysical resource base.

Availability can be stated without naming an ontology. Its truth is not
ontology-neutral evidence: it is exactly the substantive demand disputed by
C&R. Adding current future-directed derivative facts to R_t can restore
factorization without making future events currently exist. Excluding those
facts requires the intrinsic-resource restriction and a further defense of
its completeness. The argument cannot silently call R_t 'all reality'.

## Why ordinary grounding asymmetry cannot supply the temporal restriction

Let G be any nonempty strict explanatory relation, and let ℓ assign temporal
labels to its relata. Irreflexivity, asymmetry and transitivity of G place
no condition on ℓ. Replacing ℓ by −ℓ preserves every one of those relational
properties while reversing every unequal time comparison. Therefore those
properties alone cannot entail that every ground is temporally earlier
than its consequence. This is a logical observation about independent
structure, not a model of either authors' quantified tense or grounding
logic. Facts about present truth can themselves concern other times, so even
assigning a unique ℓ to a fact requires care.

A separate **No-later-grounds** axiom, G(A,B) ⇒ ℓ(A)≤ℓ(B), would select one
direction once the labels were justified. It would do so by stipulation
until an independent argument connected explanation with temporal order.
A causal arrow does not provide this connection without an additional
premise identifying or constraining grounding by causal dependence.
Epistemic access, memories and thermodynamic records likewise do not by
themselves impose a constraint on metaphysical grounds.

Nor does failure to derive a directional prohibition prove that grounding
is time-symmetric. To extend an accepted past-grounding account to future
truth one would need a time-reversal invariance premise for the relevant
semantic and grounding rules, plus admissibility of the transformed case.
Neither follows from asymmetry and transitivity, and neither has been
verified for Correia–Merlo's unread system.

## Current verdict

The accessible sources leave the proposed prohibition underdetermined.
Availability gives a precise conditional obstruction but is a contested
bridge premise. Abstract grounding-order properties give no temporal
prohibition. A source-based comparison of the strongest argument for past
grounds must await the Correia–Merlo main text, especially the two named
arguments and its treatment of factivity and tense scope. The chapter 7
parity discussion cannot substitute for that paper. No verdict on the
full coherence or superiority of either ontology follows.
