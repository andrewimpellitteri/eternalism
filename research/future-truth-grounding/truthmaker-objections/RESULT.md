# Truthmaker objection: bounded adjudication

Checked 2026-10-27. This is a selected full-text audit of one primary source, not a comprehensive truthmaking survey.

## Decisive finding

Filomeno is a direct and substantial objection to treating truthmaker maximalism as a consequence of Humean supervenience or growing-block ontology. It defeats the preliminary argument only if that argument presents its ground-completeness premise as something forced by those views. It does not defeat the conditional contradiction in PRELIMINARY: if local ground completeness, past-ground identity and outcome-matching bivalence are independently accepted for a same-past/different-future pair, the contradiction remains immediate.

The source separates three moves that should not be collapsed. First, Humean supervenience does not entail the standard truthmaker demand Briggs and Forbes require (§3.1, printed pp. 6–8). Second, the growing-block theory does not itself entail even a weaker future-directed maximalism (§3.2, pp. 9–10): Filomeno argues that existing past facts and nonexistent/unreal future facts are asymmetrical, and that the ontology can allow future-directed discourse without reifying future truthmakers. Third, one may deny present truth-values for future contingents (§3.3, pp. 10–13), eliminating a demand to ground those present truths. Each is a distinct reply; the third changes the semantic target and carries logical/assertoric costs Filomeno acknowledges.

The preliminary's strongest test survives as a conditional test, but its physical interpretation needs an important limitation. Let two admitted complete histories s and t agree exactly throughout the stipulated causal past at b and disagree about Q (rain at a specified future event). If (A) all facts relevant to Q's present truth are exhausted by the complete grounds G_b and fixed laws; (B) s and t therefore share those grounds and laws; and (C) Q is now bivalent and its value tracks which complete history is actual, then A+B imply equal values while C implies opposite values. Incompatibility follows. This toy pair tests only that semantic/grounding contract; it is not a physical result and does not show that two physically complete worlds have identical grounds.

Filomeno supplies live ways to reject the bridge from ontology to A or B: accept truthmaker gaps; treat future facts as grounds only when they obtain; speak of hypothetical future truthmakers without postulating them in present fundamental ontology; or deny current bivalence. The first three do not by themselves establish that the same *present* grounds determine truth. The temporalized view can make Q grounded later while leaving its present grounding unsettled. The gappy view rejects C. Neither is a counterexample to the conditional derivation.

## Objection/response contract for PRELIMINARY A and B

**A — Local ground completeness.** The wording “everything relevant to the truth of Q from b is exhausted by grounds G_b and fixed laws” is a substantive truth/grounding-supervenience demand, not a consequence of causal locality, Humeanism, or growing-block ontology alone. Filomeno explicitly disputes the inference from these metaphysical views to standard or revised truthmaker maximalism (intro pp. 2–3; §§3.1–3.2, pp. 6–10). If A means only that grounds, when present, necessitate the truth they ground, that is weaker than saying every truth has a ground. If A instead means same complete grounds guarantee same truth, it is a supervenience principle and must be defended as such. Do not call the weaker necessitation relation “maximalism.”

**B — Past-ground identification.** Agreement on the classical restriction to the causal past entails sameness of G_b only if G_b has already been restricted to those past facts and common laws. It does not follow from “only past and present exist”: primitive powers, tensed facts, a selected continuation, nonlocal grounds, or distinctions in law/totality facts could differ. Filomeno's treatment expressly opens discourse about hypothetical properties without ontological commitment (p. 7), and separately rejects an inference from growing-block ontology to future truthmakers (pp. 9–10). Thus B is a substantive completeness/identity hypothesis, not a reading of the ontology.

**C — Outcome-matching bivalence.** This picks a complete-history/Ockhamist truth condition. It is compatible with the growing block as a semantic option but not required by it. Filomeno's gappy alternative rejects C; his own argument that Humean growing-block laws cannot settle future outcomes is an author claim, not a theorem established by the field equations or by the present check. Determinism, probability near one, and truth are distinct: the manuscript itself warns against identifying high probability with settledness (pp. 12–13).

## Distinctions and limits

- **Truthmaker maximalism:** every truth has a truthmaker. Filomeno reports a modest formulation as “truth supervenes on being” (§2, printed p. 4), but these formulations should not be treated as interchangeable without a stated account of “being,” modal base and necessitation.
- **Necessitation:** a proposed ground/truthmaker cannot obtain while its truth is false (relative to a specified modality). Necessitation by a candidate ground does not by itself assert that every truth has a candidate ground.
- **Same-ground truth supervenience:** worlds agreeing in all specified grounds/laws agree on the truth value. This is the exact bridge used in A; it may hold even if some truths lack grounds, depending on how the base and truth-values are defined. Conversely, maximalism alone does not establish the particular past-ground identity B.
- **Temporalized truthmaking:** Filomeno's pp. 7–8 discussion allows facts to ground propositions about their own time when those facts obtain, without requiring that they exist at every utterance time. This can preserve later grounding while refusing a present truthmaker for tomorrow's event. It is not present-time truthmaker maximalism unless the temporal parameter is made explicit.
- **Rejecting present truthmaking / rejecting present truth:** These are different. A truthmaker gap retains a true proposition without a truthmaker. A gappy future-contingent semantics denies that Q is now true or false, so no present truth-value needs grounding. Neither follows merely from denying maximalism.

**Strongest test and result:** the two-history construction above is the strongest minimal test for the exact conditional obstruction. Vary only future outcome while holding all proposed present grounds and fixed laws equal. If outcome-matching present bivalence is also imposed, the package is inconsistent. If a defender identifies an additional ground (primitive selection, future-directed property, a law with governing force, or a nonlocal fact), then either B fails or the proposed ground must be shown to preserve the growing-block/Humean commitments at issue. If the defender denies present bivalence, C fails. If the defender allows truthmaker gaps, A's existential/maximalist reading fails, though truth-supervenience may remain. This diagnoses the burden; it does not decide which response is metaphysically correct.
