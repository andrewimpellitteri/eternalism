## Finding

No fatal inference error found in the reviewed sections. The main claims are appropriately conditional and the text repeatedly distinguishes extensional sufficiency from existence and explanation. The following qualifications are important to retain in summaries and future use.

### 1. Factorization iff constant fibres

The criterion is correct for the stated set-up: for a fixed proposition Q, a truth function on the image of G_b exists exactly when v_Q is constant on each fibre of G_b. Defining f_Q on the image avoids any need to choose values for unrealized signatures. This is an extensional factorization theorem only. It does not show that G_b is a ground in a metaphysical sense, that G_b exists at b, or that its contents explain Q.

This local factorization constraint and truthmaker principles are different constraints, with no unqualified entailment hierarchy between them. Factorization requires sameness of Q's value wherever the proposed signature is the same, but does not establish that the signature exists as a maker. Truthmaker maximalism requires makers for truths, but without assumptions connecting G_b to complete makers it does not entail local G_b-supervenience. Necessitation by a proposed maker is a further relation to specify; none of these principles should be ranked here without bridge assumptions. These distinctions match the preliminary's caveat about grounding being finer-grained than entailment.

### 2. Language-separating signature requirement

The lower bound in §2 follows as stated: if a language separates two models in a past-data fibre by assigning different truth values to some included proposition, a signature that factors all those values cannot identify the pair. If the language separates every pair in the fibre, the signature must be injective there, modulo any expressly adopted observational/gauge identification.

The scalar threshold predicates separate distinct real values because a rational lies between any two unequal reals. Taking them at every specified target point separates the resulting field configurations. This conclusion is restricted to that language and target set. It does not establish that an adequate signature encodes a whole global history when the language does not separate histories, nor that it is physically instantiated as a present ground. The explicit warning that one isolated claim does not entail global-history selection is apt.

In particular, an extra primitive selection fact could distinguish alternatives for a particular proposition without entailing eternalism. Its content, temporal status and grounding role would need separate specification. A selected outcome or a functionally sufficient seed is not by itself a commitment that all future events presently exist. The report does not make that inference.

### 3. Conditional positive-mass consequence

Section 3 accurately frames its result as conditional on the reviewed classification and on the specified solution class: all smooth real solutions of the same fixed linear equation on the same closed-dust background. The all-real range over an actual-past fibre uses linearity plus a past-vanishing difference with nonzero value at the target; scaling that difference reaches every real value. This is why every nonempty proper one-value truth set varies on that fibre. The caveat about common norm bounds and the distinction between individually finite energy and a uniform cap are material and correctly retained.

Agreement on I^−(b) does **not** mean equality of complete Cauchy data. The perturbations used to witness nonfixing can have different data on a complete Cauchy slice while still agreeing throughout the stipulated causal past. The relativistic-branching sidecar gives the analogous deterministic wave example and explicitly makes this distinction. Coordinator §3's “actual-past fibre” phrasing is correct; future explanations should not paraphrase it as “same initial data” or “same complete physical state.”

The positive-mass row at and after maximum expansion follows from the cited sidecar's scope and fixing-set result, not from the factorization lemma alone. The coordinator marks it conditional and does not present the elementary criterion as a new PDE proof. This review did not independently rederive that theorem.

### 4. Truthmaker and supervenience distinctions

The coordinator correctly says rejecting maximalism does not automatically reject every supervenience claim. Filomeno's objection blocks an inference from growing-block/Humean ontology to maximalism; it does not refute the constant-fibre criterion or its conditional consequence. Conversely, passing the criterion cannot establish truthmaker existence or genuine grounding. This is consistent with the preliminary's A/B/C contract and with the source-lane summary.

A remaining presentation hazard is the phrase “grounds determine the truth values.” It can be heard either as the extensional condition (1) or as metaphysical grounding. The coordinator largely disambiguates this at the outset and repeatedly calls the mathematical result extensional. Preserve that qualification whenever the criterion is restated.

## Disposition

No fatal issue found within this bounded review. The principal limitations are already acknowledged: the claims depend on the chosen model class, common reference convention, truth policy and admissibility of the proposed signature; exact-past compatibility is not finite-record agreement or equality of full Cauchy data; and sufficiency is not grounding or ontology. No new source survey was performed.

## Final manuscript-adjacent check: TWO_ORDERS.md

Reviewed 2026-10-27. The causal-diamond example correctly shows why causal precedence is not a tree order on alternative histories: b and c are spacelike yet compatible events in one spacetime, and both precede d. A tree order with linearly ordered pasts cannot preserve those relations. The report does not mistake incomparable standpoints for incompatible global histories.

The two indexed relations are kept distinct: causal advance relates (s,b) to (s,c) within one solution, while same-past agreement compares (s,b) and (t,b) at the same standpoint. Shrinking agreement classes under causal advance is a restriction fact; it does not say advance selects a branch or that exact open-past field data are an observer's records.

The strict-growth/singleton comparison is valid conditionally in the stated model: if the proposed domain is E_b=J^−(b), then a strict future timelike step makes that domain grow; for the reviewed positive-mass solution class, at and after π the past restriction already fixes the whole field solution, so later same-past classes remain singletons. This juxtaposition shows that proposed domain growth and refinement of field-solution alternatives can come apart. It establishes neither that E_b is the actual ontology nor that passage or new existence occurs. It entails no global indeterminism; indeed the singleton result is a deterministic case. Fixed-reference propositions about a named event must also remain distinct from centered or tensed content such as “x is present now,” whose content changes with the standpoint.

No fatal issue found in this bounded check. The claims are conditional on the specified domain proposal and positive-mass classification; no claim about actual ontology or passage is proved.
