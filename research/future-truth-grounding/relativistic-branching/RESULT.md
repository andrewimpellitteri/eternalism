## Exact deterministic wave counterexample

Use 1+1 dimensional Minkowski spacetime with coordinates (t,z), b=(0,0),
and the deterministic wave equation

    (∂_t² − ∂_z²)φ = 0.

Let f be a nonzero smooth bump supported in (1,2), with f(3/2)>0. Set

    φ_0(t,z)=0,                  φ_1(t,z)=f(t−z).

The two second derivatives of φ_1 cancel exactly. At every event in I^−(b),
t<−|z| and hence t−z<0. Thus both solutions vanish on the ENTIRE strict
causal past, not merely on finitely many records. But at x=(3/2,0),
φ_1(x)>0=φ_0(x). They already differ on the Cauchy slice t=−1: the pulse's
data lie in −3<z<−2. Each solution has compactly supported smooth data on
each constant-t slice and finite energy there. Unique wave evolution from
complete Cauchy data is consistent with nonuniqueness from I^−(b).

This is an exact mathematical counterexample to the inference from
same-causal-past/different-future field values to failure of deterministic
Cauchy evolution. It is not a claim that the alternatives are merely
subjective ignorance; whether their differences concern existing facts
from b is precisely the ontological question.

## A separate obstruction in our closed-dust cosmology

The exact closed-dust background used in the horizons paper is

    M=(0,2π)×S³,       g=a(τ)²(−dτ²+dΩ³²),       a(τ)>0 in M.

Take antipodal Y and −Y and events

    p=(3π/2,Y),                 q=(3π/2,−Y).

If r=(σ,Z) were a common causal future in M, the conformal metric gives

    d(Y,Z) ≤ σ−3π/2,         d(−Y,Z) ≤ σ−3π/2.

The triangle inequality would imply

    π=d(Y,−Y) ≤ 2(σ−3π/2),       hence σ≥2π.

But σ<2π everywhere in M. Therefore p and q have NO common upper bound in
the physical causal order, and M is not upward directed. The same failure
holds for simultaneous antipodes at every τ≥3π/2. Earlier points can have
a common future; this argument does not deny that.

Consequently **a complete closed-dust spacetime cannot be identified,
by an onto order isomorphism, with one history under Definition 2's directedness
requirement**. This obstruction appears even before adding alternative
field solutions or genuine indeterminism. It is a correspondence problem
for a literal transfer, not a contradiction in either theory and not a
claim of novelty. Adding an ideal final event, changing the order, using
regional histories or choosing a generalized compatibility definition may
repair a model, but each changes the representation contract. None is
silently licensed by the existing theorem.

An order embedding as a proper subset of a larger history is not excluded:
the added common future could lie outside the physical image. A single
ideal final event also does not by itself give the cited BST framework,
whose Definition 2 forbids maximal elements. These qualifications were
identified in `../growing-block/TRANSFER_REVIEW.md`.

The general problem is already recognized in the literature. Placek's
*Possibilities Without Possible Worlds/Histories* (2011), DOI
10.1007/s10992-010-9159-x, is a direct predecessor lead on alternatives to
directed histories; an indexed manuscript introduction explicitly mentions
the common-upper-bound issue in GR. Full manuscript access failed in this
pass, so its proposed repair is not verified here. Our closed-dust example
is an explicit application of a known type of limitation, not a new
objection to branching theories. Follow up this source before inventing a
generalized history framework.

A more directly verified predecessor is Placek's *Branching for General
Relativists* (2014), published chapter pp.191–221, DOI
10.1007/978-3-319-01754-9_10. Its introduction, p.192, explicitly states
the common-upper-bound limitation for GR and motivates generalized
histories. Those introductory pages were read in the original chapter
PDF; the later generalized-manifold proof was not audited. The next step
is to compare that existing repair, rather than infer that our simple
example exposes a gap nobody has noticed.

This matters because causal structure is part of our philosophical
argument. Replacing causal precedence with an order that relates all
events to a common future can erase the very horizon distinction we are
studying. Any repair must state which physical relations it preserves.

## Minimal bridge obligations

Before C_b(s*) can be called a class of possible growth histories, specify:

1. Whether a member is a complete solution, a possible event structure, an
   ersatz representation, or an actually existing branch.
2. Which equivalence or counterpart relation identifies events across
   members. Sharing fixed coordinate points does not distinguish
   incompatible outcomes as possible events.
3. Whether matching I^−(b) also matches all proposed existing grounds,
   including lawmakers, dispositions, clocks and extra fields.
4. What supplies the modal force of admissibility beyond satisfying a PDE
   with different complete Cauchy data.
5. How joint compatibility is represented in geometries without common
   futures. Definition 2 cannot simply be quoted for the full dust M.
6. Which truth and existence quantifiers shift when a standpoint changes.
   Agreement of field values is not itself growth of an ontological domain.

The safest immediate baseline remains a family of solutions plus explicit
restriction maps, without asserting that it is a BST structure. A future
semantics can be built on that baseline while its modal and ontological
interpretations remain distinct research commitments.
