# A minimal model must keep causal advance and modal alternatives apart

27 October 2026. Proposed neutral semantic baseline. Mathematical
definitions below do not identify what fundamentally exists. No originality
claim: multi-index semantics and modal/causal distinctions are prior art.

## A causal diamond is not a choice between histories

In 1+1 Minkowski spacetime, take

    a=(0,0), b=(1,−1/2), c=(1,1/2), d=(2,0).

Both b and c lie chronologically after a and before d, but b and c are
spacelike to each other. They are two compatible events in ONE spacetime.
They cannot be ordered by a tree relation with linearly ordered pasts:
both precede d, so such a relation would require b and c to be comparable.

Consequently, the order of causal standpoints is not, in general, the tree
order of alternative global stages of growth. Incomparability of two
standpoints does not mean their events cannot both occur. Replacing
histories by maximal causal chains would make the same mistake: one
chain cannot include b and c, but the physical model includes both.

Standard branching-spacetime histories address compatibility differently;
the dust directedness issue in the adjacent report further shows that
their precise definition cannot simply be assumed for every geometry.

## Two relations on a common baseline

Fix a background causal spacetime M and an admitted solution class Ω.
Use a pair (s,b) as an evaluation index: s is a complete mathematical
solution; b is a standpoint within that solution. Define distinct relations:

    Causal advance:  (s,b) -> (s,c) iff b causally precedes c.

    Past agreement: (s,b) ~ (t,b) iff s and t agree on I^−(b).

Causal advance stays within one model. Past agreement compares models at
the same standpoint. Neither relation says that alternative models or
future events are concretely actual. A specified worldline/proper-time
convention supplies “tomorrow” if the language needs it.

A proposition about a fixed target event x and field value can be evaluated
in s. Its settlement at (s,b) quantifies over the ~-class. Ontological
existence can be separately proposed as a predicate E_b(x); a candidate
past-realm interpretation takes E_b(x) iff x∈J^−(b). That last clause is
an explicit ontological proposal, not a consequence of the two relations.
It must not be built into the supposedly neutral baseline as a finding.

For fixed-reference propositions, causal advance makes past-agreement
classes shrink: I^−(b)⊆I^−(c) implies C_c(s)⊆C_b(s). That is a restriction
fact. It does not identify an observer's real records with the entire
field on an open causal past, or show that causal advance selects a branch.

## Two kinds of increase can come apart completely

If the proposed domain is E_b=J^−(b), then along a strict future timelike
step b<<c it grows strictly. It includes c at the later standpoint and
does not include c at the earlier one.

By contrast the information partition given by exact past agreement may
stop refining. In the reviewed closed-dust positive-mass example, at and
after τ_b=π the past restriction already fixes the WHOLE field solution.
Every past-agreement class is a singleton. Moving to a later c cannot
refine a singleton, even though J^−(c) strictly contains J^−(b).

Thus this exact model admits the following conditional interpretation:

    newly existing events continue to enter the proposed past realm,
    while no new distinctions between complete field solutions are resolved.

This is a useful stress test for equating coming into existence with
becoming settled. It leaves room for deterministic ontological addition;
it does not establish that such addition occurs. It also does not make
all centered sentences unchanging: “x is present now” changes its center
or tense, unlike a fixed claim about the value at a named event.

The converse identification also fails without assumptions: even before
π, some fixed future field predicates can be settled despite the proposed
domain excluding their target. The exact examples and solution classes,
not a generic intuition about openness, determine where that happens.

## What is still missing

This baseline handles variation in field solutions on fixed M. It does
not yet model changing existence of M's points, primitive passage,
counterpart identity across different geometries, a full stage-dependent
quantifier language, or complete truthmaking grounds. Those are additional
contracts. A theorist can use abstract complete solutions as semantic
representations without endorsing an eternalist concrete ontology, but
must explain why the represented possibilities are the relevant ones.

The immediate philosophical benefit is precision: causal growth of a
proposed domain, refinement of alternatives and truthmaking explanation
can be compared without identifying them by notation.
