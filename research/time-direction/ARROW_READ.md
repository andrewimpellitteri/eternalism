# Does what the past fixes have a direction?

31 October 2026. Claude Code (Opus 5.5), at the user's request. First item in PARITY_MAP.md's order.
Question: is the time orientation that *What the Past Can Fix* takes as input physical, and does the
past-directed asymmetry of fixing depend on it? No novelty claim and no ontology verdict. Books were
read in part, paraphrased, with locators. Read depth is at the end. Not yet red-teamed.

## Result

1. **Fixing has no arrow of its own.** For time-reversal invariant laws, every fixing result has an
   exact mirror image obtained by reversing the orientation. The past enters through the choice of
   base, not through the relation.
2. **So "the past is fixed and the future open" cannot be read off the fixing relation.** Under
   equal bases the past is as open from the future as the future is from the past. The asymmetry
   needs another source.
3. **There are three candidate sources, and the paper is neutral between them.** Thermodynamic
   (Albert), perspectival (Price), and primitive or causal (Tooley). Each gives a different meaning
   to the premise (CB).
4. **One hinge is exposed.** The growing-block appeal to production, and Tooley's upper-bound
   candidate, need a direction of causation that is not merely thermodynamic or perspectival. That
   is what Albert and Price dispute.
5. **The paper has a real hole here,** and it should say so with a section of its own.

## 1. The mirror

Write F^-_L(b) for what a law class L fixes from agreement on the strict past of b, and F^+_L(b) for
what it fixes from agreement on the strict future. Let θ be an isometry of M reversing time
orientation, with L invariant under pullback by θ. Then

    θ(F^-_L(b)) = F^+_L(θ(b)),   θ(Det^-(b)) = Det^+(θ(b)).

Proof: a difference solution vanishing on I_s^-(b) pulls back to one vanishing on I_s^+(θ(b)), and θ
sends past-inextendible causal curves to future-inextendible ones. Without an isometry the same holds
in the weaker form that every theorem of Parts II and III applies to the time-reversed spacetime,
because its definitions use the orientation only as a label. This is elementary. It is the fixing
instance of Price's general warning, below.

Three consequences.

- **Minkowski.** The conformal scalar is fixed from b's strict future exactly on J^+(b). The future
  is exactly as unfixed from the past as the past is from the future.
- **Closed dust.** The reflection η→2π−η is an isometry reversing orientation. So Det^+(b)=M for
  η_b<π. Every event is fully guaranteed from one side or the other, except at η_b=π. The statement
  that the guarantee changes with cosmic epoch is true, and it mirrors exactly. It marks no
  orientation-independent feature of the model.
- **Einstein–scalar.** The system is time-reversal invariant, so the same-past theorem has a same-future
  twin: the whole causal future of an event, with total outgoing mass, leaves earlier central readings
  free. Rigidity mirrors too. This is a consequence of the time-reversal step, not a separately audited
  theorem.

Theorem 7 (Stein–GR) carries its orientation in (CB). Reverse it and the same proof gives the future
lobe. The paper's Limits paragraph already says the theorem does not explain the orientation.

## 2. Three sources of the asymmetry

**Thermodynamic: Albert.** The dynamics is invariant under time reversal, and the statistical postulate
over the present macrocondition gives higher entropy on both temporal sides (ch.4). So the asymmetry
needs a further law-like posit, the Past-Hypothesis: the world began in a particular low-entropy
macrocondition (end of ch.4, p.96). Records then come from it. On Albert's account prediction and
retrodiction use the dynamics and the statistical rule, what we know of the future is reachable by prediction, and
most of what we know of the past is not reachable by retrodiction and depends on records (ch.6,
pp.113–116). The asymmetry of knowledge and of intervention is meant to follow.

Applied here: the past is a natural base because it is what we have records of, and that is
explained by a boundary condition, not by the law class L.

**Perspectival: Price.** His methodological claim is that we should not accept an argument in one temporal direction
while rejecting its mirror, the temporal double standard (ch.1; ch.2 summary; ch.3 on radiation; ch.4 on cosmology).
He separates the asymmetries of dependence, causation and agency (ch.6, pp.132–133), and defends a
perspectival view of causal asymmetry (ch.6, from p.155; ch.7, from the contents). He presupposes that flow and the
present are perspectival (ch.1, pp.5–6), so he is a party, not a neutral arbiter. His cosmology is the sharpest
pressure on section 1's Closed dust remark: if a low-entropy boundary needs explaining at one end, the
symmetric Gold universe cannot be dismissed by arguments that assume the arrow (ch.4, pp.81ff).

Applied here: the past as base reflects our position in the entropy gradient and the agent's
standpoint. The mirror in section 1 is what the view from nowhen looks like for fixing.

**Causal or primitive: Tooley.** He gives a causal theory of temporal order and direction, joined to a tensed view
and defended by an argument from the analysis of causation (ch.4; ch.9). In his account causal direction is
basic, and a cause is actual as of its effect's time and not conversely (Tooley 2010; 1997 §4.6 located, not
read closely). So the arrow is not thermodynamic. He also defends absolute simultaneity (ch.11; Earman 2008
p.149 reports an Absolute Frame), which is the clock-equipped horn of Part I, not a local relation.

Applied here: (CB) is read as expressing a real causal order, and the asymmetry of openness would be
ontological.

## 4. What a rival has a reason to revise

**Anyone who uses "the past is fixed, the future open" as evidence for a growing inventory.** In exact
law-relative terms the slogan is false as a symmetric comparison: with the base matched, each side is
equally fixed or unfixed from the other. The datum behind it, the asymmetry of records and intervention,
has a candidate explanation that uses no inventory distinction, namely Albert's. So it is not evidence for
growth until that explanation is shown to fail. The growing theorist can reply that it does fail, that
thermodynamic asymmetry presupposes a real direction, or that openness is not about records. Those replies
are the live ones. It is not a refutation of growth. It removes one support.

**Anyone who treats thermodynamics as settling the direction of time.** The Past-Hypothesis is a boundary
posit whose status is disputed, and I have read only the opening of Albert's account of intervention.
Maudlin-style primitive direction, unread here, remains a rival.

## 5. Open question

Does the Past-Hypothesis restore an asymmetry in fixing itself? If L includes it, L is not reversal
invariant, and the mirror fails. My judgment is that exact fixing barely changes, since a macrocondition does
not fix exact values, and that the asymmetry appears instead in conditional probabilities over completions. If
so, a fifth notion belongs beside exact fixing: chance given a base. "Open" in much ordinary talk may mean that.
This is unproved, and it is the next technical task.

## 6. Other direct reads today

- **Armstrong 2004.** Direct now. Tot relates a mereological object to a property (ch.6.2, pp.72–73). He means
  contingent Tot relations, says the property is often abundant and may be time-indexed, as in his swans-on-the-lake-now
  example, and grants that the relation involves negation and sets a limit. In his chapter on time he finds the
  omnitemporal view simpler than presentism (pp.145–147). So a time-indexed totality is a defined index, which fits
  the diagnostic in TOTALITY_READ.md.
- **Tooley 1997.** Direct in part: preface, table of contents, sections located. Not read closely. The claim that he pairs
  a tensed view with absolute simultaneity rests on the chapter headings and Earman 2008 p.149.
- **Dainton 2000.** Only a 28-page preview was supplied, so the experience read waits for the book.
- **Forrest 2006.** Still unread.
