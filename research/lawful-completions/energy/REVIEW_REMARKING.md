# Red-team review: re-marking one scattering spacetime

**Disposition: VALID EXISTENTIAL NONPREDICTION EXAMPLE, WITH A MARKED-EXTENSION CAVEAT.** Assuming one regular Luk–Oh–Yang (LOY) global scattering solution has an exactly flat early central past and a later positive central response, one fixed duration admits an interval of readings from changing where the abstract marked past is embedded along the flat era. Every member can use the same unmarked spacetime, so its whole-spacetime charges, whenever defined, are identical. This proves failure of prediction from the isometry class of the abstract pointed past under the stated sparse marking data. It does not show two nonisometric unmarked spacetimes or two futures branching from one geometrically anchored event.

## Exact existential argument

Let \(\tau\) be proper time on the regular center geodesic, with arbitrary additive origin, and choose \(a\) so that the central past is exactly Minkowski for \(\tau\le a\). For the fixed global solution set

\[
q(\tau)=\operatorname{Ric}(U,U)_{\gamma(\tau)}.
\]

On the field side of the LOY normalization, \(\operatorname{Ric}=2\,d\phi\otimes d\phi\), so along the center \(q=2(U\phi)^2\). The field-side quantity is continuous under the solution regularity stated in the reduced construction. Continuity of the geometric Ricci contraction at the axis additionally requires the regular geometric reconstruction/atlas hypothesis; the reduced \(C^2\) estimates alone should not be silently treated as a proof of that atlas regularity. The earlier radial uniqueness argument gives a finite \(\tau_q>a\) with \(q(\tau_q)>0\) for the chosen nontrivial profile, once that axis regularity is available. This positivity is a property to establish for the selected solution, not a consequence of geodesic completeness alone.

Fix one duration \(T>\tau_q-a\). For

\[
\beta\in[a-T,\,\tau_q-T],
\]

we have \(\beta<a\), so the mark \(b_\beta=\gamma(\beta)\) lies in the flat era. The reading after duration \(T\) is \(q(\beta+T)\). As \(\beta\) varies, its argument ranges over \([a,\tau_q]\); continuity and the endpoint values 0 and \(q(\tau_q)>0\) imply that the image contains \([0,q(\tau_q)]\). This is an interval of values at the same \(T\), on one fixed global solution.

For each such mark, its entire causal past is in the flat wedge (in the double-null construction, \(v\le\beta<a\)). Flat time translation gives an isometry between any two of these entire pointed pasts, including the marked point and central unit tangent. The scalar field vanishes there. No absolute proper-time origin is used: translating the coordinate origin shifts \(a,\tau_q,\beta\) together.

## Marked-extension precision

“Fixing \(b\)” has two different meanings. If it means a literally fixed point in one already specified ambient manifold and a comparison that keeps the ambient identification fixed, then this sliding-mark construction changes \(b\) and does not meet that stronger comparison. But fixing \(b\) as one abstract element of a past model \(P\) does **not** rule out the construction: identify each flat pointed past with the same abstract \(P\), carrying its marked point to that same abstract element, and use the different identifications to embed \(P\) into the one ambient scattering spacetime at different flat-era events. These are legitimate marked extensions of the same abstract pointed past. Their extensions need not be isometric as marked extensions, since an isometry of the flat pasts need not extend through the incoming pulse. The underlying unmarked spacetime in this example is nevertheless literally the same.

Thus the example is a nonprediction counterexample for a category whose input is only the abstract pointed past and whose allowed extensions do not include a boundary anchor fixing its placement. It is not a counterexample to uniqueness after an asymptotic time origin, pulse-relative clock, launch event, boundary frame, or other embedding anchor is added and required to be preserved. To exclude this mechanism, a theorem should impose such an anchor/comparison, or demand distinct unmarked developments if its target is solution-level nonuniqueness. Merely saying “the same marked point” is insufficient unless the point is fixed through the ambient identification or the marking includes the relevant anchor.

Every member here has the same unmarked \((M,g,\phi)\). Any invariant or charge defined for that whole unmarked solution is therefore unchanged, without assuming an ADM limit or identifying ADM with incoming Bondi mass. The marked extensions may still be distinct objects because their embeddings of \(P\) differ. The example establishes neither two nonisometric unmarked solutions nor multiple continuations from one anchored ambient event.

## Other qualifications and novelty

The construction needs a finite later point with \(q>0\), continuity on \([a,\tau_q]\), an exactly flat early central past, and a central proper-time parameter. The paper's Theorem 1.7 supplies a global solution but does not by itself establish positivity of this particular readout for every nonzero profile. For a compact incoming profile, exact flatness on \(v<A\) follows by passing finite-cone flat regions to the global compact limit. The inference from nonzero profile to a nonzero central derivative uses radial uniqueness and the regular-axis assumptions; geometric Ricci continuity remains conditional as noted above.

As a lemma, the fixed-\(T\) interval is an IVT consequence once these hypotheses hold. It is not a controllability result, a differentiability statement for a scattering map, or evidence for branching from one anchored present. No broad priority search was performed; the novelty assessment is structural, not a literature-priority claim. The example also does not establish equivalence of temporal ontologies.
