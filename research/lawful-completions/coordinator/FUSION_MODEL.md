# Fusions do not by themselves determine the point-existence realm

2026-10-28. Explicit model of selected location/residence/fusion clauses. Not a model of the entire Correia--Rosenkranz logic, and not a novelty claim. Source checked: supplied chapter 9, printed pp.144,151--152; Replies to Critics pp.486--489.

## Construction

Fix an ambient causal spacetime M and any family of point realms R(s) containing s. Let objects be nonempty finite subsets A of M, identifying singleton {p} with the spacetime-point p. Define:

- A exists at standpoint s iff A intersects R(s).
- A is located at point p iff p belongs to A; this location relation is fixed across standpoints.
- Spacetime points are exactly the singleton objects.
- Fusion is set union.

These are ambient mathematical definitions for the model, not current existential quantifiers over every ambient label. Actualist quantifiers at s range over the objects that exist there. The construction illustrates the selected non-existence-entailing predicates; a full free-logic model would also need the book's complete logical framework and appendices.

## Clauses verified directly

Location always has a spacetime point as its second argument (A25R). A spacetime point is located only at itself (A26R). Location is invariant across standpoints (A27R). Every point exists at itself because s belongs to R(s).

Each object is a resident in the sense of the conditions in D21. If p is one of A's locations and p exists at s, then p belongs to A intersect R(s), so A exists at s. Conversely, if A exists at s, choose p in A intersect R(s): an existing point locates A. Every A is located at some existing point at some standpoint, since any p in A exists at p.

Fusion supports are unions. Thus a fusion exists precisely where at least one constituent exists:

 (A union B) intersects R(s) iff [A intersects R(s) or B intersects R(s)].

This is stronger than the printed conditional that a fusion, if it exists, exists wherever a part does: the construction explicitly supplies every finite fusion. Yet an existing fusion can have locations at points that do not exist at s. That is exactly what non-existence-entailing location allows.

The same clauses work with R(s)=D_BT(s), R(s)=H(s), or R(s)=J^-(s). In a causal frame all three contain s. H and D_BT are persistent; hence object existence in this construction is persistent for those choices as well. D_BT and H differ on the verified finite-conformal-duration examples. Therefore these selected location, resident and finite-fusion clauses, EVEN WITH persistence, do not select the bow-tie point realm.

## Why this matters for the location motivation

A qualifying object can exist through one currently existing part while being located at other, nonexisting points. It follows that the chapter's location and fusion clauses do not license the inference from 'a potential-bearing object is located at x' to 'x exists here'. Such a location-to-point-existence bridge needs independent justification if the parenthetical in the later reply is to derive the bow tie.

If the unrestricted bridge is added, the future-leak lemma in LOCATION_CLOSURE.md applies. Here it is concrete: take an existing point p in R(s) and a strict future point q not in R(s). The fusion {p,q} exists at s and is located at q. If qualifying potential is inherited from p, and every location of a qualifying existing object must exist, q is admitted, contradicting the no-future rule. No contradiction follows in the selected chapter clauses themselves, which deliberately avoid that bridge.

## Exact limits

This is not a derivation of the authors' intended causal-potential semantics, a full model of their quantified logic, or a proof that they endorse upward inheritance of potential. Finite fusions suffice for this dependency counterexample; no claim about all infinite fusions is necessary. The explicit model shows only that particular selected principles leave point existence underdetermined and identifies the additional bridge a stronger derivation would require. Since the book already distinguishes pointy and bow-tie theories under shared logical resources, this logical independence is unsurprising; its use here is to keep the parenthetical's explanatory burden precise.
