# What the location clause must supply

2026-10-28. Conditional formal analysis after rereading Correia--Rosenkranz, Replies to Critics, printed pp.486--490, especially p.489. This is an audit of possible readings, not an attribution of these formal premises to the authors.

## Source boundary

The reply presents causal-network membership as one motivation, and adds locations of things that possess the relevant potential. It does not there specify an object ontology, fusion-existence principle, exact location rule, or whether an object's qualifying potential is stage-local or inherited from any part. The chapter's persistence discussion allows fusions but does not by itself establish all those additional rules. The project's chapter audit also records that its loc predicate is not existence-entailing. Therefore 'O is located at x' must not silently become 'x exists here'.

## One explicit support formalization

At a standpoint s, let L(O) subset M be an ambient mathematical support assigned to an object O. Distinguish:

- A_s(O): O exists at s;
- P_s(O): O has relevant causal potential from s;
- E_s(x): point x exists at s;
- a proposed location bridge A_s(O) and P_s(O) and x in L(O) implies E_s(x).

This bridge is an additional premise, not the definition of the chapter's loc predicate. The ambient support is bookkeeping for the audit; a complete theory must explain its reference to nonexisting locations.

Suppose potential is existential over support: P_s(O) holds when some y in L(O) has a common future with s. Then it is upward hereditary under support inclusion. If O is a qualifying existing object and F is an existing fusion with L(O) subset L(F), F also qualifies. The bridge admits every point in L(F).

**Conditional future-leak lemma.** If such an F has even one support point x in the strict causal future of s, these premises yield E_s(x), contradicting the no-future axiom of the bow-tie theory. A remote-fusion rescue must therefore address future points as well as distant spacelike points. It is insufficient merely to say that a potential-bearing part activates the fusion.

Ways to avoid the conclusion include: restrict the supports used by the bridge to existing stages, restrict admissible fusions, deny upward inheritance of potential, deny the location bridge, or add a no-future filter. These are nonexhaustive responses, and none is ruled out by this lemma.

## Why unrestricted fusion alone does not generate new existence

There is a separate actualist-generation issue. Suppose primitive available objects have supports contained in an already justified point set A, and new objects can be generated only by finite fusions of available objects with union support. By induction every generated support remains inside A. The location bridge then generates no point outside A. Allowing arbitrary unions of those supports does not change this conclusion.

Thus a fusion constructed from already licensed local objects cannot establish an initially unlicensed remote occupant's existence. To use a fusion with that remote occupant, one must independently license the occupant or permit fusion generation from constituents not yet licensed to exist at s. This is not automatically incoherent, but it is the extra ontological work required by that rescue. Do not treat an ambient name for the remote constituent as an existence proof.

Persisting primitive objects with supports extending beyond A are a different case: their supports can add locations if the bridge licenses them. The rule then depends on the selected primitive objects and their histories, rather than on causal order alone. This is not refuted by the finite-union argument.

## Why a no-future filter does not independently explain the bow tie

Adding the filter x not in the strict causal future of s prevents future leakage. If one also licenses sufficiently large existing qualifying supports covering every nonfuture point, the resulting location rule yields the bow-tie domain. But that conclusion depends jointly on support coverage, existence of those covering objects, potential inheritance and the explicit filter. Causal potential alone does not prove any of these.

This is an explanatory dependency claim, not a charge of formal inconsistency or a proof that the authors secretly assume their conclusion. They can defend the additional principles independently. The audit question is whether their text actually does so and whether those principles have consequences beyond reproducing the chosen domain.

## A discriminating comparison

The strongest next philosophical test is to give two explicit object/location models on the SAME causal spacetime, both satisfying independently defensible composition and persistence premises, that generate different location-enlarged realms; or prove a representation theorem showing that named premises uniquely select the bow tie. Without specifying those premises, the parenthetical cannot function as a unique domain-selection theorem.

Current exact result: the point-level and historical-potential constructions have been classified in named geometries. The location-enlarged construction remains underdetermined until its object/support/bridge axioms are specified. This is not an exhaustive refutation of the rationale.
