## 2. Global hyperbolicity and maximality

The polar slices are smooth spacelike Cauchy surfaces. Indeed f∈[1/2,1]
and c has a finite upper bound on each compact T interval. In the
Cartesian polar metric a causal curve satisfies

    |dX/dT|≤√2 c.

If an inextendible causal curve had a finite terminal T, its spatial
coordinates would have a finite limit. Add that endpoint and extend
along the timelike T-coordinate line, a contradiction. The past argument
is identical. Hence every inextendible causal curve meets every T-slice
exactly once. The induced Riemannian metric is at least the Euclidean
metric, so these slices are also intrinsically complete. Completeness
of a slice is not being substituted for causal geodesic completeness.

For the latter use LOY, [arXiv:1605.03893v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.03893v1),
Theorem 1.7 and §6. The source gives future and past causal geodesic
completeness for the limits under the uniform reduced bounds at issue.
The §6 argument uses those bounds and the geodesic equations; it does
not require uniqueness of a scattering map. Thus the parameter-subsequence
limit, whose limiting profile has the same hypotheses and which retains
the bounds, is covered as well. This is an external theorem input, not
a new proof of the full geodesic-completeness argument here.

The initial C¹,¹ coordinate change does not change affine completeness.
An affinely parametrized geodesic of the smooth representative, viewed
in the old C¹,¹ metric, satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation a.e. by
change of variables in the first-variation identity. Its coordinate
velocity is locally Lipschitz; the old Christoffel symbols are continuous,
so that identity is the classical geodesic ODE after integration. The
converse is the same argument. Extensions and their affine parameters
therefore correspond. The source's completeness conclusion applies to
the smooth representative, including geodesics through the axis.

Galloway–Ling–Sbierski,
[arXiv:1704.00353v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.00353v4), 2 November 2017,
Theorem 1, states that a globally hyperbolic, timelike-geodesically
complete spacetime of at least C² regularity is C⁰-inextendible. The
representative now meets all these hypotheses. Consequently there is
no proper same-dimensional Lorentzian extension even at C⁰ metric
regularity.

ϕ=√2φ to match the source normalization. Each completion has its own
