## 2. Invariant meaning of the cut and mass

Work with the given spherical symmetry and the marked central geodesic. Let
N_j be the spherical null hypersurface generated by past-directed radial null
rays from gamma(T_j). In the normalized double-null coordinates it is v=v_j,
where v_j=t(T_j) on u=v=t. Nondegenerate radial bounds extend its past end to
u tending to minus infinity. Set

    P_j = lim_(u->minus infinity) m(u,v_j).

Here m is Hawking mass of the symmetry spheres, characterized by
`1-2m/r = g^(-1)(dr,dr)`. Thus this is a geometric boundary limit in the class
with its specified spherical structure and observer, not a fixed coordinate cut
shared across models. Isometries preserving that structure and launch data
preserve gamma(T_j), N_j and P_j. No assertion about unrestricted bare-metric
isometries is needed.

For the selected global limits, the adopted mass-recovery argument gives

    P_j = (1/4) integral_(-infinity)^(v_j) Phi(v)^2 dv.

This is a global incoming-boundary datum, not energy measured by a local detector
at the center. Its identification with the integral is deferred until after the
compact limit, exactly as in [INCOMING_MASS.md](../INCOMING_MASS.md).

## 3. Basis supports and a positive compact parameter chart

Choose disjoint smooth F_j supported in small intervals about T_j, with
F_j(T_i)=delta_ji. All supports begin after a common A>0. Choose nonzero smooth
G_1 before F_1, G_j between F_(j-1) and F_j for 2<=j<=N, and G_(N+1) after
2T_N+1 and all F_j. Every F/G support is disjoint from the others, with positive
gaps. All are contained in one finite interval [A,B]. These choices are possible
for each fixed finite schedule.

Put

    A_j=integral_(-infinity)^(T_j) F_j^2,
    B_j=integral_(T_j)^(infinity) F_j^2,
    C_j=||G_j||_L2^2>0.

Use x_0=x_(N+1)=0, B_0=A_(N+1)=0. Introduce auxiliary leading-mass parameters
p=(p_1,...,p_N), with p_0=0 and p_(N+1)=1. Define

    beta_j(x,p)^2 = [4(p_j-p_(j-1))
                       -B_(j-1)x_(j-1)^2-A_j x_j^2]/C_j,
                       1<=j<=N+1,
    f_(x,p)=sum_(j=1)^N x_j F_j + sum_(j=1)^(N+1) beta_j G_j.

Always take the positive square root. At x=0,p=alpha all numerators are strictly
positive. For an explicit common chart, let

    delta=min(alpha_1,alpha_2-alpha_1,...,1-alpha_N)>0,
    Cmax=max_(1<=j<=N+1)(B_(j-1)+A_j)>0.

Choose rho>0 with rho<=delta/4 and rho^2 Cmax<=delta. On the closed 2N-dimensional
ball D defined by |x|^2+|p-alpha|^2<=rho^2, each increment p_j-p_(j-1)>=delta/2,
and every square-root numerator is at least delta. Therefore all beta_j are
smooth on a neighborhood of D and bounded away from zero there. This is a
compact smooth profile family in every fixed derivative norm.

Disjoint supports and telescoping give the exact identities

    f_(x,p)(T_j)=x_j,
    (1/4) integral_(-infinity)^(T_j) f_(x,p)^2 = p_j,
    (1/4) integral_R f_(x,p)^2 = 1.                    (A)

For the middle identity, a prefix contains every earlier F in full, the left
part A_j of F_j, and G_1 through G_j. Successive prefix differences contain
B_(j-1)x_(j-1)^2+A_j x_j^2+C_j beta_j^2, divided by four. The final increment
contains B_N x_N^2+C_(N+1) beta_(N+1)^2. This proves all of (A) for general N.

## 4. Actual clocks create only a fourth-order prefix error

Use incoming profiles Phi_(epsilon,x,p)=epsilon f_(x,p). By the common support
and C1 bound, LOY's small-data hypotheses hold with parameter C0 epsilon,
uniformly on D and in the finite-cone vertex u_n. Second and all higher fixed
profile norms are likewise uniformly bounded on D.

For each finite cone, use the central clock normalized at b:

    tau_n(t)=integral_0^t 2lambda_n(s,s) ds,
    t_n(T)=tau_n^(-1)(T).

[CUBIC_TRACE.md](../CUBIC_TRACE.md) supplies, uniformly in n and D,

    |t_n(T_j)-T_j|<=C epsilon^2,
    H_(n,j):=phi_n(t_n(T_j),t_n(T_j))-phi_n(0,0)
             =epsilon x_j+O(epsilon^3).               (B)

Define finite-cone profile functionals

    Q_(n,j)=(1/4) integral_(-infinity)^(t_n(T_j))
                              Phi_(epsilon,x,p)^2.

They are not yet called actual incoming masses of global solutions. From (A),

    |Q_(n,j)-epsilon^2 p_j|
       <= (epsilon^2/4)||f_(x,p)||_infinity^2
                                   |t_n(T_j)-T_j|
       <= C epsilon^4.                               (C)

This checks the model-dependent endpoint. Matching integrals at the coordinate
T_j alone would not establish the desired geometric equality.

For fixed n, the compact-family finite-cone continuity result applies to (x,p),
not just an N-parameter family. Uniform axis/clock convergence and the lower
clock derivative bound give continuous t_n(T_j). Hence both H_n and Q_n are
continuous on D. No differentiable or globally selected scattering map is used.

## 5. Solve all 2N conditions before taking the limit

Write z=(x,p-alpha), so D is the radius-rho ball about zero. Define

    F_n(z)=(epsilon^(-1) H_n,
            epsilon^(-2) Q_n-alpha).

Equations (B) and (C) give

    sup_(n,z in D) |F_n(z)-z|<=C epsilon^2.             (D)

Fix |w|<rho/2. On |z|=rho, the homotopy

    z+s(F_n(z)-z)-(w,0),       0<=s<=1,

has norm at least rho-C epsilon^2-|w|>0 when C epsilon^2<rho/2.
Its Brouwer degree is one. Thus for every n there exists z_n in D with

    H_n(z_n)=epsilon w,
    Q_n(z_n)=epsilon^2 alpha.                          (E)

The total profile quarter-norm is already epsilon^2 by (A). Choose epsilon_*
for the source, clock and degree bounds, then E_*=epsilon_*^2 and c=rho/2.
For any 0<E<E_* set epsilon=sqrt(E) and w=y/epsilon. This yields precisely
the quantifier order of section 1. All constants may depend on the finite
schedule, mass fractions and chosen basis.

    (1/4) integral_(-infinity)^(t_*(T_j)) Phi_*^2
