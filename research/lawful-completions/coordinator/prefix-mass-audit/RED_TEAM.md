## First kill test: zero or saturated budgets

If P_1=0 in the zero-baseline incoming class, positivity of
`P_1=(1/4) integral^v_1 Phi^2` forces Phi=0 before that cut. The central past
of gamma(T_1) then has zero scalar, so a nonzero first reading is impossible.
Similarly the present positive-compensator chart has no open neighborhood at
any zero mass increment. The theorem therefore explicitly requires
0<alpha_1<...<alpha_N<1. It makes no claim about degenerate budgets.
A failed chart at equal adjacent masses does not prove a general impossibility
for those cases; only the zero-first-budget obstruction is derived here.

## Checks that can defeat the displayed proof

1. **Count and location of controls.** N scalar controls x and N auxiliary
   prefix parameters p provide 2N variables. There are N prefix masses and N
   scalar conditions. The last tail enforces total mass algebraically; it
   is not a missing additional root equation.
2. **Partial current pulse.** At T_j, F_j contributes A_j, not its full norm.
   The remainder B_j belongs in the next increment. The uniform beta formula
   includes both pieces; dropping either invalidates the telescoping identity.
3. **Positive chart.** The proof gives an explicit lower bound delta for each
   square-root numerator. The limiting root can lie on the closed chart
   boundary without reaching a zero numerator. This is enough for smooth profile
   dependence and compactness; beta is never differentiated at zero.
4. **Physical cut.** P_j is on the radial null hypersurface ending at the actual
   proper-time event. Finite-cone Q_n uses t_n(T_j), not T_j. Its fourth-order
   unscaled error is essential to the joint scaled map being near identity.
5. **Mass is not locally measured energy.** A Hawking-mass limit at the past end
   of this null hypersurface is a boundary datum. Equality does not establish
   equal local stress, identical radiation profiles, or equal energy measured
   by a central calorimeter.
6. **Continuity.** The inherited finite-cone compactness/uniqueness proof needs
   a compact smooth family, not a particular parameter dimension. Verify that
   claim against its actual estimates; no global scattering uniqueness is
   smuggled into the degree step.
7. **Uniform estimates.** Constants are uniform over D and cone vertex, while
   N, the sample spacing, the fractions and the basis are fixed. There is no
   claim of a uniform refinement limit. The smallness variable is C0 epsilon,
   not epsilon alone; C0 is fixed before epsilon_* is chosen.
8. **Order of limits.** Q_n is a profile functional, not a global mass of a
   finite-cone approximant. Only the selected global mass recovery identifies
   the limits with P_j. A counterexample to the inherited uniform remainders
   would defeat that final step.
9. **Geometry and atlas.** The given spherical structure and central launch
   select the radial cut. The proof does not classify all bare-metric
   isometries. Smooth MGHD status requires the separate accepted family-level
   smoothness bridge and its external hypotheses.
10. **Prior work.** Adding a joint finite-dimensional degree argument is
    elementary once the estimates are available. This note is a concrete
    constrained corollary to review, not evidence of a philosophical
    breakthrough or mathematical priority.

## Philosophical consequence at its actual scope

The earlier total-mass theorem could be challenged because its compensator
arrived after the readouts. The candidate additionally matches how much incoming
boundary mass has accumulated by every sampled proper-time cut. If verified,
that particular objection cannot restore determination of the scalar readings.

The theories can still have different complete spacelike data, incoming wave
shapes and non-mass information. The mass constraints are evaluated on future
cuts; they are not asserted to be a set of records already available at b.
The construction is a comparison of lawful models, not a method for an observer
to choose future outcomes. It establishes no ontic openness, inventory rule,
truth policy or superiority of an ontology.
