# Common-subsequence selection for a finite family

Coordinator deduction, 2026-10-28. No novelty claim.

## An existential matching lemma

Global scattering uniqueness is stronger than needed to construct a finite family of matched solutions. Suppose finitely many admissible incoming profiles agree on v<V. Construct their finite-cone solutions using the **same** sequence u_n tending to minus infinity, the same normalized cone gauge and regular vertex. On each finite pre-tail triangle v<V, their prescribed cone data coincide. Finite-cone characteristic uniqueness therefore makes these solutions identical on that triangle.

Apply the compactness construction to the first family, then successively extract subsequences for the remaining finitely many families, on a common exhaustion by compact quotient sets. The final subsequence converges for every family. Their limits remain identical on every compact subset of v<V, since the approximants there were identical. The common gauge fixes the identification; the result includes equality of the central clock and all readings on observer segments contained in that wedge, subject to the source's reconstruction regularity.

Thus **existence of a finitely matched collection does not require uniqueness of the scattering solution from past-null-infinity data**. It requires the uniform compactness bounds for each of the finitely many profiles, the same finite-cone gauge, and local characteristic uniqueness on the pre-tail triangles. This does not construct a canonical solution map, prove differentiability with respect to profiles, or justify selection for an uncountable open family.

## Application to energy compensation

For each chosen early profile, compare two full profiles with that same early part and different tails, all supported after V. Joint extraction supplies solutions with exactly equal pre-tail geometry. Across finitely many different early profiles, extract all families together. The algebraic quarter-integral charge can be made equal by adjusting disjoint tail amplitudes, but identifying that charge with the incoming mass of these global limits or ADM mass still requires the boundary/joint-limit estimates identified in energy/RESULT.md.

The all-zero early profile gives an exactly flat pre-tail wedge. Any finite comparison using a nonzero early profile still needs an argument that its chosen limiting solution has the claimed differing reading. Radial uniqueness can force some future central variation from nonzero incoming data; placing that variation before a prescribed tail, or obtaining a response of full rank at prescribed times, requires additional estimates. Joint extraction alone does not supply those estimates.

## What remains

Metric regularity at the center, mass-limit identification, and profile differentiability remain independent obligations. For the ambitious local controllability result, uniqueness or another differentiable selection mechanism remains needed. For the weaker existential same-past pair, do not list global scattering uniqueness as an unavoidable prerequisite solely to preserve pre-tail agreement.
