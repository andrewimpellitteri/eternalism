## 2. An early full Cauchy slice belongs to the open realm

Normalize t(b)=0 and choose t₀<min(0,A). Every point of Σ={t=t₀} lies in W,
because t increases along every future causal curve. This is a complete
smooth Cauchy slice of M: smoothness holds in the early exterior/flat
region, and the global finite cone speed proves the Cauchy property.

W is an open past set and is causally convex. A past-directed causal curve
starting in W remains in W. Every ambient inextendible causal curve has
t-range R, so its intersection with W has a nonempty past tail. Each
connected component of that intersection, viewed as a curve in W, is
inextendible there at its endpoints. The tail meets Σ, and acausality
prevents a second meeting. Conversely an intrinsically inextendible curve
in W also meets Σ: it cannot terminate at finite t<t₀, since the only
ambient boundary of W is in t≥0, nor escape to spatial infinity at finite
t by the cone bound. Thus Σ is Cauchy in W as well.

## 4. The philosophical contrast

Subject to the full interpolation audit, the SAME physical class has:

* open variation of finitely many future scalar readings with the entire
  pointed causal past and actual incoming mass fixed;
* no such variation under the stronger whole-W metric-and-scalar matching
  and marked-germ contract above.

The word “present” cannot be substituted for both data bases. The latter
realm includes an entire early spacelike Cauchy surface, including exterior
radiation missed by the observer's causal past. Both conclusions fit
deterministic Einstein–scalar dynamics.

Nor does the second conclusion establish that the determined future events
exist from b. Interpreting B(b) as the entire concrete inventory excludes
strict future events by stipulation while retaining their determination
from admitted exterior data. Interpreting M as concrete and B(b) as a
distinguished causal realm gives a different ontology with the same
physical equations. A truth/grounding policy must still be supplied.

The positive lesson is a constraint on an ontological argument: its
comparison base must match the rival's proposed concrete inventory. The
stronger smoothness and maximality results remove clipping/center
artifacts; they do not turn causal-past variation into variation under
whole-realm matching. The metric, fields, object ontology and truth facts
must not be silently treated as the same comparison object.
