# Open two-reading range cannot be explained by countably many time shifts

2026-10-28. Elementary geometric deduction, conditional on finite interpolation; no novelty claim.

## Structures and exact claim

Consider time-oriented solutions equipped with their central timelike geodesic as a distinguished **unparametrized** curve Gamma. Comparison isometries preserve the fields and Gamma, but do not preserve a chosen launch event. Equivalently, use the spherical solutions with their specified symmetry action and equivariant isometries, whose fixed center is Gamma. These are stronger comparison structures than a bare spacetime without any distinguished center. No claim of unique intrinsic SO(3) action is made.

Suppose the central scalar trace is C1 in proper time and the entire center geodesic is parametrized by a proper-time interval. Fix N>=2 positive durations T_1,...,T_N. For one global centered solution define its possible mark-readout vectors

 F(beta)=(phi(gamma(beta+T_j))-phi(gamma(beta)))_{j=1}^N

on the set of beta for which all those events exist. This is a C1 curve in R^N, hence has N-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. To see this without a global boundedness assumption, cover its open proper-time domain by countably many compact intervals; F is Lipschitz on each, and a Lipschitz image of a real interval has zero N-dimensional measure for N>=2. The same conclusion holds on any subset of allowed marks.

A time-orientation-preserving isometry preserving Gamma restricts to proper-time translation on Gamma. Therefore every marked readout vector obtained from any centered-isometric copy of this solution lies in this one curve image. If internal scalar sign reversal is also allowed, at most the two images F and -F occur, still a null set. A scalar shift cancels in the displayed differences.

Consequently, if a class of such centered solutions realizes an open ball in R^N through these readings, it cannot consist of only countably many centered-isometry classes. A countable union of the null sets just described cannot contain an open ball. It has **uncountably many centered-isometry classes**. Their common incoming mass and common entire pointed past, if supplied by FINITE_CONTROL.md, are retained.

## What this eliminates, and what it does not

This eliminates explanation of an open N-reading range by sliding the launch event through one spacetime, or through countably many spacetimes, in the specified centered comparison category. It strengthens the elementary one-reading MARKING_THEOREM.md, whose interval can indeed come from a single shifted trace.

It does not establish nonisometry as bare spacetimes if an isometry could send the designated symmetry center to a different timelike curve. To conclude that stronger result, prove that the center is intrinsically characterized and preserved by every allowed isometry, or bound all allowable observer markings by a finite-dimensional parameter space and use more readings than its dimension with sufficient regularity. A space-filling continuous curve is not a counterexample here because the trace map is C1; dropping that regularity would defeat this proof.

This is not yet a theorem establishing finite interpolation. It is a consequence available once FINITE_CONTROL.md's analytic gates pass. It also does not make the finite interpolation mathematically novel; the entire package still needs a primary prior-art comparison.
