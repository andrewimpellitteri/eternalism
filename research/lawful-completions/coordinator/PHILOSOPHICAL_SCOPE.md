## The exact mathematical content

Let X be the specified class of marked models. Let P(x) be the isometry class of the entire pointed causal past, including matter, launch tangent and stated observer data. Let E(x) be the verified incoming boundary mass. Let R(x) in R^N be the vector of invariant scalar differences at chosen future proper times.

The proposed finite-control theorem says a fiber

 F={x in X: P(x)=p_0, E(x)=e_0}

has R(F) containing an open ball. In particular R does not factor through (P,E): there is no function f on those past-and-mass data with R=f(P,E) throughout X. The relevant class X and regularity are substantive physical/mathematical premises, not metaphysical possibilities established by definition.

For N>=2, C1 central traces and center-preserving comparisons yield an additional consequence: F contains uncountably many centered-isometry classes, if finite interpolation passes its gates. This follows from NONISOMETRY_LEMMA.md, not merely from counting targets. It rules out one-history time shifts as an explanation of the entire open range in that comparison category. Bare-spacetime nonisometry needs an intrinsic-center argument.

## Finite precision makes the nonprediction claim stronger

Suppose R(F) contains a Euclidean ball B(c,r). Any deterministic predictor using only the common (p_0,e_0) outputs one vector a. Its supremum error over F is at least r, since

 sup_{z in B(c,r)} ||z-a|| = r+||c-a|| >= r.

Thus the failure is not only exact real-number disagreement. Uniform error smaller than r is impossible from the specified information. This is a worst-case theorem over a model class; it is not an observational noise model or a probability distribution over futures.

If an additional datum S is to restore prediction to tolerance eta, choose M attainable target vectors separated by more than 2 eta. Distinct targets cannot share the same S-value if a deterministic decoder from (P,E,S) must be accurate on all of them. Hence S needs at least M distinguishable states on that finite family (at least log_2 M bits if encoded discretely). This is an information-counting requirement, not thermodynamic entropy, quantum information capacity, or a bound on physically measurable data. The missing information may simply be exterior radiation data.

## Why this is not a proof of physical indeterminism

The models can obey deterministic Einstein--scalar evolution from complete Cauchy data. The observer's causal past does not contain all such data; the incoming exterior radiation differs. A uniqueness theorem from complete initial data and a nonfactorization theorem from accessible past data are compatible.

Accordingly, the theorem would concern the inadequacy of a particular information base for future prediction. It would establish neither objective branching at one fully specified global state, nor quantum randomness, nor probabilities for outcomes. A family of lawful models is not automatically a family of metaphysically possible actualizations, and an open range is not a probability measure.

The quantified result also does not make a physical detector or inference algorithm available. The ideal observer reads a modeled scalar functional; its entire-past data are a stipulated comparison object, much richer than any realistic finite measurement record.

## The spatial loophole in an ontological inference

Most importantly, P is a **causal** past. It is not automatically the intrinsic constitution of a growing block's entire existing realm. A view admitting spacelike events can count the incoming pulse outside J^-(b) as already existing. Two models with the same P can therefore differ in existing concrete radiation even before the observer encounters it. Fixing one total mass does not fix that radiation's distribution or phase.

In particular, Correia--Rosenkranz's relativistic bow-tie realm includes spacelike events. A same-causal-past construction alone does not show two identical bow-tie realms with different futures. It therefore does not refute their current-realm supervenience claim, if one is made, and does not force them to appeal to later grounds just to distinguish these models. Exterior existing facts might already distinguish them. Testing their full realm requires a separate isometry/matter-matching condition for that realm, not an extension of the name 'past'.

Even a narrower view on which only P exists can respond by allowing present future-directed truth facts not fixed by P's intrinsic fields, as the earlier author-reply audit explains. Excluding that response needs a defended grounding/supervenience premise; the PDE result does not supply it.

## The honest philosophical fork

A theory that identifies all present facts with the intrinsic modeled causal past plus fixed laws and the common charge cannot also make a unique future reading a function of just those facts across this lawful model class. At least one restriction must change: enrich the present base (for example with spacelike incoming data), restrict the admissible models, allow future truth facts to exceed that intrinsic base, or deny that the future reading already has a unique truth value under the chosen semantics. These are different commitments; mathematics does not select one.

Eternalism can locate the distinguishing future events in a tenseless history. A later-grounded bivalent theory can allow future truths without requiring their concrete grounds to exist now. A gappy theory can withhold current truth for un-settled outcomes. A deterministic growing-block theory can encode exterior data in what already exists. The proposed result by itself excludes none of these broad ontological families.

## A more discriminating next question

Which extra distinguishing datum is both (i) allowed by a proposed ontology and (ii) available as part of the stipulated present physical base without defining it as the future outcome itself?

This question separates three bases that must not be conflated: observer-accessible past data, the ontology's full existing concrete realm, and its total admitted facts including future-directed truths. A theorem about the first cannot silently settle supervenience on the second or third. A useful next theorem would classify prediction from an enlarged concrete realm, or prove a precise obstruction to a proposed causal-potential realm, while retaining the rival's actual definitions.

The frontier is therefore not 'physics proves the future does not exist.' It is a precise classification of which physical bases fix which future observables, and what additional philosophical principle identifies such fixation with existence or truth.
