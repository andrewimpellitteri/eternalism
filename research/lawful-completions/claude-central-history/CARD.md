## The six items

**1. Target.** Technical question: does the past of a central event, with fixed
incoming mass, leave its later scalar readings free? The strongest existing answer
is yes, by domain of dependence, since the readings depend on data outside J⁻(b).

**2. Contribution.** A candidate exact range statement for the coupled system, with
a threshold independent of the number of readings. No philosophical move is
contained in it. It is a stronger bounded theorem on a question already answered in
principle.

**3. Execution.** The mathematics is partly executed. There is no philosophical
bridge, and none is claimed.

**4. Rival response.** The unrestricted event-realist and the Cauchy determinist
retain everything: each model is one whole spacetime, with different exterior data.
The growing-block and presentist rivals also retain everything, because the result
is silent on which events exist. Nothing here forces a revision.

The narrowest position that might have to revise is that coupling and conserved
mass would tie later readings to the past. That is weak and may not be held by
anyone, and PRIOR_WORK.md finds it already undercut in print.

**5. Independent consequence.** Second case: the linear wave equation on Minkowski,
where φ(t,0)=Φ(t) exactly. Every small central history is prescribable, with the
same past. The whole moral is already there. So the ontological content of the
result is **vacuous beyond the linear case**. What coupling adds is mathematical:
the moral survives gravity, mass constraints and the axis. Exposure would be a
reason why the coupled case differs in kind from the linear one. None has been found.
