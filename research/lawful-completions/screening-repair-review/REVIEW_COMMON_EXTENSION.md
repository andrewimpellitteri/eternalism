# Red-team review: common-development repair

**Disposition: pass for vacuum geometric readings on common finite intervals, under the standard open-domain theorem and the independently established screening containment; no whole-future or lifetime conclusion without the added completeness premise.** The coordinator's component refinement closes the connected-initial-data issue for Sbierski. His vacuum common-GHD theorem does not, by itself, compare arbitrary matter fields.

## Counterexample-first boundary checks

The coordinator's sharpness example is valid. In Minkowski spacetime let `b=(0,0)` and `q=(T,0)` with `T>0`; set `M_2=Mink` and `M_1=Mink\\J⁺(q)`. Since `J⁺(q)` is closed, `M_1` is open. It is causally convex: if `x≤z≤y` and `q≤z`, then `q≤y`, so endpoints `x,y∈M_1` cannot have `z∈J⁺(q)`. An open causally convex subset of Minkowski is globally hyperbolic: causal diamonds with endpoints in the subset are the ambient compact diamonds and remain in the subset. The slice `t=0` is Cauchy in both, so both are GHDs of the same flat vacuum data there; `M_1` is nonmaximal.

In Minkowski, `I⁻(I⁺(b))=Mink`, so `W_2=Mink\\J⁺(b)`. In `M_1`, one must also show every nonfuture point still has a common **chronological** successor lying in `M_1`; equality does not follow solely from the set inclusion of futures. Since `M_1` is causally convex, its causal relations between its points agree with Minkowski's. For `x≤b`, choose a sufficiently small timelike successor `y` of `b` before `q`; then `x≪y` by push-up and `y∈M_1`. For spacelike `x=(t,z)`, put `r=|z|>|t|`, `n=z/r`, and `λ=(r+t)/2>0`. Then `z_*=(λ,λ n)` is null-related to `b` and `x`, since `z_*-x=((r-t)/2,-((r-t)/2)n)` is future null. Also `z_*∉J⁺(q)`: if `λ<T`, it is earlier than `q`; if `λ≥T`, its radius `λ` exceeds the radius `λ-T` of `q`'s future cone at that time. The complement of the closed set `J⁺(q)` is open, so choose `y` sufficiently close to `z_*` in `I⁺(z_*)`; then `b≪y`, `x≪y`, and `y∈M_1`. Thus every point outside `J⁺(b)` lies in `I⁻_{M_1}(I⁺_{M_1}(b))`. Finally `J⁺_{M_1}(b)=J⁺_{Mink}(b)∩M_1` and `J⁺(q)⊂J⁺(b)`, so `W_1=W_2=Mink\\J⁺(b)`. The identity preserves the metric and marked germ near `b`.

The central timelike geodesic from `b` reaches `q` after proper time `T` and cannot continue in `M_1`, while it continues indefinitely in `M_2`. This is a concrete counterexample to upgrading finite common-domain agreement to equal observer survival times without extra hypotheses. It is **not** a counterexample to localized `L′` or to a statement whose inputs are maximal globally hyperbolic developments: `M_1` is explicitly nonmaximal. The example is consistent with the coordinator's proposed completeness strengthening, since the central geodesic from `b` in `M_1` is incomplete.

More generally, common-extension embeddings alone do not imply that either embedded spacetime covers the other's entire future. Geodesic uniqueness compares only the intersection of the two parameter domains. Thus the finite-overlap conclusion is the right unconditional strength; no equality of lifetimes, completeness, or whole-future onto-isometry follows.

Likewise, vacuum Sbierski cannot establish equality of independently specified scalar or other matter-field readouts. An isometric embedding preserves the metric and its geometric invariants. It preserves matter only when a field-preserving common-development theorem is available and the embeddings preserve those fields, or when an additional fixed-background uniqueness argument establishes their equality on the relevant common region. The coordinator's hypothesis 4 acknowledges this for coupled systems; the vacuum instance alone must not be promoted to a coupled theorem.

## Vacuum common-extension argument

Assume the geometric transport result in `SCREENING.md` (Theorem 3(a)): `K_i` are matched smooth spacelike acausal partial hypersurfaces and `J⁺(b_i) ⊂ D⁺_{M_i}(K_i)`. Each `K_i` is intrinsically a smooth hypersurface without boundary because it is open in its smooth Cauchy slice; its ambient edge, if any, lies outside its domain of dependence. By the standard domain-of-dependence theorem used in the coordinator's revision, the full two-sided `D_i=D_{M_i}(K_i)` is open globally hyperbolic with `K_i` Cauchy. Let `C_i` be the connected component containing `b_i`, and `K_i^b=C_i∩K_i` its connected Cauchy component. Cauchy splitting gives the component correspondence. The theorem's `J⁺(b_i)⊂D⁺(K_i)` plus causal convexity of the full domain places the entire marked future in `C_i`.

The marked-path argument also proves the needed component match: choose `p∈K_1` on a timelike curve to `b_1`; the curve segment `α` and its image `h(α)` lie in the respective full domains by the domain lemma and marked-past transport. Thus `h(p)` lies in the component `C_2` containing `b_2`. Since `h:K_1→K_2` is a diffeomorphism, it maps the whole connected component `K_1^b` onto `K_2^b`. Restrict to `C_i`, which are GHDs of the same data on the connected smooth Riemannian manifold `K_1^b≅K_2^b`. Sbierski Definition 2.1 explicitly requires the initial Riemannian manifold to be connected; it imposes no completeness condition. Theorem 2.7 then gives a common vacuum GHD `N` and time-orientation-preserving isometric embeddings `F_i:C_i→N` compatible with the initial-data embeddings. Neither these input components nor the ambient `M_i` need be maximal.

The first-jet step can be made precise. Choose `p∈K_1` with `p≪b_1`, and a timelike curve `α` from `p` to `b_1`. At `p`, initial-data compatibility gives `F_1(p)=F_2(h(p))`. Along `T_pK_1`, the differentials agree by differentiating this equality of the restricted embeddings. Since each `F_i` is a time-orientation-preserving isometry, the future unit normal is mapped to the same future unit normal of the common embedded initial hypersurface. Their full differentials therefore agree at `p`.

Let `O` be the connected component containing `p` of the open set `C_1∩W_1∩h^{-1}(C_2)`. The point `p` is interior to both domains and to `W_1`, since `K_i⊂D_i` and `p∈K_1⊂W_1`. Every interior point of `α` is in `D⁺(K_1)`, and marked-past transport plus the same domain lemma puts `h(α)` in `D⁺(K_2)`; these lie in the respective open components. The whole half-open path therefore lies in `O` by connectedness. The maps `F_1|O` and `F_2∘h|O` are isometric immersions into `N`; Sbierski Lemma 3.1 says two such immersions from a connected source agreeing in value and differential at one point agree on the source. Hence they agree along `α` before its endpoint.

Taking limits gives `F_1(b_1)=F_2(b_2)` provided both marked endpoints belong to `N_i` and `h(α(t))→b_2` in the stipulated marked homeomorphic extension. This supplies the shared launch point. The transition isometry between the open embedding images is defined near that point, so it matches the observer germ/tangent when the tangent is transported by this transition. An independently selected tangent is not automatically matched.

For any matched unit timelike launch tangent, the embedded observer curves are geodesics in the same smooth `N` with the same initial position and velocity. Geodesic initial-value uniqueness makes them identical for every parameter value in the common domain. Because the embeddings preserve proper time, every metric/geometric reading at each finite `T` for which both original curves are defined agrees.

## Exact gates / limits

- **Domain theorem:** The component objection is closed by the coordinator's explicit domain/component argument: restrict to `C_i` and the connected Cauchy component `K_i^b`; `h(α)` identifies the marked component; every point of `J⁺(b_i)` remains in it. This uses the standard result that the full domain of an acausal topological hypersurface without boundary is open GH with that hypersurface Cauchy. `K_1` is open in a smooth Cauchy hypersurface; `K_2` is its smooth acausal topological-hypersurface image under `h`, without intrinsic boundary. Apply the domain theorem to each; do not require `K_2` to lie in a global Cauchy slice. The domain uses the open convention, not a closed edge disk. No extra completeness assumption is needed for Sbierski's connected initial manifold.
- **Endpoint inclusion:** The marked points lie in the open full domains because `b_i∈J⁺(b_i)⊂D⁺(K_i)⊂D(K_i)`; the component selection puts them in `C_i`. The half-open timelike path lies in the comparison overlap, and the assumed homeomorphic mark extension supplies the endpoint limit.
- **Connected comparison set:** No residual component gap remains: `O` is the connected component containing `p`, and the path itself lies in that component. The full first jet agrees at `p` by initial-data compatibility and common future normal; Lemma 3.1 then applies.
- **Fields:** Sbierski Theorem 2.7 and Lemma 3.1 are vacuum metric results. A coupled theory needs its own common-GHD extension theorem for the same regularity and data, including field compatibility. A fixed-background test-field theorem is a separate result and cannot be silently attached to the vacuum embedding.
- **No strengthening:** The embeddings need not identify `J⁺(b_1)` onto `J⁺(b_2)` and the proof says nothing at times when only one observer curve exists. It establishes neither equal lifetimes nor future completeness, maximality, topology, nor whole-future equivalence.

**Review conclusion:** Accept as a vacuum-geometric finite-reading lemma under the stated standard open-domain theorem and screened containment. The component/connected-data issue is closed by the added argument. For scalar/matter readings, accept only after a field-preserving common-development result or a separate uniqueness proof on the shared domain is cited. Do not retain an unqualified phrase “every corresponding scalar reading” on the strength of the vacuum theorem.

## Optional completeness strengthening

The proposed stronger corollary is valid with the stated, separate completeness premise. In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, every `q∈J⁺(b)` is joined to `b` by a future causal geodesic (Avez–Seifert; Minguzzi, *Lorentzian causality theory*, Theorem 4.123 states the result and gives a proof); every `q∈I⁺(b)` is joined by a timelike geodesic. Transfer the initial tangent using the local transition isometry at the marks established above. If all future causal geodesics from both marks are complete, the two curves exist to the endpoint parameter and common-development geodesic uniqueness identifies their images. This proves inclusion of the image of each causal future in the other; reverse the roles to obtain equality. Since the common embedding maps are open isometric embeddings, their transition yields an onto isometry between the causal-future regions. Under only timelike-geodesic completeness from both marks, the same argument gives the chronological futures. These hypotheses concern all relevant geodesics from the marks and are not consequences of equality of realms or of the finite-reading argument. All domain/component and field-category caveats above remain unchanged; the corollary is vacuum-geometric unless a coupled common-development theorem is supplied.
