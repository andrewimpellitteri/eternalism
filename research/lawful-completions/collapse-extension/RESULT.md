# Full-past completion audit for the scalar-collapse cone

**Disposition: CONDITIONAL for a local extension; the requested full-past
extension is an unresolved gate.** I found no primary theorem in the bounded
source check that promotes the 1991 future-of-a-cone development to a maximal
globally hyperbolic Einstein–massless-scalar development whose *entire*
`I^-(b)` is exactly flat. Thus the cone solution supports a local flat-observer
collapse candidate, but not yet the stronger matched-whole-past claim.

## Source facts

Christodoulou’s 1991 paper specifies the spherical Einstein–massless,
minimally coupled scalar equations and evolves data on the central outgoing
cone `C⁺₀`. On printed p356 (extracted text, around line 407), it explicitly
sets the spacetime under discussion to the future of the initial cone. Its
Proposition 4.1, pp356–358, establishes the relevant flat filled-cone region
from vanishing scalar derivative on the cone interior. Theorem 5.1,
pp363–364, gives the annular concentration criterion and future trapped/singular
conclusions. Those statements concern this cone-generated future development.
They do not prescribe or construct the portion of a complete Cauchy development
outside the cone’s domain, in particular the remote past of the marked event.
The supplied paper audit and extracted text are project-provided; I did not
re-audit the theorem proof here.

Christodoulou’s 1986 *The problem of a self-gravitating scalar field* is a
closely relevant primary lead. Its source abstract says the initial surface is
a future light cone at the centre and establishes local existence in retarded
time and global uniqueness of classical solutions. That scope does not state
backward continuation to complete Cauchy data or exact preservation of an
entire pointed causal past. The 1987 collapse paper cited by the coordinator
studies late-time behavior in the future of its characteristic data; the
checked abstract likewise gives no such past-completion assertion.

A precise modern local comparison is Angelopoulos–Kehle–Unger (2025),
Proposition 2.5: smooth bounded bifurcate characteristic data for the
spherically symmetric Einstein–Maxwell–scalar system extend uniquely to a
thin slab. This is a local characteristic existence result, with a different
matter model (charge is included); it neither supplies the required global
asymptotically flat Cauchy completion nor proves whole-`I^-(b)` flatness. It
illustrates what local extension can establish, not a substitute theorem for
this task.

## Conditional lemma and exact missing step

The following is a sufficient *conditional* route, not a theorem extracted
from the cone-collapse papers:

> **Cauchy-footprint flatness lemma (conditional).** Let smooth complete
> Einstein–massless-scalar Cauchy data `(Σ,h,K,φ,Π)` have a maximal globally
> hyperbolic development `M`. Suppose there is an open region `Ω ⊂ Σ` on which
> the data are exactly the induced Minkowski data with constant scalar, and,
> literally,
> `I⁻_M(b) ∪ {b} ⊂ D_M(Ω)`, where `D_M(Ω)` is the domain of dependence of
> `Ω` in `M`. Require the marked observer germ at `b` to lie in the resulting
> flat domain as well. Uniqueness for the Einstein–massless-scalar Cauchy
> problem then makes the metric, scalar, and marked germ on `I⁻_M(b) ∪ {b}`
> agree with the corresponding Minkowski solution.

The inclusion is a separate strong hypothesis. The weaker statement
`Σ ∩ J⁻_M(b) ⊂ Ω` does **not** by itself imply it: causal curves and the
development's domain of dependence must be checked in the actual maximal
development, including the global boundary behavior of the footprint.

The substantive, presently unsupported assertion is the hypothesis that
links the *particular cone data used by Theorem 5.1* to such complete smooth
Cauchy data while (i) retaining the same solution on the cone-collapse region,
(ii) satisfying the Einstein constraints and regular-centre/asymptotic
conditions, and (iii) ensuring the full past footprint of `b` stays in the
flat-data domain in the resulting maximal development. Local characteristic
well-posedness near a cone corner does not prove this; nor does agreement on a
finite diamond. The geometry/constraint lane’s independent Cauchy-data and
barrier work may provide ingredients, but no result was assumed here.

There is a further distinction: a local backward extension around `b` is only
a neighborhood result. Extending the *cone solution* backward/exterior to a
maximal development, and proving no nonflat events enter `I^-(b)` at arbitrarily
large distance or in the remote past, is a global matching assertion. Neither
the 1986 abstract nor the 1991 cone theorem verifies it. If a characteristic
extension theorem is used instead of complete Cauchy data, it must explicitly
control the entire past footprint and be compatible with the coupled
constraints; a finite null slab is insufficient.

**Falsifier for this gate:** exhibit a smooth complete constraint-satisfying
Cauchy completion of the specified 1991 cone data, with a proof by uniqueness
and causal-domain containment that `I^-(b)` is Minkowski with constant scalar
in the MGHD. Conversely, a constraint or global-causality obstruction to every
such completion would kill this route. No such proof or obstruction was found
in the bounded audit.

## Scope and limits

- This audit establishes a source-scope gap, not nonexistence of a completion.
- The local slab result is smooth and for Einstein–Maxwell–scalar; it is not
  silently transferred to the neutral massless-scalar equations.
- No BV theorem was used to infer a smooth result, and no smooth theorem was
  inferred from BV existence.
- The coordinator’s full-past quantifier is retained: exact agreement of
  metric, scalar and the needed marked observer germ on all of `I^-(b)` is the
  target. Matching a finite diamond or a null boundary alone is inadequate.
- No claim about novelty, a constructed Cauchy completion, or a full theorem
  is made.
