\begin{abstract}
Physical theories constrain temporal metaphysics, but the target of a constraint
must be specified. Presentism, eternalism, and accounts of becoming do not, by
their labels alone, determine experimental probabilities. This draft argues
for a contrast-sensitive burden of proof: an inference from physics to a
temporal ontology should identify an admissible physical realization, the
bridge premises connecting its structures to existence or passage, and the
alternative that the evidence or argument discriminates against. We distinguish
physical incompatibility, empirical discrimination, and representation
equivalence. Special-relativistic simultaneity and relational quantum clocks
illustrate why these inferential forms cannot substitute for one another. In the relativistic case, a definability theorem classifies becoming relations
under explicit structural premises, while preferred-frame effects can be tested
in specified coupled models. In the clock case, interacting examples show why
equivalence claims must fix the constraint, physical inner product and
observables. An elementary finite-history example makes record preservation
precise while exposing its limits; causal-set growth supplies a related
covariance problem, without an embedded-record model.

The position is neither that physics is irrelevant to
metaphysics nor that empirical equivalence makes ontologies identical.
The proposed framework and examples are expository and argumentative; no
mathematical or philosophical priority, new physical prediction, or preferred
temporal ontology is established here.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question is contrastive}

What would it mean for physics to favor eternalism over presentism, or local
becoming over a moving spotlight? At least three answers are possible. A view
might contradict a well-supported physical structure. A realized model might
predict observations more successfully than a rival. Or the physical theory
might provide an explanation whose ontological commitments are better
understood in one way than another. These are serious routes to philosophical
justification, but they have different premises and different conclusions.

The danger is not merely an insufficient number of citations. It is an
inference in which a result answering one question is taken to answer another:
the absence of a distinguished simultaneity relation becomes the nonexistence
of passage; the representation of an entire history becomes determinism; or
an equivalence of relational dynamics becomes the elimination of fundamental
time. A dense catalogue of physical theories cannot repair that inferential
gap without identifying the premises that cross it.

Our methodological starting point is physics-constrained but non-reductive.
Empirical adequacy matters greatly, while meaningful claims about grounding,
existence, and explanation are not defined away because they lack a direct
meter reading. This starting point is itself a philosophical commitment,
not a theorem of physics. The breadth of the field is usefully represented by
\citet{Callender2011}. The contrast between the programs of
\citet{Callender2017} and \citet{Maudlin2007} motivates our caution about
treating one account of passage as the neutral interpretation of scientific
practice; that methodological inference is ours.

Our thesis is therefore a burden-of-proof claim:
\begin{quote}
An argument that physics favors a temporal ontology must identify the
realization and bridge premises under which the physical result bears on that
ontology, and specify the contrast that the result actually supports.
\end{quote}
This does not require every philosophical argument to predict a new
measurement. It requires an argument to say whether its force is empirical,
logical, explanatory, or a combination, and to preserve that scope at its
conclusion.

\section{What is being compared?}

\subsection{Independent questions}

For this draft, presentism restricts unrestricted concrete existence to what
is present; eternalism admits past and future entities as well as present
ones. Growing-block views retain the past and present while rejecting future
entities; moving-spotlight accounts combine a complete temporal ontology with
objectively distinguished presentness. These are schematic families, not
complete theories. Direct defenses differ substantially: compare
\citet{Bourne2006}, \citet{CorreiaRosenkranz2018}, and \citet{Cameron2015}.
Their detailed replies have not yet been fully audited for this manuscript.

Several further questions must remain separate. Are tensed facts irreducible?
Does something objectively become present, existent, or definite? Do laws
and complete appropriate data select one history? How do objects persist?
Is time fundamental? Which asymmetries need explanation? An eternalist can
accept indeterministic laws. A four-dimensional spacetime does not by itself
make ordinary objects temporal worms. Knowing that an event is now is not
automatically knowing an extra objective spotlight property.

These distinctions are not concessions invented to protect a preferred
position. McTaggart's argument already differentiates temporal order,
change, and A-determinations \citep{McTaggart1908}; its contested premises
cannot be replaced by the phrase ``time is an illusion.'' Likewise,
\citet{Skow2015} distinguishes robust from less demanding senses of passage.
Clock-rate comparisons constrain metric relations. Connecting those relations
to one of these senses of passage requires a further bridge argument.

\subsection{Realizations, not bare labels}

Represent a comparison target schematically as
\[
 M=(P,O,B,A,S;\Obs),
\]
where $P$ specifies a physical model and dynamics, $O$ an ontological
interpretation, $B$ the bridge premises connecting physical structure to
ontological claims, $A$ auxiliary and boundary assumptions, $S$ semantic
conventions, and $\Obs$ the allowed preparations, observations, and
interventions. The tuple is bookkeeping rather than a new physical formalism;
its components need not be independent.

An ontology may have several proposed realizations. Write $\Real(O)$ for the
specified admissible class, where admissibility must be defended independently
of rescuing a view after each objection. An empirical likelihood is supplied
by a realized package and an experimental protocol, not simply by its label.
This observation does not make ontological hypotheses impossible to assess:
one may compare independently justified realization classes or marginalize
over auxiliaries with explicit priors. It blocks only the unannounced move
from a result for one realization to a verdict on every realization.

\paragraph{Constraining admissibility without settling the dispute by fiat.}
Before inspecting a proposed discriminator, register at least four conditions:
(i) mathematical consistency and empirical adequacy in the declared regime;
(ii) fidelity to the ontology's explicitly stated constitutive commitments,
rather than merely retaining its name; (iii) a specified dynamics, probability
model and operational interface, including proposed auxiliaries; and (iv) an
independent motivation for additional structure, assessed against the same
standards used for rivals. The first conditions constrain which models enter
the comparison. Simplicity, explanatory contribution and costs of primitive
structure then help rank the survivors; they are not covert empirical vetoes.

The proponent bears the burden of describing a realization and defending its
bridges; the exclusion advocate bears the stronger burden of defending the
scope of the excluded class. Neither party may change those standards silently
after a counterexample. Where reasonable standards disagree, report conditional
verdicts under both rather than call one class uniquely admissible. This does
not mechanically settle abduction, but it makes auxiliary rescue and asymmetric
demands on rivals inspectable.
The constitutive commitments and permitted auxiliary changes should be recorded
before evaluating the discriminator. Symmetric treatment alone does not show
that an admissibility criterion is independent of the intended verdict. A
comparison should therefore state which conclusions change under reasonable
alternative admissibility profiles. Defending those profiles remains a task
for the stronger philosophical argument, rather than a result of this checklist.

\paragraph{A worked case: Brading's law-constituted present.}
Brading accepts special relativity as a complete framework for organizing
spatiotemporal knowledge in its regime, while rejecting the inference that this
epistemic framework must ground ontological unity
\citep[][Sec.~5, pp.~17--18]{Brading2015}.

She proposes that what exists presently is fixed by the extent
of a dynamical system's law-constituted unity rather than by a simultaneity
hyperplane. To avoid the block, that extent must be smaller than the
universe's full history; it need not be smaller than the system's own complete
history. The extent is system-relative and need not be transitive, so the view
does not imply that everything outside a chosen system's present is unreal.
Brading herself leaves open whether there are genuine dynamical subsystems
smaller than all of spacetime, and she supplies no common solution, access
protocol or probabilities against which a rival could be scored
\citep[][Sec.~5, p.~18]{Brading2015}.

A block-compatible rival in this case is a composite we assemble,
not a position any single cited author develops against Brading. It grants
law-based dynamical unity but denies that a system's law-sustaining region
exhausts that system's existence. Explanatory arguments about the direction of
explanation between spacetime structure and dynamics bear on it:
\citet{BalashovJanssen2003} argue that spacetime structure unifies the Lorentz
invariance of matter laws, against a neo-Lorentzian rival (Secs.~9--11 of the
longer PhilSci archive preprint, the version read), and \citet{BrownPooley2006}
defend the reverse direction (Secs.~3--5 of arXiv:physics/0403088v1). Neither
addresses Brading's ontology directly. If both realizations are granted a
common, consistent and empirically adequate model, matched likelihoods give no
empirical preference, and this draft has established no decisive independent argument for either
existence bridge. This is an illustrative conditional comparison, rather than
a demonstrated pair of admissible physical realizations. The matched
likelihoods are stipulated here. Brading's argument from law-based unity to
endurance and thence toward presentism offers an explanatory route
\citep[][Sec.~5]{Brading2013}; its final step remains contestable because
endurance itself is compatible with eternalism. A further objection remains: if each local
present may include its system's entire history, the restriction of its extent
does not by itself show that the union of all system-presents excludes any
past or future entities. That ontological inference needs a separate account
of existence and of the relations between overlapping systems.

\section{Three different inferential burdens}

\subsection{Exclusion}

A physical incompatibility argument has a form such as
\[
 O\land B\land A\ \Longrightarrow\ F,
 \qquad P\ \Longrightarrow\ \neg F.
\]
It follows that $P$ excludes the conjunction $O\land B\land A$.
Concluding $\neg O$ requires independent support for the indispensable bridge
and auxiliary premises, or an argument that every admissible realization
of $O$ encounters the conflict. Otherwise the result identifies a conditional
cost: retain the ontology only by revising the disputed structure or bridge.

This is not the claim that a bare ontology can always be rescued. A bridge
may be constitutive of a precisely defined view, or its removal may destroy
the explanation that made the view attractive. That stronger argument should
be made rather than hidden in a generic ontology label.

\subsection{Empirical discrimination}

Empirical discrimination requires different probability assignments to a
common experimental question. At fixed realized packages, if
\[
 p(D\mid M_1,e)=p(D\mid M_2,e)
\]
for every outcome event $D$ and protocol $e\in\Obs$, those observations cannot
discriminate the packages by their likelihoods. This statement is restricted
to the declared protocol class, not all conceivable future observations.

For composite hypotheses, pointwise equivalence at matched parameter values
is insufficient for equal Bayesian evidence. The marginalized likelihoods
must also agree. For example,
\[
 p(D\mid O_i,e)=\int p(D\mid \theta_i,O_i,e)
                   \,\pi_i(d\theta_i\mid O_i,e)
\]
can differ because the realization or parameter priors differ. Equal
prior-predictive distributions give a Bayes factor of one wherever the common
likelihood is nonzero; equal predictions at selected parameter settings need
not. Prior preference remains possible, but must not be redescribed as an
empirical likelihood advantage.

\subsection{Representation equivalence}

A structure- or probability-preserving map can establish equivalence for its
specified domain. It does not automatically establish identical metaphysical
interpretations, gauge redundancy, or equal explanatory value. Nor does a
mathematically invertible reparameterization necessarily preserve which
operations an observer can perform. An equivalence argument must state the
common state space, physical inner product when relevant, observable map, and
access restrictions. A failure of such an equivalence can be physically
important without immediately deciding an ontology.

\section{Case I: what a relativistic present would require}

The elementary simultaneity result is exact within its standard domain. A
boost along one spatial axis gives
\[
 t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2),\qquad
 \gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2},\qquad |v|<c.
\]
An event $(0,L)$ with $L\ne0$ is simultaneous with the origin in one frame but
has $t'=-\gamma vL/c^2\ne0$ for a nonzero boost. Thus that simultaneity
hyperplane is not invariant under all boosts fixing the origin. This is
standard kinematics, not a new result or a proof that nothing changes.

To obtain an ontological conclusion one must say why presentness must be that
kind of hyperplane and respect the relevant symmetries. Putnam's argument
invokes an observer-neutral reality-transfer principle; its broader closure
argument includes further assumptions about observers \citep{Putnam1967}.

The definability literature shows how much the structural premises carry.
Clifton and Hogarth classify absolute, worldline-independent becoming relations
in time-oriented Minkowski spacetime. With their convention that chronological
and causal connectibility include identity, their Theorem 1 leaves only past
chronological connectibility, past causal connectibility and the universal
relation, provided the becoming relation is implicitly definable from the
time-oriented metric, transitive, and includes chronological connectibility
\citep[][pp.~360--365]{CliftonHogarth1995}. The proof uses invariance under the
structure's automorphisms. It excludes these premises together with any demand
for a relation outside those three alternatives; it does not exclude the
premises themselves. Requiring invariance under the larger automorphism group
of the unoriented structure leaves only the universal candidate
\citep[][Cor.~1(A), p.~367]{CliftonHogarth1995}. Conversely, adding distinguished
worldlines weakens the invariance requirement: causal pasts of the associated
simultaneity planes, or suitably covering past-null-cone surfaces, yield
further examples \citep[][pp.~376--377]{CliftonHogarth1995}. These are changes
of available structure, not a complete independence analysis. Whether an
ontology must accept the joint premises, and whether a surviving relation
captures genuine becoming, remain bridge questions.

Stein's event-relative approach supplies a different account of definite
past structure \citep{Stein1991}. The present draft does not reproduce Stein's
characterization theorem: the isolated OCR implication is false as stated
without additional contextual constraints. The surrounding argument includes
causal-past inclusion and unsettled states; comparison with the original
printed theorem remains unresolved. No error in the printed paper is inferred.

One response adds a preferred foliation. That is a substantive extra structure,
not a free conclusion of SR. But observability also requires care. Appending
a structure without changing old dynamics, probability assignments, or
accessible operations need not change the old predictions. This conditional
observation does not establish that any particular preferred-foliation theory
is admissible or empirically equivalent; a coupling can make the structure
observable. Nor does mere covariance of a notation establish absence of a
physical preferred structure.

The difference is concrete. Einstein-aether theory couples a dynamical unit
timelike vector to gravity and can produce preferred-frame PPN effects
\citep{FosterJacobson2006}. Assuming a preferred rest frame aligned with the
CMB, Lucchesi et al.'s analysis of about thirty years of LAGEOS laser ranging
gives $\alpha_1=(+1\pm2)\times10^{-5}$ with SATAN and
$(+2\pm3)\times10^{-5}$ with GEODYN II, and an estimated systematic error
of about $8\times10^{-6}$ (arXiv:2501.10588v2, Eq.~12). Both estimates are
compatible with zero \citep{LucchesiEtAl2026}. This constrains the specified
signal, not every coupled preferred-frame theory: nonzero aether couplings can
also give vanishing preferred-frame PPN parameters
\citep[][Eqs.~10--12]{FosterJacobson2006}. A foliation stipulated to leave the
underlying relativistic model's dynamics, observables and access rules unchanged
is invisible to those tests by construction. Neither comparison selects a
temporal ontology without further bridge premises.

Another response disputes that simultaneity must provide the unity of what
exists. Brading develops such a presentist program
\citep{Brading2013,Brading2015}, worked through in Section~2: it relocates the
contested premise from simultaneity to the extent of law-constituted unity, and
it supplies no quantitative discriminator. That is enough to identify a premise
needing argument, not enough to vindicate the whole program.

There are real philosophical costs on both sides. A preferred structure may
be explanatorily idle; a local becoming relation may do no more than restate
causal order; and a block account owes an account of the phenomena motivating
passage. The point is to locate these costs accurately. A geometrical
constraint on one global-present realization is not a completed comparison
of all these packages.

The strongest schematic foliation reply is not that the structure must be
detectable. It is that a primitive foliation constitutes which entities exist
together, even if it changes no measured quantity. A critic can question its
motivation or its explanatory return; merely noting observational redundancy
does not answer a constitutive claim. The same standard applies to a local
becoming bridge: it could be defended as specifying definiteness rather than
causing a new detector response, but must explain what motivates that role
instead of simply assigning a temporal name to the causal past. These are
deliberately schematic rival arguments, not fully audited reconstructions of
a particular defender. The comparative explanatory judgment remains open.

\section{Case II: relational dynamics without an ontological shortcut}

Relational clock constructions expose a second inference risk. A constrained
global description can admit conditional evolution relative to a chosen
clock. The relational-trinity work relates Dirac-observable, conditional
Schrodinger, and reduced Heisenberg descriptions under specified hypotheses
\citep{HoehnSmithLock2019}. Its noninteracting setup and clock conditions
matter. Interaction treatments already exist, including the kernel equation
studied by \citet{SmithAhmadi2019}; ``add interactions'' is not a generic
unoccupied research gap.

Two implications should be resisted. First, a stationary constraint equation
does not by itself mean that subsystems cannot change: correlations can support
conditional dynamics. Second, successful conditional dynamics does not by
itself establish that correlations exhaust temporal reality. A reductionist
argument needs a defensible sufficiency principle identifying all relevant
temporal roles and showing that the construction realizes them. A primitive
passage advocate cannot dismiss the construction, but may dispute that
exhaustiveness premise.

The inverse mistake is equally important. An interaction-dependent or
nonunitary conditioned evolution does not establish objective passage or
an open future. One must specify the physical state space, clock instrument,
normalization, accessible measurements, and what the proposed ontology would
predict differently. A transformation that changes access restrictions
cannot be treated as experimentally neutral merely because it is invertible
on an enlarged mathematical space.

Where does a published equivalence stop? The trinity results assume no
clock--system interaction \citep{HoehnSmithLock2019}; the periodic extension
does too, noting that interactions typically spoil periodic dynamics unless
fine-tuned \citep[][Sec.~II.2]{ChataignierEtAl2026}. Interacting models require
new checks of the reduction maps, inner products and observables. A normalized
conditional trajectory alone does not establish a unitary map on a common
initial-state space. Cafasso et al.'s gravitational-like commuting construction
has constant conditional normalization
\citep[][Sec.~IV and App.~B]{CafassoEtAl2024}, while finite noncommuting
examples can also preserve norms
\citep[][Sec.~4.3]{SmithAhmadi2019}
\citep[][Sec.~II]{BoetteRossignoli2018}.
The later finite-group example supplies another norm-preserving construction
\citep[][App.~B.2, Ex.~32]{LudescherEtAl2026}.
Hausmann et al.'s discrete model uses prescribed unitary steps of the system
and measurement ancilla with a bandlimited clock
\citep[][Sec.~5]{HausmannEtAl2025}. These are specific constructions; they do
not establish unitarity for arbitrary Hamiltonian interactions or equivalence
between the interacting formulations.

Our three-state-clock/qubit calculation illustrates these checks without
claiming a new result. We choose a total Hamiltonian
$H=H_C\otimes 1+1\otimes H_S+gK\otimes\sigma_x$ and a physical sector
given by one eigenspace of $H$. If $G(X)=\sum_\lambda P_\lambda X P_\lambda$,
then $\langle G(X)\rangle=\langle X\rangle$ in that sector. This elementary
pinching identity also preserves the corresponding conditional ratio whenever
its denominator is nonzero; it is not a construction of the interacting
trinity's relational observables. For the tested noncommuting $K$ and
couplings, clock-reading probabilities become nonuniform and the specified
reduction maps are nonisometric. The chosen commuting control preserves their
isometry. At the sampled generic couplings, each selected energy sector is
one dimensional, so it supplies no arbitrary initial qubit state and no
state-space dynamical map on which to assess memory. A constraint imposed at a
fixed energy can also lose its solutions under perturbation. Structured
clock-step constraints can instead retain a larger sector; this follows from
their construction, not from group invariance alone. Comparing different
constraints therefore requires showing whether they describe the same physical
model and access rules. Constraint choice is part of specifying the package,
not automatically a change of representation.

\section{A finite record example and its exact limits}

The following elementary construction makes one possible equivalence burden
explicit. It is an expository application of ordinary probability, not a
novel theorem about becoming.

\begin{proposition}[Probability preservation under matched kernels]
Let $(X,\mu)$ and $(Y,\nu)$ be probability spaces, and let $F:X\to Y$ be
measurable with $F_*\mu=\nu$. The outcome kernels share one measurable outcome
space. For each allowed complete observation protocol $e$, suppose
$K_X^e(x,D)=K_Y^e(F(x),D)$ for $\mu$-almost every $x$, for every outcome
event $D$. Then the two packages assign the same probabilities to those
protocols.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any declared $e,D$, the probability in $X$ is
\[
 \int_X K_X^e(x,D)\,d\mu(x)
 =\int_X K_Y^e(F(x),D)\,d\mu(x)
 =\int_Y K_Y^e(y,D)\,d\nu(y),
\]
which is the probability in $Y$. The result assumes rather than derives
the measure and kernel preservation.
\end{proof}

For a finite horizon $T$ and finite state space $S$, let
\[
 \mu(x_0,\ldots,x_T)=
 \pi(x_0)\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}P_t(x_{t+1}\mid x_t).
\]
One implementation samples an initial state and then successive transitions;
another samples an entire path with this law and releases its entries in
order. Any common protocol restricted to the revealed prefixes, whose auxiliary
randomness has the same conditional law given those prefixes and no access to
the unrevealed path, yields the same record distribution.
Adaptive choices of which revealed data to read can be included in the complete
kernel. Operations modifying future transitions are not included unless their
conditional dynamics are separately matched.

\paragraph{An exact numerical illustration.}
Take $S=\{0,1\}$, $T=2$, a fair initial state, and at each step a probability
$3/4$ of staying in the same state. The path $000$ has probability
$(1/2)(3/4)(3/4)=9/32$; $010$ has probability $1/32$.
Both implementations assign these same probabilities and the same prefix
marginals. A companion standard-library check enumerates all paths using
exact fractions. It verifies this example, not the general measure-theoretic
argument or a theory of time.

Several limits are decisive. A sequential generator is not thereby the growing
block theory; a sampled path is not thereby eternalism. The observer-access
condition, the measure, and the identification of records do the operational
work. If a different package gives access to unrevealed entries, or responds
differently to interventions, the stated equivalence need not apply. Such
extra access is a changed model assumption, not something entailed by one
ontology label.

Observational agreement also falls short of interventional agreement. Consider
a fair latent bit $Z$. In model A set $X=Z$ and $Y=X$; in model B set $X=Z$
and $Y=Z$. Both observational distributions have $X=Y$ with a fair marginal.
Here $Z$ is exogenous in both models. Under an intervention replacing only
the assignment for $X$ by $X:=0$, model A
has $Y=0$, whereas model B retains a fair $Y$. This familiar type of causal
example shows why accessible interventions must be specified. It does not
attribute either causal model to a temporal ontology.

The philosophical payoff is limited but useful: records cannot favor one
implementation when measure and complete allowed response kernels are matched.
To turn that into an argument about becoming requires a separately defended
interpretation. Calling the implementations two ontologies would skip exactly
the premise the paper asks us to inspect.

Classical causal-set growth provides a related covariance example. Its
one-element-at-a-time dynamics imposes discrete general covariance
\citep{RideoutSorkin1999}. At a fixed finite stage, conditional on the
unlabelled poset, its compatible generation orders are equiprobable
\citep[][Secs.~1, 4]{BrightwellLuczak2011}. This order-invariance concerns
labelling information beyond causal precedence. Interpreting the remaining
labelling distinctions as gauge limits the physical questions; identifying
that restriction with an observer's record access is a further premise.

Completion raises a separate issue. On the full labelled-history space, the
finite-cylinder algebra contains no nontrivial covariant event
\citep[][Sec.~2]{SuryaZalel2020}. This does not prohibit covariant facts with
finite positive witnesses or establish that a mathematically completed history
is an already existent future. Arageorgis criticizes a growing-block reading's
dependence on completion \citep[][Sec.~4, p.~52]{Arageorgis2016}; Sorkin
acknowledges the formal tension while defending becoming
\citep[][Secs.~5, 7]{Sorkin2007}, and W{\"u}thrich and Callender explore a
qualified growth response \citep[][Sec.~4]{WuthrichCallender2017}.
For complex percolation with $p\notin[0,1]$, the prescribed scalar amplitude
premeasure has no countably additive extension to the full cylinder-generated
sigma-algebra \citep{DowkerJohnstonSurya2010,SuryaZalel2020}. This constrains
preservation of that amplitude rule, not the existence of every measure on
covariant events. A reading that requires real propositions to belong to the
finite-cylinder algebra, or a reading that requires this specific countably
additive extension, consequently faces a conditional obstruction. Neither
requirement follows from a temporal-ontology label. Revising the model,
event algebra or extension rule remains an option requiring its own defense.

\section{Objections and replies}

\paragraph{Bridge premises are unavoidable; your test is too demanding.}
Correct: every interpretation uses bridges. Their presence is not a defect.
The demand is that the bridge be visible, defensible, and relevant to the
contrast. A bridge can be constitutive of a view or supported by explanatory
arguments. We reject the inference ``extra premise, therefore arbitrary,''
as firmly as ``physical equation, therefore metaphysical conclusion.''

\paragraph{This makes every view compatible with physics.}
Only if admissibility is unconstrained, which the framework rejects. A
view that discards its defining commitments, adds ad hoc structure, or fails
empirical adequacy is not rescued by renaming the remainder. Conversely,
showing one admissible realization establishes compatibility, not equal
plausibility. A full exclusion claim has a stronger quantifier burden than a
counterexample to one proposed proof of exclusion.

\paragraph{Relativity and parsimony already favor the block.}
They may favor a specified block package over a competitor with unjustified
structure. The paper's demand is to articulate that explanatory comparison:
which structure is avoided, whether the rival needs it, and what explanatory
work competing packages perform. An inference to the best explanation is not
discredited by being philosophical rather than a direct detector reading.
It is also not rendered deductively compulsory by the success of SR.

\paragraph{Phenomenology supplies evidence for passage.}
It supplies phenomena a satisfactory account should explain. It does not
automatically choose among explanations. The advocate of primitive passage
must show why experience supports that cause over alternatives; the critic
must explain the experience rather than simply call it illusory.
The accounts discussed by \citet{Callender2017} and \citet{Skow2015} are
relevant comparators, not settled answers inherited by this draft.

\paragraph{An inaccessible ontology is physically meaningless.}
That conclusion needs a verificationist or other semantic argument which we
have not assumed. Empirical equivalence can coexist with differences in
grounding, explanation, simplicity, or modal commitment. The narrower
conclusion is that matched observational likelihoods do not themselves select
between the packages. Physics can still contribute through structural
constraints and explanatory considerations.

\paragraph{Your record example is trivial and deliberately restrictive.}
The mathematics is elementary and the restrictions are deliberate. It is
not offered as the novel contribution. Its purpose is to distinguish a
proved conditional equivalence from an unsupported claim about all ontologies.
A publishable extension would need a nontrivial physical class, a justified
access model, and a result not already provided by the nearest predecessors.

\section{What would turn this into a research contribution?}

The present argument supplies a way of auditing claims, not a discovery that
underdetermination exists. At least three developments could make it more
than an organized restatement of familiar cautions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A source-faithful reconstruction showing that a widely used inference
      requires a neglected bridge, and that the strongest published defenses
      and replies do not already establish the same point.
\item A nontrivial classification of when specified physical realizations
      preserve records and interventions, with explicit counterexamples at
      the boundary and a comparison to known equivalence results.
\item A substantive defense of one bridge principle that survives alternative
      temporal interpretations and explains why the resulting constraint is
      not merely a preference built into a definition.
\end{enumerate}
The independent novelty gate is unmet. Neither the elementary proposition nor
the use of agents, Python, or a future Lean proof supplies priority. Source
audits, model reproductions, and the strongest contrary philosophical arguments
are still needed. General relativity and cosmological time also require a
separate extension: the existence of smooth Cauchy slicings under global hyperbolicity
\citep{BernalSanchez2005} is not uniqueness of a metaphysical present.

\section{Provisional conclusion}

Physics can make temporal metaphysics harder, more precise, and sometimes
empirically vulnerable. It does not follow that every temporal distinction
is physically idle; nor does a successful mathematical representation settle
all questions of existence and passage. The appropriate unit of comparison
is a defended ontology--physics--bridge package with explicit access conditions.
An argument should state whether it excludes such a package, discriminates
its predictions, or establishes a representation equivalence. Keeping those
conclusions distinct is a precondition for discovering, rather than assuming,
what physics tells us about time.
