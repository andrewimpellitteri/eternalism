\title{Local presents and the block: Brading's P1\*\*\\ and the coverage problem}
\author{Working manuscript}
\date{21 October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Brading's 2015 formulation of presentism replaces P1\* with P1\*\*, whose
clause (a) says that each thing's present is less than the entire spatiotemporal
evolution of the universe. She says that (a) is all that is needed to avoid the
block universe. This paper argues that (a) is local boundedness and that local
boundedness does not exclude the family of system-presents covering the whole
history. The result is a source-grounded negative result: Brading's own stated
sufficient condition does not secure a global existential restriction. It is not
a refutation of presentism, a proof of eternalism, or a new theory of time.
Nearby relativized views are distinguished, and the remaining repairs are
stated with their costs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Brading's \emph{Presentism as an Empirical Hypothesis} and its follow-up
\emph{Physically Locating the Present} offer one of the most careful attempts to
keep a presentist ontology alive within special relativity
\citep{Brading2013,Brading2015}. She rejects the shared assumption that
spacetime is the ontological principle of unity of what exists, and replaces it
with a law-constitutive principle: the dynamical laws constitute genuine
unities, and the present of a system is the spatiotemporal region needed to
sustain its dynamics.

Her 2013 paper states P1\*: for each thing, it exists only presently, where the
spatiotemporal extent of that present is dynamics-dependent and empirically to
be determined \citep[p.~1110]{Brading2013}. She then notices that this extent
might be the whole universe, in which case labeling the entire block ``present''
does not save what the presentist hoped to save. The 2015 paper therefore
states P1\*\*:

\begin{quote}
(P1\*\*) For each and every thing that exists, that thing exists only presently,
where for each thing the spatiotemporal extent of the present for that thing is
dependent on the dynamics and is (a) less than the entire spatiotemporal
evolution of the universe as represented in Minkowski spacetime, and (b) less
than the entire spatiotemporal evolution of that thing as represented in
Minkowski spacetime \citep[p.~17]{Brading2015}.
\end{quote}

She adds: ``In order to avoid the block universe, all we need is (a)''
\citep[p.~17]{Brading2015}.

This paper asks whether (a) does what she says it does. The answer defended
here is negative and limited. Clause (a) is local boundedness: for every system
$i$, its present $\U_i$ is a proper part of the world history $\W$. Local
boundedness does not imply that the union of the $\U_i$ is a proper part of
$\W$. If the actual system-presents cover $\W$, the account divides the history
into dynamical unities without excluding any event from existence. Brading's
own sufficient condition therefore does not secure the global existential
restriction.

This is a negative result about one published argument. It does not refute
Brading's broader project, does not prove eternalism, and does not claim that
every presentism fails. It also does not claim that the broader idea of
system- or frame-relative existence is new: Balaguer's R-presentism
\citep{Balaguer2026} and Mason's domain- and level-dependent empirical
presentism \citep{Mason2026} already develop versions. The target here is
narrower: Brading's P1\*\* (a) and the claim that it suffices to avoid the block.

\section{The existential contrast}

\subsection{What is being claimed}

Brading begins from

\begin{itemize}
\item (P1) All and only things that exist now are real;
\item (P2) Special relativity is a complete account of spatiotemporal
structure \citep[p.~1101]{Brading2013}.
\end{itemize}

P1 is a global restriction on existence: what there is is what is present, and
events outside the present are not real. P2 says that nothing is missing from
the special-relativistic account of spatiotemporal structure. The apparent
conflict is that relativity supplies no global present.

Brading's response is not to add a preferred foliation. It is to reject the
assumption that spacetime is the ontological ground of unity, and to ground
unity in the dynamical laws instead \citep[pp.~1104--1108]{Brading2013}.
Spacetime becomes an epistemic principle of unity, the framework in which we
organize knowledge beyond the here and now. The present becomes the region
needed to sustain a dynamical system, and P1 is rewritten first as P1\* and then
as P1\*\*.

\subsection{Existence, presence, unity and endurance}

Four notions must be kept apart:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Existence}: the claim that a thing or event is part of what there
is.
\item \emph{Presence}: a thing's or event's belonging to a system's sustaining
region.
\item \emph{Unity}: the law-constituted integration that makes a system one
thing.
\item \emph{Endurance}: the persistence of that unity through change.
\end{enumerate}

Brading's argument moves from unity to presence. The further move from presence
to a restriction on existence is the one at issue. P1\*\* (b) is a claim about
the extent of each thing's present---an endurance/presence claim---while (a) is
a claim about the relation between each present and the whole history. Neither
is yet a claim about the domain of existence.

\subsection{The domain}

For regimentation, let $\W$ be the block as represented in Minkowski spacetime,
and for each genuine dynamical system $i$ let $\U_i\subseteq\W$ be its
system-sustaining present. This follows Brading's own phrasing in P1\*\* (a):
the comparison is made ``as represented in Minkowski spacetime.'' The
regimentation is a representational choice, not an ontological commitment. If
Brading's fundamental entities are unities rather than events, the argument is
to be read as a claim about the spatiotemporal extents of unities; the coverage
point concerns the extents, not the category of the bearers.

Brading's 2013 footnote 7 denies determinate ontological spatiotemporal
relations between systems \citep[p.~1109]{Brading2013}. That denial is
compatible with the representation: the block representation is the common
frame in which the comparison is stated, not a claim that the systems stand in
determinate ontological relations. If no common representation is allowed, the
comparison becomes a comparison of frameworks; that is the deflationary
fallback discussed in the conclusion.

\section{Brading's law-constitutive present}

Brading's positive proposal has three parts.

First, the laws constitute genuine unities. A physical thing, simple or
composite, is what it is in part because it satisfies the laws; the laws also
constitute the parts of a composite and their persistence through change
\citep[pp.~1105--1108]{Brading2013}. This is offered as an alternative to
essentialism and haecceitism, and as an account of numerical identity through
qualitative change.

Second, spacetime is demoted from an ontological to an epistemic principle of
unity. It is the framework in which we organize knowledge of the not-here and
not-now, not the ground of what exists \citep[pp.~1108--1109]{Brading2013}.

Third, the present is the region needed to sustain the dynamical system:

\begin{quote}
The present is a spatiotemporal region of whatever size is necessary to sustain
the dynamical system in question \citep[p.~1109]{Brading2013}.
\end{quote}

The region may be much smaller than the block, or it may be the size of the
block. If the only genuine system is the entire universe, Brading says that
``this version of presentism is defeated and the block universe triumphs''
\citep[p.~1109]{Brading2013}. The empirical question is whether there are
genuine subsystems whose sustaining regions are proper parts of the whole.

P1\* states the first reformulation. Its full text is:

\begin{quote}
(P1\*) For each and every thing, that thing exists only presently, where the
spatiotemporal extent of that ``present'' is dependent on the dynamics, and it
is something to be determined empirically \citep[p.~1110]{Brading2013};
\citep[p.~17]{Brading2015}.
\end{quote}

Brading then observes that the extent might be the entire universe, so that
``having the entirety of the block universe labeled as the `present' doesn't
seem to save anything that the presentist was hoping to save''
\citep[p.~17]{Brading2015}. P1\*\* is the response. Clause (a) bounds each
present relative to the universe; clause (b) bounds it relative to the thing's
own history. Brading says (b) is optional for avoiding the block, and that
``all we need is (a).''

A distinguished predecessor is Stein's event-relative notion of definiteness
\citep{Stein1991}. Stein's printed theorem (p.~149) lists reflexivity,
transitivity, invariance under time-orientation-preserving automorphisms, and
one past-pointing related pair, with the dimension qualification ``of any number
of dimensions---of course at least two.'' It is not self-contained: the proof
also uses the non-universality condition (iv) and the prose premise that every
event in the causal past---timelike or null---is definite as of the point
\citep[p.~149]{Stein1991}. The reflexive strict chronological past is
non-universal but omits past-null pairs, so condition (iv) alone is not enough.
The local-present strategy inherits this distinction; it does not inherit a
global restriction.

\section{The coverage objection}

\subsection{Local boundedness is not global restriction}

Clause (a) says, for every system $i$,

\[
\U_i\subsetneq\W .
\]

It does not imply

\[
\bigcup_i \U_i\subsetneq\W .
\]

If every event belongs to at least one system-present, the family
$\{\U_i\}$ covers $\W$. The account then divides the history into dynamical
unities; it does not exclude any event from existence. The present can be
``local, not global'' \citep[p.~1110]{Brading2013} while the union of local
presents is the whole history.

\begin{proposition}[Non-implication]
Local boundedness of every $\U_i$ is consistent with
$\bigcup_i\U_i=\W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\W$ be a nonempty set and take two overlapping proper subsets whose union
is $\W$. Every member is locally bounded, and the union is $\W$.
\end{proof}

The proposition establishes a non-implication, not that Brading's actual
family covers $\W$. The further premise is:

\begin{quote}
(C) The actual system-presents cover $\W$.
\end{quote}

If (C) holds, no event is excluded. If (C) fails, some event lies outside every
system-present; that is a substantive no-coverage claim, not a consequence of
P1\*\* (a). Brading does not state (C) or its negation. The coverage objection is
therefore exact as a non-implication and conditional as an objection: it shows
that (a) does not by itself secure the restriction.

There are two textual routes to (C), both suggested by Brading's own text.

First, the law-constitutive approach applies to ``the totality of existing
things'' as well as to simple and composite things \citep[p.~1105,
n.~3]{Brading2013}. If the totality is a genuine unity, its sustaining region is
plausibly the whole history. Then its present is not less than the universe's
evolution, contrary to (a), or the totality is not a thing to which (a) applies.
Either way, P1\*\* faces an internal pressure.

Second, Brading notes that she ``can be made of things whose dynamically
sustained regions of spacetime are much smaller than mine''
\citep[p.~1109, n.~6]{Brading2013}. A plurality of nested, short-lived things
whose presents populate the history is exactly the kind of family for which (C)
is natural. If their presents cover $\W$, local boundedness is satisfied and no
event is excluded. This is an argument sketch, not a proof; it is enough to
show that (C) is not excluded by Brading's premises.

If (C) is not established, the conclusion is a burden-shift: (a) is insufficient
unless a no-coverage premise is supplied. The paper should not call the
objection ``exact'' without that qualification. What is exact is the
non-implication.

\subsection{The interaction passage}

Brading's interaction passage is often read as a system-relative existence
claim. In full:

\begin{quote}
The ground of what is real is the dynamics, and we belong to the same world as
whatever we interact with, and the rest of that world is as real as we are.
\ldots\ Does my son exist relative to me when he is in London and I am here? Of
course. There are plenty of interactions going on that link us. Is there a
determinate fact of the matter about what he is doing right now? No.
\citep[p.~1110]{Brading2013}
\end{quote}

This passage is better used for the negative verdict than for a relativized
existence attribution. ``We belong to the same world,'' ``the rest of that world
is as real as we are,'' and ``the unity of what there is, both of the parts and
of the whole'' are single-domain claims. ``Exists relative to me'' is glossed by
interaction and followed by a claim about the \emph{determinacy} of what the son
is doing now, not about his existence. The passage supports the claim that no
event is excluded merely by lying outside a local present; it does not establish
a relativized existential quantifier. The paper therefore withdraws the stronger
attribution.

Relativized existence is a real position, but it is not Brading's explicit
P1\*\*. Balaguer's R-presentism makes existence relative to a frame of
reference, with no frame-independent fact about which 3-manifold is real
\citep{Balaguer2026}. Mason's empirical presentism makes the present
domain- and level-dependent and treats ``what exists'' as perspective-relative
\citep{Mason2026}. Those are nearby views; the present paper's target is P1\*\*
(a), and its conclusion is compatible with theirs.

\section{Repairs and costs}

If one wants the original global restriction, two repairs attempt to restore
it.

\paragraph{Restoring repair 1: privileged actual systems.}
Only some systems' presents are actual. This restores a restriction, but the
selection principle is not derived from the physics or from dynamical unity
alone. Brading's own text supplies a candidate: the dynamics grounds ``the
unity of what there is, both of the parts and of the whole (consisting of
interacting parts)'' \citep[p.~1110]{Brading2013}, and the law-constitutive
approach applies to the totality. A maximal-interacting-unity principle is a
dynamical selection principle. If the maximal unity is the selected system, its
present is the whole history and the view yields the block. If it is not, the
principle needs independent defense. Either way, the cost is real.

\paragraph{Restoring repair 2: restrictions on coexistence.}
Not all local presents can coexist. This may take the form of a maximal family
of presents related by a stated coexistence relation, or a dynamics that selects
one present. Without a formal relation, ``maximal consistent set'' is a
placeholder; with one, the union of the family need not be a single present,
and the view risks reintroducing a global present or a privileged foliation.
The physics does not supply the relation.

\paragraph{Revisionary option 1: system-relative existence.}
Replace a single unrestricted existence predicate with ``exists-for-system-$i$.''
This blocks the coverage objection by changing the question, but it gives up the
global presentist claim. It is the route developed by Balaguer and Mason, not a
restoration of P1.

\paragraph{Revisionary option 2: revised presentism.}
Give up global exclusion and accept local or functional becoming. This is
coherent, but it is a different thesis: it may be an eternalist history with a
local becoming, not presentism as the restriction on existence. If nothing
distinguishes it from eternalism except the label, this is the paper's real
conclusion.

The primitive-actuality reply deserves a hearing. A presentist may say that
actuality is primitive and cannot be derived from the physics; the privileged
system is simply the actual one. That is a coherent metaphysical posit. Its cost
is that the physics does not supply it, and the paper's claim is only that
P1\*\* (a) does not supply it either. The reply does not restore the derivation;
it relocates the premise.

\section{A parity verdict}

The conclusion is not that the evidence is silent. It is a parity verdict:

\begin{quote}
On the P1\*\* (a) reading, the physics does not favor presentism over eternalism;
any advantage presentism can claim is purchased by an actuality or privilege
premise the physics does not supply.
\end{quote}

This is weaker than exclusion and stronger than a catalogue. A composite
ontology can be favored without excluding every rival, but here the comparison
yields a tie unless the actuality premise is added. Adding it is a metaphysical
choice, not a physical result.

\section{Strongest objections}

\paragraph{Saunders: the equivalence-relation requirement.}
Saunders argues that presentism needs an invariant, definable equivalence
relation of co-presence: ``nothing less than an equivalence relation will do for
the presentist''; ``not only transitivity, but also symmetry, is required''
\citep[p.~3]{Saunders2000}. The definability results leave past-timelike,
past-causal or universal relations; none is a non-trivial symmetric equivalence
relation of a present \citep{CliftonHogarth1995}. The ``non-trivial'' qualifier
is this paper's strengthening: the universal relation is an equivalence relation,
so Saunders's bare ``no equivalence relation'' needs it.

A system-relative present is not an equivalence relation among all events; it
is a region assigned to a system. Brading's view therefore either gives up the
equivalence-relation requirement and with it the standard presentist claim, or
needs an additional structure that defines co-presence. The coverage objection
is the set-theoretic shadow of the first horn. Saunders's reason for the
requirement---the present must be a public, non-provincial ``whole of
reality''---is not answered by a local present; if Brading rejects that starting
point, she owes an account of why ``present'' is still a single public notion.

\paragraph{Pooley: taking both passage and relativity seriously.}
Pooley distinguishes two easy routes: a deflationary analysis of passage
compatible with the block, which does not take passage seriously, and a
privileged global present, which does not take relativity seriously
\citep{Pooley2013}. His hard route eschews a global Now and links passage to the
open future. Brading's paper is about existence, not passage; she is not a
candidate for Pooley's route. The honest formulation is that the coverage point
is a \emph{precondition} for any non-deflationary hard route: a non-deflationary
structure is needed, and Brading's local present supplies a deflationary one
unless an actuality premise is added. This is a concession, not a refutation of
Pooley.

\section{Conclusion}

Brading's P1\*\* is her strongest anti-block formulation. Clause (a) is local
boundedness, and local boundedness does not imply a global existential
restriction. If the actual system-presents cover the history, no event is
excluded. Brading's ``all we need is (a)'' is therefore insufficient as stated.
The conclusion is a limited, source-grounded negative result about one published
argument.

The broader lessons are these. A local, law-constitutive present can be a
serious account of unity and presence; it does not by itself yield a global
ontology of existence. A presentist who wants the global restriction must add
an actuality or privilege premise, restrict coexistence, or revise what
presentism claims. The first is a metaphysical posit, the second risks a global
present, and the third may be eternalism with a local becoming. The paper does
not choose among them; it states the cost.

\paragraph{What would change the verdict.}
A proof that the actual system-presents cannot cover $\W$ would restore the
restriction without an actuality premise. A physically motivated selection of
privileged actual systems would supply the missing premise, though its cost
would still need defense. A revised P1\*\* that explicitly restricts the domain
of existence, rather than the extent of each thing's present, would change the
target. A demonstration that system-relative existence is the same global claim
under another notation would defeat the revisionary route.

\paragraph{Limits.}
This is a philosophical argument, not an empirical prediction or an ontology
selection. The coverage non-implication is elementary set theory; the
contribution is the source-grounded application to P1\*\* and the cost analysis.
Brading 2013 and 2015 are read at full text (2015 pp.~17--18 in particular);
Saunders and Pooley are read at the preprint depths stated in the review record;
Balaguer and Mason are used at abstract/chapter level. No global priority or
novelty claim is made beyond the modest P1\*\* point identified in the
accompanying novelty audit.
