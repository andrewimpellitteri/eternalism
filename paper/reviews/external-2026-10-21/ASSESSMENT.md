## Overall verdict

I accept the reviewer's bottom line: **the central methodological claim
survives, but the draft is not yet an original research contribution.** It is a
careful prospectus. The review's most damaging points are not that the
distinctions are wrong; they are that the draft repeatedly stops at the point
where a substantive argument would begin.

The concrete Stein objection is correct and has been fixed in the manuscript and
the source records. The remaining points are architectural and require a
decision about what the paper is for.

## What I do not concede

- The five surviving points are correct and should remain: conjunction failure
  is not premise failure; matched likelihoods give no discrimination in the
  declared class; representation equivalence is not metaphysical identity;
  completeness of a history representation is not determinism; operational
  access matters.
- The paper does not claim that physics is irrelevant, that every ontology is
  rescuable, or that empirical equivalence entails ontological identity. The
  reviewer agrees; the problem is emphasis and incompleteness, not the opposite
  overclaim.
- The Stein problem is local. It does not show Stein's whole argument fails,
  and the article now says so.

## What this means for the draft

The draft should not be sent out as a research paper in its current form. It can
be sent as a working draft, but the reviewer's verdict will repeat. The choice is
between:

1. **Focused Brading/existence-bridge paper** (recommended): one question, one
   argument, one limited conclusion. This uses the strongest material already in
   the draft and answers the most substantial objection.
2. **Methodological paper with one completed application**: keep the framework,
   but cut it to the minimum and demonstrate it on one published inference,
   ending with a defeasible favoring verdict rather than another disclaimer.

`REVISION_PLAN.md` sets out both.
