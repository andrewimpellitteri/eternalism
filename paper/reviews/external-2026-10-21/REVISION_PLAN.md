# Revision plan after the external review

Date: 2026-10-21. The current draft is preserved; this plan proposes two
substantive rebuilds. It does not pretend that more qualifications will fix the
architectural problem.

## Recommendation: option 1, a focused Brading/existence-bridge paper

**Working title:** *Local presents and unrestricted existence: when does a
dynamical present restrict what exists?*

**Target question.** Under what conditions does a physically defined local
present constitute a restriction on what exists, rather than merely a division
of an eternalist history into dynamical unities?

This is the reviewer's preferred option, and it uses the strongest material
already in the draft: Brading 2013/2015, the union/coverage objection, Stein's
event-relative definiteness, Clifton–Hogarth, and the project's admissibility
work.

### Structure

1. **The existential contrast, stated precisely.** Presentism as a restriction
   on unrestricted concrete existence; eternalism as no such restriction;
   system-relative and local presents as candidate intermediate positions.
   Define the domain of quantification and what counts as an exclusion.
2. **Brading reconstructed charitably.** Separate dynamical unity, endurance,
   presentness and existence. Her direction of explanation: law-constituted
   unities determine the present, not a prior present determining existence.
   Read depths: 2013 and 2015 at the recorded full-text levels.
3. **The coverage problem.** For system-relative presents `U_i`, local
   boundedness (`U_i` proper subset of the universe history for each `i`) does
   not imply `union_i U_i` is proper. The union objection is exact. It shows
   that local boundedness alone does not supply the advertised global
   existential difference.
4. **Possible repairs.** (a) system-relative existence; (b) privileged actual
   systems; (c) restrictions on which local presents coexist; (d) a revised
   presentism that gives up global exclusion. For each: what it adds, whether
   it is physically motivated, and whether it introduces the restriction
   independently of the physics.
5. **Assessment.** A limited conclusion: dynamical individuation alone is
   insufficient to establish the intended existential restriction. The
   remaining routes require an additional actuality principle or a privileged
   system, and their costs must be stated.
6. **What would change the verdict.** A physically motivated account of which
   systems are actual/privileged; a proof that overlapping presents cannot
   cover the history; or a revised presentism that does not need global
   exclusion.

### What to cut or move

- Stein paragraph: retain only the corrected four-way distinction, as a
  supporting source note, not a central section.
- Quantum numerical example: cut. If retained, fully specify a nontrivial
  sector; the one-dimensional calculation is only a diagnostic.
- Record proposition: move to a short appendix or cut.
- Causal-set discussion: move to future work unless it becomes the central
  case.
- LAGEOS numbers: compress to one sentence; the parameter bound does no
  ontological work.
- Repeated audit/novelty disclaimers: consolidate into one status paragraph.

## Alternative: option 2, methodological paper with one completed application

Keep the framework, but cut §§1–3 to the minimum and demonstrate it once.

1. **Framework in brief:** exclusion, likelihood comparison, representation
   equivalence; eligibility vs discriminating evidence; no fiat.
2. **One published inference:** reconstruct Saunders's structural-completeness
   challenge (or Pooley's compatibility/robust-passage distinction) in its
   strongest form.
3. **The exact bridge:** identify the premise that turns the physical result
   into an ontological verdict.
4. **Alternative bridges:** show two defensible bridges and how the conclusion
   changes.
5. **Verdict:** a defeasible favoring judgment, e.g. "physics favors package A
   over B under bridge X, but does not exclude B under bridge Y."
6. **Limits:** one consolidated status note.

This option is more conservative but requires actually reaching a comparative
judgment. It cannot end with another "further work is needed."

## Decision

- **Option 1** is recommended: it has a real target, a precise objection, and a
  limited but substantive conclusion.
- **Option 2** is viable if the author wants to keep the methodological frame.
- Do not submit the current 12-page draft as a research paper. It can be sent as
  a working draft, but the same review will recur.
