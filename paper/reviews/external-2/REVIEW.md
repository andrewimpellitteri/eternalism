## 1. Verdict

**Major revision. The draft is no longer a prospectus, but it is not yet a
research contribution, because it argues against a formulation its target
has already superseded.**

The form is now right. There is one question, one target, one objection, one
verdict, and the verdict is stated rather than deferred. The reconstruction of
Brading's unity/epistemic-role/present structure is accurate against the 2013
text, the coverage non-implication is correct, and the Saunders/Pooley material
is engaged and partly conceded. That is a real change from the previous draft,
which the first reviewer and the coordinator rightly called a prospectus.

The substance is not yet right, for a specific and fixable reason. The paper
attacks P1\* from Brading 2013. In Brading 2015 the author herself replaces P1\*
with P1\*\* because, in her words, P1\* is a formulation on which "having the
entirety of the block universe labeled as 'present' doesn't seem to save
anything that the presentist was hoping to save" (2015, p. 17). P1\*\* then states
the anti-block condition explicitly, and it is, in effect, the paper's
local-boundedness condition: for each thing, the extent of its present is "less
than the entire spatiotemporal evolution of the universe," and "In order to
avoid the block universe, all we need is (a)" (2015, p. 17). The coverage
objection applies to P1\*\* verbatim and more tellingly than to P1\*, because P1\*\*
is Brading's own stated sufficient condition. The draft does not mention P1\*\* at
all. As it stands, the paper re-derives a limitation its target had already
identified in a later paper, and leaves the strongest version untouched.

That omission is compounded by the paper's headline interpretive claim:
"Brading's own reformulation and her interaction passage support a
system-relative existence reading" (abstract). The claim is not established.
P1\* makes the *extent of the present* dynamics-dependent; that is a claim about
each thing's temporal extent, which the paper's own §2.2 distinction classifies
as presence/endurance, not existence. The interaction passage says "we belong
to the same world as whatever we interact with, and the rest of that world is as
real as we are" (2013, p. 1110) — a single-domain claim, not an
"exists-for-system-i" predicate. The paper's own distinction between presence
and existence tells against the reading it endorses.

So the contribution is identifiable but not yet delivered. With two changes —
retarget §§3–4 at P1\*\* and withdraw or defend the system-relative-existence
attribution — the paper becomes a modest, genuine contribution: a
source-grounded demonstration that Brading's own strongest anti-block condition
is local boundedness, and that local boundedness does not exclude coverage. That
is a negative result about one published argument, not a new theory of time,
and it is enough for a short paper.

Without those changes I would recommend rejection, because the target is a
formulation its author superseded and the central attribution is unsupported.

## 3. Section-by-section assessment

### §1 Introduction

The question is well posed and the negative answer is stated up front. The
paper correctly identifies the issue as a bridge from a local, physically
described present to unrestricted existence.

Problems: (a) the abstract and introduction assert the system-relative
existence reading before any argument for it; (b) the P1\* quotation is altered
(see M3); (c) the paper says "The contribution is not a new general theory of
time" and then describes the contribution as "a focused reconstruction … and a
precise objection." That is honest, but the objection is only as good as its
target, and the target is P1\* rather than P1\*\*. The introduction should say
which formulation of Brading's view it is attacking and why that is the
strongest one.

### §2 The existential contrast

The P1/P1\* distinction is formally correct: P1 is a global claim about what is
real; P1\* quantifies over things and assigns each a present (more precisely, a
present extent). The four-way distinction (existence, presence, unity,
endurance) is the right instrument and is what makes the coverage problem
visible.

But the section has three defects that propagate.

1. **Category mixing.** The four "notions" are not the same kind of thing:
   existence is presented as "the unrestricted claim that an event is part of
   what there is" (a claim, not a property of an event); presence is a relation;
   unity is a property; endurance is a process or a relation between stages. A
   paper that insists on distinctions should define them in one category. As
   written, "existence" is a claim in the definition and a predicate
   ("exists-for-system-i") in §5.
2. **The domain is events, but Brading's domain is things/unities.** She says
   "what exists (what is real) are the genuine unities" (2015, p. 17), and P1 is
   about "things." The paper's `W` is a set of concrete *events* and `U_i` are
   regions of events. That regimentation may be defensible, but it is not
   Brading's ontology and the paper does not say what it costs. This matters
   because a presentist can say: events are not the bearers of existence; things
   are, and each thing exists only during its present. The union-of-events
   argument then does not by itself settle the thing-domain question.
3. **`W` is contested by Brading's own text.** Footnote 7 of 2013 (p. 1109)
   says: "There are no determinate ontological spatial or temporal relations
   within that region, and that system stands in no determinate ontological
   spatial or temporal relations to any other system." If systems stand in no
   determinate spatiotemporal relations, then "the world history" as a single
   set of events with a single containment relation is not obviously hers. The
   paper needs either to justify `W` as a neutral regimentation (e.g., the block
   as represented in Minkowski spacetime, which is how Brading 2015 states
   condition (a)) or to restate the objection over unities and their extents.
   As it stands the formal setup may presuppose the shared spacetime structure
   she rejects — the kind of question-begging the project's own standards warn
   against.

The definition of "global existential restriction" also has a small category
slip: "events that do not satisfy it." Facts are not satisfied; events either
are or are not part of what exists. Rewrite as "events that are not part of
what exists simpliciter."

### §3 Brading's law-constitutive present

This is the strongest section. The reconstruction is accurate, the quotations
are exact, and the direction of explanation is preserved. The Stein note is
compact and accurate as far as it goes (see m3 for the omitted dimension clause
and the missing locator for the prose premise).

The fatal omission is here: **the 2015 paper is dispatched in one sentence**
("The 2015 follow-up develops the same system-sustaining-region formulation").
That sentence is true but materially incomplete. The 2015 paper does not merely
restate the formulation; it adds P1\*\* because the author sees that P1\* does not
secure the anti-block result. A paper that cites 2015 — and whose scope document
(`REFRAME.md` §3) requires full-text 2013/2015 — cannot omit the successor
formulation. This is not a minor citation gap; it is the difference between
attacking a live position and attacking a version its author has already moved
past. (The draft's limits note says 2015 was read only at "abstract/indexed
depth." That is inconsistent with `paper/SOURCE_AUDIT.md`, which records the
full published-layout copy, and it is the likely cause of the omission.)

The section also misses two passages that would strengthen the paper:

- **2013, p. 1105 n. 3:** the law-constitutive approach "applies to all three"
  — simple things, composite things, and "the totality of existing things."
  If the totality is a genuine unity, its sustaining region is plausibly the
  whole history, and the union of presents is `W` by construction, not merely by
  accident. (This is my inference from n. 3 and p. 1110; the paper should check
  it against the text rather than take it over.)
- **2013, p. 1109 n. 6:** "I can be made of things whose dynamically sustained
  regions of spacetime are much smaller than mine." This is Brading's own
  warrant for a plurality of short-lived things whose presents populate the
  history — exactly what the coverage argument needs.

### §4 The coverage objection

The non-implication is correct and the clarifications are correct. The
proposition, however, establishes less than the surrounding prose suggests.
"Local boundedness of every `U_i` is consistent with `⋃U_i = W`" is a
satisfiability claim; the proof gives a model ("take two overlapping proper
subsets"). That is fine for consistency. But the paper slides from consistency
to the claim that the coverage objection is "exact" (abstract, §8). What is
exact is the non-implication. Whether the actual family of Brading's
system-presents covers `W` is a further premise, stated by the paper only
conditionally: "If every event belongs to at least one system-present, the
family covers `W`." That conditional *is* the objection, and the paper never
argues its antecedent. It should either argue coverage (the n. 3/n. 6 route
above, or P1\*\* plus the plurality of things) or downgrade "exact" to "the
non-implication is exact; coverage is a further premise Brading does not
exclude."

The subsection "Brading's text points to system-relative existence" is where
the paper is most vulnerable (M2). The reading is asserted, not argued, and the
passage is at least as naturally read as a single-domain claim. The P1\*
quotation in §1 is also truncated in a way that changes its meaning (M3).

### §5 Four repairs and their costs

The map is useful and the costs are real, but the list is not yet a cost
analysis of Brading's position.

- **Repair 1 is not a repair.** It does not restore P1; it changes the subject.
  The paper says so ("gives up the global presentist claim"), but then includes
  it in a list of repairs "if one wants the original global restriction." Only
  repairs 2 and 3 are attempts to restore the restriction; 1 and 4 are
  revisionary alternatives. Separate the two categories.
- **Repair 2's cost is stated too quickly.** The paper says the selection
  principle "is not derived from the physics or from dynamical unity." But
  Brading's own text supplies a candidate: the dynamics grounds "the unity of
  what there is, both of the parts and of the whole (consisting of interacting
  parts)" (2013, p. 1110), and the law-constitutive approach applies to the
  totality (n. 3). A maximal-interacting-unity principle is a dynamical
  selection principle. If the maximal unity is the selected system, its present
  is the whole history and the view yields the block — which supports the
  paper's verdict, but by a different route, and the paper must address it
  rather than assert that no such principle exists.
- **Repair 3 is thin.** "Maximal consistent set" is undefined; consistency of
  what, under what relation? A maximal consistent set of pairwise-overlapping
  presents is a filter, and its union need not be a single present. The paper
  should say which formal object it has in mind.
- **Repair 4 is probably Brading's actual position.** "May be an eternalist
  history with a local becoming" hedges. On the coverage reading, this is where
  she is. The paper should say what would make it *not* an eternalist history;
  if nothing, repair 4 is eternalism with a label, and that is the paper's real
  conclusion.
- **The primitive-actuality reply is missing.** `REFRAME.md` §7 required the
  paper to answer or concede "the presentist's reply that actuality is
  primitive." The paper does not address it. It says the repair "needs an
  independent actuality premise" and "may reintroduce a privileged perspective,"
  but it does not consider the reply that primitiveness is acceptable. That is
  an unmet obligation from the paper's own scope document.

### §6 Favoring without exclusion

This section does not deliver a verdict; it delivers a schema. The Bayes-factor
example is the first reviewer's example, reused (honestly, but without new
content). The application to Brading yields: under the system-relative
realization, "the comparison does not favor presentism"; under a
privileged-actuality realization, "it may" favor it, "but the privilege premise
is doing the work." That is a conditional tie and a conditional advantage, not
a comparative judgment about the actual case.

Two sentences overclaim. "The conclusion is not that the evidence is silent" is
contradicted by the application, which says the system-relative realization does
not favor presentism — i.e., a tie. And "That is a substantive comparative
verdict, not a catalogue of failed exclusions" describes a tie as a verdict. A
tie is the absence of a favoring verdict.

There is a legitimate verdict available: *the physics does not favor presentism
over eternalism; any advantage presentism can claim is purchased by an
actuality premise the physics does not supply.* That is a parity/underdetermination
verdict, and it follows from the coverage argument. The paper should either
state it plainly or cut §6 to a paragraph. Dressing a tie in the reviewer's toy
numbers does not make it a favoring result.

### §7 Strongest objections

**Saunders.** The reconstruction is better than the first reviewer's
"structural-completeness" phrase, because it uses Saunders's actual requirement:
"nothing less than an equivalence relation will do for the presentist" (2000,
preprint p. 3), and "not only transitivity, but also symmetry, is required"
(ibid.). Two accuracy points: the "non-trivial" qualifier is the paper's
addition, not Saunders's wording (Saunders says "Therefore there is no
equivalence relation," which is strictly false unless non-triviality is added,
since the universal relation is an equivalence relation); and the paper gives no
locator. Mark the strengthening.

The response is a horn-mapping, not an answer. It says Brading either gives up
the requirement or needs an additional structure, and that coverage is "the
set-theoretic shadow of the first horn." That is a fair mapping, but it does not
engage Saunders's *reason* for the requirement: the present must be a public,
non-provincial "whole of reality" (Saunders: "it is a public space … this is the
whole of reality"; "it had better not be a view which is provincial," 2000,
p. 1). If Brading rejects that starting point, the paper should say so and
say what she owes instead — an account of why "present" is still a single public
notion. Otherwise Saunders's challenge is conceded by relocation.

**Pooley.** The reconstruction is accurate: the two easy routes and the
"eschew a global Now" formulation are verbatim in the preprint's abstract and
introduction. But the application overreaches. Pooley's challenge is about
*objective passage*; Brading's paper is about *existence* and makes no passage
claim. Calling her local present "a candidate for that route" slides from
existence to passage — the very distinction §2.2 insists on. The honest
formulation is: the coverage point is a *precondition* for any Pooley-style
hard route (a non-deflationary structure is needed), and Brading's view, as
reconstructed, supplies a deflationary one. The paper should not say "That is
the deflationary horn" as if Brading had offered an answer to Pooley's question;
she did not.

### §8 Conclusion

The verdict is clear and appropriately limited. The "what would change the
verdict" paragraph is good. Two problems: the system-relative-existence claim is
repeated as established; and the limits note is factually wrong about Brading
2015 ("abstract/indexed depth"), contradicting the project's own source audit
and `REFRAME.md`'s requirement of full-text 2013/2015. The phrase "the
accompanying note" has no referent in a submitted article; replace it with the
actual depth statement or cut it.

## 4. Major issues

### M1. The paper attacks P1\*, but Brading's strongest formulation is P1\*\* (2015, p. 17), and P1\*\* is the paper's local-boundedness condition

**Location:** §3 (2015 sentence), §4, §5, abstract, §8.

**Evidence.** Brading 2015, p. 17: after P1\* she writes, "As we noted, this
spatiotemporal extent might turn out to be the entire spatiotemporal extent of
the universe, and having the entirety of the block universe labeled as 'present'
doesn't seem to save anything that the presentist was hoping to save. So one
more attempt seems in order":

> (P1\*\*) For each and every thing that exists, that thing exists only
> presently, where for each thing the spatiotemporal extent of the present for
> that thing is dependent on the dynamics and is (a) less than the entire
> spatiotemporal evolution of the universe as represented in Minkowski
> spacetime, and (b) less than the entire spatiotemporal evolution of that thing
> as represented in Minkowski spacetime.

She adds: "In order to avoid the block universe, all we need is (a)."

Clause (a) is `∀i U_i ⊊ W` — the paper's definition of local boundedness,
almost verbatim. Clause (b) is a restriction on each thing's temporal extent,
i.e., on endurance, not on the domain of existence. So Brading's own strongest
anti-block condition is the premise the paper says is insufficient. That is the
"consequential mistake in a published argument" the first reviewer asked for,
and it is sitting in the text the paper cites.

**Why it matters.** Without P1\*\*, the paper's target is a formulation Brading
herself says does not "save anything that the presentist was hoping to save"
(2015, p. 17) and which she therefore replaced. A referee will say the paper is
attacking a version the author has already moved past. With P1\*\*, the paper's
negative verdict becomes a criticism of a stated claim ("all we need is (a)"),
which is exactly what a research contribution needs. (To be precise about the
quotations: "doesn't really say anything" in 2015, p. 17, refers to the bare
formulation "For each and every thing, that thing exists only presently" without
the dynamics clause, not to P1\*. The "doesn't seem to save anything" remark is
about P1\*.)

**Fix.** Replace the one-sentence 2015 mention with a reconstruction of P1\*
and P1\*\*; quote both in full with the p. 17 locator; show that (a) is local
boundedness and (b) is an extent claim; then run the coverage argument against
(a). This is a substitution in §3–§4, not a rewrite of the paper.

**A detail that strengthens the point.** §8 says "A revised P1\* that explicitly
restricts the domain of existence, rather than assigning each thing a present,
would change the target." Brading 2015 is exactly such a revision — P1\*\* adds
explicit conditions to P1\* — but it restricts the *extent* of each thing's
present, not the *domain* of existence. So the revision the paper says would
change the target is already in the literature, and it does not change the
target in the way the paper needs. That is worth a sentence in §8: it shows the
paper has considered the strongest revision and can say why it is not enough.

**Related question for the author.** On 2013 p. 1105 n. 3 the law-constitutive
approach applies to "the totality of existing things." If the totality is a
thing, P1\*\* (a) requires its present to be less than the universe's evolution.
Either the totality is not a thing (then the principle does not apply to it,
contrary to n. 3), or its present is proper (then what sustains the universe's
dynamics?). This looks like an internal tension in P1\*\* and is worth a
paragraph. It is my inference, not Brading's claim; label it as such.

### M2. The "system-relative existence" reading is not supported, and the paper's own distinction tells against it

**Location:** abstract; §1; §2 ("P1\* is the textual pivot"); §4.2; §5 repair 1;
§8.

**Evidence.** P1\* as Brading writes it (2013, p. 1110; 2015, p. 17): "For each
and every thing, that thing exists only presently, where the spatiotemporal
extent of that 'present' is dependent on dynamics, and it is something to be
determined empirically." The dynamics-dependence attaches to the *extent of the
present*, i.e., to how long/where the thing exists. That is a claim about each
thing's temporal extent — the paper's own category of presence/endurance
(§2.2) — not a relativized existential quantifier.

The interaction passage (2013, p. 1110) reads in full: "The ground of what is
real is the dynamics, and we belong to the same world as whatever we interact
with, and the rest of that world is as real as we are. The dynamics grounds the
unity of what there is, both of the parts and of the whole (consisting of
interacting parts), and this is what prevents the presentist from becoming a
solipsist. Does my son exist relative to me when he is in London and I am here?
Of course. There are plenty of interactions going on that link us. Is there a
determinate fact of the matter about what he is doing right now? No."

"Belong to the same world," "the rest of that world is as real as we are," and
"the unity of what there is, both of the parts and of the whole" are
single-domain claims. "Exists relative to me" is the strongest textual support
for the paper's reading, and the paper should say so; but the sentence is
glossed by "plenty of interactions going on that link us" — co-presence via
interaction — and is followed by a claim about *determinacy* of what the son is
doing "right now," not about his existence. It sits between two single-domain
claims and does not by itself establish a relativized existential quantifier.
The passage supports the paper's negative verdict (the rest of the world is as
real as we are; no event is excluded), but not the stronger attribution.

**Why it matters.** The abstract's headline is this attribution. If it fails,
the paper's self-description fails, even if its negative verdict survives. It
also creates the dialectical oscillation in M4.

**Fix.** Choose one: (i) argue the relativized reading against the
single-domain reading, using something stronger than the passage itself
(candidate: 2013 n. 7, "no determinate ontological … relations to any other
system"); or (ii) withdraw the attribution and use the passage for the coverage
verdict: Brading's own gloss concedes that the rest of the world is as real as
we are, so the existential restriction is not in her text. (ii) is better
supported and makes the paper's conclusion stronger.

### M3. The P1\* quotation is truncated without ellipsis, changing the antecedent of "it"

**Location:** §1 (block quote), §2 ("P1 is rewritten as P1\*"), §8.

**Evidence.** The paper quotes: "(P1\*) For each and every thing, that thing
exists only presently, where it is something to be determined empirically
(Brading 2013, p. 1110)." The original is: "… where the spatiotemporal extent
of that 'present' is dependent on dynamics, and it is something to be determined
empirically." The paper silently deletes "the spatiotemporal extent of that
'present' is dependent on dynamics, and." In the original, "it" refers to the
extent of the present; in the paper's version, "it" floats and can be read as
referring to the existence claim itself. The deletion is not marked with an
ellipsis.

**Why it matters.** P1\* is the textual pivot of the paper. A referee who checks
the quotation will find that the paper's reading is produced partly by the
truncation. This is the kind of defect that costs a paper its credibility even
when the surrounding argument is sound.

**Fix.** Quote P1\* in full, or use an explicit ellipsis that preserves the
antecedent: "… where the spatiotemporal extent of that 'present' is dependent
on dynamics, and it is something to be determined empirically."

### M4. The dialectic oscillates between "Brading already went system-relative" and "Brading's bridge is missing"

**Location:** §1, §4.2, §5, §8.

**Evidence.** Reading A: "Brading's own reformulation and her interaction
passage support a system-relative existence reading" (abstract); "That is
system-relative existence, not the global restriction of P1" (§4.2). On Reading
A, the coverage objection is not an objection to Brading; it is an explanation
of why she had to move. The conclusion "dynamical individuation alone is
insufficient" then targets a view she does not hold, and "the global restriction
is not established" becomes "she does not try to establish it," which is not a
criticism.

Reading B: Brading presents P1\* as "a version of presentism that endorses (P2)"
(2013, p. 1110) and says "a sufficient core remains to warrant the label"
(2013, p. 1101) — she intends P1\* as a reformulation of presentism, not an
abandonment of the existential claim. On Reading B, the coverage objection is a
real criticism, but the paper cannot also assert that her text "points to
system-relative existence"; that is a rival reading it must argue against.

The paper needs one primary line. I recommend B, with P1\*\* as the target: she
claims (a) suffices to avoid the block; it does not secure the restriction. Then
A becomes a fallback diagnosis: if the system-relative reading is right, the
label is the only issue left and the view is repair 4. That structure is
coherent and gives the paper a real critical edge.

### M5. The formal setup uses a domain Brading does not accept

See §2 above. `W` is a set of events in a single world history; Brading's
fundamental entities are genuine unities ("what exists (what is real) are the
genuine unities," 2015, p. 17), and she denies determinate spatiotemporal
relations between systems (2013, p. 1109 n. 7). The paper must either justify
the event regimentation as a neutral representation (and say what it assumes) or
restate the coverage argument over unities and their extents. Otherwise the
objection may be an artifact of the formalism.

**Fix.** Add a short paragraph in §2: "For regimentation we treat the world
history as a set of events, as the block is represented in Minkowski spacetime
(Brading 2015, p. 17, condition (a)). This is a representational choice; if
Brading's unities are not events, the argument is to be read as a claim about
the spatiotemporal extents of unities." Then check that the coverage conclusion
survives the translation.

### M6. Coverage is never established; "exact" overclaims

The non-implication is exact. The objection needs the further premise that the
family of actual system-presents covers `W`, which the paper states only
conditionally. Either argue coverage — the n. 3/n. 6 route, or P1\*\* (a) plus the
plurality of things — or state the conclusion as a burden-shift and remove
"exact" from the abstract and §8 where it is applied to the objection rather
than the non-implication.

### M7. The repairs section misses the most natural dynamical privilege principle and the primitive-actuality reply

See §5 above. The maximal-interacting-unity candidate is in Brading's own text
(2013, p. 1110; n. 3). The primitive-actuality reply is required by
`REFRAME.md` §7 and is absent. Repair 2's cost claim ("not derived from the
physics or from dynamical unity") is therefore too strong as stated.

### M8. §6 delivers a schema, not a favoring verdict

See §6 above. The paper can state a parity verdict ("the physics does not favor
presentism; any advantage is bought with an actuality premise") or cut the
section. The current sentences "The conclusion is not that the evidence is
silent" and "That is a substantive comparative verdict" are contradicted by the
application.

### M9. §5 localizes the weak/strong dilemma rather than resolving it

The calibration research result replaced a uniform checklist with a four-row
table keyed to inference type (contradiction, likelihood, explanatory
preference, admissibility) and claimed the dilemma is resolved by that
replacement. The paper keeps only the admissibility row, without the table,
without an argument that Brading's case *is* an admissibility judgment, and
without a second application (LAGEOS is cut). So the section does not resolve
the dilemma; it narrows the paper until the dilemma no longer bites. That is a
legitimate move, but then §5 should be two sentences and the eligibility/
discrimination distinction should either be applied to Brading (is "genuine
subsystems exist" background `D_0` or discriminating `D_1`?) or cut. As it
stands, §5 is the methodological residue the first reviewer criticized, in
compressed form.

### M10. §7 slides from existence to passage and concedes Saunders by relocation

See §7 above. Pooley's challenge is about objective passage; Brading makes no

## 7. Answers to the assigned checks

**Brading reconstruction and the coverage/union objection.** The
reconstruction of 2013 is accurate and charitable on unity, epistemic role,
present and defeat condition. The union point is correct as a non-implication,
and the overlap/disjointness clarifications are correct. But the objection is
aimed at the wrong formulation (P1\*, not P1\*\*), coverage itself is never
established, and `W` is not Brading's domain. The objection is therefore
correct but under-targeted and under-argued.

**P1/P1\* distinction and the claim that Brading's text supports
system-relative existence.** The distinction between P1 and P1\* is formally
real. The inference to system-relative existence is not supported: P1\* is about
the extent of each thing's present (presence/endurance), and the interaction
passage is a single-domain claim about interaction and determinacy. The paper's
own four-way distinction undercuts its headline reading. The P1\* quote is
truncated in a way that helps the reading.

**The four repairs and their costs.** A useful map, but not yet a cost analysis
of Brading's position: 1 and 4 are not repairs of P1; 2's cost ignores the
maximal-unity candidate in Brading's own text; 3 is under-specified; the
primitive-actuality reply is missing. Repair 4 is probably where Brading
actually is, and the paper should say so.

**Burden calibration.** It does not resolve the weak/strong dilemma; it
localizes it. The paper keeps the admissibility row of the calibration table
without the table, the classification argument, or a second application. The
dilemma no longer bites because the paper's thesis is now narrow, not because
the calibration has been vindicated. Cut §5 to two sentences or make it do work
on Brading (eligibility vs discrimination).

**Favoring account.** A schema, not a verdict. The example is the first
reviewer's; the application yields a tie and a conditional advantage. The
legitimate verdict is parity/underdetermination ("the physics does not favor
presentism; any advantage requires an actuality premise"), and the paper should
state it plainly or cut §6.

**Saunders/Pooley.** Saunders is reconstructed at reasonable strength (better
than "structural completeness"), but "non-trivial" is the paper's addition and
the response is a horn-mapping that does not engage Saunders's public-uniqueness
reason. Pooley is paraphrased accurately, but the application slides from
existence to passage and treats Brading as a candidate for a route she never
takes. Both are conceded rather than answered, which is honest but leaves the
paper's contribution purely negative.

**Overclaims, missing premises, citation accuracy.** Overclaims: "supports a
system-relative existence reading" (abstract, §4.2, §5, §8); "the coverage
objection is exact" (abstract, §8); "not that the evidence is silent" and "a
substantive comparative verdict" (§6). Missing premises: coverage; the single
world history; the event domain; the claim that dynamical unity supplies no
selection principle; the applicability of Saunders's and Pooley's challenges to
Brading. Citation accuracy: P1\* truncation (major); 2015 read-depth
contradiction; Saunders "non-trivial"; Clifton–Hogarth uncited; Stein dimension
clause and prose-premise locator; dangling "accompanying note."

**Substantive but limited conclusion.** The conclusion is substantive in form
and appropriately limited, but it is only substantive if it is established
against P1\*\*. Against P1\*, it restates a limitation Brading identified in 2015
and went beyond. Against P1\*\*, it is a real negative result about her stated
sufficient condition.

## 8. What would make this a contribution

The paper would be a modest, publishable contribution if it did the following.

1. **Retarget at P1\*\*.** Reconstruct P1\* and P1\*\* from 2013 and 2015 (p. 17),
   quote both in full, and show that (a) is local boundedness and (b) is an
   extent claim. Then the coverage argument defeats Brading's own stated
   sufficient condition ("all we need is (a)"), not a formulation she has
   already moved past.
2. **Withdraw or defend the system-relative existence attribution.** The
   better-supported line is that her own gloss concedes the rest of the world is
   as real as we are, so the existential restriction is not in her text. That
   makes the verdict stronger, not weaker.
3. **State the coverage premise and either argue it or downgrade "exact."**
   The n. 3 (totality as unity) and n. 6 (short-lived things) passages give the
   author the materials for an argument that the union covers `W`, not merely
   that it can.
4. **Regiment the domain explicitly.** Say that `W` is the block as represented
   in Minkowski spacetime, or restate the argument over unities and extents.
   Address n. 7.
5. **Fix the P1\* quotation.**
6. **Cut or convert §5 and §6.** A two-sentence burden paragraph and a parity
   verdict are enough. The current sections are residue from the old paper and
   invite the same review.
7. **Address the maximal-unity principle and the primitive-actuality reply.**
   Both are in Brading's text or in the presentist's standard repertoire; the
   paper's "not derived from physics" claim is too quick without them.
8. **Reframe Pooley as a precondition, not a horn Brading falls into; mark the
   Saunders "non-trivial" strengthening.**
9. **Correct the read-depth note** (2015 full text) and remove the dangling
   reference.

With 1–5 the paper would be a real contribution. With 6–9 it would be a clean
one.

## 9. Recommendation

**Major revision.** Not acceptable as submitted; not a reject, because the core
argument is sound and the fix is specific. If the author is unwilling to engage
P1\*\* and to defend or withdraw the system-relative-existence attribution, the
paper should be rejected: in that form it argues against a version its target
has already moved past, and the first reviewer's verdict would recur in a new
form.

The paper has crossed the line from prospectus to argument. It has not yet
crossed the line from argument to contribution. The distance between the two is
one section of Brading 2015 and one honest sentence about what her own gloss
concedes.
