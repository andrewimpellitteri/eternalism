## 1. Verdict

**Needs revision.** The argument form is valid: if the universe is an existing
thing and its sustaining region is the whole history `W`, then P1\*\* (a) is
false of it. The draft has also found a real tension in Brading’s texts: the
p. 16 claim of scope “all the way up to the material universe as a whole,” the
p. 17 universal quantifier “for each and every thing that exists,” and the p. 17
admission that a system’s region “might turn out to be the entire
spatiotemporal history of the universe” do not sit comfortably together.

But the paper’s two load-bearing premises are asserted, not established, and
its dilemma misreads two of Brading’s terms.

1. **T1 is not a textual entailment.** “Applies all the way up to the material
   universe as a whole” gives the principle’s *scope*, not the universe’s
   *unity*. The p. 16 aggregation sentence is a condition on a collection’s
   forming a unity, not a statement that the universe satisfies it. The
   “insofar as” at p. 16 is a genuine conditional; p. 17’s “we begin from the
   premise” shows Brading adopts the identification as a working premise, not
   that she has established it (she says at p. 16 that she has not argued for
   the law-constitutive approach). For an internal reductio that is enough — but
   the paper should say so, not claim the text “removes the doubt.”
2. **T4 is not sound as stated.** “Necessary to sustain” is a dynamical notion,
   not “contains all members.” A closed system’s evolution is determined by its
   state on a Cauchy slice; that slice is a proper region, and Brading’s
   rejection at p. 17 is of “a spatially global present **common to all
   systems**,” not of the universe’s own global slice. The paper’s dilemma is
   also not exhaustive, and it equivocates on “local”: Brading uses “local” for
   *any* present “less than the extent of the entire ‘block’” (p. 17), and she
   explicitly says her local now “does not lead to solipsism” (2013, p. 1110).
   The paper’s §8 concedes that a proper local region could make (a) true,
   which contradicts the categorical conclusion of §3.
3. **The 2013 and 2015 criticisms attack conditions Brading states as
   sufficient and necessary, not as exhaustive and sufficient respectively.**
   The 2013 condition is sufficient for defeat; the 2015 condition is necessary
   for survival. The paper needs to show that Brading treats them as the
   decisive questions, not merely that they are not logically exhaustive. The
   2015 framing does provide that evidence (“the crucial question would
   remain”), but the draft does not use it.
4. **The four-event model is valid but models the paper’s formalisation, not
   Brading’s conditions.** It drops “necessary to sustain,” assumes a common
   event domain `W`, and treats (a) and (b) as extensional set constraints.
   Brading’s 2013 n. 7 explicitly denies determinate ontological
   spatiotemporal relations between systems. The draft’s previous version
   addressed n. 7; this one does not.
5. **The “scale-free” diagnosis misdescribes Brading.** 2013 n. 3 explicitly
   allows “different principles of unity … for the different cases,” and 2015
   p. 18 says genuine subsystems are “as picked out by the dynamical laws.” She
   has a selection procedure, though no guarantee that it selects a proper
   scale. “Brading supplies neither” is false as stated.

The core idea is publishable as a short note **if** T4 is either defended with a
substantive account of “sustain” and the common domain, or the result is
reframed as a trilemma (P1\*\* false of the universe / quantifier restricted /
universe not a unity / proper sustaining region) and the categorical reductio is
dropped. As written, the paper is a strong draft of a note, not a finished
refutation.

## 2. Q1: Is T1 sound? Does “applies all the way up” entail unity and existence?

**Answer: no, not as stated.** The argument form is valid, but T1 is an
interpretation presented as a textual entailment.

### 2.1 The exact sentences

Brading 2015, p. 16:

> “We are being offered a principle of ontological unity that applies across the
> board to simple things, composite systems, and component parts of those
> systems, and which applies all the way up to the material universe as a whole.
> Insofar as what exists is what is one, the law-constitutive approach to unity
> is a metaphysical thesis about what exists.”

Brading 2015, p. 17:

> “we begin from the premise that what exists (what is real) are the genuine
> unities, and we offer a law-constitutive approach to determining what these
> entities are.”

Brading 2015, p. 16, two sentences earlier:

> “I have not argued for the law-constitutive approach to unity here, nor for its
> basis in Newton’s work. For that I refer to Brading (2011, 2012, 2013).”

Brading 2015, p. 16, on aggregation:

> “By aggregation I mean ‘If the world contains a multiplicity of genuine
> unities, how do they together form a whole, rather than being merely a
> collection?’ The members must stand in relations to one another, and those
> relations must be necessary and sufficient for the collection to form a
> unity.”

Brading 2013, p. 1105 n. 3:

> “I use this phrase neutrally here to cover the unity of simple things,
> composite things, and the totality of existing things. Different principles of
> unity might be used for the different cases. One feature of the
> law-constitutive approach discussed here is that it applies to all three.”

### 2.2 Assessment

The draft’s T1 conjoins two different claims:

- (T1a) “What exists are the genuine unities.”
- (T1b) “the law-constitutive principle applies to the material universe as a
  whole.”

(T1a) is not the p. 16 sentence. The p. 16 sentence is explicitly conditional:
“**Insofar as** what exists is what is one, the law-constitutive approach to
unity is a metaphysical thesis about what exists.” The unconditional-sounding
formulation is at p. 17: “we begin from the premise that what exists (what is
real) are the genuine unities.” So the draft’s citation of p. 16 for the whole
of T1 is imprecise: the p. 16 clause is a hedge, and the p. 17 clause is a
working premise. The draft’s claim that “the surrounding text removes the doubt”
(l. 119) is half right. It shows that Brading adopts the identification as a
premise for her argument; it does not show that she has established it, and she
says at p. 16 that she has not argued for the law-constitutive approach at all.
For an internal reductio, adoption is enough. The paper should say that
explicitly — “granting Brading’s identification” — rather than claim the text
removes the doubt. That would be both more accurate and dialectically stronger.

(T1b) is supported by p. 16, but “applies all the way up to the material
universe as a whole” gives the principle’s **scope**, not the universe’s
**unity**. A principle of unity can apply to a collection and deliver the verdict
that the collection is not one. On Brading’s own aggregation condition, a
collection is a unity only if “the members must stand in relations to one
another, and those relations must be necessary and sufficient for the collection
to form a unity” (p. 16). That is a condition, not an assertion that the universe
satisfies it. The draft’s T4 says “The universe’s unity is constituted by the
relations among its members (p. 16)” (l. 163–164). That is not what p. 16 says.
P. 16 states a necessary condition on aggregation; it does not say the universe
is a unity or that its unity is constituted by relations. The draft’s citation
is a non sequitur.

The strongest textual support for the universe’s unity is elsewhere:

- 2013, p. 1110: “The dynamics grounds the unity of what there is, both of the
  parts and of the whole (consisting of interacting parts).”
- 2013, p. 1105 n. 3: the law-constitutive approach “applies to all three,”
  including “the totality of existing things.”
- 2015, p. 16: the composition question “Are there any composite wholes less
  than the world as a whole?” — which at least treats the world as a whole as a
  composite whole.

But each has a qualifier the draft ignores. The p. 1110 “whole” is “consisting
of interacting parts,” not obviously the entire spatiotemporal history. The
2013 footnote immediately adds “Different principles of unity might be used for
the different cases.” The p. 16 composition question is a question, not an
assertion. So the inference from “applies to” to “is a genuine unity” needs a
further premise. The draft supplies it by assertion (“The universe is not a
collection of genuine unities that falls outside the principle; it is the
maximal case to which the principle is meant to apply,” l. 133–135), but not by
argument or quotation.

The same gap appears in the draft’s “A reader who accepts it must follow it to
the universe” (l. 146–147). Accepting “what exists are the genuine unities” does
not force accepting that the universe is a genuine unity; one can accept the
identification and hold that the universe is a collection whose members do not
satisfy the aggregation condition. The draft needs to argue that the universe
does satisfy it, or that the law-constitutive account’s isolation/conservation
condition applies to the universe (2013, p. 1105: “to be a physical thing, be it
simple or composite, is (in part) to conserve total quantity of motion (when
isolated from other physical things)”; 2015, p. 16: a composite whole is a unity
“when that system as a whole is free from external interactions”). That route is
available and would strengthen T1, but the draft does not take it.

**Verdict on Q1.** T1 is a defensible interpretation but not a sound textual
premise as stated. “Applies all the way up” does not entail unity; unity plus
the identification entails existence only if the identification is read as an
identity (which p. 17 supports as a premise, p. 16 hedges). The minimal repair is
to mark T1 as an internal premise and to add a separate, defended premise that
the universe satisfies Brading’s condition for being a genuine unity.

## 3. Q2: Is T4 sound? Must the universe’s sustaining region be `W`?

**Answer: no.** T4 is the weakest premise, as the draft itself concedes at
l. 403. The text does not require `W`, and the draft’s dilemma does not exhaust
the alternatives.

### 3.1 The exact sentences

Brading 2015, p. 17:

> “The proposal is that the present is a spatiotemporal region of whatever size
> is needed to sustain the dynamical system in question. It might turn out that
> this is the entire spatiotemporal history of the universe. If this is the case,
> we are back to the block universe and there is nothing in the structure of the
> physical theory in question that can be correlated with anything we would want
> to call ‘present’.”

Brading 2015, p. 17, defining “local”:

> “For example, such a ‘present’ is local, in being a spacetime region less than
> the extent of the entire ‘block’.”

Brading 2015, p. 17, on relativity of present:

> “This comes about because being present is relative to the dynamical system in
> question: it is localized to the spatiotemporal region necessary to sustain
> that system and is not transitive among systems and their subsystems.”

Brading 2015, p. 17, on the global present:

> “Thus, the idea of a spatially global present common to all systems is not
> preserved in this version of presentism, but the rejection of a spatially
> global present is one of the central lessons of special relativity, one that
> any presentist should accept, so this is all to the good.”

Brading 2013, p. 1109 n. 7:

> “There are no determinate ontological spatial or temporal relations within that
> region, and that system stands in no determinate ontological spatial or
> temporal relations to any other system. All the ontology is carried by the
> dynamics, and we frame the dynamics spatiotemporally, but ontologically, the
> dynamics requires no such spatiotemporal underpinning.”

Brading 2013, p. 1105:

> “to be a physical thing, be it simple or composite, is (in part) to conserve
> total quantity of motion (when isolated from other physical things).”

Brading 2015, p. 16:

> “From the law-constitutive perspective, such a collection of interacting bodies
> forms a composite whole in virtue of conserving its total quantity of motion
> and its overall direction of motion (the direction of motion of the center of
> mass of the collection). Thus, conservation of quantity and direction of motion
> can be understood as a principle of unity for a composite system, when that
> system as a whole is free from external interactions.”

### 3.2 “Necessary to sustain” is not “contains all members”

The draft’s T4 argues: the universe’s members are spread through the history; a
region that omitted any of them would omit part of what the universe is; so the
region necessary to sustain the universe is `W` (l. 162–166). This conflates two
notions:

- **Containment**: the region contains all the universe’s members (and their
  relations).
- **Sustaining**: the region whose dynamical data are needed to sustain the
  system’s evolution.

Brading’s phrase is the second. For a closed system, the state on a Cauchy slice
plus the laws determines the whole history; the slice is a proper region, and it
is spatially global. It is not the whole 4D history. If “necessary to sustain”
means “minimal determining data,” the universe’s present is a slice, not `W`.
If it means “the region in which the unity is realized,” the argument for `W`
needs the containment premise. The draft asserts the containment premise; it
does not argue that Brading’s “sustain” is containment. The p. 16 aggregation
sentence does not supply it: it is a condition on a collection’s forming a
unity, not a statement that the sustaining region must contain all the relata.

The draft’s own §8 concedes the point:

> “The weakest premise is (T4). A defender might say that the universe’s unity is
> sustained by a proper region that is neither a global slice nor a point: a
> local patch whose laws determine the rest. If such a region exists, (a) could
> be true of the universe. … the note does not claim that no such account can be
> given. It claims that Brading does not give one” (l. 403–412).

That concession is fatal to the categorical conclusion of §3 (“clause (a) is
false of it,” l. 171–173; abstract, l. 28–29). If the most the paper establishes
is that Brading does not give an account of a proper sustaining region, the
conclusion is “Brading’s text leaves the universe’s region undetermined,” not
“the universe’s region is `W`.” The paper cannot have it both ways.

### 3.3 The “spatially global” horn

The draft says: “If it is proper, it is either spatially global or local. A
spatially global present is rejected by Brading” (l. 176–178). The quoted
sentence has two clauses, and the first is qualified:

> “the idea of a spatially global present **common to all systems** is not
> preserved in this version of presentism, but the rejection of a spatially
> global present is one of the central lessons of special relativity.”

The draft quotes both clauses but does not address the qualifier. A spatially
global present for the universe alone is not “common to all systems” if
subsystems have smaller, non-transitive presents (p. 17: present “is not
transitive among systems and their subsystems”). So the qualifier leaves open
exactly the horn the draft needs to close: the universe’s present could be a
global slice (or a thick global slab), while subsystem presents are local and
non-transitive. The second clause (“the rejection of a spatially global
present”) is more general, but its target is the presentist’s global now, not the
universe’s own sustaining region; the draft needs an argument that the general
clause covers the latter. It does not give one.

A related point: Brading says the present is “extended in time just as it is
extended in space, precisely because it is extended in spacetime” (p. 17). A
three-dimensional Cauchy slice is not her paradigm of a present; a thick
spacelike slab is. But a thick slab is still a proper region, and it is
spatially global. The draft’s dichotomy “spatially global or local” does not
distinguish a thick global slab from a thin slice or a local patch. The
alternatives are richer than the draft’s two horns.

### 3.4 The “local” equivocation

The draft’s second horn is a textual error. It says:

> “A local or point-like present is the position she associates with Stein’s
> ‘peculiarly extreme (but pluralistic!) form of solipsism’ (2013, p. 1102), and
> she says her view avoids it” (l. 181–183).

But Brading’s own usage is explicit: a present “is local, in being a spacetime
region less than the extent of the entire ‘block’” (2015, p. 17). On that usage,
**every proper present is local**. So “a proper region that is neither global
nor local” (l. 184) is empty: if it is proper, it is local in her sense. The
draft’s third horn is not a further option; it is a contradiction in terms on
Brading’s definition.

Conversely, the draft’s “local or point-like” amalgam conflates two different
things. Brading rejects the *point* present:

> “we will not be driven by our considerations of the structure of Minkowski
> space-time to the conclusion that the present is merely a point in Minkowski
> space-time and that therefore this is all that is real. In other words, since
> we are not using a ‘now’ derived from the structure of Minkowski space-time to
> ground what is real, we do not end up at pluralistic extreme solipsism”
> (2013, p. 1109).

And she explicitly says her *local* present does not lead to solipsism:

> “So let us begin from the position that there are genuine subsystems. This
> means that the size of the spatiotemporal region required to sustain the
> system is less than the entire block universe, and so the present is (at least
> in the first instance) local, not global. This ‘local now’ does not lead to
> solipsism, however, because it is not the ground of what is real” (2013,
> p. 1110).

The solipsism is the point present grounded in Minkowski “now” (2013, p. 1103:
“nothing is real with respect to e1 except e1 itself (hence the extreme
solipsism), although each event is real with respect to itself (hence the
pluralism)”). The local region is Brading’s own position. The draft’s
“local or point-like” is therefore a false amalgam, and the horn it uses to
exclude a proper local present is excluded by Brading’s text.

### 3.5 Does the “members must stand in relations” sentence require `W`?

No. The sentence is:

> “The members must stand in relations to one another, and those relations must
> be necessary and sufficient for the collection to form a unity” (2015, p. 16).

It is a condition on a collection’s forming a unity. It does not say:

- that the universe satisfies the condition;
- that the sustaining region must contain all the members;
- that the relations are spatiotemporal (Brading denies that spacetime is the
  ontological principle of unity: 2013, pp. 1104–1108; 2015, p. 17);
- that the relations span the whole history.

If the relations are dynamical, they may be local; if they are spatiotemporal,
they are not the ground of unity on Brading’s account. The draft’s “a region that
omitted any of them would omit part of what the universe is” (l. 165–166)
assumes a container/sum model: the universe is the sum of its members, so
omitting a member omits part of it. That is not Brading’s law-constitutive
model, in which the laws constitute the unity. A referee will say the draft has
substituted a mereological container for Brading’s dynamical account.

### 3.6 The 2013 n. 7 problem

The draft formalises presents as subsets of `W` (`U_i ⊊ W`) and speaks of the
universe’s members as “spread through the history.” But 2013 n. 7 says:

> “There are no determinate ontological spatial or temporal relations within that
> region, and that system stands in no determinate ontological spatial or
> temporal relations to any other system. All the ontology is carried by the
> dynamics, and we frame the dynamics spatiotemporally, but ontologically, the
> dynamics requires no such spatiotemporal underpinning.”

On that view, `W` is the epistemic representation (“we frame the dynamics
spatiotemporally”), not the ontological container. The claim that the universe’s
members are “spread through the history” and related within `W` is a claim about
the representation. The draft’s previous version addressed n. 7 and said the
representation is “the common frame in which the comparison is stated, not a
claim that the systems stand in determinate ontological relations.” The new
draft drops that discussion entirely. That is a regression. A referee who knows
2013 will ask whether the whole formalisation is an artifact of the epistemic
frame. The draft needs either to defend the common domain (and explain why n. 7
does not undermine it) or to restrict the conclusion to the representation.

**Verdict on Q2.** T4 is not sound as stated. The universe’s sustaining region
need not be `W` on Brading’s text: a Cauchy slice or thick global slab is a
proper, spatially global region, and the p. 17 rejection is qualified by “common
to all systems.” The p. 16 aggregation sentence is a condition, not a premise
about the universe. The draft’s dilemma is not exhaustive and equivocates on
“local.” The §8 concession contradicts the categorical conclusion.

## 4. Q3: Is the 2013 defeat-condition criticism correct?

**Answer: the distinction is correct, but the charge of “incompleteness” is too
strong as phrased, and the section’s opening sentence is wrong.**

### 4.1 The exact passage

Brading 2013, p. 1109:

> “It might turn out that, for whatever system we try to consider, the size of
> the spatiotemporal region necessary to sustain it is the entire history of the
> universe, encompassing all that ever has been and ever will be. In other
> words, the only dynamical system that there is, is this entirety, and there
> are no genuine subsystems of the universe. If that turns out to be the case,
> then this version of presentism is defeated and the block universe triumphs.”

### 4.2 The quantifier and the “in other words”

“For whatever system we try to consider” is a universal quantifier over systems.
So the “in other words” is valid in the forward direction:

- If every system’s sustaining region is `W`, then no system has a proper
  region, so there are no genuine subsystems other than the entirety.

It is not valid as an equivalence:

- “There are no genuine subsystems” does not entail that every system’s region
  is `W`; the only system might have a proper region.

So if Brading intends “in other words” as a restatement, it is one-directional
and not an equivalence. The draft could make this a stronger textual criticism
than it does. As written, it says the passage “addresses one scenario” and “the
defeat condition does not cover this case.” That is true of the antecedent, but
it is a criticism of a *sufficient* condition for not being exhaustive. Brading
does not say the condition is exhaustive. The draft needs to show that she
treats it as the decisive empirical question, not merely that it is not the only
logical route to defeat.

### 4.3 The 2015 framing supplies the missing premise

The 2015 text does treat subsystem existence as the decisive question:

> “Even were this approach to succeed, the crucial question would remain of
> whether there are genuine subsystems of the world, as picked out by the
> dynamical laws, that are smaller in spatiotemporal extent than the entirety of
> spacetime. Only if there are such can the version of presentism offered here
> survive” (2015, p. 18).

And 2013, p. 1110:

> “A great deal rests on whether there are genuine subsystems of the universe,
> and that is something that we can find out only through empirical inquiry.”

The draft quotes the “only if” sentence but not “the crucial question” or “a
great deal rests.” It should. The criticism is then about the **framing**: Brading
presents the existence of subsystems as the empirical question on which the view
turns, while the universe-own-region scenario would be a distinct case that does
not depend on subsystems. That is a fair and substantial criticism. The current
wording (“the defeat condition is incomplete”) suggests a logical defect in a
sufficient condition, which is not the same point.

### 4.4 The opening sentence is wrong

The section begins: “Brading’s 2013 defeat condition makes the same point in a
different form” (l. 205). That is false. The 2013 condition is about **every**
system’s region being `W`; the draft’s point is about the **universe’s** region
being `W` while subsystems have proper regions. Those are different scenarios.
The next paragraph says so explicitly. Delete or rewrite the opening:
“The 2013 defeat condition is a different scenario, and it is not exhaustive.”

### 4.5 Three scenarios to keep distinct

1. **Universal scenario:** ∀ systems, region = `W` → no proper subsystems →
   defeat (Brading’s stated sufficient condition).
2. **Universe-own-region scenario:** universe’s region = `W`, subsystems proper
   → not covered by the antecedent; P1\*\* false of the universe **if** the
   universe is in the domain.
3. **Subsystem-only scenario:** universe excluded or not a unity; subsystems
   proper → P1\*\* restricted; global claim given up.

The draft conflates (1) and (2) in its opening, then distinguishes them in the
body. Fix the framing and the section becomes a genuine contribution.

**Verdict on Q3.** The distinction between the universal scenario and the
universe-own-region scenario is correct and worth keeping. The “incomplete”
charge needs restatement: the 2013 condition is sufficient, not exhaustive; the
“in other words” is one-directional; the criticism should target Brading’s
framing of subsystem existence as the decisive empirical question, using p. 18
(“the crucial question”) and p. 1110 (“A great deal rests…”).

## 5. Q4: The 2015 survival condition and the quantifier over “things”

**Answer: “necessary but not sufficient” is logically correct, but the draft
attacks a claim Brading does not make. The quantifier question is the real
crux, and the draft states it too strongly in the body.**

### 5.1 The exact sentence

Brading 2015, p. 18:

> “Only if there are such can the version of presentism offered here survive.”

This asserts a necessary condition for survival: survival → genuine subsystems.
It does not assert sufficiency. The draft says:

> “The survival condition is necessary for the local presents of subsystems; it
> is not sufficient for the truth of P1\*\*, because P1\*\* also applies to the
> universe” (l. 231–234).

That is logically correct, but it is not a criticism of the conditional. Brading
never says the condition is sufficient. The criticism should be:

> “Brading presents the existence of genuine subsystems as the crucial empirical
> question on which the version’s survival turns. But even if there are such
> subsystems, P1\*\* is false of the universe on the unrestricted reading. Her
> necessary condition is therefore not the question on which the truth of
> P1\*\* turns, even though it may be the question on which the local presents
> of subsystems turn.”

That is the same point without attacking a straw man.

### 5.2 Does P1\*\* apply to the universe?

The text supports the unrestricted reading:

- “For each and every thing that exists” (2015, p. 17).
- “what exists (what is real) are the genuine unities” (p. 17).
- “applies all the way up to the material universe as a whole” (p. 16).
- “the totality of existing things” is covered by the law-constitutive approach
  (2013, p. 1105 n. 3).

But a defender can read “thing” as a term of art for proper unities below the
whole. The textual support for that restriction is weaker but not absent:

- “genuine subsystems of the world … smaller in spatiotemporal extent than the
  entirety of spacetime” (2015, p. 18).
- “Are there any composite wholes less than the world as a whole?” (2015,
  p. 16) — the world as a whole is the limit case.
- “the whole (consisting of interacting parts)” (2013, p. 1110) — the whole may
  be the interacting whole, not the entire history.

The draft’s §5 treats the restriction as a repair that gives up the global
claim, which is the right move. But §3 and §4 assert the unrestricted reading as
fact:

> “P1\*\*, which quantifies over every thing that exists, is false” (l. 172–173).
> “the universe is a counterexample whether or not subsystems exist” (l. 239–240).

The conclusion’s hedged form is better:

> “P1\*\* is either false of the universe or restricted in a way that gives up
> the global presentist claim” (abstract, l. 39–40; conclusion, l. 423–424).

The body should match the conclusion. As written, the paper establishes a fork,
not a falsity. That is not a fatal objection — the fork is the interesting
result — but the paper’s headline (“the universe as a thing”; “clause (a) is
false of it”) outruns it.

**Verdict on Q4.** The “necessary but not sufficient” description is correct, but
the criticism is misdirected. The real criticism is that Brading treats
subsystem existence as the decisive empirical question while the universe case
is independent of it. The quantifier question is genuinely open; the paper should
present it as the central interpretive fork and make the body’s conclusion match
the abstract’s “either/or.”

## 6. Q5: The retreat to subsystems and the four-event model

**Answer: the quantifier restriction follows, but the “two costs” are not
independent as stated. The four-event model is valid as a formal non-implication,
and its histories/presents are coherent, but it models the paper’s formalisation,
not Brading’s conditions.**

### 6.1 The quantifier restriction

If the universe is excluded, P1\*\*’s “each and every thing” is restricted, and
the global claim is weakened. The draft’s “A restricted quantifier is a
different thesis, not a reading of the original” (l. 254–255) is defensible. The
text’s “each and every thing” is strong evidence for the unrestricted reading,
but not conclusive: “thing” may be a term of art. The draft should acknowledge
that the restriction is a repair, not a refutation, and that its cost is the
loss of the global claim. It does.

### 6.2 The “two costs” are not independent

The draft says:

> “A defender who restricts the quantifier to avoid the first falls into the
> second” (l. 313–314).

That is wrong as stated. A defender who restricts the quantifier has already
given up the global claim; the coverage argument is then moot for her. The
coverage argument bites a defender who **keeps** the global claim but tries to
secure it via (a) and (b) for subsystems. The correct statement is:

- A defender who restricts the quantifier gives up the global claim (cost 1).
- A defender who keeps the global claim cannot restrict, and must answer the
  universe case (cost 2 is not available).
- A defender who tries to secure the global claim through (a) and (b) for
  subsystems needs the missing joint constraint (cost 3).

The three options are distinct. The draft’s “falls into the second” collapses
them.

### 6.3 The four-event model

The model:

- `W = {a,b,c,d}`
- `H_1 = {a,b,c}`, `U_1 = {a,b}`
- `H_2 = {b,c,d}`, `U_2 = {c,d}`

Checks:

- `U_1 ⊊ W`, `U_2 ⊊ W`: (a) holds.
- `U_1 ⊊ H_1`, `U_2 ⊊ H_2`: (b) holds.
- `U_1 ∪ U_2 = W`: coverage.

The histories overlap at `b,c`; the presents are disjoint; each present is a
proper part of its own history. The model is coherent, and (b) is **not**
trivially violated by the thing’s history: `U_1` is a proper part of `H_1`, and
`U_2` is a proper part of `H_2`. The proposition is valid as a formal
non-implication.

But the model has three limitations the draft does not fully state:

1. **It drops “necessary to sustain.”** The model stipulates `U_i`; it does not
   show that `U_i` is the region necessary to sustain system `i`. The draft
   admits this (“The model is set-theoretic, not dynamical,” l. 294), but then
   says “if (a) and (b) are offered as the conditions that exclude the block, a
   model that satisfies them and covers the history shows that they do not, by
   themselves, exclude it” (l. 295–297). That is true only if (a) and (b) are
   read as the bare set constraints. Brading’s (a) and (b) are dynamical
   conditions; the model is a model of their extensional shadow, not of the
   conditions.
2. **It assumes a common event domain `W`.** 2013 n. 7 denies determinate
   ontological spatiotemporal relations between systems. If `W` is the epistemic
   representation, the model shows that the representation can be covered; it
   does not show that the ontology is a block. The draft needs to state and
   defend the domain assumption.
3. **It treats (a) and (b) as extensional set constraints.** “Less than” is an
   extent comparison; `⊊` is a regimenting choice. The draft should mark it as
   such, as the previous version did.

The model is a clean formal point and worth keeping, but its significance is
limited: it shows that per-item properness is coverage-neutral, not that
Brading’s conditions fail to exclude the block. The draft’s own sentence “A
defender who says that the model is not law-constituted must supply the further
dynamical condition” (l. 297–299) is the right burden-shift; it should be the
conclusion, not a footnote to a stronger claim.

**Verdict on Q5.** The quantifier restriction follows; the “two costs” need
separation. The four-event model is valid and coherent, and (b) is not trivially
violated, but it models the formalisation rather than Brading’s dynamical
conditions. Keep it as a formal non-implication with explicit assumptions.

## 7. Q6: Is the “scale-free criterion” diagnosis sound?

**Answer: no, it misdescribes Brading in two respects. She has a selection
procedure, and 2013 n. 3 explicitly allows different principles for different
cases.**

### 7.1 The exact texts

2013, p. 1105 n. 3:

> “I use this phrase neutrally here to cover the unity of simple things,
> composite things, and the totality of existing things. Different principles of
> unity might be used for the different cases. One feature of the
> law-constitutive approach discussed here is that it applies to all three.”

2015, p. 18:

> “genuine subsystems of the world, as picked out by the dynamical laws, that are
> smaller in spatiotemporal extent than the entirety of spacetime.”

2013, p. 1105:

> “to be a physical thing, be it simple or composite, is (in part) to conserve
> total quantity of motion (when isolated from other physical things).”

2015, p. 16:

> “a composite whole in virtue of conserving its total quantity of motion and its
> overall direction of motion … when that system as a whole is free from external
> interactions.”

### 7.2 Assessment

The draft says:

> “The problem is that the criterion is scale-free. It applies to simple things,
> composite systems, component parts, and the material universe as a whole. A
> criterion that confers existence on every level of unity cannot, by itself,
> select a local level as the level of existence-conferring presents. It confers
> existence on the maximal unity too” (l. 321–326).

Two problems.

First, “scale-free” is the draft’s reconstruction, not Brading’s stated view.
2013 n. 3 says “Different principles of unity might be used for the different
cases.” That is a licence for scale-dependence, not a commitment to one
scale-free criterion. The draft quotes the first sentence of n. 3 for T1 but
omits the second, which is material here. A referee will notice the selective
quotation. The most the draft can say is that the **approach** is scale-general
(it applies to all three cases), not that one criterion applies uniformly at
every scale.

Second, Brading does have a selection procedure: genuine subsystems are “as
picked out by the dynamical laws” (p. 18), and the law-constitutive account
selects systems that conserve total quantity of motion when isolated (2013,
p. 1105; 2015, p. 16). The draft’s “Brading supplies neither” (l. 307–308) is
false as stated. She supplies a procedure, though not a guarantee that it selects
a proper scale. The correct diagnosis is:

> “The criterion is scale-general, and the dynamical laws may select proper
> subsystems, but Brading leaves the answer empirical. The missing premise is a
> guarantee that the procedure selects a proper scale, not a criterion of
> existence.”

That is a weaker but accurate criticism. It also connects to the 2013 defeat
condition: the defeat scenario is exactly the case where the dynamical laws pick
out no proper subsystem. The draft’s “the criterion applies at every scale, so
the maximal unity and its proper parts can both be genuine” (l. 350–351) assumes
that both satisfy the unity condition; the criterion does not confer unity
automatically. “Applies to” is not “confers existence on.”

**Verdict on Q6.** The diagnosis needs restatement. Brading’s approach is
scale-general, and she explicitly allows different principles for different
cases; she has a selection procedure (dynamical laws, isolation) but no guarantee
of a proper scale. Replace “scale-free” with “scale-general,” quote n. 3 in
full, and replace “Brading supplies neither” with the guarantee formulation.

## 8. Q7: Strongest referee rejection and minimal repairs

### 8.1 The strongest rejection

A referee who knows Brading will reject the paper in something like this form:

> “The paper’s central premise (T4) is not in Brading and is in tension with her
> account. ‘Necessary to sustain’ is a dynamical notion; for a closed system the
> sustaining data are a Cauchy slice, which is a proper region, and Brading
> explicitly leaves open whether a system’s region is the whole history (p. 17:
> ‘It might turn out that this is the entire spatiotemporal history of the
> universe’). The paper converts that open empirical possibility into an a priori
> necessity by assuming that the sustaining region must contain all members.
> But Brading’s law-constitutive account grounds unity in laws, not in the
> containment of all relata, and her 2013 n. 7 denies determinate spatiotemporal
> relations between systems. The dilemma also misreads ‘local’ (which Brading
> uses for any proper present, p. 17) and ‘spatially global’ (which she rejects
> as ‘common to all systems,’ not as the universe’s own slice), and its ‘neither
> global nor local’ horn is empty on her usage. The 2013 condition is a
> sufficient condition, and the 2015 condition is a necessary one; the paper
> criticizes them for not being exhaustive and sufficient, which Brading never
> claims. The four-event model is a model of the paper’s formalisation, not of
> Brading’s dynamics, and it presupposes the common event domain that n. 7
> denies. The paper’s own §8 concedes that a proper local region could make (a)
> true, which contradicts its categorical conclusion. What remains is a real and
> interesting tension, but it is a trilemma about the domain, not a reductio.”

That is the rejection the paper must answer. It is not a rejection of the
interest of the topic; it is a rejection of the paper’s claim to have established
a falsity.

### 8.2 Minimal repairs

1. **Reframe as an internal, conditional argument.** At l. 151, say “granting
   Brading’s identification…” and mark T1 and T4 as interpretive premises, not
   entailments. Delete “must follow it to the universe” (l. 147). The argument
   is stronger, not weaker, if it is presented as an internal reductio: it does
   not need the identification to be true, only adopted.
2. **Separate T1 into T1a (the identification, p. 17) and T1b (the universe is
   a genuine unity).** For T1b, use the aggregation condition plus either
   p. 1110 (“the whole (consisting of interacting parts)”) or the
   isolation/conservation condition (2013, p. 1105; 2015, p. 16). Address the
   “interacting” qualifier: is the material universe an interacting whole, or
   only a spatiotemporal totality? If the latter, T1b fails.
3. **Reframe T4.** Quote the full p. 17 “It might turn out…” passage. Distinguish
   “sustain” from “contain.” Add the Cauchy-slice/thick-slab horn and argue why
   it is not the sustaining region on Brading’s account, or concede it. Address
   2013 n. 7 and the epistemic status of `W`. Remove the categorical “clause (a)
   is false” until T4 is established; replace with “on the reading that the
   universe’s sustaining region is `W`…”
4. **Fix the dilemma.** Use Brading’s “local = proper” definition (p. 17);
   separate the point-present solipsism (2013, p. 1103, p. 1109) from the local
   present she endorses (p. 1110); distinguish “a spatially global present
   common to all systems” from “the universe’s own global slice”; add the “no
   present / not a dynamical system” horn. The dilemma is at least five options,
   not two.
5. **Fix §4’s framing.** Delete “makes the same point in a different form”
   (l. 205). Say the 2013 condition is sufficient, not exhaustive; distinguish
   the universal scenario, the universe-own-region scenario, and the
   subsystem-only scenario. Quote p. 18 “the crucial question” and p. 1110
   “A great deal rests…” to show the framing is the target.
6. **Fix §4’s 2015 paragraph.** Say Brading asserts necessity, not sufficiency;
   the criticism is that she treats subsystem existence as the decisive
   empirical question, not that her conditional is false. Make §3/§4’s “P1\*\*
   is false” match the abstract/conclusion’s “either false or restricted.”
7. **Fix §5.** Separate the three defender options; delete or qualify “falls
   into the second” (l. 313–314); label the four-event model as a formal
   non-implication under the bare set reading; state the common-domain
   assumption and n. 7.
8. **Fix §6.** Replace “scale-free” with “scale-general”; quote n. 3 in full
   (“Different principles…”); quote p. 18 “as picked out by the dynamical laws”;
   replace “Brading supplies neither” with “she supplies a selection procedure
   but no guarantee of a proper scale.”
9. **Fix locators and citations.** p. 1106 n. 3 → p. 1105 n. 3 (l. 133). T1
   p. 16 → pp. 16–17 (l. 154–156), since the unconditional “what exists are the
   genuine unities” is p. 17. If P1\* is quoted, cite 2013 p. 1110 for the
   “dependent on dynamics” wording and 2015 p. 17 for “dependent on the
   dynamics.” Check the Savitt citation against the live §3.3.
10. **Fix the abstract.** Replace “The alternatives … are each excluded by
    Brading’s text” (l. 29–31) with “the first two are not clearly available, and
    the third contradicts T1.” Replace “The universe is therefore a genuine
    unity” (l. 27–28) with “On Brading’s identification, if the universe is a
    genuine unity…” or mark the inference.
11. **Qualify the Savitt contrast.** Savitt’s universal premise (“every dynamical
    system that has existed, does exist or will exist had, has or will have a
    region”) applied to the universe as a dynamical system gives a nearby
    result. The delta is the target and the conclusion (P1\*\* and the
    universe’s own region vs. a semantic verdict about presentism), not the
    premise. Say so; do not claim “distinct” without the qualification.
12. **Qualify the interaction passage.** “Does my son exist relative to me…?”
    is a relativized formulation; the single-domain reading is supported by “we
    belong to the same world” and “the rest of that world is as real as we are,”
    but the question itself is not. The draft’s “better read” is contestable and
    should be presented as one reading, not the reading.

### 8.3 What is sound and should be kept

- The core tension is real. The combination of p. 16 scope, p. 17 universal
  quantifier, and p. 17 “might turn out to be the entire spatiotemporal history”
  is a genuine question about the universe.
- The quotation discipline is good. Every Brading quotation I checked is
  verbatim, apart from the p. 1106/p. 1105 locator error and the selective
  quotation of n. 3.
- The distinction between the universal scenario and the universe-own-region
  scenario is a real contribution, even if the framing is too strong.
- The four-event model is a clean formal non-implication; Proposition 1 is
  correctly proved.
- The refusal to claim Savitt’s objection is good; the abstract’s hedged
  conclusion is the right shape.
- The non-claims in §8 are appropriate.

### 8.4 Verdict

**Needs revision.** Publishable as a short note **if** the author either:

- (a) defends T4 with a substantive account of “sustain” and the common domain,
  including an answer to 2013 n. 7 and the Cauchy-slice alternative; or
- (b) reframes the result as a trilemma — P1\*\* false of the universe /
  quantifier restricted / universe not a unity / proper sustaining region —
  and drops the categorical reductio.

Without (a) or (b), the paper is not publishable as a refutation: it would be a
restatement of Brading’s own p. 17 empirical possibility (“It might turn out
that this is the entire spatiotemporal history of the universe”) with the
“might” converted into “must.” The note is still a useful contribution as a
careful statement of the tension, but it should not claim more than the fork.

## 10. Limits of this audit

- I did not re-read Saunders, Pooley or Clifton–Hogarth; their use is assessed
  only against the project’s recorded quotations. The paper’s Saunders and
  Pooley paragraphs are not the load-bearing argument, so this does not affect
  the verdict.
- I did not collate the Brading PDFs against the publisher’s HTML; the text is
  from the published-layout PDFs with the hashes recorded in `SOURCES.md`.
- I did not read Brading 2011, 2012 or 2014. The 2015 p. 16 identity is offered
  as a summary of those works, and the 2014 chapter
  (“Explanation, Special Relativity, and Presentism,” pp. 16–31) is the most
  likely place for a restatement or qualification. The paper’s “Brading holds”
  is therefore inherited from 2015, not independently established across the
  corpus. A referee may ask for those texts.
- This is a logic and text audit, not a priority certificate. I did not search
  for a published statement of the same totality argument; the
  `research/existence-form/alternative-contribution/REVIEW.md` search is the
  project’s record, and its limits (PhilPapers and Scholar unavailable) carry
  over.
- The audit does not claim that Brading’s view is true or false, or that
  presentism fails. It assesses whether the paper’s argument establishes its
  conclusion from Brading’s texts.
