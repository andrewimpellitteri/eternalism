The article is no longer unreadable for claim-level review.

The manuscript's short characterization of Brading is supported, but the source's proposal is stronger and more particular than a generic alternative presentism. Brading rejects the shared assumption that spacetime supplies the world's ontological unity. She sketches a law-constitutive account: the dynamical laws ground the unity of systems and their parts; a system's spatiotemporal extent is what is needed to sustain its dynamics; on that account, a present is local and its extent is empirically determined. She says the proposal needs significant elaboration and that if only the whole universe is one genuine system, the proposal loses to a block account. This is Brading's conditional philosophical/physical program, not an independently demonstrated quantitative discriminator, confirmed empirical result, or reason to select its ontology. It preserves no claim that ontology has been chosen.

The Brading claim in the present draft (that she disputes spacetime simultaneity as the ground of ontological unity and develops an alternative presentist program) is accurate. Its support was previously abstract plus selected passages; this review now has a complete primary-text read at the argument level. The three source records for one paper should still be canonicalized by the assigned owner. The source audit should distinguish a full-text read of the accessible published-layout copy from authentication against the publisher-hosted version of record. I did not independently assess the underlying Newton interpretation or test Brading's empirical proposal.

**Putnam wording matches the second-pass primary audit, but the source records remain inconsistent.** The paper says that Putnam invokes an observer-neutral reality-transfer principle, while the broader closure argument brings in transitive closure and an abundance-of-observers assumption. The second-pass audit of pp. 241–247 supports that distinction and explicitly corrects the first-pass shorthand that attributed the work to mere transitivity of equal existence. It also distinguishes this bridge from geometric transitivity of simultaneity. The existing paper wording is already restrained and should not be strengthened. The first-pass record still describes a partial reading and an imprecise bridge, while a duplicate proposed record exists; harmonize these in source canonicalization before submission. Do not turn Putnam's argument into a consequence of Lorentz geometry alone, and do not collapse determinism, future-contingent truth, or eternalism.

The surrounding argument has two additional contextual assumptions: all causal-past points are already definite as of the point, and condition (iv) leaves some states unsettled at each point. The source-context argument uses those assumptions with invariance and transitivity to rule out added spacelike/future pairs. The source was read as OCR and a journal-paginated reproduction; no printed-page image comparison was possible. Thus neither the printed text's correctness nor an error in Stein's published theorem is established. The manuscript's phrase “too weak” should be aligned with the audit's more exact “false if read in isolation,” while retaining the context/OCR caveat and not presenting the project's reconstructed theorem as established. The frozen passage still awaits its stated printed-page and RC-2 gates.

## Bounded McTaggart 1908 passage check

I checked the original article's opening distinction and immediately following argument in the complete JSTOR-scan reproduction at `https://r.jordan.im/download/philosophy/mctaggart1908.pdf`. The scan identifies the publication as J. Ellis McTaggart, “The Unreality of Time,” *Mind*, New Series 17(68) (October 1908), 457–474, and provides JSTOR stable URL `http://www.jstor.org/stable/2248314`; Oxford Academic independently confirms author, issue, date, pages and DOI `10.1093/mind/XVII.4.457`. The scan is hosted by a third party, not Oxford or JSTOR's article page, so I treat it as a public copy of the original journal scan rather than a publisher-hosted file.

The narrow manuscript claim is confirmed by the primary passage. On p. 458, McTaggart distinguishes permanent earlier/later ordering (his B-series) from changing past/present/future determinations (his A-series). On pp. 459–461 he asks whether a B-series without an A-series can constitute time and argues that change cannot be accounted for by B-order alone; the changing past/present/future determination supplies the relevant change. This is enough for the draft's modest statement that his argument distinguishes temporal ordering, change and A-determinations. It does not verify his overall argument that time is unreal, the controversial premises behind that conclusion, or any solution to the argument.

This is a bounded passage-level confirmation only. The knowledge source record `phil-mctaggart` remains at discovery depth; no whole-paper audit or global depth upgrade is proposed. It resolves only the old paper-ledger mismatch where `SOURCE_AUDIT.md` said selected public-domain passages but gave no locator while the source record said discovery.
