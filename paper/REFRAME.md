## Thesis

Brading's system-relative present does **not**, by itself, establish a
restriction on unrestricted concrete existence. Her account supplies a
principled way to individuate dynamical unities and their presents, but the
local boundedness of each system-present is compatible with the union of all
system-presents covering the entire world history. Recovering a presentist
existential restriction therefore requires an additional actuality or
privilege premise that is not derived from the physics or from dynamical unity
alone. The paper defends this limited conclusion and maps the costs of the
available repairs.

## Target question

Under what conditions does a physically defined local present constitute a
restriction on what exists, rather than merely a division of an eternalist
history into dynamical unities?

This is narrower than "is presentism or eternalism true?" It is a question
about the bridge from a local, physically described present to a claim about
unrestricted existence.

## The existential contrast

Let `W` be the set of concrete events in the world history, and let `U_i` be
the spatiotemporal region that is the present of a dynamical system `i`.

- **Presentism (target):** there is a restriction on unrestricted concrete
  existence; not every event in `W` exists. The restriction is not merely a
  partition of `W` into smaller regions.
- **Eternalism (rival):** no such restriction; every event in `W` exists,
  whatever local presents or dynamical unities are distinguished.
- **System-relative present (Brading's candidate):** `U_i` is fixed by the
  conditions needed to sustain system `i`'s law-constituted unity. It need not
  be a global simultaneity slice and need not be transitive across systems.

The contrast must be stated without assuming that "exists" is tensed or
tenseless. The paper will distinguish:

1. existence (the unrestricted existential claim);
2. presence (being in a system's present);
3. dynamical unity (the law-constituted integration of a system);
4. endurance (the persistence of a system through its present).

Conflating these is the main route by which the coverage problem is hidden.

## The coverage objection

Local boundedness says:

```
U_i is a proper subregion of W, for every system i.
```

It does **not** imply:

```
the union of all U_i is a proper subregion of W.
```

If every event belongs to at least one system-present, the system-relative
account covers `W` and excludes no event from existence. It divides the history
into dynamical unities; it does not restrict what exists. This is the exact
form of the objection. It does not assume Brading's view is false, and it does
not show that every presentist account fails. It shows that local boundedness
alone is insufficient for the advertised global difference.

Two clarifications the paper must make:

- Overlap does not rescue the restriction: overlapping presents can cover `W`
  even more easily.
- Disjointness does not rescue it either: a partition of `W` into disjoint
  proper parts still covers `W`.

## Four repairs and their costs

1. **System-relative existence.** Replace a single unrestricted existence
   predicate with "exists-for-system-i." This blocks the coverage objection by
   changing the question, but it gives up the global presentist claim: there is
   no system-independent fact that future events do not exist. The paper must
   say whether this is still presentism or a relativized ontology.
2. **Privileged actual systems.** Only some systems' presents are actual. This
   restores a restriction, but the selection principle is not derived from the
   physics or from dynamical unity. It needs an independent actuality premise;
   otherwise it is arbitrary.
3. **Restrictions on coexistence.** Not all local presents can coexist. This
   may take the form of a maximal consistent set, a single actual present, or a
   dynamics of present-selection. It risks reintroducing a global present or a
   privileged foliation, and it needs a rule the physics does not supply.
4. **Revised presentism.** Give up global exclusion and accept local or
   functional becoming. This is coherent, but it is a different thesis: it may
   be an eternalist history with a local becoming, not presentism as the
   restriction on existence.

The paper does not select among these. It argues that at least one is needed,
and that each has a cost not settled by the physics.

## What the paper will conclude

**Limited conclusion:** dynamical individuation alone is insufficient to
establish the intended existential restriction. A presentist reading of
Brading's local presents needs an additional actuality/privilege premise, or a
revision of what "presentism" claims. The coverage objection is a burden-shift,
not a refutation of Brading and not a proof of eternalism.

## Section outline and argument obligations

1. **Introduction.** State the target question, why Brading is the test case,
   and the limited conclusion. Do not begin with a general survey of time.
   Obligation: make clear that the paper is about an existence bridge, not
   about all of temporal ontology.
2. **The existential contrast.** Define `W`, `U_i`, existence, presence,
   unity, endurance. Distinguish local boundedness from global restriction.
   Obligation: the definitions must make the coverage objection stateable
   without begging the question.
3. **Brading reconstructed charitably.** Dynamical unity, law-constituted
   present, system-relativity, extended present, non-transitivity. Obligation:
   use her direction of explanation; do not turn her into a global presentist.
   Full-text read depth for Brading 2013/2015.
4. **The coverage objection.** State and defend the union point; give the
   overlap and disjointness clarifications; explain why it is not a refutation.
   Obligation: exact, not rhetorical.
5. **Repairs and costs.** The four repairs above. Obligation: distinguish
   physical motivation from an added actuality principle; do not smuggle in a
   privileged frame.
6. **Assessment.** The limited conclusion; what would change it; how it bears
   on the eternalism/presentism comparison. Obligation: reach a verdict, even a
   negative one, rather than another checklist.
7. **Strongest objections.** Saunders's structural-completeness challenge and
   Pooley's compatibility/robust-passage distinction; the eternalist's reply
   that local presents are admissible; the presentist's reply that actuality is
   primitive. Obligation: answer or concede explicitly.
8. **Conclusion.** What has and has not been established. No ontology
   selection, no empirical prediction.

## Non-claims

- Not a refutation of Brading.
- Not a proof that eternalism is true.
- Not a claim that every presentism fails.
- Not a new empirical prediction or discriminator.
- Not a global priority or novelty claim; the coverage point is elementary and
  the paper's contribution is the focused reconstruction and cost analysis.
- Not a claim that physics alone selects an actuality principle.
